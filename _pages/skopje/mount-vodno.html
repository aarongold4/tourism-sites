Mount Vodno & Millennium Cross

The 66-meter cross above the city, a cable car up, and a ridge walk that ends in a canyon.

Skopje's southern wall is a mountain with a landmark you cannot lose: the Millennium Cross, 66 meters of white steel raised in 2002 on Vodno's summit, visible from every corner of the city by day and glowing over it by night. The mountain beneath it is the capital's playground — forest roads thick with weekend walkers, picnic clearings, and a summit panorama that puts the whole Vardar valley at your feet.

Getting up

The standard route stacks three stages: city transport (or taxi) to Middle Vodno, then the cable car — about ten scenic minutes — to the summit station beside the cross. Fares are a few euros return; the crucial fine print is the schedule, since the lift rests every Monday plus a monthly maintenance day (typically the last Tuesday). Walkers can skip the cable car entirely: the summit trail from Middle Vodno takes a steady 1.5–2 hours, and the smart combination rides up and strolls down.

At the top

The terrace under the cross delivers the full sweep — the city gridded below, the Šar mountains walling the Kosovo horizon, Matka's gorge cutting the ridge to the west. A small café handles coffee and the view; an elevator inside the cross itself operates intermittently and is a bonus rather than a plan. Sunset up here, with the city lights coming on during the descent, is the best free spectacle in Skopje — just confirm the last cable car down.

The ridge to Matka

Vodno's best-kept open secret is that it connects: a marked ridge trail runs west from the summit to Matka Canyon in three to four hours of mostly gentle, mostly shaded walking — combining the city's two essential outdoor sites into one linear day. Walk it westward (Vodno → Matka), reward yourself with trout and a boat at the canyon, and ride bus 60 home. Carry water; the route is well-trodden on weekends and quiet midweek.

Practical notes

Mornings are clearest for photography — summer afternoons haze over the valley. The summit sits 600 meters above the city and runs noticeably cooler; bring a layer outside high summer. And if the cable car's rest day catches you out, treat it as a sign: the walk up through the pines is the better half of the mountain anyway.

Frequently asked questions

When does the Vodno cable car run?

Generally daytime hours, but it closes every Monday and one further day a month for maintenance (commonly the last Tuesday) — check locally before building your day around it. The ride up from Middle Vodno takes about ten minutes and costs a few euros return.

Can you walk up Mount Vodno?

Yes — a well-used trail climbs from Middle Vodno to the summit in roughly 1.5–2 hours, shaded in stretches and busy with locals every weekend morning. Many ride the cable car up and walk down.

How high is the Millennium Cross?

The cross stands 66 meters tall on Vodno's 1,066-meter summit — among the largest crosses in the world, visible from virtually everywhere in Skopje and lit at night.

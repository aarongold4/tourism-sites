HOTELS

Petra Guest House Hotel

The only hotel sharing a wall with the Petra entrance gate. The famous Cave Bar built into Nabataean sandstone, $90-160 nightly rates, and a property whose location-and-character argument is stronger than its consistency-of-quality argument.

Last reviewed April 28, 2026 · 7-minute read · See our editorial standards

The Petra Guest House Hotel has a unique selling proposition: it is the only hotel that shares a wall with the Petra archaeological park entrance. You step out of the hotel, walk 50 meters, and you're at the ticket booth — closer than any other hotel in Wadi Musa, including the Mövenpick Resort across the street and the Crowne Plaza a few minutes' walk away. Combined with its famous Cave Bar (a bar built into a natural sandstone cave on the property, reportedly one of the oldest continuously-operating bars in the world), the Petra Guest House has been a fixture of Wadi Musa for decades.

The honest version of the property today: it's an aging hotel with extraordinary location, distinctive character, and inconsistent execution. The location is what brings travelers here; the Cave Bar is what makes them remember the stay; the rooms are what produce the mixed reviews. This page works through what to actually expect in 2026, including the somewhat confusing brand history (it was historically managed under Crowne Plaza; that relationship has evolved, with the Crowne Plaza brand now associated with a separate newer property in Wadi Musa).

Location

The Petra Guest House sits at the very end of Tourism Street, directly adjacent to the Petra Visitor Centre. The property's eastern wall is essentially the western boundary of the archaeological park itself. From your hotel room to the Petra ticket booth: less than 60 seconds of walking.

This is meaningfully closer to the gate than the Mövenpick Resort Petra (which sits across Tourism Street, perhaps 100 meters from the gate) and significantly closer than the Petra Marriott (about a 12-minute uphill walk) or the Crowne Plaza Resort Petra (about a 3-5 minute walk). For travelers who specifically want the absolute shortest walk to Petra, the Petra Guest House is uniquely positioned.

What the proximity buys you:

  • True 06:00 entry possible without any logistical effort — wake at 05:45, be at the gate at 05:58
  • Frictionless lunch return — you can break the Petra day for a real meal at the hotel
  • Easy Petra by Night logistics — short walk back at 22:30 through quiet courtyards
  • For visitors with limited mobility, the shortest possible distance from accommodation to the Petra entrance

The Cave Bar

The single most-loved feature of the property. The Cave Bar is built into a natural sandstone cave attached to the hotel, with stone walls, low ceilings, antique Bedouin-style decor, and a small outdoor balcony area. Various sources describe it as "one of the oldest continuously-operating bars in the world," with parts of the cave reportedly dating to Nabataean times — claims that are hard to verify rigorously but are part of the property's well-established mythology.

What's verifiable: the Cave Bar is a unique and genuinely interesting space, drinks are reasonably priced for a hotel bar (5-8 JOD for a beer, 12-18 JOD for cocktails), happy hour typically runs 15:00-16:00, and the atmosphere is consistently described in reviews as one of the highlights of the property. Many travelers who critique the rooms still recommend the Cave Bar enthusiastically.

One practical note from recent reviews: the Cave Bar is sometimes used for hotel events that displace casual drop-in service, particularly during peak season. Calling ahead or checking with the hotel desk before walking in for drinks avoids the occasional closed-for-event situation.

The property and rooms

The hotel is built in a low-rise style that fits the Wadi Musa landscape, with rooms in the main building plus a separate cluster of "chalet" rooms with mountain views. The overall aesthetic is traditional Jordanian rather than international-modern — wooden accents, traditional textiles, antique-style furniture in the public areas. The lobby and Cave Bar are the architectural highlights; the standard rooms are less distinctive.

Room categories per public listings:

  • Standard rooms in main building — basic doubles and twins, no view guaranteed, ~$90-130 USD per night
  • Chalet rooms — separate building, often with mountain views, larger, ~$130-220 USD per night
  • Suites and family rooms — variable availability and pricing

Reviews on room quality are mixed in a way that the Mövenpick and Crowne Plaza reviews are not. Some recent reviewers describe rooms as "spacious," "comfortable," and "good value." Others note "dated decor," "occasional maintenance issues," and "inconsistent housekeeping." The chalet rooms with views consistently fare better in reviews than the standard rooms in the main building.

Practical recommendation from review patterns: specifically request a chalet room with view when booking. The price difference (~$40-60 USD per night) is worth it both for the better physical room and for the view, which is genuinely good in the upper chalets looking toward the Petra mountains.

Dining

Two main on-site restaurants per recent reviews:

The breakfast room. Breakfast buffet, consistently well-reviewed. Variety of Jordanian and Western options, fresh, reasonable for the property's category.

The Basin Restaurant. Dinner buffet, more mixed in reviews — "good selection" but "ordinary hotel buffet" themes appear frequently. Around 18-25 JOD per person.

Most travelers who stay here for multiple nights walk out to Tourism Street for dinner at least one night. The Petra Guest House dining is functional rather than destination-worthy.

The Crowne Plaza brand history

The Petra Guest House was historically operated under the Crowne Plaza/IHG brand, with the property's name and the Crowne Plaza branding appearing together in older listings. Based on recent reviews and property listings, the Crowne Plaza brand has shifted to a separate newer property in Wadi Musa (now branded the Crowne Plaza Resort Petra), and the Petra Guest House operates under different management.

Some review templates and aggregator listings still mention "Crowne Plaza managed" or "previously Crowne Plaza" — this is partly residual labeling and partly traveler confusion. The practical implication: don't expect the consistent international-brand service standard that an active Crowne Plaza affiliation would imply. The Petra Guest House is now best understood as an independent property with distinctive character and inconsistent execution, not as a brand-standard 4-star hotel.

Pricing — what to expect in 2026

Recent booking-platform pricing (Booking.com, Trip.com, Hotels.com) shows the following typical ranges:

  • Summer low season: $80-110 USD per night for standard rooms, breakfast included
  • Shoulder season: $110-150 USD per night
  • Peak season (October-November, March-April): $130-180 USD per night
  • Chalet rooms: $40-60 USD premium over standard rates

The pricing is positioned roughly half the Mövenpick rate and at the upper end of the mid-range Wadi Musa market. For travelers who specifically want the location and Cave Bar character at a much lower price than the Mövenpick, this is the right hotel.

The decision: Petra Guest House vs. Mövenpick

The cleanest comparison in the Wadi Musa luxury market. The trade-offs:

Petra Guest House wins on:

  • Absolute location (literally at the gate)
  • Price ($90-160 vs $180-400, roughly half)
  • Unique character (Cave Bar is genuinely one-of-a-kind)
  • Property scale (smaller, less institutional)

Mövenpick wins on:

  • Consistency of quality (predictable 5-star standard)
  • Restaurant variety (4 venues vs 2)
  • Property condition (newer renovations, less wear)
  • Service polish (international-brand training)
  • Room quality consistency (less reviewer variance)

For most travelers, the choice hinges on price tolerance and quality consistency preferences. Travelers comfortable with the chance of a worn room in exchange for genuine character and significant savings should choose the Petra Guest House. Travelers wanting a predictable smooth stay should choose the Mövenpick.

Our practical recommendation: For a one-night Petra visit, the Mövenpick is the cleaner choice. For a 2-3 night Petra visit with budget consciousness, the Petra Guest House (specifically requesting a chalet room with view) often produces a more memorable stay — the Cave Bar evenings genuinely add to the trip in a way the Mövenpick can't replicate.

Frequently asked questions

Where exactly is the Petra Guest House located?
Sharing a wall with the Petra archaeological park entrance. The property is the closest hotel to the Petra gate of any in Wadi Musa — closer than the Mövenpick Resort Petra (across the street) or the Crowne Plaza (3-5 minute walk). You can step out of the hotel and be inside Petra in less than 60 seconds. This proximity is the property's primary value proposition.
Is it still managed by Crowne Plaza?
The relationship has evolved. The Petra Guest House was historically operated under the Crowne Plaza/IHG brand. Based on recent reviews and property listings, the Crowne Plaza brand has shifted to a separate newer property in Wadi Musa (Crowne Plaza Resort Petra), and the Petra Guest House now operates with a different management arrangement. The famous Cave Bar and the architectural character remain, but the international-brand service standard has loosened. Some recent reviews mention "previously Crowne Plaza, no longer" as context.
What is the Cave Bar?
A bar built into a natural sandstone cave on the property — reportedly one of the oldest continuously-operating bars in the world (parts of the cave structure date to Nabataean times). The Cave Bar is the property's most-loved feature and a genuine reason to stay here even if the rooms aren't the best in town. Drinks are reasonably priced for a hotel bar, the atmosphere is genuinely unique, and the small balcony area is a nice spot for sunset.
How much does it cost?
Recent booking-platform pricing shows standard rooms at $90-160 USD per night depending on season — significantly cheaper than the Mövenpick ($180-400) or Petra Marriott ($200-320). The chalet rooms with mountain views run $130-220 per night and are worth the upgrade if available. Rates typically include breakfast.
What's the room situation actually like?
Reviews are inconsistent — some travelers report excellent, recently-updated rooms; others report dated decor, occasional maintenance issues, and inconsistent housekeeping. The chalet rooms with views are widely preferred over the standard rooms in the main building. The hotel is not new construction, and the wear shows in the standard rooms more than the chalets.
Should I stay here or at the Mövenpick?
Depends on your priorities. The Petra Guest House wins on absolute location (literally at the gate vs across the street), price ($90-160 vs $180-400), and unique character (the Cave Bar is genuinely one-of-a-kind). The Mövenpick wins on consistency of quality, polished 5-star service, restaurant variety, and newer renovations. For budget-conscious travelers or those who specifically value the location and Cave Bar, the Petra Guest House is the better choice. For travelers who want the smoothest possible Petra visit and don't mind paying premium for it, the Mövenpick is right.

Petra tours from Eilat

One of the most common day-trip patterns to Petra globally. 110 km / 2 hours from the Wadi Araba border, 13-14 hour day tours at $220-320 USD per person. Here's the practical breakdown including border procedures.

Eilat sits 5 km from one of the most-used border crossings between Israel and Jordan, and Petra is 110 km / 2 hours of driving on the other side. The combination has made Petra day trips from Eilat one of the standard tourism patterns of the broader Israel-Jordan-Sinai region — thousands of travelers each year cross from Eilat in the morning and return the same evening, treating Petra as a day excursion from an Israel-based trip.

This page covers the practical details: how the day tours actually work, what they cost, what the border crossing involves, and the alternatives for travelers wanting more than a single day at Petra. The framing throughout: efficient for time-constrained Israel travelers, less compelling when there's flexibility to spend a night or two in Wadi Musa.

The route

Eilat is at the northern tip of the Red Sea / Gulf of Aqaba, separated from Jordan by 5 km of desert containing the Wadi Araba (Israeli name: Yitzhak Rabin) border crossing. The crossing is the southernmost of three Israel-Jordan border points. Once across, you're 5 km from central Aqaba; from there it's the standard 130 km Desert Highway route north to Wadi Musa.

Total driving from Eilat to Petra: about 110 km via the border (the cross-border distance is shorter than the full Aqaba-Petra leg because the border is southwest of Aqaba proper). About 2 hours of pure driving plus 30-60 minutes for the border crossing.

The day tour format

A typical Eilat-to-Petra day tour:

  • 06:00-06:30: Pickup from your Eilat hotel.
  • 06:30-07:00: Drive to the Wadi Araba border (5 km / 10 minutes).
  • 07:00-08:00: Border crossing — Israeli exit, walk across, Jordanian entry, visa processing.
  • 08:00-10:00: Drive from Aqaba to Petra (130 km / 2 hours).
  • 10:00-10:15: Arrive Petra Visitor Centre, ticket processing.
  • 10:15-12:30: Guided visit: Siq, Treasury, Street of Facades, Royal Tombs, Roman theatre.
  • 12:30-13:30: Lunch (Basin Restaurant inside Petra or a Wadi Musa restaurant).
  • 13:30-15:30: Continued visit — typically Monastery climb OR lower-city monuments.
  • 15:30-16:00: Return walk to Visitor Centre.
  • 16:00-18:00: Drive back to Aqaba.
  • 18:00-19:00: Border crossing (reverse direction).
  • 19:00-19:30: Drop-off at Eilat hotel.

Total: 13-14 hours door-to-door, with about 5-6 hours actually at Petra.

What it costs

Day tour pricing breaks down approximately:

  • Tour operator fee: $150-220 USD per person for shared group tours (8-15 people in a minivan or small bus).
  • Jordan visa at Wadi Araba border: 60 JOD (~$85 USD), paid in cash or by card at the border. Note this is higher than the 40 JOD visa fee at Jordanian airports — the Wadi Araba crossing has a premium.
  • Israeli border exit tax: 109 NIS (~$30 USD), payable at the border on the Israeli side.
  • Petra entrance fee: 50 JOD (~$70 USD) for the 1-day non-accommodated visitor rate. Most tour operators include this in the package price; verify before booking.

All-in total: typically $220-320 USD per person for a standard group tour, with entrance fee included. Private tours run $300-500 per person depending on group size. Premium tours with luxury vehicles, senior guides, or specific add-ons (helicopter, etc.) range higher.

The Wadi Araba border crossing

The crossing is well-organized and routine for tour operators. Practical details:

Hours: Sunday-Thursday 06:30-20:00, Friday 08:00-20:00, closed Saturdays for Israeli Shabbat. Also closed for Yom Kippur and major Israeli national holidays. Tour operators check the schedule when booking; confirm if your tour date falls near a holiday.

Process: Israeli exit (passport stamped, exit tax paid), short walk across the border zone (about 200 meters), Jordanian entry (passport stamped, visa processed if you're paying at the border), customs (typically minimal for tour groups). Total process takes 30-60 minutes for most travelers; longer if the border is busy or if individual processing complications arise.

Documents needed: Passport with at least 6 months validity remaining, sufficient blank pages for stamps. No advance visa needed for most nationalities (US, EU, UK, Australia, Canada, etc. — Jordan offers visa-on-arrival at the border). Children require their own passports.

Passport stamps: Israeli stamps in your passport are NOT a problem for entering Jordan. The reverse — Jordan stamps causing issues for later travel — is also generally not a problem these days, though travelers planning future visits to specific countries (Iran, Sudan, some others) should check current entry policies.

Tour vs DIY

Three considerations weigh in favor of tours for day trips:

Logistics handling. A 13-14 hour day with border crossings, long drives, and a Petra visit on a tight schedule is genuinely stressful to navigate independently. Tour operators handle every transition smoothly. For a one-time, time-constrained visit, the convenience is significant.

Border efficiency. Tour vehicles often process faster at the border than individual travelers, with pre-cleared passenger manifests and operator-staff escort assistance. The time savings vary but can be meaningful when the border is busy.

Cost competitiveness. Once you factor in the unavoidable costs (visa fees, exit tax, entrance fee, transport, fuel), DIY day-tripping isn't dramatically cheaper than tour pricing. The tour might be $50-80 more than the absolute DIY minimum, but you get vehicle, driver, guide, and logistics support for that premium.

DIY makes more sense for:

Multi-day trips. If you're staying overnight in Jordan, the Jordan Pass becomes valuable, the daily-tour logistics premium disappears, and the flexibility of self-directed travel becomes meaningful.

Travelers with prior Middle East experience. If border crossings and Middle East logistics are familiar territory, the tour premium is less worth paying.

Budget-focused Israel-side travelers. Some Israeli travelers cross to Jordan independently with rental cars, public buses, or hired drivers on the Jordanian side, finding ways to reduce costs below standard tour pricing. Requires significant logistical work.

Multi-day alternatives

For travelers with more flexibility, multi-day trips from Eilat are typically more rewarding than day tours:

Petra overnight (2 days, 1 night). Cross the border, drive to Petra, overnight in Wadi Musa, full second day at Petra, return to Eilat. Allows for the morning Treasury experience in proper morning light plus a complete Monastery climb without time pressure. Pricing 380-580 USD per person.

Petra and Wadi Rum (3 days, 2 nights). Adds an overnight Bedouin camp in Wadi Rum to the basic Petra trip. The most-recommended format for first-time Jordan travelers from Eilat — adds significantly to the experience while keeping the overall trip length manageable. Pricing 580-880 USD per person.

Full Jordan loop (4-7 days). Petra + Wadi Rum + Dead Sea + Jerash + Amman, returning to Eilat or onward to Israel via the northern border crossings. Pricing 1,200-2,200+ USD per person depending on accommodation tier.

Practical considerations

Currency: Israel uses the New Israeli Shekel (NIS); Jordan uses the Jordanian Dinar (JOD). The Wadi Araba border has currency exchange on both sides; rates are acceptable but not great. Tour operators typically suggest carrying both Israeli shekels and US dollars (the latter for visa fees and Jordan-side purchases). ATMs in Jordan dispense JOD using international cards.

Language: Hebrew on the Israeli side, Arabic on the Jordanian side. English is widely understood in tourist contexts on both sides; tour guides are generally fluent in English plus other languages depending on the operator.

Time zones: Both Israel and Jordan are in the same time zone (UTC+2 standard, UTC+3 daylight saving). No time adjustment needed.

Where to book: Standard channels — Viator, GetYourGuide, and direct booking with Eilat-based or Jordanian tour operators. Hotel concierges in Eilat can book through their preferred operators. Cruise passengers should compare ship shore excursions against independent operators; the price difference is significant and the independent quality is generally comparable.

Frequently asked questions

Can I do Petra as a day trip from Eilat?
Yes — this is one of the most common Petra day-trip patterns globally. Eilat sits 5 km from the Wadi Araba border crossing into Jordan, and from there Petra is 110 km / 2 hours by road. A typical day tour from Eilat runs 13-14 hours door-to-door, with about 4-5 hours actually inside Petra. The compressed format works but Petra rewards more time than a day tour allows.
What does it cost?
Approximately $220-320 USD per person for a standard day tour from Eilat in 2026, including all fees. The total includes: tour operator fee ($150-220), Jordan visa fee at the border ($60 for the Wadi Araba crossing — note this is higher than the $40 visa at airports), Petra entrance fee (50 JOD for non-Jordan-Pass day visits, included by most operators), and the Israeli border exit tax (currently 109 NIS / ~$30). Cruise shore excursions from Eilat run higher ($250-380) for the ship-hold guarantee.
What about the Jordan Pass — does it work for day trips from Eilat?
No, in the typical sense. The Jordan Pass waives the visa fee if you stay 2+ nights in Jordan. Day trippers from Eilat (returning to Israel the same day) don't meet the 2-night minimum, so the visa waiver doesn't apply. You pay the 60 JOD Wadi Araba visa fee separately. If you're staying overnight in Petra or elsewhere in Jordan, the Jordan Pass becomes worthwhile.
Is the border crossing complicated?
It takes time but isn't complex. The Wadi Araba (also called Yitzhak Rabin) crossing south of Aqaba is open Sun-Thu 06:30-20:00, Fri 08:00-20:00, closed Saturdays for Israeli Shabbat. The full crossing process typically takes 30-60 minutes including Israeli exit, Jordanian entry, visa processing, and customs. Tour operators handle the logistics; independent travelers walk the same process with their own documentation. Israeli passport stamps in your passport are fine for entering Jordan; the reverse can complicate later travel to certain countries.
Should I take a tour or DIY?
Tours are usually better from Eilat for two reasons. (1) The day-trip rhythm benefits from someone else handling all the border and Petra logistics on a tight schedule. (2) Tour vehicles cross the border with pre-cleared passenger lists, often moving faster than individual border processing. The cost difference is modest ($50-80 versus the DIY total once you factor in border taxes and transport) and the convenience is significant. DIY makes more sense for multi-day trips where flexibility outweighs the day-trip logistics premium.

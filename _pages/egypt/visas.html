EGYPT TRAVEL

Egypt visas, current and decoded

Thirty days, around thirty dollars, two ways to get it — online before you fly or at a bank counter after you land. The fee just rose and may rise again, which is exactly why this page exists with a date on it.

Last reviewed June 6, 2026◆5-minute read

Photo: Radosław Botev · CC BY 3.0 pl · Wikimedia Commons

Egypt's tourist visa is genuinely easy — most Western passports walk in on a purchased 30-day visa — and genuinely volatile in its details. The fee rose from $25 to $30 for single-entry visas (both on-arrival and e-visa) in spring 2026, multiple-entry moved to the $60-65 band, and reporting since points to further legislated increases working through the system. Treat the numbers here as the verified-at-review-date snapshot, and the official portal — visa2egypt.gov.eg — as the price of record the week you fly.

Option one: the e-visa (the default)

Apply at the official portal with passport details and a card; approval typically lands within days; print it and carry it. Single-entry: valid three months from issue, 30-day stay. Multiple-entry: six months' validity, 30 days per stay. Why it's the default: same price as the counter, zero airport queue after a long-haul, and the approval-in-hand calm. The one warning that matters: use only the official portal — a thriving industry of lookalike visa sites charges double for the same form, and search results rank them above the real thing.

Option two: the visa on arrival

Before immigration at Cairo airport, bank counters sell the visa sticker — exact USD cash is the reliable currency, clean bills, with euros and pounds accepted at unfavorable arithmetic. Buy, stick it in the passport, queue for immigration. It adds 15-45 minutes to arrival depending on the hour, which is why the layover plan budgets for it explicitly. It remains the right choice for spontaneous trips and the occasional nationality the e-portal mishandles.

The fine print worth knowing

A visa is permission to ask, not a guarantee — immigration makes the final call, and the standard kit (onward ticket, first hotel address) answers the standard questions. Overstays are fined at departure; extensions are possible through the Mogamma's successors but are a bureaucratic day you should plan not to need. Sinai-only resort trips have their own free-stamp regime that does not cover Cairo — pyramid-bound travelers need the real visa. And passports want six months' validity from arrival, the global default Egypt enforces.

Frequently asked questions

How much is the Egypt visa?

As of the most recent verified change (March 2026), the single-entry visa-on-arrival and e-visa both run $30 USD, the multiple-entry around $60-65 — and at least one report points to a further legislated increase since. This is the fastest-moving fact on this site: confirm the current figure on the official visa2egypt.gov.eg portal before flying.

Visa on arrival or e-visa — which should I get?

E-visa for most travelers: same price, done before departure, no bank-counter queue after a red-eye, and a printed approval in hand. Visa on arrival remains perfectly workable — bank counters before immigration, payable in exact USD cash — and suits spontaneous bookings and nationalities the e-portal serves poorly.

Which nationalities can get the visa on arrival?

Most Western passports — US, UK, EU, Canada, Australia, NZ and others — qualify for the 30-day tourist visa on arrival; many other nationalities must apply in advance through an embassy or the e-portal. Eligibility lists shift: check your specific passport on the official portal, not a tour company's summary.

Do I need cash for the visa on arrival?

Bring exact USD cash for it regardless of what you've read about card acceptance — the bank counters have historically wanted clean dollar bills, and 'exact change' prevents the no-change dance starting before you've even entered the country. Euros and pounds are usually accepted at worse math.

Why this site exists

Most Giza guides are written from press releases and recycled blog posts. They'll tell you the Great Pyramid is 4,500 years old and that you should "beware of scams" — and then leave you to discover at the gate that there are three different ticket tiers, that the camel handlers operate on a system designed to separate you from cash in stages, and that the difference between a good morning visit and a miserable midday one is about two hours and a smart entrance choice.

gizatourism.com covers Giza the way it deserves: the plateau in detail, Cairo as the base it actually is, and the day-trip triangle of Saqqara, Memphis, and Dahshur that turns a pyramid photo stop into a genuine Old Kingdom education. Every page carries a "last reviewed" date and gets re-checked as prices, hours, and policies change — Egypt changes these often.

When tour and hotel recommendations carry booking links, those links may earn this site a commission at no cost to you. Recommendations are made first and linked second — never the other way around. More in our affiliate disclosure and editorial standards.

If you only read three pages

Start with entrance fees explained — the tiered ticket system is the single most confusing thing about Giza and the page settles it at current prices. Then the tout playbook, because the six standard routines stop working once you've read them. Then opening hours, which is really a page about one decision: being at the gate at 7am, the highest-value move in all of Giza planning.

The shape of a great visit

The pattern that works, distilled from everything on this site: sleep pyramid-side the night before (a $35 rooftop guesthouse or the Mena House, per budget), walk to the gate for opening with tickets already bought online, spend the cool first hours on the Great Pyramid and your chosen interior, ride the free shuttle to the panoramic viewpoint, walk down through the valley temple to the Sphinx, and exit before noon as the heat and the buses peak. Give the afternoon to the Grand Egyptian Museum at the plateau's edge — where Khufu's 4,500-year-old ship now lives — and the evening to a rooftop facing the floodlit skyline. If you have a second morning, spend it at Saqqara, where the pyramid form was invented and the crowds never arrive.

The plateau in numbers, right now

The operational facts as of this page's review date — each linked to its living page, because these change: general admission 700 EGP (~$14), Great Pyramid interior 1,500 EGP with a ~150-visitor daily cap, the second pyramid's interior on rotation at 200-280 EGP (the full tier system). Hours run 7am-4pm daily, compressed in Ramadan (details). Private vehicles are banned on-site — a free shuttle loops the monuments — and payment has gone card-first at the gates, with online purchase at the Ministry's portal the smart default. The Grand Egyptian Museum at the plateau's edge is fully open, holds Tutankhamun and Khufu's ship, and sells foreign tickets online only (museum guide). A ride-hail from central Cairo runs about 100-150 EGP at dawn (the crossing, solved).

The fastest route through these seventy-plus pages depends on where you are in the planning arc. Just decided to go: start with best time to visit and the itineraries — pick the day-count that matches your trip and let its links pull you outward. Deciding what to book: Tours and Hotels carry the frameworks and the honest reviews. Curious what you'll be looking at: Things to See for the monuments, History for the evidence behind them. Every page links its dependencies, so any entry point eventually surfaces everything that matters to your trip.

What this guide refuses to do

A short list that defines the rest: no recycled prices without dates, no fabricated author personas, no recommendations sold to the highest commission, no pretending the touts and the heat away, and no padding where a straight answer fits in a sentence. Giza has enough mystique without its guides manufacturing more; what it lacks is someone keeping the practical facts current and the verdicts honest — the entire job this site applied for.

Kept current, on purpose

Egypt revises ticket prices, opening hours, and access rules more often than any guidebook cycle can track — general admission has stepped upward repeatedly in recent years, interior access rotates between pyramids with conservation work, and 2025 alone brought a vehicle ban, a free shuttle system, and card-first payment to the plateau. Every operational page on this site carries a "last reviewed" date and gets re-checked on a rolling basis, and reader corrections via the contact page are applied with updated dates. If a date looks stale, say so — that's the system working.

HOTEL REVIEW

Old Village Resort

The most distinctive luxury property in the Petra area — a small boutique resort on the Tayba ridge with traditional stone architecture, villa-style buildings, and a strong sense of secluded retreat. Different rhythm from the chain 5-stars. Here's what staying here actually involves.

Last reviewed June 5, 2026 · 6-minute read · See our editorial standards

Old Village Resort occupies an unusual position in the Petra hotel landscape — a boutique luxury property in a market dominated by international chains and mid-range independents. The combination of distinctive architecture, small scale (about 60 rooms across multiple villa-style buildings), traditional materials, and a hillside garden setting makes it the most character-driven of the Petra-area luxury hotels. For travelers who specifically value boutique-style accommodation and are willing to trade some chain-hotel consistency for atmosphere, Old Village is genuinely worth considering.

This page covers the property in practical terms — what it offers, what staying there is like, who it works for, and how it compares to alternatives. The framing throughout: a distinctive option for travelers willing to lean into the boutique character rather than treating it as a less-amenity-rich version of a chain 5-star.

The setting

The hotel occupies a hillside position on the Tayba ridge, about 5 km from the Petra Visitor Centre and roughly 200 meters above central Wadi Musa. Like the other Tayba ridge properties (Mövenpick Nabatean Castle, Petra Marriott), the elevation provides views back toward the Petra mountains across the valley.

What distinguishes Old Village specifically is the property layout. Rather than a single large hotel building, the resort is configured as a cluster of stone-built villa-style structures connected by garden paths, with terraced areas at multiple elevations. The result feels more like a small village (the property name is on the nose) than a conventional hotel — guests wander between buildings, sit on multiple garden terraces, eat in different dining locations depending on the meal and weather. The atmosphere is closer to a Mediterranean or Aegean boutique resort than to a standard Middle Eastern 5-star hotel.

The hillside terracing creates microclimates and visual variety — some rooms have garden-level entries with private outdoor space, others occupy upper levels with elevated views. Walking between the property's various amenities (rooms, restaurants, pool, lounge areas) involves steps and paths rather than corridors. This is part of the property's character but worth knowing if mobility is a consideration.

The rooms

About 60 rooms across multiple categories:

  • Standard rooms. 30-35 sq m, traditional stone construction, hand-crafted furniture mixing modern comfort with Bedouin-style decor. 160-220 USD per night for 2026 rates.
  • Deluxe rooms. 40-45 sq m, slightly larger spaces with better positioning for views, often with private terraces. 220-280 USD per night.
  • Villa suites. 60-90 sq m, multi-room villa-style accommodations, sometimes with private gardens or pools, designed for couples wanting maximum privacy. 350-500 USD per night.

Room finishes use traditional materials throughout — stone walls, wooden ceiling beams, hand-woven textiles, copper and brass fixtures. The aesthetic is consistent across categories; the differences are in size and positioning rather than style. Modern amenities (Wi-Fi, climate control, contemporary bathrooms, satellite TV) are present but discrete rather than prominent.

The variability point: not every room offers the same view quality, and the hillside terracing means some rooms get better natural light or quieter positioning than others. Booking specific room categories is more meaningful than at standardized chain hotels — discuss positioning preferences when booking.

Dining

Three dining spaces on the property:

Main restaurant. Stone-walled dining room serving breakfast (included with rates), lunch, and dinner. Menu mixes traditional Jordanian cuisine (mansaf, maqluba, mixed mezze) with Mediterranean and continental options. Breakfast is buffet-style with regional specialties; dinner is à la carte. Quality is consistently strong with occasionally exceptional executions of traditional dishes. Expect 25-45 JOD per person for dinner.

Garden terrace. Outdoor seating used for breakfast and lunch in good weather, and for sunset dining. The terrace sits at one of the property's better vantage points — sunset light on the Petra mountains from this position is a distinctive feature of the stay.

Lounge bar. Smaller stone-walled bar serving drinks and light snacks throughout the day. Decor leans heavily into Bedouin aesthetics (low seating, cushions, copper pieces, shisha service). Popular for pre-dinner drinks and late-evening conversation. Drink pricing is upper-tier but consistent with luxury-property norms.

Pool, wellness, and grounds

The pool is small by chain-hotel standards (designed for a 60-room property rather than a 300-room resort) but pleasantly proportioned. Surrounded by garden terraces and stone walls, it has a more secluded feel than the larger chain pools. Open seasonally (April-October typically), heated during shoulder seasons.

A small spa offers traditional Middle Eastern wellness treatments — hammam-style steam treatments, scrub massages with locally-sourced ingredients (Dead Sea salts, olive oil-based products), traditional massage techniques. The spa is intimate rather than elaborate but the treatment quality is strong; expect 60-100 JOD for 60-90 minute treatments.

The property's gardens are a genuine feature — extensive terraced plantings with native Mediterranean species, hidden seating areas, walking paths connecting the buildings. Travelers who appreciate landscape design will spend meaningful time exploring the property's outdoor spaces.

Service character

The service at Old Village leans toward attentive personalization rather than standardized chain efficiency. Staff get to know returning guests; the front desk is more conversational than transactional; restaurant servers and bartenders tend to spend longer engaging with guests. For travelers who prefer this style of service, it's a genuine plus.

The trade-off: things sometimes operate on Jordanian time rather than corporate-precision time. Restaurant reservations may run slightly behind; shuttle pickups may not adhere to exactly published schedules; check-in and check-out processes are more relaxed than rapid. Travelers expecting Hyatt-level service consistency will sometimes find the pace frustrating; travelers comfortable with a more relaxed rhythm appreciate the human texture.

The Petra access logistics

Like the other Tayba ridge properties, Old Village runs a complimentary shuttle to the Visitor Centre. The schedule is more flexible than at the larger Mövenpick properties — typically arranged on request through the front desk or via a brief phone call, rather than running on a fixed timetable. This works well for travelers who want flexibility; it requires slightly more communication than the larger properties.

Taxis from the property to the Visitor Centre cost 5-8 JOD one-way and are readily available through the front desk. For Petra by Night travelers, the property arranges dedicated transport for the 20:30 show.

Who it's best for

Old Village works particularly well for several profiles:

  • Couples on romantic getaways or honeymoons. The boutique character, atmospheric setting, and personalized service align with couples-focused travel. Several rooms and villa configurations are specifically positioned for romantic use.
  • Travelers prioritizing distinctive character. If you've stayed in many chain 5-stars and want something with a stronger sense of place, Old Village delivers that.
  • Slower-paced multi-night visits. The property rewards 2-3 night stays where you can engage with the multiple dining spaces, walk the gardens, and use the spa.
  • Travelers who value architecture and design. The traditional construction and considered design make it interesting in itself, not just a base camp for the Petra visit.

It's less ideal for:

  • Families with young children. The pool isn't large, the room layouts don't favor families, and the contemplative atmosphere clashes with high-energy family travel.
  • Travelers expecting full chain-hotel amenities. No business center, limited fitness facilities, no major entertainment options. The property is intentionally smaller in scope than chain 5-stars.
  • Mobility-impaired travelers. The hillside terracing and multi-building layout involve stairs and uneven paths in ways that the more standardized chain properties don't.
  • Single-night quick stops. The property's value compounds across multiple nights; a single overnight doesn't fully access what's distinctive about it.

How to book

Standard channels (Booking.com, Expedia) list the property reliably. Direct booking via the resort's website sometimes offers slightly better rates or modest perks. Travelers booking villa-suite categories often benefit from direct conversation with the property to discuss positioning preferences and any specific requests.

High-season booking (March-May, September-November) should happen 4-8 weeks ahead for best rate and category availability; the small 60-room scale means peak periods can sell out earlier than at larger properties. Shoulder-season flexibility is greater.

Frequently asked questions

What's distinctive about the Old Village Resort?
The boutique character — Old Village is a small luxury property (about 60 rooms across multiple villa-style buildings) that operates more like a private resort village than a conventional hotel. Architecture uses traditional stone construction with reclaimed Bedouin furniture and decor. The property occupies a hillside position on the Tayba ridge with extensive gardens, multiple terrace areas, and a strong sense of secluded retreat. For travelers who prefer character properties over standardized 5-star chains, it's the most distinctive option in the Petra area.
How does it compare to the Mövenpick Nabatean Castle?
Same neighborhood (Tayba ridge), very different style. The Mövenpick Nabatean Castle is a substantial 5-star chain hotel with corporate amenities and standardized 5-star service. Old Village is a smaller boutique property with more character and less standardization — fewer amenities, more atmosphere, slightly more variable service. Pricing is roughly comparable at the upper end. For travelers prioritizing consistency and full amenities, choose the Mövenpick; for travelers prioritizing distinctive character, choose Old Village.
What does it cost?
Approximately 160-280 USD per night for a standard room in 2026, depending on season. Upper-tier suites and villas range to 350-500 USD. Rates typically include breakfast at the main restaurant. The pricing overlaps with the Mövenpick Nabatean Castle but skews slightly lower for comparable standard categories; the premium villas command upper-tier pricing comparable to the Mövenpick's executive suites.
How do I get to Petra from the hotel?
Complimentary shuttle service to the Visitor Centre, similar to the other Tayba ridge properties. Schedule is more flexible than the larger Mövenpick property — typically on-demand rather than fixed timetables, reflecting the smaller scale and more personalized service. Taxis from the property cost 5-8 JOD one-way for trips outside the shuttle hours. The 5 km distance to the Visitor Centre means you depend on transport rather than walking.
Is it good for families?
Mixed. The property's strengths (small scale, distinctive character, contemplative atmosphere, hillside gardens) work better for couples and adults than for families with young children. The villa-style accommodations can work for families with older children. For families with under-10s prioritizing entertainment options, swimming pool focus, and standardized amenities, the larger chain properties (Mövenpick Resort Petra, Petra Marriott) are typically a better fit. Old Village is the romantic-getaway/honeymoon-retreat type rather than the family-vacation type.

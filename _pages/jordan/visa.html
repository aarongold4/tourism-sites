JORDAN VISA

Jordan visa requirements

The visa-on-arrival rules, the 40 JOD fee at airports versus 60 JOD at Wadi Araba, eligible nationalities, and the Jordan Pass waiver. The practical guide to entering Jordan for visitors from most Western nations.

Last reviewed April 28, 2026 · 7-minute read · See our editorial standards

Jordan operates one of the more straightforward visa regimes in the Middle East: most Western travelers can obtain a 30-day single-entry visa on arrival at the major airports and land borders for 40 JOD (~$56 USD), with a few specific exceptions. Pre-arranged e-visas and the Jordan Pass waiver provide alternatives for travelers who want to handle visa logistics in advance or save money on the combined visa-plus-Petra-entry cost. This page covers the practical details — who needs what, where to get it, what it costs, and how the Jordan Pass changes the math.

A note before the details: visa policies change. The facts on this page reflect the situation as of April 2026, but always check the Jordan Ministry of Interior's official portal or your local Jordanian embassy before booking expensive travel. The rules below are accurate at the time of writing.

Who can get a visa on arrival

Approximately 120 nationalities are eligible for visa on arrival in Jordan. The list includes:

  • North America: United States, Canada
  • European Union: All 27 member states
  • United Kingdom: Yes
  • Schengen non-EU: Switzerland, Norway, Iceland
  • Asia-Pacific: Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Malaysia, Thailand
  • Latin America: Most countries including Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Mexico
  • Middle East and North Africa: Most countries except some neighbors with specific arrangements

Nationalities not eligible for visa-on-arrival — including most of South Asia (India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka), parts of Africa, and a number of other countries — must apply for an e-visa through the Ministry of Interior's online portal in advance. Approval typically takes 1-2 weeks but can run longer. Check your specific nationality on the Jordan Ministry of Foreign Affairs website before assuming visa-on-arrival applies.

The cost

The standard single-entry visa fee is 40 JOD (~$56 USD), payable in:

  • Jordanian Dinars (cash)
  • US Dollars (cash, often accepted)
  • Credit or debit card

The visa is valid for 30 days from entry and is single-entry — if you leave Jordan and want to return, you'll need to pay again (or use a multi-entry visa from the start). Multi-entry visas valid for 6 months cost 120 JOD; these can be obtained at airports on arrival or through the e-visa portal.

The Wadi Araba premium. The land border crossing at Wadi Araba (the one travelers from Eilat use) charges 60 JOD for the same visa — a 20 JOD ($28 USD) premium over the airport rate. This is the official rate, not a scam or negotiation. The premium exists because the Wadi Araba border is in a free trade zone with separate fiscal arrangements. For travelers crossing from Eilat, this is one of the financial considerations that makes the Jordan Pass particularly attractive — the pass waives the visa fee entirely, eliminating both the standard and premium charges.

Where to get a visa on arrival

Queen Alia International Airport (Amman / AMM): The main international entry point. Visa counter is clearly marked before passport control. Payment by cash or card. Process takes 5-15 minutes in typical conditions; longer during peak arrivals.

King Hussein International Airport (Aqaba / AQJ): Similar process to Amman. Aqaba is a Special Economic Zone, which sometimes affects entry processing — but visa fees are the standard 40 JOD.

Sheikh Hussein Bridge (Jordan River crossing from northern Israel): Visa on arrival available. The crossing is north of the Sea of Galilee, used by travelers coming from northern Israel or Jerusalem (when not crossing at Allenby).

Wadi Araba / Yitzhak Rabin Border Terminal (from Eilat): Visa on arrival available at the 60 JOD rate. See the getting from Eilat page for the full crossing logistics.

The critical exception — Allenby/King Hussein Bridge: The major crossing between Jordan and the West Bank (used by most travelers going to or from Jerusalem) does NOT issue visas on arrival. If you're crossing here from the Palestinian side, you must have a pre-arranged e-visa or a valid Jordan Pass. This catches many travelers off guard.

Saudi Arabia land borders (Durra, Mudawara): Standard visa-on-arrival process, but bring cash for fees since card processing is less reliable than at airports.

The e-visa option

The Jordan Ministry of Interior operates an official e-visa portal where eligible travelers can apply for a visa in advance. The process:

  1. Confirm your nationality is eligible (check the Ministry's list)
  2. Prepare a passport scan, photo, and travel itinerary
  3. Complete the online application (about 15-20 minutes)
  4. Pay the 40 JOD fee online
  5. Receive the e-visa PDF by email in 1-10 business days
  6. Print the e-visa and carry it with your passport on arrival

The e-visa is useful primarily for travelers who:

  • Are entering at the Allenby Bridge (no visa-on-arrival)
  • Hold nationalities not eligible for visa-on-arrival
  • Want to pre-clear paperwork rather than queue at arrival
  • Are entering on a tight connection where airport processing time matters

For most Western visitors flying into Amman or Aqaba on direct flights with reasonable layover time, visa-on-arrival is simpler and equally fast. The e-visa process adds a week or two to your pre-travel checklist for no real benefit.

The Jordan Pass alternative

The Jordan Pass is a combined tourism product that includes the visa fee waiver plus entry to Petra and 40+ other Jordanian attractions. The pass costs 70 JOD (Wanderer, 1-day Petra), 75 JOD (Explorer, 2-day Petra), or 80 JOD (Expert, 3-day Petra). For travelers visiting Petra and staying 2+ nights, the Jordan Pass is almost always the better financial choice — see the detailed comparison on the Jordan Pass vs visa page.

The critical requirements:

  • Purchase the pass online before arriving in Jordan
  • Stay in Jordan a minimum of 2 nights (3 days) — verified by border officials at exit
  • Be eligible for a Jordan visa-on-arrival (Jordan Pass only waives the fee, not the underlying eligibility requirement)

If you fail to meet the 2-night minimum, border officials will charge the 40 JOD visa fee at your exit. The Jordan Pass is then effectively a slightly-discounted Petra ticket rather than a real saving — still useful if you visit other attractions, but the visa waiver value is lost.

Other practical notes

Passport validity. Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months beyond your intended exit date. Jordan strictly enforces this — a passport with less than 6 months validity will be refused entry.

Israeli passport stamps. A persistent rumor suggests Israeli stamps cause problems entering Jordan. In practice, this is not an issue — Jordan and Israel have a formal peace treaty (1994) and travelers cross between the countries freely. The opposite direction (using a Jordanian-stamped passport to enter certain Arab countries) can create complications, but that's a different problem.

Overstaying. Fines for short overstays are modest (1.5 to 3 JOD per day, payable on exit) and routine. Longer overstays (weeks) can create more serious complications. Extend at any Jordanian police station before your visa expires — extensions of up to 2 additional months are typically free.

Registration with police. Travelers staying in private accommodations (not hotels) are technically required to register with the local police station within 14 days. In practice, this is rarely enforced for short-term Airbnb-style stays.

Customs. Standard tourist customs allowances. Importing currency above 15,000 JOD requires declaration. Drones require a separate Royal Aviation Commission permit. Antiques may not be exported without authorization. Drugs, weapons, and pornographic material are prohibited.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa to enter Jordan?
Almost certainly yes, but the question is which kind. Citizens of about 120 countries — including the US, UK, EU member states, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea, and most Western nations — can obtain a visa on arrival at major airports and most land borders. The visa-on-arrival costs 40 JOD (~$56 USD) for a single-entry 30-day visa. A few nationalities require pre-arranged visas through Jordanian diplomatic missions; the Jordan Ministry of Foreign Affairs maintains the authoritative list.
How much does the visa cost?
40 JOD (~$56 USD) for a single-entry 30-day visa, whether obtained at an airport on arrival or through the official e-visa portal in advance. The land border at Wadi Araba (from Eilat) charges 60 JOD — higher than the airport rate. Multi-entry visas valid for 6 months cost 120 JOD. The fees are paid in cash (Jordanian Dinars or US dollars) or by credit card at the visa counter.
Where can I get a visa on arrival?
Queen Alia International Airport (Amman/AMM), King Hussein International Airport (Aqaba/AQJ), the Sheikh Hussein Bridge (north, from Israel near Beit She'an), and the Wadi Araba crossing (south, from Eilat). The major exception: the Allenby/King Hussein Bridge crossing — the one Palestinians and many West Bank-related travelers use — does NOT issue visas on arrival. If you're crossing there from the West Bank or East Jerusalem, you must pre-arrange your visa.
Can I get an e-visa in advance?
Yes. Jordan operates an official e-visa portal (the Ministry of Interior's online system) at the same 40 JOD fee. Processing takes 1-10 business days. The e-visa is useful for travelers entering at the Allenby Bridge (no visa-on-arrival), travelers from nationalities that aren't on the visa-on-arrival list, and travelers who prefer pre-clearance over arrival queues. For most Western visitors flying into Amman or Aqaba, visa-on-arrival is faster and equally cheap.
How does the Jordan Pass affect the visa?
The Jordan Pass waives the visa-on-arrival fee entirely IF you purchase it before arriving and stay a minimum of 2 nights (3 days) in Jordan. The pass costs 70-80 JOD depending on tier (Wanderer/Explorer/Expert) and also includes Petra entry plus 40+ other attractions. For travelers visiting Petra and staying 2+ nights, the Jordan Pass is almost always the better financial choice than paying the visa separately. See <a href="/jordan/jordan-pass-vs-visa">the comparison page</a> for the full math.
What if I overstay my visa?
Modest fines — typically 1.5 to 3 JOD per day, payable when you exit Jordan at the border. Short overstays (a few days) are handled routinely at airport exit. Longer overstays (weeks or months) can produce more serious problems including detention. If you need to stay longer than 30 days, extend the visa at any Jordanian police station before expiry — extensions of up to 2 additional months are typically free if requested before the original visa runs out.

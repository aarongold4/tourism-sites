GETTING THERE

How to get to Petra from Amman

235 kilometers, three hours on the Desert Highway or five on the King's Highway. The JETT bus is cheapest, a rental car is most flexible, and a private taxi is easiest. Here's the full breakdown with current 2026 pricing.

Last reviewed April 28, 2026 · 9-minute read · See our editorial standards

The Amman-to-Petra journey is the most common single leg of any Jordan trip, and the transport options for it are well-established. You have four real choices: the daily JETT bus (cheapest, but tied to a single 06:30 departure), a rental car (most flexible, lets you take the scenic King's Highway), a private taxi or hired driver (easiest, particularly for groups), or a guided day tour (most expensive, but takes all logistics off your hands and includes a guide on site at Petra).

The right answer depends on how many days you have, how many travelers you are, and how much you value flexibility versus simplicity. For a quick reference: solo travelers on a budget should take the JETT; couples or small groups doing a full Jordan loop should rent a car; first-timers worried about logistics should take a tour; and groups of three or four should consider a private hired driver, which often works out cheaper per person than buses or tours.

The JETT bus — cheapest, fixed schedule

JETT (Jordan Express Tourist Transport) runs the only direct scheduled bus service from Amman to Petra. The current 2026 schedule:

  • Amman → Petra: 06:30 departure from Abdali station. Arrives in Wadi Musa around 10:00. One bus daily.
  • Petra → Amman: 17:00 departure from the Wadi Musa stop near the Visitor Centre. Arrives back in Amman around 20:30. One bus daily.
  • Price: 10–11 JOD ($14–17 USD) one-way. Round-trip around 20 JOD.
  • Booking: jett.com.jo, or in person at Abdali station. Book 1–2 days ahead in spring and autumn peak.
  • Vehicle: Air-conditioned coach with reserved seating. Comfortable.

The single biggest constraint of the JETT bus is the once-daily schedule. If you miss the 17:00 return from Petra, you're spending the night in Wadi Musa whether you planned to or not — and the only options to get back to Amman are a private taxi (130+ JOD one-way) or hitching with a tour operator's empty van (rare, opportunistic). For travelers staying overnight in Wadi Musa anyway, this is moot. For day-trippers, it's a real risk: you need to be back at the bus stop by 16:30, which means leaving the lower city by 15:30, which means you've effectively had a 5-hour visit.

Rental car — most flexible

Renting a car is the right choice for travelers doing a multi-stop Jordan loop, photographers who want to time their arrivals to the light, or anyone who wants the option of the scenic King's Highway route. Rental costs start around $30–45 USD per day for an economy car from international agencies (Avis, Sixt, Europcar, Budget) at Queen Alia International Airport or downtown Amman.

Two routes south to Petra:

The Desert Highway (Route 15): 235 km, ~3 hours non-stop. Divided highway most of the way, well-signed in English and Arabic, frequent fuel stops, easy driving. This is what the JETT bus and most tours take. Best for travelers prioritizing speed.

The King's Highway (Route 35): 280 km, 5–6 hours with stops. Two-lane road with frequent turns and elevation changes, passes through Madaba (Byzantine mosaic map), Mount Nebo (Moses's view of the Promised Land), Karak Castle (12th-century Crusader fortress), and the Dana Biosphere Reserve. Worth taking in one direction and the Desert Highway in the other.

Practical notes on driving in Jordan: drivers are aggressive on highways but generally courteous, lane discipline is loose, speed cameras are present but rarely checked. Oncoming cars flashing their headlights are warning you of police or a hazard ahead. Petra has free parking at the Visitor Centre, and most Wadi Musa hotels have free parking too. Fuel is cheap by Western standards (~0.85 JOD/liter for unleaded as of April 2026).

Private taxi or hired driver — easiest

For travelers who want the flexibility of a private vehicle without the responsibility of driving, hiring a Jordanian driver is the right move. Three sub-options:

One-way private taxi: 70–100 JOD ($100–140 USD) for the car (up to 4 passengers). Booked through your hotel, typically with 30 minutes notice. The fare is for the whole car, so for 3–4 travelers the per-person cost drops below the JETT bus.

Round-trip private taxi with waiting time: 130–180 JOD round-trip including the driver waiting at Petra. Practical for a day trip; the driver typically waits at a Wadi Musa cafe or hotel for 6–7 hours while you visit the site.

Hired driver for the day: 150–180 JOD per day including fuel. Most useful if you're combining Petra with stops along the way (Karak Castle, Madaba, the Dead Sea). Several reputable operators run this service; ask at your Amman hotel for a recommended driver. A good Amman driver who knows the route to Petra is worth their fee — they handle the language barrier at gas stations and rest stops, they know which restaurants to stop at, and they understand the rhythm of the King's Highway in ways that a rental car driver can't replicate on a first trip.

Guided day tour — easiest and most expensive

Door-to-door from your Amman hotel, guide on site, no logistics. Day tours from Amman typically cost $80–400 USD per person depending on group size, inclusions, and operator. We compare specific operators on the Petra tours from Amman page, but the general pricing tiers:

  • Budget group tours ($80–120 per person): Large groups (15–30 people), basic transport, light commentary. Standard for cruise-ship passengers and budget travelers.
  • Small-group tours ($150–250 per person): Groups of 6–12, more flexible pacing, licensed guide at Petra. The sweet spot for most travelers.
  • Private day tours ($300–500 per person for two travelers): Private vehicle and guide, custom pace, includes everything. Best for families and couples who want the experience without the logistics.

The trade-off with a tour is fixed timing. Most day tours from Amman leave at 06:00–07:00, arrive at Petra around 10:00, give you 5–6 hours on site, and depart back to Amman around 16:00. You skip the Monastery climb (too far to fit in), the Royal Tombs in their best afternoon light, and Petra by Night.

What we recommend

For most travelers: Spend two nights in Wadi Musa, take the JETT bus south on the morning of your arrival day (or hire a driver if you're 3+ travelers), do two days at Petra, and take the JETT back to Amman or continue south to Wadi Rum.

For a tight Jordan loop (5–7 days): Rent a car. The flexibility to take the King's Highway in one direction, time your Petra arrival to the morning light, and reach Wadi Rum the same day you leave Petra is worth the extra cost.

For first-time travelers worried about logistics: Take a small-group tour from Amman. The premium over independent travel buys you a guide at the site, door-to-door pickup, and the security of knowing everything works.

Frequently asked questions

What's the cheapest way to get from Amman to Petra?
The JETT bus. One-way fares are 10–11 JOD ($14–17 USD), round-trip about 20 JOD. The 06:30 departure from Amman's Abdali station arrives in Wadi Musa around 10:00, and the 17:00 return from Petra arrives back in Amman around 20:30. Book at jett.com.jo a day or two ahead in peak season — the single daily bus regularly sells out in spring and autumn.
What's the fastest way?
A private taxi or rental car via the Desert Highway — 235 km, roughly 3 hours non-stop. Tours and the JETT bus take the same physical route and the same approximate time. The King's Highway via Madaba and Karak is more scenic but adds 2–3 hours and is realistically a one-day journey in itself rather than a transit option.
How much does a private taxi cost?
70–100 JOD ($100–140 USD) one-way for the full car (up to 4 passengers and luggage). Your hotel can arrange this in 30 minutes. For groups of 3–4 travelers, the per-person math often works out better than a JETT bus plus the inconvenience of the once-daily schedule. For a day trip with return, expect 130–180 JOD round-trip including the driver's waiting time.
Is it worth renting a car?
Yes, if you're comfortable with Jordanian driving and want flexibility. Rental car prices start around $30–45 USD per day for an economy car. The Desert Highway is a divided highway most of the way, well-signed in English and Arabic. Petra has free parking at the Visitor Centre. The flexibility lets you stay overnight in Wadi Musa, do Petra by Night, take the King's Highway one direction and the Desert Highway the other, and detour to Karak Castle. International Driving Permits are technically required and almost never checked in practice.
Which Amman station does the JETT bus leave from?
The main Abdali JETT terminal in central Amman is the primary departure point. JETT also runs a 7th Circle pickup in west Amman for some routes. The official jett.com.jo schedule will tell you which station applies to your departure. Most central Amman hotels can arrange a taxi to Abdali for 3–5 JOD; allow 30 minutes for traffic in the morning.
Can I do Petra as a day trip from Amman?
Technically yes, practically not great. A day trip gives you about 5–6 hours on site if you take an organized tour (door-to-door in air-conditioned transport, guide on site), less if you self-drive or take the JETT bus. The Treasury hits peak crowds while you're there, the Monastery climb is realistically out of reach, and you pay the higher 90 JOD non-accommodated entry fee instead of 50 JOD overnight rate. A two-night Wadi Musa stay is dramatically better. If you can only spare one day, take a tour rather than the JETT.

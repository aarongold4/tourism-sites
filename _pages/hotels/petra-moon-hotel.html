Petra Moon Hotel

The mid-range value pick in Wadi Musa. 5-minute walk to the Petra gate, family-run with a rooftop terrace, and $70-140 per night — half the price of the Petra Guest House at the gate. Here's what it actually delivers.

The Petra Moon Hotel is the property that consistently shows up in mid-range Wadi Musa recommendations — and the public review data backs that reputation up. It's a family-run 3-star hotel about 400 meters from the Petra Visitor Centre, with around 25-30 rooms, a well-regarded rooftop terrace, and pricing that sits at roughly half the rate of the Petra Guest House at the gate. For travelers who want comfortable, walking-distance accommodation without paying for international 5-star branding, this is the most-recommended option in the town and the one to look at first if your budget is in the $70-140 USD per night range.

This review covers what public sources show about the property in 2026 — location specifics, room categories, the rooftop story, current pricing, and how it compares to the alternatives at both the cheaper and more expensive ends of the Wadi Musa market.

Location

The Petra Moon sits on Tourism Street, the main road that runs from the Petra Visitor Centre uphill into Wadi Musa town. The walk to the Petra gate is about 400 meters and 5 minutes downhill — faster going down at the start of the day, longer coming back up at the end of a tired Petra day. The position is a good compromise: close enough to walk to a 06:00 entry (no taxi required), close enough to walk to the better Wadi Musa restaurants (My Mom's Recipe, The Three Steps, several cheap shawarma places) without arranging transport.

The 5-minute walk to the gate is meaningful. Most under-$100 Wadi Musa hotels are 15-25 minutes uphill from the gate, which means you're cabbing (3-5 JOD each way, multiple times a day) to get the early-morning Treasury entry that's the main reason to stay in Wadi Musa. The Petra Moon eliminates that — you walk down at 05:50, you're at the gate at 06:00.

The property

Roughly 25-30 rooms across three or four floors. The property is family-run rather than chain-managed, which shows in both directions: service is warmer and more personal than international 5-star hotels, but also more variable (one guest's check-in might be excellent and another's average). Family-run also means no 24-hour front desk; arrivals after roughly 22:00 sometimes need to call ahead.

Room categories per recent listings:

  • Standard rooms — ~20-22 sqm doubles and twins, no view guaranteed, comfortable but not large. $70-110 USD/night typical.
  • Deluxe rooms — slightly larger, often with a partial mountain view. $90-130 USD/night.
  • Rooftop rooms — top floor, mountain view, the upgrade most travelers should request. $100-140 USD/night.

Reviews on room quality are consistently positive — significantly more so than the Petra Guest House where review variance is higher. Beds are described as comfortable, bathrooms as clean and functional, hot water as reliable. The decor is traditional Jordanian (warm tones, rugs, brass accents) rather than international-modern — some travelers find this charming, others dated.

The rooftop terrace

The property's most-mentioned feature in reviews. The rooftop has full 360-degree views over Wadi Musa, the surrounding mountain ridges, and (on clear days) into the Petra area beyond. The terrace is used for breakfast in good weather and for evening drinks — a small bar setup runs into the evening.

The rooftop is also the reason to specifically request a rooftop room rather than a standard. The top-floor rooms have direct rooftop access via stairs and the morning light at this altitude is genuinely beautiful — significantly better than what the standard rooms below offer. The $20-30 USD per night upgrade is the best value-add in the hotel's price tier.

Dining

Breakfast is included on most rates and is widely praised — a full buffet with both Western and Jordanian options, fresh juice, decent coffee, served on the rooftop in good weather. This is consistently the most-mentioned highlight in reviews.

The hotel has a small evening restaurant for guests who don't want to walk out into town, with traditional Jordanian dishes (mansaf, maqluba) at modest prices (8-15 JOD per person). The food is described in reviews as honest home-cooking quality — not destination dining, but more than serviceable for a tired post-Petra day.

Most multi-night guests walk into Wadi Musa town for dinner at least once or twice. The 5-10 minute uphill walk to My Mom's Recipe or The Three Steps is part of why the location works.

Pricing — what to expect in 2026

Recent booking-platform pricing:

  • Summer low (Jun-Aug): $60-90 USD/night standard, breakfast included
  • Shoulder (Mar-May, Sep): $70-110 USD/night
  • Peak (Oct-Nov, Mar-Apr): $90-140 USD/night
  • Rooftop rooms: $20-30 USD premium over standard rates

Direct booking through the hotel website (petramoonhotel.com) is sometimes 10-15% cheaper than Booking.com or Hotels.com. The trade-off is no buyer protection — Booking.com's free-cancellation policy is worth the small markup for most travelers.

How it compares

Vs. Petra Guest House Hotel

The Petra Guest House sits at the gate (vs 5 minutes away) and has the famous Cave Bar (vs the rooftop), but costs roughly $30-50 USD more per night. The Guest House has more variable reviews (some reviewers report dated rooms, the Petra Moon doesn't have that pattern). For travelers who specifically want at-the-gate location and the Cave Bar character, Guest House. For travelers who want better-reviewed rooms and lower price at near-equivalent location, Petra Moon.

Vs. budget hotels ($30-60 USD/night)

The cheaper Wadi Musa hostels and guesthouses (Cleopetra Hotel, Valentine Inn, etc.) save another $20-40 USD per night but typically sit further from the gate (15-25 minute walk uphill) and have less consistent quality. For travelers comfortable with hostel-tier accommodations, the savings are real; for travelers who want a proper hotel without the international-brand premium, the Petra Moon is the right tier.

Vs. Mövenpick / Petra Marriott

Different category entirely. The luxury options at $180-400/night offer service polish, restaurant variety, and pool/resort amenities that the Petra Moon doesn't try to provide. The Petra Moon's pitch is value — getting most of what matters (location, breakfast, comfortable rooms, friendly service) at a quarter to half the price.

When the Petra Moon is the right choice

  • Mid-range budget ($70-140/night). The sweet spot of the Wadi Musa hotel market.
  • You want to walk to the gate. The 5-minute walk is the practical equivalent of the Mövenpick's location at half the price.
  • You prefer family-run hospitality. The personal service and warmer atmosphere is a real plus for many travelers.
  • You're booking 2-3 nights. The longer stay magnifies the value advantage vs the luxury options.

When to consider alternatives

  • You want 5-star service polish: Go to the Mövenpick or Marriott.
  • You want at-the-gate location specifically: Petra Guest House or Mövenpick.
  • You're on a tight budget: The Wadi Musa budget hotels save more money.
  • You have mobility constraints: The stairs and the uphill walk back from the gate may be challenging.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the Petra Moon Hotel located?
About 400 meters from the Petra Visitor Centre — a 5-minute walk downhill, slightly longer back uphill at the end of a full Petra day. The hotel sits on Tourism Street between the Visitor Centre cluster and the upper town, which makes it well-positioned for both early gate access and easy access to Wadi Musa town restaurants.
How much does it cost?
Recent booking-platform pricing in 2026 shows standard rooms at $70-110 USD per night in shoulder season and $90-140 USD in peak. Rooftop rooms with mountain views (the upgrade most consistently mentioned in reviews) run an additional $20-30 USD per night. Breakfast is typically included on direct bookings.
What's the property like?
Mid-range, family-run feel rather than corporate. Around 25-30 rooms across three or four floors with a rooftop terrace that's the property's most-cited feature — sweeping views over Wadi Musa and the surrounding mountains. Decor is traditional Jordanian rather than international, which travelers either love or find dated depending on preference. Wi-Fi is reliable, breakfast is widely praised as generous and varied (better than the average mid-range Wadi Musa property), and the family that runs it is consistently described as warm and helpful.
How does it compare to the Mövenpick or Petra Guest House?
Different category entirely. The Mövenpick ($180-400/night) and Petra Guest House ($110-160/night) are 4-5-star international-brand properties at the gate. The Petra Moon is a 3-star family-run hotel at half the price of the Guest House and roughly a quarter the price of the Mövenpick at peak. For travelers on a budget who still want a comfortable property within walking distance, the Petra Moon hits the sweet spot. The trade-off is service consistency — family-run means warm and personal, but also means occasional service variability and no 24-hour reception.
Should I book a standard or rooftop room?
Rooftop, if available. The view upgrade is meaningful — Wadi Musa is built on a hillside and the rooftop rooms catch the morning light over the mountains in a way standard rooms don't. The $20-30 USD premium per night is the best value upgrade in the hotel's price tier. Book direct or via Booking.com with the "Rooftop" or "Mountain View" room type specifically requested.
When would this be the wrong hotel?
Three scenarios. First, if you have mobility constraints — the hotel has stairs and limited elevator access. Second, if you want true 5-star service polish and don't want to manage variability — go to the Mövenpick. Third, if you're traveling in winter (December-February) and want pool time — there's a small pool but it's seasonal and the property doesn't have the resort feel of the Mövenpick or Marriott.

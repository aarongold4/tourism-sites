Itineraries

Routes built on real bus timetables, honest driving times and the things actually worth your days.

North Macedonia is a small country with two unmissable anchors — which makes itinerary design unusually clean. Every good route is a variation on one spine: Skopje plus Lake Ohrid, connected by a three-hour bus, with the remaining days deciding how much of the in-between you see.

How the trip lengths shake out

3 days — the essentials

One day in Skopje (bazaar, monumental center, Vodno cable car), an early bus to Ohrid, then a day and a half at the lake (old town and Kaneo, plus a fast St. Naum run). Tight but genuinely satisfying — this is the best three-day country in the Balkans.

5 days — the comfortable classic

The same spine, unhurried: two Skopje nights with a Matka Canyon half-day, three Ohrid nights adding beach time at Trpejca and a proper St. Naum boat day. This is the route we recommend to most first-timers.

7 days — add the second tier

The week version threads in Bitola and Heraclea between Skopje and Ohrid (or as an Ohrid day trip), plus either a Galičica hike or a Tikveš wine day depending on taste. With a rental car, the wine day slots neatly into the Skopje–Ohrid drive via Stobi.

10 days — the full loop

Ten days does the country justice as a circle: Skopje, Matka, Tetovo's Painted Mosque, Mavrovo and Bigorski Monastery, down to Ohrid, around to Bitola and Pelister, then back via Prilep and Kruševo or the Tikveš wineries. It also leaves room for the move most travelers should consider — crossing into Albania at St. Naum to continue down the Albanian Riviera, or pairing the trip with Kosovo's Prizren, two hours from Skopje.

The practical rules

  • Buses work for the spine, not the loop. Skopje–Ohrid, Skopje–Bitola and Ohrid–Bitola all run frequently. Mavrovo, Stobi, Treskavec and the wine estates effectively need a car or a tour.
  • Sequence around market days and Mondays. Several museums close Mondays; the bazaar is best Tuesday–Saturday mornings.
  • Fly open-jaw if you can. Skopje (SKP) in, Ohrid (OHD) out — Ohrid's small airport has seasonal low-cost links — saves a backtrack.
  • Budget reality: a comfortable mid-range week runs roughly €300–450 per person on the ground, excluding flights.

The transport spine, with numbers

Every route below runs on the same few legs, so memorize them once: Skopje–Ohrid ~3 hours by bus, €8–14, 16–20 departures daily in season (morning buses land you by lunch); Skopje–Bitola ~2.5–3 hours; Ohrid–Bitola ~90 minutes over the scenic Galičica shoulder; Skopje airport–center 25 minutes (€20–25 fixed taxi or the cheap shuttle); Ohrid airport–town ~15 minutes. Rental cars (€25–35/day) compress nothing on the spine — the buses are genuinely good — but unlock everything between the lines: Mavrovo, the wineries, Treskavec, Kokino. The single best ticketing move remains the open-jaw flight (SKP in, OHD out or reverse), which deletes the only redundant leg in the whole country.

Extending into the neighbors

North Macedonia sits at the Balkans\' crossroads and every itinerary here extends naturally. Albania: cross at St. Naum and you\'re an hour into a different country — Tushemisht and Pogradec continue the lake; Tirana is ~2.5 hours; the Riviera beckons beyond (confirm rental-car border permission). Kosovo: Prizren, the Balkans\' prettiest small city, is two hours from Skopje by direct bus — an easy overnight bolt-on (green-card coverage check for drivers). Greece: Thessaloniki is 3.5 hours down the motorway, with Stobi and the wineries as the en-route day. The Schengen arithmetic helps too: days here don\'t touch the 90/180 clock, making the country the natural hinge of any long Balkan route.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do you need for North Macedonia?

Five days covers Skopje and Ohrid comfortably; seven adds a second-tier destination; ten lets you loop the whole country. Even three days delivers a real trip thanks to the short distances.

Can I do North Macedonia without a car?

The classic Skopje–Ohrid–Bitola triangle, yes — buses are frequent and cheap. For Mavrovo, the wine country and the monasteries, plan on a rental car (from ~€25–35/day) or day tours.

Does North Macedonia combine well with neighboring countries?

Exceptionally. Ohrid sits an hour from the Albanian border, Skopje two hours from Prizren in Kosovo, and Thessaloniki in Greece is a 3.5-hour drive — Balkan multi-country routes naturally pass through.

Do these itineraries work in winter?

The Skopje weekend works year-round; the 3- and 5-day versions compress honestly (museums absorb weather, Ohrid turns atmospheric-but-quiet); and winter's real itinerary swaps the lake days for Mavrovo's January–March ski season. The 7- and 10-day loops are May–October routes at heart.

Which itinerary should a first-timer pick?

The 5-day classic — it covers both anchors properly with Matka and St. Naum included, fits a working week with weekend flights, and leaves you wanting exactly the things the 7- and 10-day versions add. Most return visits start from there.

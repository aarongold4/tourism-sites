Affiliate disclosure

How this site is funded, stated plainly.

Last reviewed June 6, 2026

Some links on gizatourism.com are affiliate links. If you click one and book or buy through it, this site may earn a commission from the booking platform — at no additional cost to you. The price you pay is the same with or without our link; the commission comes from the platform's marketing budget, not your pocket.

Why this model, and not the alternatives. A travel site has three honest ways to keep its lights on — subscriptions, advertising, and commissions — and this site chose the mix it did deliberately: no paywall, because trip-critical facts behind a subscription fail the travelers who need them most; advertising confined to informational pages, so commercial pressure never shares a page with a recommendation; and affiliate commissions as the core, because they pay only when a recommendation was useful enough to act on — the rare revenue model whose incentive points the same direction as the reader's interest, provided the recommendations-first rule below is enforced. It is.

The partners this model involves are the major travel booking platforms — the likes of Booking.com for accommodation and Viator and GetYourGuide for tours and experiences — chosen because they're the venues where review volume makes quality verifiable, not because of their commission terms. Specific recommendations carrying affiliate links are marked by context: they appear where the surrounding text recommends something bookable.

The rule that governs everything: recommendations are made first, linked second. Tours and hotels are assessed on merit — and our reviews state what's wrong with an option as plainly as what's right. Where the best option in a category has no affiliate program, it's recommended anyway, without a tracked link. Where an affiliate-linked option stops deserving its recommendation, the recommendation changes and the link goes with it. Commission rates never influence rankings, and no operator or property can pay to be recommended; that policy is part of our published editorial standards.

This site may also display advertising (such as Google AdSense) on informational pages. Advertising is suppressed on pages where booking recommendations are the primary content, so the two revenue models never share a page with conflicting incentives. Advertisers have no input into editorial content.

How to tell what's a commercial link. The working rule: links that take you to a place where money changes hands (a booking platform, a hotel's reservation system, a tour checkout) may be affiliate links; links that take you to information (official ticketing portals, government sites, museums, other pages of this site) are not. The official Egyptian platforms this site constantly cites — egymonuments.com, visit-gem.com, visa2egypt.gov.eg — pay nothing and are linked because they're the source of record. No affiliate consideration ever moves a link onto an official-source citation.

What the commission actually is. For the curious: travel platforms typically share a single-digit-to-low-teens percentage of a booking's value with the referring site, paid from their marketing budget weeks after a completed stay or tour. Commissions are reported to this site in aggregate and anonymously — we see that bookings happened, never who made them, when they travel, or any personal detail. The privacy implications, or rather their absence, are covered in the privacy policy.

Questions about any specific link or relationship are welcome via the contact page, and this disclosure is updated whenever a partner is added or a practice changes — its review date is the version marker.

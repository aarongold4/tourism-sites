Petra Marriott Hotel

The luxury option on the Tayba ridge above Wadi Musa — spectacular valley views, a real resort feel, and a 10-minute taxi to the Petra gate. Here's when the view premium pays off and when it doesn't.

Last reviewed April 28, 2026 · 9-minute read · See our editorial standards

The Petra Marriott Hotel is the second of Wadi Musa's three full-service 5-star properties, sitting on the Tayba ridge above town with sweeping views west over the Jordan Rift Valley. It's the property travelers consider when they want the hotel-resort experience that a few hours at the Mövenpick Resort can't quite deliver. The trade-off is real: you give up the 50-meter walk to the Petra gate that makes the Mövenpick uniquely valuable for a 1.5-kilometer taxi ride that costs you 30 minutes of logistics each day. Whether that's worth it depends on what you want from your Petra stay.

This review covers the property as the public record shows it in 2026: location, room categories, restaurants, current pricing, and an honest comparison against the Mövenpick (the obvious comparison) and the Petra Guest House (the cheaper alternative). The framing throughout: the Marriott is the right call for travelers who want a destination hotel experience, and the wrong call for travelers who want to maximize Petra time.

Location — the ridge trade-off

The Marriott sits on the Tayba ridge, an elevated strip about 1.5 km north and uphill from the Petra Visitor Centre. The ridge runs east-west along the lip of the Wadi Araba valley, and the hotel orientation puts most rooms facing west toward the valley — which means sunset views directly into one of the great desert vistas in Jordan. The ridge also catches the wind, so balconies and the rooftop terrace are usable even in midsummer when town can be uncomfortable.

The downside is the logistics. A 1.5-km walk down to the gate is theoretically doable — you'd lose about 20-25 minutes downhill — but the return walk is genuinely uphill and dusty, and almost no one does it twice in a day after the Monastery climb. The reality is that you'll take a taxi to and from the gate (3-5 JOD each way, 5-7 minutes), or you'll use the hotel's complimentary shuttle which runs on a fixed schedule starting around 08:00. The shuttle is fine for arrival but doesn't help you achieve the 06:00 Treasury entry that's the strongest argument for staying in Wadi Musa at all.

The property

The Marriott has roughly 100 rooms in a low-rise building that follows the ridge contour. The architecture is "international resort" rather than locally distinctive — comfortable but generic. The pool is one of the property's real strengths: a large infinity-edge pool oriented west, with the valley view as backdrop, surrounded by full lounger setup. In summer this is genuinely useful; the Mövenpick pool by comparison is smaller and faces into town.

Room categories per the official listings:

  • Standard / Superior — entry tier, ~28 sqm, partial or no view. The default booking.
  • Deluxe valley view — same room size, oriented west toward the valley. The upgrade worth paying for; the view is the entire reason to stay here.
  • Suites — Junior Suite and several full Suite categories with separate living areas.

Aggregated review trends: rooms are consistently described as "spacious," "well-maintained," and "good for the price." The property is somewhat newer than the Mövenpick (relatively recent renovation) and reviews on physical condition trend more enthusiastic. Service receives consistent praise — Marriott's international service standard is reliably strong here.

Restaurants and dining

Three on-property venues:

Al-Iwan. The main restaurant — breakfast buffet (well-reviewed, varied), lunch and dinner with international and Jordanian dishes. Standard 5-star hotel restaurant quality at corresponding prices (15-30 JOD per person for dinner).

The rooftop terrace. Views over the valley, particularly at sunset. Cocktails and casual food. The setting is the draw; the food is fine.

Lobby cafe. Standard hotel cafe — coffee, light meals, snacks.

The major weakness in the dining: variety. Three restaurants is fewer than the Mövenpick (four) and the food rotation is more limited. Most multi-night guests walk down into town for dinner at least once or twice — taxi is 3-5 JOD and the Wadi Musa town restaurants (My Mom's Recipe, The Three Steps) are significantly better value.

Pricing — what to expect in 2026

Recent booking-platform pricing (Booking.com, Marriott.com, Hotels.com, Expedia) shows the following typical ranges for a Standard room:

  • Summer low (Jun-Aug): $170-220 USD/night, breakfast included
  • Shoulder (Mar-May, Sep): $200-280 USD/night
  • Peak (Oct-Nov, Mar-Apr): $280-400 USD/night
  • Christmas/New Year: Sometimes $450+ for standard rooms

Deluxe valley-view rooms typically add $40-60 USD per night to the Standard rate. Suites run $400-700+. Marriott Bonvoy elite status holders can sometimes get complimentary view upgrades at check-in (worth asking).

The Marriott is consistently $40-80 USD per night cheaper than the Mövenpick at comparable room categories, which is a real consideration over multi-night stays. The savings cover the daily taxi costs to and from the gate with money left over.

How it compares

Vs. Mövenpick Resort Petra

The cleanest comparison in the Wadi Musa luxury market. The Mövenpick wins on: location (50m vs 1.5km to gate), 06:00 access (you can walk; Marriott shuttle starts later), restaurant variety, and slightly more polished service. The Marriott wins on: views (valley vs facing town), price ($40-80 cheaper for comparable rooms), pool (larger and more dramatic), property condition (newer renovations), and the resort feel. For one-night Petra-focused visits, Mövenpick. For 2-3 night stays where you want time at the pool, Marriott.

Vs. Petra Guest House

Different price tiers. The Petra Guest House ($110-160/night) sits at the gate; the Marriott ($200-400/night) sits on the ridge with views. Travelers choosing between them are really choosing between "cheap-and-convenient" and "premium-with-a-view" — different trips, not really competing offers. If you can afford the Marriott, you're not really considering the Guest House.

Vs. Mövenpick Nabatean Castle

The Nabatean Castle is the second Mövenpick property in Wadi Musa, also on the ridge area but distinct from the Marriott. Similar pricing ($200-300/night). The Marriott has the better-known brand and more consistent reviews; the Nabatean Castle has different room layouts and a slightly more traditional Bedouin-themed decor. Either works; the Marriott is the safer default choice for first-time Petra travelers.

When the Marriott is the right choice

Three traveler profiles where the Marriott premium pays off:

1. Multi-night Petra travelers who want pool time. If you're staying 2-3 nights and want afternoons at the pool with valley views between Petra days, the Marriott's pool and view setup is the most resort-like option in Wadi Musa.

2. Sunset photographers. The valley view at sunset is a real visual highlight. Photographers and visual-experience-focused travelers benefit from the orientation.

3. Marriott Bonvoy elite status holders. Status benefits (complimentary upgrades, late checkout, lounge access where available) make the property more competitive vs the Mövenpick. Free upgrades to Deluxe valley-view rooms are the most common upgrade and meaningfully improve the experience.

When to consider alternatives

  • You want 06:00 Treasury entry: The Mövenpick's 50-meter walk wins clearly.
  • One-night stay: The location penalty bites hardest on short stays. Stay closer to the gate.
  • Budget-conscious luxury: The Petra Guest House at half the price covers most luxury needs at the gate.
  • Mobility constraints: The ridge property and the walk to the shuttle are not friendly.

Frequently asked questions

How far is the Petra Marriott from the Petra Visitor Centre?
About 1.5 kilometers — a 10-minute taxi ride or a 20-25 minute walk downhill. The Marriott sits on the Tayba ridge above Wadi Musa, not at the entrance like the Mövenpick. The hotel runs a complimentary shuttle to and from the Petra gate at fixed times during the day; if you want to enter at 06:00, you'll be taking a taxi (~3-5 JOD each way) since the shuttle starts later.
What's the room and price situation in 2026?
Booking-platform pricing in 2026 shows standard rooms running $200-280 USD per night in shoulder season, $300-400+ in peak (October-November and March-April). Summer rates drop to $170-220. The hotel has roughly 100 rooms across several categories: Standard, Deluxe (with valley view — the upgrade worth paying for), and several suites. Breakfast is included on most direct-booking rates.
Is the view actually worth it?
Yes, and it's the property's real differentiator. The Petra Marriott sits on a ridge facing west over the Jordan Rift Valley toward the Wadi Araba and Israel beyond. Sunset from a Deluxe valley-view room or from the rooftop bar is one of the genuine highlights of any Petra stay. The Mövenpick at the gate has no equivalent — its location is functional rather than scenic. If you're booking the Marriott, pay the extra ~$40-60 per night for a valley-view room rather than the standard.
How does the Marriott compare to the Mövenpick Resort Petra?
They optimize for different things. The Mövenpick wins on location (50 meters to the Petra gate vs 1.5 km), service consistency (international 5-star training is more uniform), and breakfast variety. The Marriott wins on views (the valley vista vs facing town), pool (larger, more dramatic), and price (often $40-80 USD per night cheaper for comparable rooms). For travelers prioritizing maximum Petra time and minimum logistics, the Mövenpick is the better choice. For travelers who want a real hotel-resort experience with a few hours at the pool between Petra days, the Marriott is.
What about restaurants?
Three on-property venues: the main restaurant Al-Iwan for international and Jordanian buffet, a rooftop venue with the valley views, and a casual lobby cafe. Reviews are positive on breakfast and the rooftop atmosphere, mixed on dinner — the food is fine, the prices are 5-star, the variety is limited compared to Wadi Musa town restaurants. Most multi-night guests walk down into town for dinner at least once or twice (taxi is 3-5 JOD).
When would the Marriott be the wrong choice?
Three scenarios. First, if you specifically want the 06:00 Treasury entry — the shuttle doesn't start that early and you'll be cabbing daily. Second, if you have mobility constraints — the property is built on a ridge with stairs and the walk to the gate is non-trivial. Third, if you're only staying one night — the location penalty bites hardest on short stays where you can't fully enjoy the ridge experience.

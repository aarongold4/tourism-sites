JORDAN

ATMs and credit cards

Visa and Mastercard widely accepted, ATMs available throughout cities, but cash still required for taxis, tips, and Bedouin services. Here's the practical guide — what to bring, where ATMs are, and how to minimize fees.

Last reviewed June 5, 2026 · 6-minute read · See our editorial standards

Jordan's banking infrastructure is well-developed by regional standards — ATMs are widely available in cities, credit card acceptance is high at travel-relevant businesses, and international cards work reliably. The practical challenge for travelers isn't access but cost: ATM fees stack on both sides (home bank and local bank), foreign transaction fees from credit cards add 1-3% on every purchase, and the wrong card choices can turn a 7-day trip into a $50-100 fee waste. With the right preparation, you can essentially eliminate these costs.

What cards to bring

Credit cards — primary recommendation. Bring a Visa or Mastercard with no foreign transaction fees. Major US options: Chase Sapphire Preferred, Capital One Venture, Bank of America Travel Rewards, Amex Platinum, Citi Premier. UK/EU: Wise, Revolut, Monzo, Halifax Clarity. Canadian: Scotiabank Passport Visa Infinite, Brim Mastercard. If you don't already have a travel-focused card, apply 4-6 weeks before your trip — the fees you'll save often pay for the annual fee in a single trip.

Debit card for ATM withdrawals. Best US option: Charles Schwab Investor Checking — reimburses all ATM fees globally, no foreign transaction fees. Strong alternatives: Fidelity Cash Management, Capital One 360 Checking. UK/EU: Wise debit card, Revolut, Monzo, N26.

What to avoid: American Express as your only card (acceptance is limited compared to Visa/Mastercard). Discover and Diners Club (rarely accepted). Cards with foreign transaction fees (common in older US cards — costs you 3% on every purchase).

Before you travel — the setup

Notify your bank. Set a travel notice through your bank's app or call the customer service number. Schwab, Capital One 360, Wise, Revolut, and similar travel-focused providers handle international transactions natively without notification.

Pre-load travel cards. If you use Wise or Revolut, pre-load with approximately what you expect to spend on cards in Jordan ($500-1,000 USD per person for a 7-day trip). Top up via the app if needed.

Save international support numbers. Store the international support number for each of your cards in your phone before traveling.

Where to find ATMs

Queen Alia airport: Multiple ATMs in arrivals past customs. Arab Bank, Jordan Kuwait Bank, and Cairo Amman Bank machines all accept international cards. Withdraw enough for the immediate trip (200-300 JOD per person covers the first few days).

Amman: ATMs are everywhere — every major bank branch, most upscale supermarkets (Carrefour, Cozmo, Spinneys), and most shopping malls have multiple. Jabal Amman, downtown, and Abdoun all have dense coverage.

Petra (Wadi Musa town): Multiple ATMs in the town center, including at the Mövenpick Resort, the Visitor Centre area, and along Tourism Street. Stock up on cash here before heading to Wadi Rum.

Wadi Rum: No ATMs in the protected area. The closest is at Wadi Rum village (outside the protected area), one ATM that's sometimes out of cash. Don't rely on this; bring enough cash from Petra.

Aqaba: Good ATM coverage in the city center, at major resort hotels, and along the corniche.

Smaller towns and the King's Highway: Limited. Madaba, Karak, and Tafilah have ATMs at their main bank branches. Smaller villages often don't have functioning machines.

Card acceptance in practice

Reliably card-accepting: Hotels (essentially universal), upscale and mid-range restaurants in Amman and major tourist areas, tour operators, rental car companies (require credit card, not debit), supermarkets (Carrefour, Cozmo, Spinneys), larger souvenir shops, cooking class providers, the Jordan Pass purchase online.

Cash-only or strongly preferred: Most taxis (Uber/Careem accept cards via app), all tipping, small restaurants and street food, Bedouin tea stalls and camp extras, most small shops and markets, public buses and shared "service" taxis, smaller hotels (under $50/night tier), border crossing fees (cards work but cash is faster).

The fee minimization strategy

For a 7-day Jordan trip, the right strategy minimizes total fees to near zero:

  1. Apply for a no-foreign-transaction-fee credit card 4-6 weeks before your trip. Saves 1-3% on every card purchase.
  2. Use a Schwab/Wise/Revolut debit card for ATM withdrawals.
  3. Withdraw 400-500 JOD at a time rather than frequent small withdrawals.
  4. Use the credit card for any purchase 5 JOD or higher.
  5. Don't exchange JOD back at the end of the trip. Spend it down via tips and last-day purchases.

With this approach, total card-and-cash fees for a 7-day trip can be under $20 USD per person — versus $80-200 with worse card choices.

Frequently asked questions

Will my credit card work in Jordan?
Almost certainly yes. Visa and Mastercard are widely accepted at hotels, restaurants, larger shops, tour operators, and rental car companies. American Express has more limited acceptance — usable at major hotels and some upscale restaurants but not universal. Discover is rarely accepted. Bring a Visa or Mastercard as your primary card.
Are there ATMs everywhere?
In Amman, Petra (Wadi Musa town), Aqaba, and major cities — yes. Most major banks (Arab Bank, Cairo Amman Bank, Jordan Kuwait Bank, Bank of Jordan, Housing Bank) have machines accepting international cards. In smaller towns and remote areas (Wadi Rum interior, smaller villages), ATM access is limited or absent. Plan to withdraw cash in Amman or Wadi Musa before heading to remote areas.
What are the ATM fees?
Two fees stack. (1) Your home bank's international withdrawal fee ($3-5 USD plus 1-3% currency conversion). (2) The Jordanian bank may add 3-5 JOD per withdrawal. Total: roughly $8-15 USD per withdrawal regardless of amount. Withdraw larger amounts (300-500 JOD) less frequently to minimize per-withdrawal costs.
Should I get a travel-specific card?
If you don't already have one, yes. Travel-focused credit cards (Chase Sapphire Preferred, Capital One Venture, Amex Platinum) waive foreign transaction fees. Travel debit cards (Charles Schwab Investor Checking, Fidelity Cash Management) reimburse ATM fees globally. For UK/EU travelers: Wise, Revolut, Monzo all offer fee-free international withdrawals up to monthly limits.
What if my card gets blocked?
Inform your bank before traveling — most modern fraud-detection systems will flag Jordan transactions as suspicious without prior notice. Set a travel notice through your bank's app for the dates you'll be in Jordan. If a card gets blocked, the international support number on the back works from any Jordanian phone.
Debit cards vs credit cards?
Credit cards are preferred for purchases — better fraud protection, easier dispute resolution, and many include travel insurance. Use debit cards primarily at ATMs for cash withdrawals. Most rental car companies require a credit card (not debit) for the deposit. Bring at least one of each for flexibility.

Getting There

From Tbilisi Airport (TBS)

Seventeen kilometers, three real options, and one structural quirk: a large share of flights land between midnight and dawn. Decide your transfer before wheels-down, not after.

Last reviewed: June 7, 2026

Bolt is what most visitors use, and it deserves the default: order from arrivals, walk to the signed rideshare pickup, and ride twenty-five to forty minutes into the center at a fare that splits painlessly. Its real value is at night, when the alternatives thin out and the freelance terminal "taxi" economy — the one Bolt was built to kill — revives around sleepy arrivals. The official taxi desk inside the terminal is the legitimate non-app route at posted fixed rates; the gentlemen approaching you unsolicited are not.

Bus 337 is the budget play: into the city via the center toward Station Square, paid by tapping any contactless bank card on board, costing small change, and taking over an hour door to district. Reported operating hours have conflicted across recent sources — some list round-the-clock service, others an early-morning-to-late-evening window — so we flag the night-bus question as unsettled and suggest the curbside signage as the tiebreaker. Daytime, with light luggage, it's unbeatable value; at 4 a.m. with a family, it's a false economy even when running.

The night-arrival playbook

Tbilisi's route map loads flights from Europe and the Gulf into the small hours, so assume yours lands ugly until proven otherwise. The checklist: hotel confirmed for genuine 24-hour check-in (ask explicitly — see our hotels cluster for why this matters in historic buildings), Bolt installed and payment-attached before you fly, a screenshot of your hotel's address in Georgian for the driver, and small cash for nothing in particular, which is the traveler's universal insurance. Arrive in daylight instead and everything above relaxes into trivial. Onward planning: the getting there hub covers the Kutaisi gateway, trains, and overland routes.

Landing and getting in

Tbilisi International (TBS) sits about 17 km southeast of the center, and the good news is that the transfer is short, cheap, and low-drama. The default for most arrivals is Bolt: open the app on the airport wifi, request from the designated rideshare pickup, and pay a metered fare that lands far below anything the taxi touts at arrivals will quote. Those touts are the one mild hazard — they cluster at the doors offering flat “good price” rides at several times the app rate, and the move is simply to walk past them to the Bolt point. If the app is slow to match at odd hours, wait a few minutes rather than surrendering to a curbside quote.

Budget travelers and the carry-on-only crowd have a genuinely useful public option. Bus 37 runs between the airport and the center (via the main train station at Station Square) for the standard flat city fare, paid by contactless card or Metromoney at the door — slow, but a fraction of any taxi and perfectly comfortable in daylight with light luggage. There is also a train connection on a limited daily schedule that suits almost nobody's flight times. For late-night arrivals, Bolt is the realistic answer, since bus frequency thins after dark. Whatever you choose, grab a tourist SIM or eSIM sorted before you need a ride, and read our Bolt guide for the app mechanics.

Frequently asked questions

What’s the cheapest way into the city?

City bus 337 toward Station Square via the center — the fare is small change, tapped with a contactless bank card on board (the system is cashless). The trade is time: over an hour for the full run, with luggage space at rush hour a contact sport. Published service hours have varied between sources recently; check the stop signage on arrival rather than trusting any single listing, ours included.

How long does a Bolt take, and from where?

Twenty-five to forty minutes to the center depending on traffic. Order in the app from arrivals and walk to the marked rideshare pickup — ignore the freelance “taxi?” offers inside the terminal, which exist precisely to charge what Bolt doesn’t. The official fixed-rate taxi desk is the legitimate non-app alternative.

I land at 3 a.m. — what works?

Bolt works at all hours and is the night default; the taxi desk operates around the clock. The night-bus picture is the thing not to gamble on — see above. The more important 3 a.m. preparation is your hotel: confirm genuine 24-hour check-in before you fly, not from the curb.

Is there a train from the airport?

A rail link to Central Station exists but runs only a couple of times daily — charming, cheap, and almost never aligned with your flight. Treat it as a curiosity, not a plan.

Where do I get a SIM at the airport?

Operator counters in arrivals sell tourist SIMs at fair prices, and eSIM activation has become the lowest-friction option for short stays. If the counters are mobbed off a night arrival, city-center operator shops the next morning lose you nothing.

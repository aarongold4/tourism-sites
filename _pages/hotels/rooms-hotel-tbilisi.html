Rooms Hotel Tbilisi

Before Stamba there was Rooms: the Vera original that invented Tbilisi's design-hotel scene — smaller, warmer, moodier, and quietly the value pick of the city's top tier.

Last reviewed: June 7, 2026

Rooms opened first and set the template its famous sibling later monumentalized: industrial heritage, deep-pile design, and a public ground floor the neighborhood actually uses. Where Stamba goes cathedral — concrete, atrium, scale — Rooms goes den: timber, leather, brass lamplight, and corridors that feel edited rather than staged. The two share the garden block on Vera's Kostava Street, and the relationship is symbiotic enough that Stamba sends its guests here for breakfast at The Kitchen, the all-day restaurant whose brunch terrace is a neighborhood institution in its own right.

Recent listings put nightly rates from around the high $80s into the low $100s by season — a meaningful step below next door, with guest scores that match or beat it. That gap is the quiet argument of this review: for travelers who want the design-tier experience as a base rather than a spectacle, Rooms delivers the same address, the same gardens, and a warmer register at the better price.

The honest ledger

For: atmosphere that reviewers describe as instantly comfortable, The Kitchen's breakfasts, garden-facing calm in the middle of Vera's cafe-and-wine-bar grid, and the value math above. Against: the same modest wellness footprint as its sibling, smaller rooms in the entry categories, and a moodiness of palette that sun-seekers may read as dim. It books well ahead on autumn weekends — the high-season calendar applies doubly to the design tier — and refundable rates are the standard play. If your trip is built around eating and drinking Vera properly, this is arguably the single most logical address in the city.

Booking it smart

Rooms rewards the same homework as its sibling. The entry-level categories run genuinely smaller, so if space matters, read the room descriptions and consider the step up; the palette runs warm and dim by design, which most guests read as cozy and a few find dark, so know your own preference going in. The headline amenity is The Kitchen, the all-day restaurant whose breakfast and brunch are a Vera institution — the fact that Stamba sends its own guests here for breakfast tells you most of what you need to know — and the garden terrace it opens onto earns its reputation on a slow morning. As at its sibling, wellness amenities are modest; this is a design-and-food hotel, not a resort.

The quiet argument of the place is value: recent rates run a meaningful step below Stamba next door for the same address, the same gardens, and guest scores that match or beat it, which makes Rooms arguably the smartest booking in Tbilisi's top tier for travelers who want the design experience as a base rather than a spectacle. Book refundable, compare the direct rate against the marketplaces, and reserve well ahead on autumn weekends, when the whole design tier fills. If your trip is built around eating and drinking Vera properly — and the natural wine bars make a strong case for that — this is the single most logical address in the city.

Frequently asked questions

How does Rooms differ from Stamba?

Same group, same garden block, different temperament: Rooms is the older sibling (a converted publishing-adjacent building reopened as the city’s first design hotel), warmer and moodier where Stamba is monumental — leather, timber, and lamplight rather than concrete and atrium. Guests who find Stamba theatrical often prefer Rooms; guests chasing the showpiece go next door.

What does it cost?

Meaningfully below Stamba: recent listings show rates from around the high $80s–$100s nightly by season and category, with the usual refundable options. For the neighborhood, the gardens, and the kitchen, it’s arguably the value play of Tbilisi’s design tier.

Is the restaurant worth it for non-guests?

Yes — The Kitchen’s breakfast and brunch are a Vera institution (Stamba guests are sent here for breakfast, telling you something), and the terrace onto the shared gardens earns its reputation on slow mornings.

Who is Rooms best for?

Travelers who want the design-hotel experience dialed toward cozy: couples, solo aesthetes, food-led trips basing in Vera, and anyone planning long breakfasts. Families and spa-seekers should size expectations — like its sibling, wellness amenities are modest.

How far are the sights?

Vera’s standard math: Rustaveli below, the funicular up Mtatsminda nearby, Liberty Square 15–25 minutes on foot, the Old Town a pleasant downhill walk or short Bolt. You commute gently to the postcard and return to leafy quiet.

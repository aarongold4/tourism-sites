ITINERARY

Petra and Wadi Rum in 3 days

The minimum-viable combination of Jordan's two headline destinations. 2 full days at Petra, 1 night at a Bedouin camp in Wadi Rum, structured for efficient travel without losing the essential character of either place. Here's the day-by-day plan.

Last reviewed June 5, 2026 · 8-minute read · See our editorial standards

The Petra-plus-Wadi-Rum combination is one of the standard Jordan trip formats. Both destinations are extraordinary, both are within reasonable distance of each other (100 km, 2 hours of driving), and combining them gives travelers a substantial Jordan experience without requiring the longer 5-7 day commitments of full-country tours. The 3-day version of this combination is the minimum-viable format — enough time for both destinations at a pace that doesn't feel rushed, while staying within the time budget that international travelers often have for Jordan as part of broader regional trips.

This page is the detailed itinerary: what to do each day, what each day costs, what to book in advance, and how to handle the practical logistics. The framing throughout: a workable plan for travelers with exactly 3 days, with notes on how to extend or compress as needed.

The basic structure

The standard 3-day arrangement:

  • Day 1: Arrival in Petra area, afternoon Petra entry, overnight Wadi Musa
  • Day 2: Full second Petra day, late afternoon transfer to Wadi Rum, overnight Bedouin camp
  • Day 3: Wadi Rum sunrise tour, breakfast, departure to next destination

This structure gives Petra about 1.5 full days of visiting time (afternoon + full day + early morning if you re-enter on Day 2 before checkout, plus another sunrise possibility on Day 1 morning if you arrive the night before officially). Wadi Rum gets the standard 24-hour camp experience (afternoon arrival + jeep tour + sunset + Bedouin dinner + overnight + sunrise + breakfast + morning departure).

Day 1 — Arrival and afternoon Petra

Morning: Arrival in Wadi Musa

The arrival logistics depend on your origin:

  • From Amman: JETT bus departs Queen Alia Airport or central Amman at 06:30, arriving Wadi Musa around 10:30-11:00. Alternative: rental car or private driver covers the same route in 3-4 hours. See getting to Petra from Amman.
  • From Aqaba: 2-hour drive via Desert Highway. Morning departure works well. See from Aqaba.
  • From Eilat/Israel: Cross the Wadi Araba border (60-90 minutes), then 2-hour drive. Arrival by mid-afternoon. See from Eilat.

Check into your Wadi Musa hotel by early afternoon. Recommended: a 4-star walking-distance hotel like the Petra Guest House or Petra Moon Hotel for the convenience of multi-entry days. See Visitor Centre strip hotels.

Afternoon: Petra entry (14:00-17:30)

Buy your Jordan Pass online before arrival (see Jordan Pass guide) — the pass covers Petra entrance plus the visa fee with a 2-night minimum stay, saving substantial money over individual tickets.

Your Day 1 afternoon Petra visit (~3-4 hours):

  • 14:00 — Enter at the Visitor Centre, walk through the Siq (25-30 minutes)
  • 14:30 — Treasury reveal and time in the plaza (15-30 minutes)
  • 15:00 — Walk through the Outer Siq to the Street of Facades
  • 15:30 — Royal Tombs (Urn Tomb, Silk Tomb, Corinthian Tomb, Palace Tomb)
  • 16:30 — Roman theatre and colonnaded street area
  • 17:00 — Return walk through the Siq, exit by 17:30

The afternoon visit captures the headline introduction to Petra without committing to the more demanding climbs (Monastery, High Place) — saved for Day 2 when you have more energy.

Evening

Dinner at a Wadi Musa town centre restaurant (see Jordanian food). Early bed — Day 2 starts at 06:00.

Optional: Petra by Night

If Day 1 is a Sunday, Monday, Wednesday, or Thursday, Petra by Night runs from 20:30. Worth the additional 30 JOD for the candle-lit Siq walk and Treasury concert experience. See Petra by Night.

Day 2 — Full Petra day + transfer to Wadi Rum

Morning: Dawn Petra entry (06:00-12:00)

The single most important day-planning decision: enter Petra at 06:00 for the dawn Treasury experience. The morning light on the Treasury facade between 06:30-08:30 is the best photographic and aesthetic window of the entire day.

The morning itinerary:

  • 05:30 — Wake, quick breakfast at hotel (or take to go)
  • 06:00 — Enter at the gate when it opens
  • 06:25 — Reach the Treasury in the early morning quiet
  • 07:00 — Walk to the Royal Tombs area, then return toward the Treasury
  • 08:00 — Climb the Al-Khubtha trail to the Treasury overlook (45-60 minute climb)
  • 09:00 — Spend time at the overlook for the high-angle Treasury view
  • 10:00 — Descend, continue to the lower city
  • 11:00-12:00 — Lower city monuments (Great Temple, Qasr al-Bint, colonnaded street)

Mid-day: Lunch and Monastery climb (12:00-15:30)

Lunch at the Basin Restaurant inside Petra (12:00-13:00), then the Monastery climb. The 800-step ascent takes 60-90 minutes; allow 30-45 minutes at the top, then 45-60 minute descent.

Afternoon: Transfer to Wadi Rum (16:00-18:00)

Check out of your Wadi Musa hotel (most hotels accept late checkout to 14:00-15:00 with notice; arrange this in advance), then transfer to Wadi Rum:

  • By rental car: 100 km / 2 hours on the Desert Highway. Arrive at Wadi Rum Visitor Centre by 18:00.
  • By private transfer: Most Bedouin camps arrange private transport from Petra for 70-90 JOD. Pre-book through your camp.
  • By JETT bus: The Wadi Musa-Wadi Rum service runs once daily, typically afternoon departure. Check current schedule.
  • By shared taxi: 7-12 JOD per person; less predictable schedule. See shared taxi guide.

Evening: Wadi Rum arrival and dinner

Your camp will arrange the transfer from the Visitor Centre to the camp (4WD vehicle, 30-45 minutes into the protected area). Standard arrival pattern: short rest at camp, sunset viewing from a designated viewpoint, Bedouin dinner (typically zarb — meat slow-cooked underground — with rice and vegetables), traditional tea and music around the campfire, sleep under the stars.

Day 3 — Wadi Rum and departure

Sunrise and morning jeep tour (06:00-11:00)

Wadi Rum's sunrise is the experiential highlight of the camp stay. Wake by 05:30, head to a viewing position for sunrise around 06:30 (timing varies seasonally). After sunrise, breakfast at camp (07:00-08:00), then the morning jeep tour.

The standard jeep tour covers the major Wadi Rum highlights:

  • Lawrence Spring (T.E. Lawrence connection, dramatic spring in rock formation)
  • Khazali Canyon (narrow slot canyon with Nabataean petroglyphs)
  • Lawrence House (small Bedouin structure attributed to Lawrence)
  • Red sand dune (climbing optional but rewarding)
  • Anfeshyyah inscriptions (rock art panel)
  • Burdah rock bridge (natural arch, optional climb)
  • Um Frouth rock bridge (smaller natural arch)

The tour typically returns to camp around 11:00-11:30, where you collect your belongings and transfer back to the Visitor Centre.

Late morning/early afternoon: Departure (12:00-onward)

From the Wadi Rum Visitor Centre, your onward travel options:

  • To Aqaba: 60 km / 1 hour for travelers continuing in Jordan, crossing to Israel via Wadi Araba, or flying out of AQJ.
  • To Amman: 320 km / 4 hours via the Desert Highway. Long day; consider breaking the journey with a Dead Sea stop if time allows.
  • Back to Petra and Israel via Eilat: Wadi Rum → Aqaba → border → Eilat. Total transit ~2.5 hours including border.

What to book in advance

Pre-trip bookings that should happen 1-4 weeks ahead:

  • Jordan Pass: Buy online at jordanpass.jo before traveling. Saves time at border + Petra entrance.
  • Petra hotel: Book 2 nights in Wadi Musa, ideally the same hotel for both nights. High season (March-May, September-November) books up 4-8 weeks ahead.
  • Wadi Rum Bedouin camp: Multiple price tiers ($50 basic to $400+ luxury bubble domes). Book direct or via Booking.com/aggregators 2-6 weeks ahead.
  • Transport between Petra and Wadi Rum: If using private transfer, book through your camp 1-2 weeks ahead. JETT bus booking opens online a few days before departure.
  • Petra by Night tickets: Buy on the day or day before at the Visitor Centre. Rarely sells out except peak holidays.

What it costs

Approximate per-person costs (double occupancy) for the 3-day trip:

Budget tier ($150-250 per person)

  • Jordan Pass: 70-80 JOD ($100-115)
  • Wadi Musa budget hotel × 2 nights: $40-60 total
  • Wadi Rum basic Bedouin camp (1 night incl meals): $50-70
  • JETT bus Amman-Petra-Wadi Rum: 20-25 JOD ($28-35)
  • Meals not included: $20-30
  • Local taxis and incidentals: $10-15

Mid-range tier ($300-500 per person)

  • Jordan Pass: 70-80 JOD
  • Wadi Musa 4-star hotel × 2 nights: $120-220
  • Wadi Rum comfort camp: $90-150
  • Rental car for 3 days or private transfers: $80-150
  • Meals: $50-80
  • Petra by Night: 30 JOD

Luxury tier ($700-1,400 per person)

  • Jordan Pass: 70-80 JOD
  • Mövenpick Resort Petra or Tayba ridge 5-star × 2 nights: $400-650
  • Premium Wadi Rum bubble camp: $200-400
  • Private driver/guide: $150-250
  • Premium dining: $80-150
  • Optional add-ons (helicopter, premium activities): variable

When to extend or compress

4-day version (add 1 day at Petra)

Add a full third day at Petra — the High Place of Sacrifice climb, the back trails, the second visit to favorite monuments at different light. Petra rewards more time; if your schedule allows, this is the highest-value way to extend.

4-5 day version (add Dead Sea or Jerash)

Adding the Dead Sea after Wadi Rum extends the trip by 1-2 days but provides a third major Jordan destination. Adding Jerash on Day 1 (before Petra) similarly extends but provides the Roman archaeological complement to Petra's Nabataean character.

Compressed 2-day version

Possible but unsatisfying. Single full day at Petra (06:00 entry, 16:00 exit) covering the Treasury, Royal Tombs, and either Monastery OR Al-Khubtha — not both. Then immediate transfer to Wadi Rum for one overnight. Misses too much.

Frequently asked questions

Is 3 days enough for Petra and Wadi Rum?
Yes — 3 days is the minimum viable format for the combination, and it works well if you're efficient with timing. The standard structure: 2 full days at Petra (covering the headline monuments and at least one major hike), then 1 night and morning at Wadi Rum (jeep tour, sunset, overnight Bedouin camp, sunrise). For travelers with more time, adding a 4th day allows greater depth at either destination; with less time, this combination becomes compressed enough that you should consider skipping one destination.
Should I start with Petra or Wadi Rum?
Most travelers do Petra first, then Wadi Rum — the geographic and logistical flow works better that way. From Amman or the Eilat/Wadi Araba border, you arrive at Petra naturally (closer or more direct routing); from Petra, the drive south to Wadi Rum is 100 km / 2 hours; from Wadi Rum, you can continue to Aqaba (1 hour) for ongoing travel or fly out. The reverse order works but requires backtracking. For travelers arriving via Aqaba, the southern-to-northern flow (Wadi Rum first, then Petra) makes equal sense.
What does the 3-day trip cost?
Approximately $250-500 USD per person for mid-range independent travel including hotels, entrance fees, and transport (rental car or hired driver). Tour packages range $400-700 per person at the same quality tier. Luxury versions (5-star Petra hotel, premium Wadi Rum bubble camp, private vehicle) run $800-1,400 per person. Budget travelers using JETT bus, basic hotels, and basic Bedouin camps can do the trip for $150-250 per person total.
Where do I stay each night?
Standard pattern: Nights 1 and 2 in Wadi Musa (the town serving Petra), Night 3 at a Bedouin camp in Wadi Rum. Wadi Musa accommodations range from $40 budget guesthouses to $250+ luxury Tayba ridge properties; see hotel zones. Wadi Rum camps range from $50 basic Bedouin tents to $400+ luxury bubble domes with full amenities. Most travelers do the same Wadi Musa hotel for both nights to avoid moving luggage; the Wadi Rum camp is by definition a single-night stop.
Can I combine this with the Dead Sea or other destinations?
Yes, with at least 1-2 additional days. A 4-5 day version adds the Dead Sea (typically as a return-to-Amman destination after Wadi Rum). A 5-7 day version adds Jerash and Madaba on the way south. The 3-day Petra+Wadi Rum format is the minimum-viable Jordan combination; longer trips allow more of the country to be included.

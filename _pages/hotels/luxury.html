HOTELS

Best luxury hotels in Petra

Three 5-star properties in Wadi Musa, each optimizing for something different. The Mövenpick Resort wins on location. The Marriott wins on views. The Nabatean Castle wins on character. Here's how to choose.

Last reviewed April 28, 2026 · 8-minute read · See our editorial standards

Wadi Musa has three full 5-star hotels, all priced in the $180-400 USD per night range and all delivering the international luxury hotel experience travelers expect at that tier. They differ in three specific ways: location relative to the Petra gate, view orientation, and brand character. This page covers the trade-offs and helps you pick the right one based on what you want from your Petra stay.

The honest framing: any of the three is a good choice. There's no luxury 5-star property in Wadi Musa that we'd actively warn travelers away from. The decision is about matching the property to your priorities, not about avoiding the wrong choice.

The three options at a glance

Mövenpick Resort Petra. 50 meters from the Petra Visitor Centre. 183 rooms, four restaurants, Arabesque interior design. Premium location commands premium pricing. $180-400+ USD per night.

Petra Marriott Hotel. On the Tayba ridge, 1.5 km from the gate. ~100 rooms, three restaurants, large pool with valley views. The best views in the Wadi Musa luxury market. $200-400 USD per night.

Mövenpick Nabatean Castle Hotel. Also on the ridge area, similar distance from the gate. Smaller property than the Mövenpick Resort, distinctive Bedouin-themed decor, slightly less polished reviews than the other two. $220-300 USD per night typical.

The decision framework

If your priority is maximum Petra time

Mövenpick Resort Petra. The 50-meter walk to the gate is genuinely useful — it enables the 06:00 Treasury entry without taxi logistics, the mid-day return to the hotel for lunch and a rest, and the late-afternoon return for golden hour. No other luxury option in Wadi Musa can match this. The pricing premium is the cost of that proximity, and for serious 2-3 day Petra visitors it's worth paying.

If your priority is the hotel-resort experience

Petra Marriott. The ridge property has the better pool, the better views, and the more "destination" feel — afternoons at the pool with valley views are a real experience the Mövenpick doesn't offer. Combined with the lower price and the Marriott's reliable service standard, this is the right pick for travelers who want a real resort stay alongside their Petra visit.

If your priority is character and distinctive design

Mövenpick Nabatean Castle. The decor is more locally rooted than the international 5-star defaults at the Marriott and the Mövenpick Resort. Some travelers find it overdone (lots of brass, heavy textiles, traditional ornament); others find it the most genuinely Jordanian luxury option in Wadi Musa. Reviews are slightly more variable than the other two — service consistency isn't quite at the Marriott level — but the character is the real differentiator.

If your priority is loyalty program benefits

Petra Marriott (Bonvoy) or either Mövenpick property (Accor Live Limitless). Both programs are well-honored at their respective properties. Marriott Bonvoy elite members can often get complimentary upgrades to Deluxe valley-view rooms at the Marriott, which meaningfully improves the experience.

Pricing comparison (April 2026)

Typical standard-room rates across the three properties:

  • Summer low (Jun-Aug): Mövenpick Resort $150-180, Marriott $170-220, Nabatean Castle $180-220
  • Shoulder (Mar-May, Sep): Mövenpick Resort $200-260, Marriott $200-280, Nabatean Castle $220-280
  • Peak (Oct-Nov, Mar-Apr): Mövenpick Resort $280-400, Marriott $280-400, Nabatean Castle $260-340

Direct booking through the brand websites (movenpick.com, marriott.com) sometimes matches third-party platform rates and occasionally offers loyalty benefits the platforms don't (Bonvoy points, Accor breakfast credits). Worth comparing 2-3 sources before booking. Booking.com's free-cancellation flexibility is valuable for trips booked more than 30 days out — rates often drop in the 60-90 days before peak season as inventory clears.

The case against luxury at Wadi Musa

Worth saying honestly: for many travelers, the $180-400 USD per night spend on a luxury hotel in Wadi Musa is not the highest-value way to spend the budget. The Petra Guest House at $110-160/night sits at the gate and delivers most of what the Mövenpick Resort offers in terms of location. The Petra Moon Hotel at $70-140/night sits 5 minutes from the gate with a well-regarded rooftop. Spending the saved money on a longer Jordan trip (an additional night in Wadi Rum, a stop at the Dead Sea, a guide for the harder Petra trails) often delivers more travel experience than a 5-star hotel does.

That said, if luxury is what you want, the three properties above all deliver it. The decision is then about matching property to priorities, as outlined above.

Frequently asked questions

How much do luxury hotels in Wadi Musa cost?
$180-400+ USD per night for standard rooms in 2026. The three main 5-star options — Mövenpick Resort Petra, Petra Marriott Hotel, and Mövenpick Nabatean Castle — all fall in this range. Peak season (October-November and March-April) can push standard rooms to $400-500 USD. Suites at any of these properties run $400-1,000+ USD per night.
Which luxury hotel is closest to the Petra entrance?
The Mövenpick Resort Petra is essentially across the street from the Visitor Centre — a 50-meter walk. The Petra Marriott is on the Tayba ridge, about 1.5 km from the gate (10-minute taxi). The Mövenpick Nabatean Castle is also on the ridge, similar distance. For travelers prioritizing maximum Petra time and the 06:00 Treasury entry, the Mövenpick Resort wins clearly.
Which has the best views?
The Petra Marriott. Its Tayba ridge orientation puts most rooms facing west over the Jordan Rift Valley — sunset views are spectacular. The Mövenpick Resort at the gate has no equivalent — it sits in town facing other buildings. The Mövenpick Nabatean Castle has similar valley orientation to the Marriott but is slightly less consistently reviewed.
Which is the best value among the luxury options?
The Petra Marriott consistently comes in $40-80 USD per night cheaper than the Mövenpick Resort for comparable room categories. Combined with the better views and the larger pool, the Marriott often delivers more hotel for the money — IF you can accept the 10-minute taxi to the gate. For travelers who specifically want maximum Petra time and minimum logistics, the Mövenpick Resort's premium is defensible despite the higher price.
Should I get Marriott Bonvoy or Accor Live Limitless status?
If you have it, use it — both properties recognize their respective elite tiers and offer the usual benefits (room upgrades, late checkout, breakfast credits where applicable). Free Deluxe-valley-view upgrades at the Marriott are particularly valuable. If you don't have status, neither program is worth specifically pursuing for a Jordan trip — the loyalty value at single-trip volumes is too modest.

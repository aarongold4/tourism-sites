JORDAN

Jordanian currency

The Jordanian Dinar (JOD), pegged to the US dollar at 1 JOD = 1.41 USD. Here's the practical guide — bills and coins, where to exchange, how much cash to bring, and the cash-only situations to plan for.

Last reviewed June 5, 2026 · 6-minute read · See our editorial standards

Jordan uses the Jordanian Dinar (JOD), one of the most stable currencies in the Middle East. The dinar has been pegged to the US dollar at 1 JOD = 1.41 USD since 1995, which means exchange rates against the USD essentially don't move. Rates against the euro, pound, and other currencies fluctuate based on those currencies' positions against the dollar — currently roughly 1 JOD = 1.30 EUR or 1.10 GBP at typical rates.

This page covers what travelers actually need to know: the bills and coins you'll handle, where to exchange or withdraw cash, the cash-vs-card balance for a typical Jordan trip, and the small-denomination issue that catches travelers off guard.

The currency itself

Jordan uses dinars (JOD) and fils (1 JOD = 100 fils). In practice, travelers handle dinars and a higher subdivision called the piaster (1 JOD = 10 piasters). Most prices are quoted in dinars and piasters rather than fils.

Bills: 1, 5, 10, 20, and 50 JOD notes. The 50 is the largest commonly circulated note.

Coins: Half-dinar (50 piaster) and quarter-dinar (25 piaster) coins are the most useful, plus smaller denominations for very small amounts.

Where to get cash

ATMs — the default choice. Widely available in Amman, Petra (Wadi Musa town), Aqaba, and most cities. Major networks: Arab Bank, Jordan Kuwait Bank, Cairo Amman Bank, Bank of Jordan, Housing Bank. Most accept Visa, Mastercard, and Maestro. Typical withdrawal limit: 500 JOD per transaction. Fees: your home bank's international withdrawal fee ($3-5 plus 1-3% conversion) plus a Jordanian-side fee of 3-5 JOD. Withdraw larger amounts less frequently to minimize per-transaction costs.

Bank exchanges offer the best rates (within 1-2% of mid-market). Independent exchange shops are 2-4% off market. Hotel exchanges are the worst (5-8% below market).

Cash from home: Generally not worth ordering JOD from your home bank — rates are typically worse than Jordanian ATMs. Bring some USD or EUR as backup and rely on ATMs for the main supply.

How much cash to bring

For a 7-day trip, plan for 150-250 JOD per person in circulation. This covers taxis (3-15 JOD each), tips, Bedouin services in Petra and Wadi Rum, small purchases at markets, and camp extras (camel rides, optional balloon flights). Credit cards cover hotels, larger restaurants, tour bookings, rental cars, and the Jordan Pass. Withdraw as you go, with a 200-300 JOD float for flexibility.

The small-bills problem

One of the most common practical frustrations: ATMs often dispense only large notes, but you need small bills for taxis, tips, and small purchases. Bedouin tea stalls, small shops, and taxi drivers genuinely cannot make change for a 50 JOD note.

Workarounds: break large bills at hotels and restaurants by paying with a larger note and requesting mixed denominations in change; use ATMs that offer denomination choices; keep a dedicated stash of 1s and 5s in your daypack (aim for 20-30 JOD in small bills before any day involving taxis, Bedouin services, or rural travel); pre-tip in advance when you have small bills.

Cards vs. cash

Card-accepting: Hotels (almost universally), mid-range and upscale restaurants, larger souvenir shops, rental car companies, tour operators, the Jordan Pass purchase, supermarkets (Carrefour, Cozmo, Spinneys), border crossings (cards work but cash is faster).

Cash-required: Most taxis (Uber and Careem accept cards via app), small restaurants and street food, all tips, Bedouin tea stalls and camp extras, small shops and markets, public buses and shared "service" taxis, smaller hotels.

At the end of your trip

The exchange rate when changing JOD back to USD or EUR is typically 2-4% worse than the buy rate. Spend or tip down remaining dinars before departure rather than re-exchanging. Final hotel tips, driver tip, last-minute souvenirs, and final restaurant meal in cash all serve this purpose. If you do have leftover JOD, the airport exchanges work but at mediocre rates.

Frequently asked questions

What's the Jordanian Dinar pegged to?
USD at 1 JOD = 1.41 USD (1 USD = 0.71 JOD). Stable since 1995, so exchange rates against USD don't move. Rates against EUR and GBP fluctuate based on those currencies' positions against the dollar.
How much cash should I bring?
For a 7-day trip, 150-250 JOD per person in cash covers taxis, tips, Bedouin services, and small purchases. Credit cards cover hotels, larger restaurants, tours, and the Jordan Pass. ATMs are widely available so you don't need to bring it all at once.
Where do I get the best exchange rate?
ATMs and bank exchanges. Hotel exchanges are typically 5-8% below market. The Queen Alia airport exchange counters are convenient on arrival; in-town bank exchanges have slightly better rates.
Do I need small bills?
Yes — 1 and 5 JOD notes are essential for taxis, tips, and Bedouin services. ATMs often dispense only large notes (20 and 50). Break large bills at hotels or restaurants and keep a stash of small ones in your daypack.
Is the JOD a closed currency?
Partially. Not widely available outside Jordan and immediate neighbors. Get JOD on arrival rather than ordering from your home bank in advance (rates are typically worse there). When leaving, spend or tip down remaining JOD before departure — the buy-back rate is mediocre.

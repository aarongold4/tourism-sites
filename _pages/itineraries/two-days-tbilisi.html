Itineraries

Two days in Tbilisi

Day one takes the right bank — lanes, fortress, bath. Day two crosses the river for the cathedral, the flea market, and the funicular at sunset. Together: the complete city.

Last reviewed: June 7, 2026

Day one is the essential loop, run exactly as written: Sololaki's lanes and Kala's churches in the morning, the bath district and gorge waterfall after lunch, the cable car to Narikala for golden hour, then the booked hour at the sulfur baths and a reserved dinner. The only upgrade two days permits: take the evening food tour tonight instead of a static dinner — it pre-scouts every meal that follows.

Day two, 9:30 — Sameba. Metro one stop to Avlabari and climb through the quarter's old lanes to the cathedral while the morning is quiet — give it the underground level too. 11:30 — down to the river. Walk the left bank north, coffee en route, to Fabrika for the courtyard and lunch among the murals. 14:00 — the Dry Bridge. An hour or two in the open-air attic — cash, small notes, gentle haggling — plus the painting railings in the park.

16:30 — cross to Rustaveli. The avenue's opera-house stretch on foot, with the National Museum's gold room if energy allows. 18:00 — Mtatsminda. The funicular up the holy mountain — Pantheon stop on request — for sunset from the long terrace, the city's full light map switching on below. 20:00 — Vera. Descend into the creative quarter for amber wine and the dinner the food tour told you about. Two days, both banks, both summits, zero wasted hours.

Adjustments

Rain moves the museum into day two's afternoon and the market to any dry window (sellers vanish in real rain). With kids, Mtatsminda's amusement park converts the sunset stop into the headline. And if a third day materializes, don't add city — add Georgia: the three-day plan spends it outside the walls, where it belongs.

Two ways to spend the second day

With the right bank's core covered on day one, the second day forks. Stay in the city and cross the river: a morning at Sameba and through Avlabari, the Dry Bridge market and the painters' park, then Fabrika for the afternoon and a Mtatsminda sunset to mirror the Narikala one — you'll have seen Tbilisi from both shoulders and both banks. Or escape the city for a half-day to Mtskheta, easy and close, back in time for an evening wine bar crawl in Vera.

Two days is also the sweet spot for adding an evening food tour on the first night, because the venue intelligence it hands you reshapes every meal of the second day. Pace the walking — both days involve real climbing — bank one of the two sunsets for a viewpoint, and treat the sulfur baths as the natural end to whichever day leaves you most footsore. The honest limit of two days is that it's all city: the mountains and Kakheti stay on the wish list, which is exactly the gap the three-day plan closes with its first proper day trip.

Frequently asked questions

Does day two require transport?

A little, all easy: the metro to Avlabari for Sameba (one stop from the center, tap a contactless card), feet for the riverside stretch, and the funicular up Mtatsminda at the end. Nothing needs advance booking except dinner.

Can I swap the day order?

Yes, with one rule: keep the bath at the end of the heavier walking day, and keep one summit — Narikala or Mtatsminda — for each sunset. The days are designed as right-bank/left-bank halves; their internal sequences matter more than which comes first.

Where do food tours fit?

Evening one, replacing the dinner reservation — it doubles as reconnaissance for evening two. See our food tours page for choosing a format that genuinely replaces a meal.

Is two days enough without day trips?

For the city itself, comfortably — this plan covers both banks, both summits, the baths, and two proper evenings. What it can’t cover is what surrounds the city; that’s the three-day plan’s job, and the honest reason most visitors should find the third day.

What would a third evening add?

Vera. The wine bars and the current restaurant generation cluster there, and the two-day plan only grazes it. If your flight leaves late on the final day, spend the spare evening glass-in-hand in Vera and consider the trip completed properly.

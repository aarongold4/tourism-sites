Petra tours are a strange product. The destination is one of the most photographed places on earth, the logistics are genuinely tricky if you don't know Jordan, and the price range for what is essentially the same day trip stretches from about $80 to over $400 per person. We've taken Petra tours from every major origin — Amman, Aqaba, Eilat, even one ambitious round trip from Tel Aviv — and across that price range. The honest summary up front: most travelers don't need to spend more than $200, but most travelers also shouldn't pick the cheapest option.

What you're really paying for is some combination of (1) transport that doesn't waste your day, (2) a guide who knows when to stop talking, and (3) time on site before the cruise-ship and Israeli day-trip crowds arrive at around 11:00 AM. Cheap tours skimp on at least two of those. Expensive tours often bundle a private guide and flexible timing you may or may not need. The middle is where most people land happily.

This page is the index. Each origin gets its own dedicated breakdown linked below, with operator-by-operator notes, current pricing, and the things we wish we'd known beforehand. Below that, the basics — how Petra entry works, why the Jordan Pass changes the math, what time of year matters, and what to actually expect on a tour day.

The price ranges, demystified

Tour pricing follows a few predictable tiers. Knowing where a tour sits is the easiest way to filter the field before you start reading reviews.

$80–120 per person (large group, from Amman). 25–40 people on a coach bus, multi-hotel pickup that adds an hour to your morning, group guide at Petra, fixed return time. Petra entrance is almost never included. These are the tours you book through hostels and budget hotels, and they're a perfectly reasonable choice for solo travelers who are price-sensitive and don't mind a herded experience.

$150–250 per person (small group from Amman, or private from Aqaba). Capped at 8–15 people, often a single hotel pickup, more time at the site (4–5 hours instead of 3), and a more capable guide. This is where the genuine sweet spot lives. Most travelers should start their search here and work outward only if they have a specific reason to.

$300–500+ per person (private from Amman). A driver-only or driver-plus-guide private tour, fully flexible timing, often with hotel pickup at any hour you want. Worth it for couples on a milestone trip, photographers, families with small kids, and anyone with mobility needs that group tours handle badly. Not worth it for most other travelers — you're mostly paying for flexibility you may never use.

$700–1,500+ (multi-day private). 2-, 3-, or 5-day private tours combining Petra with Wadi Rum and the Dead Sea, including hotels and meals. We cover these on the multi-day tours page. The math here often works better than it looks — once you factor in the hotels and transport you would have paid for separately, the markup over a comparable independent itinerary can be quite small.

What about just taking the bus?

The JETT bus from Amman to Petra is the answer most cost-conscious travelers eventually reach. It runs once a day in each direction: roughly 06:30 from Abdali station in Amman, arriving at the Petra Visitor Centre around 10:00, with a return at 17:00 that gets you back to Amman around 20:30. The fare is around 10 JOD one-way, or close to 20 JOD round-trip. That's about $14–28 versus $80+ for the cheapest tour.

The downsides are real. There is no flexibility — miss the 17:00 return and you're booking a hotel in Wadi Musa or paying for an expensive taxi back. There is no guide; you're on your own at the site. And there is exactly one bus per day, which sells out in spring and autumn. We cover the bus option in detail on the how to get to Petra from Amman page, including how to combine it with hiring a freelance guide at the Visitor Centre once you arrive.

Why the Jordan Pass changes the math

The Jordan Pass is an 80 JOD government-issued pass that bundles your visa-on-arrival fee (worth 40 JOD), one to three days of Petra entry (worth 50 to 60 JOD), and free entry to over 40 other sites. It pays for itself the moment you land in Jordan and visit Petra once. The catch: you must buy it before you arrive in Jordan — the visa waiver is the part that makes the math work, and that only happens at immigration. A separate caveat that catches people out: the visa waiver requires staying at least two nights (three days) in Jordan, so single-night transits don't qualify.

Most tour operators do not include the Petra entrance fee in their tour price, which means you'll need either a Jordan Pass or to pay 50 JOD at the gate. We have a full breakdown on the Jordan Pass page, including the unusual "non-accommodated visitor" 90 JOD fee that catches Eilat day-trippers off guard if they don't sleep in Jordan.

What time of year matters

Petra tour pricing is fairly stable year-round, but availability and the experience itself vary sharply. March through May is peak — wildflowers in the canyon, ideal walking temperatures (high 60s to high 70s °F), and the most crowded site. Tours sell out 2–4 weeks ahead. September through November is the second peak — slightly less crowded than spring, still warm enough that the Monastery hike is comfortable. December through February is the local off-season — cold mornings, occasional rain, dramatically thinner crowds. We genuinely like winter visits if you can handle the temperatures. June through August is brutal — temperatures regularly hit the high 90s °F in the canyon, the Monastery hike becomes dangerous past mid-morning, and you should plan to be on site by 6 AM or skip the trip entirely.

Frequently asked questions

Is it cheaper to take a tour or do Petra independently from Amman?
Independent travel is cheaper if there are at least two of you splitting transport. The JETT bus from Amman is roughly 10 JOD each way (about 20 JOD round-trip), versus 80–120 USD per person for a typical small-group tour that bundles transport, sometimes lunch, and a guide. Solo travelers usually break even once you add the entrance fee and any guide hire at the gate; couples and groups of three or four save real money going independent.
Are Petra tours from Amman worth it?
For most first-time visitors, yes. The drive is roughly 3 hours each way along the Desert Highway, which makes for a long day if you are also doing the driving. A tour handles the logistics, gives you 4–5 hours on site, and includes a guide who knows where to stand for the Treasury reveal. The exception is if you can stay overnight in Wadi Musa, which is what we recommend whenever a traveler can swing it — Petra rewards two days far more than one.
What's the difference between an $80 tour and a $400 tour from Amman?
Cheap tours are large group bus trips with 25–40 people, multi-hotel pickups, a fixed schedule, and limited time at the site. Expensive tours are private cars with one driver per group, flexible timing, often a private licensed guide inside Petra, and sometimes overnight extensions. The $150–250 USD range is where most travelers find the right balance: small group or private transport, a guide, and enough time on site without paying for luxury extras.
Should I book a tour in advance or wait until I arrive?
Book in advance for any tour during the spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) high seasons — popular small-group tours regularly sell out 2–4 weeks ahead. In summer and winter you can sometimes book a day or two before, but the best operators still fill up. Walk-in bookings through hotels in Amman exist, but they are usually no cheaper and give you no time to research the operator.
Do Petra tours include the entrance fee?
Most do not. The 50 JOD entrance fee for accommodated visitors (those staying at least one night in Jordan) is typically extra, paid at the Visitor Centre. Some premium private tours bundle it; cheaper group tours almost never do. If you are buying a Jordan Pass — and you almost certainly should — Petra entry is included in that pass and you simply show the QR code at the gate, which makes the question moot.
How long is a typical Petra day from Amman?
Plan for 14 hours door to door. Pickup is usually 6:00–7:00 AM, you arrive at the Visitor Centre around 10:00, spend 4–5 hours on site, leave around 15:00–16:00, and return to your Amman hotel by 19:00–20:00. Tours that promise more time on site by leaving earlier exist; tours that promise less driving are lying — Amman to Petra is a 3-hour drive whatever the brochure says.

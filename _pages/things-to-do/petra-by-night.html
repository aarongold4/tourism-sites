Petra by Night

Two thousand candles lining the Siq, traditional Bedouin music at the Treasury, and a 3D projection show on the most photographed facade in the Middle East. Sunday through Thursday at 20:30. Worth doing once.

Petra by Night is the evening companion to a daytime Petra visit — a roughly two-hour candle-lit walk from the Visitor Centre through the Siq to the Treasury plaza, where a Bedouin musician plays traditional rababa music and a projection-mapping show is cast onto the Treasury facade. It has run in some form since 1998 and is one of the few legitimate "must do if you can" evening experiences in Jordan. As of late 2025, it has been expanded from three nights a week to five (Sunday through Thursday) and the ticket price has risen to 30 JOD.

This page covers the current 2026 logistics — schedule, pricing, what happens, what to bring — and gives an honest assessment of whether it's worth fitting into your itinerary. The short version: for travelers staying overnight in Wadi Musa, yes, once. For day-trippers from Eilat, Aqaba, or Amman, it's logistically difficult and we don't recommend trying.

What the experience is

You arrive at the Petra Visitor Centre by 20:15. The gate opens at 20:30 and the assembled crowd — typically 50 to 200 people — sets off down the path toward the Siq entrance. From there, you walk the same 1.2 km canyon route as the daytime visitors take, but in near-total darkness, with the path lit only by over 2,000 paper-bag candles placed along both walls of the Siq. The effect is genuinely striking. The canyon walls disappear into shadow, the candles form a flickering line stretching ahead, and the silence — broken only by footsteps and the occasional whispered conversation — is profound.

At the Treasury plaza, you sit on rugs spread across the sand. A Bedouin musician plays for 10–15 minutes — traditional rababa (the single-stringed Arab fiddle) and sometimes oud or flute. Then the 3D projection-mapping show begins: roughly 15 minutes of light and animation cast onto the Treasury facade, telling a heavily-stylized version of the Nabataean caravan-trade story. After the show, you walk back through the Siq, retracing your steps.

The whole experience takes about two hours: 30–40 minutes walking in, 30 minutes at the Treasury, 30–40 minutes walking out.

Schedule and tickets

Days: Sunday through Thursday (5 nights a week). Closed Friday and Saturday for public events; private bookings are available those nights at premium pricing through the Petra by Night office.
Start time: 20:30 (gates open). Arrive by 20:15 to queue.
Duration: ~2 hours total.
Ticket price: 30 JOD per adult, free for children under 10.
Booking: On-site only at the Petra Visitor Centre kiosk. No online booking as of April 2026.
Jordan Pass: Not included. Petra by Night requires a separate ticket regardless of any daytime pass you hold.

The schedule can shift during Ramadan (the show is typically suspended for the holy month) and around major Jordanian holidays. The official website is petrabynight.jo, which posts schedule changes when they happen.

Whether it's worth it

For overnight visitors in Wadi Musa, yes — once. The candle-lit Siq is the part most travelers remember, and the 30 JOD price is reasonable for a two-hour, genuinely unique experience. The projection show itself is more divisive — some visitors find it moving, others find it kitsch and prefer the older pre-projection format. Either way, the Siq walk is the load-bearing part of the experience.

For day-trippers, no. The 20:30 start time means you cannot fit Petra by Night into a same-day visit from Eilat, Aqaba, or Amman — the last buses back to those cities leave well before 20:00, and a private taxi from Wadi Musa back to Amman after 22:30 will run 150 JOD or more. If you want to do Petra by Night, you need to be staying in Wadi Musa.

For repeat visitors, the value is lower. The experience hasn't changed dramatically over the years, and most repeat travelers find one visit is sufficient.

What to bring

Layers. Petra at night, particularly from October through March, is cold — canyon temperatures regularly drop below 10°C (50°F), and there is no shelter at the Treasury plaza. A warm jacket and a hat make the experience significantly more comfortable. Comfortable shoes are essential; the Siq is uneven underfoot and lit only by the candles, so you'll want footing you trust.

A camera with long-exposure capability if you have one. The standard smartphone night-mode shot of the Treasury surrounded by candles is one of the defining images of any Petra trip. Do not use a flashlight in the Siq — the experience depends on the candlelight, and using a flashlight is actively discouraged by the staff.

Frequently asked questions

When does Petra by Night run?
Sunday through Thursday, 20:30 to 22:30. The experience expanded from three nights a week (Mon/Wed/Thu) to five nights in late 2025, so older guides may show the old schedule — check the current days when you plan your itinerary. Petra by Night does not run on Fridays and Saturdays, when private bookings are possible at premium pricing. Schedule can shift during Ramadan and around Jordanian national holidays.
How much is a ticket?
30 JOD per person (up from 17 JOD in earlier years). Children under 10 are free. Tickets are sold on-site only at the Petra Visitor Centre kiosk — online booking is not available as of April 2026. The 30 JOD is in addition to your daytime Petra entrance ticket; Petra by Night is not included in either the Jordan Pass or the standard one/two/three-day Petra ticket.
How does Petra by Night work?
You arrive at the Visitor Centre by 20:15, walk down to the Siq entrance with the other guests (the group is typically 50 to 200 people), and follow the candle-lit canyon walk to the Treasury plaza. The Siq is illuminated with over 2,000 paper-bag candles placed along both walls. At the Treasury, you sit on rugs while a Bedouin musician plays traditional rababa and oud, and a 3D projection-mapping show is projected onto the Treasury facade. The whole experience takes about 2 hours including the round-trip walk.
Is Petra by Night worth it?
For travelers staying overnight in Wadi Musa, yes, once. The candle-lit Siq is genuinely magical — many visitors describe it as more memorable than the daytime Treasury reveal — and the experience of sitting in front of the illuminated Treasury at night is unique. The reservations: the projection show itself is short (~15 minutes) and has divided opinion; the crowd can detract from the contemplative atmosphere; and the 30 JOD price has risen sharply in recent years. If you have one evening in Wadi Musa and the schedule fits, go. If you have a tight budget or want pure candlelight without projection-mapping theater, the older quieter format may be missed.
What should I wear and bring?
Petra at night is cold — particularly from October through March, when canyon temperatures regularly drop below 10°C (50°F). Wear layers, including a warm jacket and a hat. Comfortable shoes are essential; the Siq is uneven underfoot and lit only by the candles. Do not use a flashlight — the experience depends on the candlelight, and flashlights are actively discouraged. Bring a small camera (long-exposure capability if you have it); the standard smartphone night-mode shot of the Treasury surrounded by candles is one of the trip's defining images.
Is Petra by Night accessible?
Partially. The Siq itself is paved and walkable for most visitors, but the path is uneven, lit only by candles, and 1.2 km each way. Wheelchair access is technically possible with assistance, and the Petra authorities can arrange a club car for elderly visitors and visitors with mobility constraints on advance request through the Visitor Centre. Younger children may struggle with the 2-hour duration and the late hour.

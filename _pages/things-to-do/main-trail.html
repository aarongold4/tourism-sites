The Main Trail

The principal walking route through Petra. 4 km from the Visitor Centre through the Siq to the western end of the lower city, with every headline monument along the way. Here's the route, the timing, and what to look for at each stop.

The Main Trail is what most visitors think of as "doing Petra" — the principal walking route from the Visitor Centre through the Siq to the major monuments and into the lower city. It's not a single named trail in the formal sense (there's no signpost saying "Main Trail starts here") but a coherent path that connects all the headline monuments in sequence. Almost every Petra visit follows the Main Trail; everything else is variation on it or extension beyond it.

This page describes the Main Trail as a complete walking experience: the route, the timing, what to see at each stop, and how to plan around it. The framing throughout: the foundation route for any Petra visit, with information that helps you walk it more rewardingly than just following the crowd.

The basic route

The Main Trail runs roughly east-to-west from the Petra Visitor Centre to the western end of the lower city. The major sections:

  1. Visitor Centre to the Siq entrance: 700 meters along a graded path. Includes the small Bab as-Siq area (entrance plaza) with the Obelisk Tomb and a few smaller carved facades along the way.
  2. The Siq: 1.2 km through the narrow canyon with carved water channels along both walls and dramatic high walls overhead. The route concludes with the famous Treasury reveal.
  3. Treasury to Street of Facades: 400 meters from the Treasury plaza into the broader open area with the Street of Facades (carved tombs along the cliff faces) on the right.
  4. Street of Facades to Royal Tombs: 500 meters bringing you to the major Royal Tombs (Urn Tomb, Silk Tomb, Corinthian Tomb, Palace Tomb).
  5. Royal Tombs to the Roman theatre: 200 meters past the theatre carved into the cliff face.
  6. Theatre to colonnaded street: 300 meters into the lower city with the colonnaded street, Great Temple, and Temple of the Winged Lions on the left and right.
  7. Colonnaded street to Qasr al-Bint: 250 meters along the paved Roman-era street to the largest free-standing temple.
  8. Qasr al-Bint to Basin Restaurant: 200 meters to the lunch area at the western end of the standard visitor circuit.

Total Main Trail distance: approximately 4 km one-way from the Visitor Centre to the Basin Restaurant. The Monastery climb is an additional 800 vertical meters and 1 km horizontal beyond the Basin — adding it to the Main Trail produces a 6 km one-way / 12 km round-trip walk.

What you see at each stop

The Bab as-Siq approach

The 700-meter walk from the Visitor Centre to the Siq entrance passes several carved monuments worth attention:

  • The Obelisk Tomb / Bab as-Siq Triclinium. A combined upper-and-lower-level carved facade with four obelisks crowning the upper level. About 1st century BCE.
  • The Djinn Blocks. Three large carved square stone blocks of debated function — possibly religious markers, possibly tomb monuments. The name "Djinn Blocks" comes from Bedouin folklore.
  • Smaller tombs. Several smaller carved facades along the path, easy to walk past without noticing.

This section is often hurried (visitors are heading for the Siq and the Treasury) but rewards slowing down. Allow 15-20 minutes here.

The Siq

The 1.2 km canyon walk is the dramatic approach. Key features:

  • Carved water channels. Visible at chest height along both walls. The Nabataean water management system carried water through the Siq for the city.
  • Carved betyl niches. Small carved cavities in the rock face originally housing sacred stones representing deities.
  • The camel relief. Partial carving of a camel-and-handler on the right wall, about halfway through the Siq.
  • The Treasury reveal. The famous moment when the Treasury facade comes into view through the final narrows of the canyon.

The Siq walk takes 25-30 minutes at moderate pace. Photographers should expect to slow down significantly for image-making opportunities throughout.

The Treasury (Al-Khazneh)

The headline monument. Allow 30-60 minutes at the Treasury plaza:

  • The 40-meter carved facade, visible from the plaza
  • The interior chamber (accessible from the lower entrance — limited but worth seeing)
  • The plaza itself, with Bedouin tea stalls and the typical visitor activity
  • The afternoon-light photography opportunity if you're doing a second pass through

See the detailed Treasury page.

Street of Facades

Walking past the Treasury, the trail opens into the Street of Facades — a stretch of cliff face with dozens of carved tomb facades at various scales. Many are pylon-style (the older simpler facade type); some are more elaborate. Worth 10-15 minutes of attention while walking.

The Royal Tombs

The major royal-funerary monuments carved into the eastern cliff above the lower city. Climb up via the staircase to the platform:

  • The Urn Tomb. The largest and southernmost. Converted to a Christian church in 446-447 CE.
  • The Silk Tomb. Named for the colorful banded sandstone visible inside.
  • The Corinthian Tomb. A Hellenistic-style facade.
  • The Palace Tomb. The largest in dimensions, with a multi-story facade.

Allow 30-45 minutes for the Royal Tombs cluster.

The Roman theatre

The 6,000-8,500 seat carved theatre, just below the Royal Tombs. See the dedicated page. Allow 15-20 minutes.

The colonnaded street and lower city

The civic axis of Petra with the major Roman-era civic buildings. Includes:

  • The Nymphaeum (large public fountain)
  • The Great Temple complex
  • The Temple of the Winged Lions
  • Qasr al-Bint (the largest free-standing temple)
  • The Triumphal Arch and various civic foundations

See the colonnaded street page and Qasr al-Bint. Allow 45-60 minutes for this section.

The Basin Restaurant

The lunch stop at the western end of the standard Main Trail. Buffet-style lunch (15-25 JOD per person), modern facilities, restrooms. The natural midday break point on a full-day visit.

Walking the trail in practice

Direction and pacing

The trail descends about 200 meters from the Visitor Centre to the Basin Restaurant, so the outbound walk is mostly downhill and the return is mostly uphill. Most travelers find the morning outbound walk easy and the late-afternoon return walk tiring — particularly in summer heat. Pace yourself accordingly: don't sprint outbound and exhaust yourself before the return walk.

What to bring

  • Water (1.5-2 liters per person per day, more in summer)
  • Sun hat and sunscreen (the Siq provides shade, but most of the trail is exposed)
  • Comfortable walking shoes (the path is uneven, with cobblestones in places)
  • Snacks (the Basin Restaurant is the only formal food option until you return)
  • Light layers (mornings can be cold, afternoons hot, especially in winter and spring)
  • Cash for tea stalls, snacks, donkey rides, tips (10-30 JOD usually sufficient for a full day)

Animals along the trail

Horse rides through the Siq are included with the Petra entry ticket (though tips of 5-10 JOD are expected). Donkey rides through the lower city and to the Monastery cost 15-25 JOD round trip. Camel rides for short distances cost 5-10 JOD. The Bedouin operators are mostly Bdoul community members; see the Bdoul page.

Animal welfare concerns about heavy tourist use have been raised. Ethical travelers should consider walking when possible and politely declining animal services. The walk through the Siq is short enough to be entirely manageable on foot.

Common timing patterns

  • Compressed (4-5 hours): Visitor Centre → Treasury → Royal Tombs → quick lower-city look → return. Misses the Monastery climb. Suitable for day-trippers from Aqaba or Eilat.
  • Standard (7-9 hours): Full Main Trail + Monastery climb + sufficient time at each major monument. Day 1 of a multi-day visit.
  • Extended (full day): Standard route + Al-Khubtha trail to Treasury overlook + High Place of Sacrifice. Requires 06:00 entry and willingness to keep moving through the day.

Extensions beyond the Main Trail

For travelers spending 2+ days at Petra, the Main Trail is the foundation that other routes branch from:

  • The Monastery climb — 800 steps from the lower city to the Monastery facade. The single most important extension. See the Monastery hike page.
  • The Al-Khubtha Trail — climb from the Royal Tombs area to a high overlook of the Treasury. See the Al-Khubtha trail page.
  • The High Place of Sacrifice — climb from the Street of Facades to the mountain summit south of the lower city. See High Place page.
  • The Jebel Haroun trail — full-day hike to Aaron's Tomb on the highest peak in the Petra area.
  • The back route from Little Petra — alternate entry avoiding the Siq.
  • The Wadi Mataha route — back canyons and less-visited carved tombs.

Frequently asked questions

What's the Main Trail at Petra?
The principal walking route from the Visitor Centre through the Siq to the major monuments and into the lower city — the path that all standard Petra visits follow. The full Main Trail is approximately 4 km one-way from the Visitor Centre to the western end of the lower city (where the Monastery climb begins). Following the Main Trail and returning the same way produces a basic 8-9 km round-trip walk that covers the headline monuments.
How long does walking the Main Trail take?
Variable depending on pace and stops. Walking the full Main Trail one-way without major stops takes about 90 minutes; with extended stops at the Treasury, Royal Tombs, and lower-city monuments, expect 4-6 hours for the round trip. Adding the Monastery climb (recommended) extends the total to 7-9 hours for a comprehensive Day 1 visit. The trail is mostly downhill on the way in (descending the canyon to the lower city) and mostly uphill on the return.
Is the trail well-marked?
Yes. The Main Trail is the most visible and obvious path through Petra, with the canyon walls and natural geography providing clear orientation. Signage marks major monuments and side trail junctions. GPS and Google Maps work reasonably well in the lower city; the Siq section has limited GPS reception due to the narrow canyon walls. For first-time visitors, the trail is essentially impossible to lose — you follow the obvious main path and emerge at the major monuments in sequence.
Can I rent a horse, donkey, or camel?
Yes, at multiple points along the Main Trail. Horse rides through the Siq from the Visitor Centre to the Treasury are included with the Petra entrance ticket (though tips of 5-10 JOD per person are expected). Donkey rides through the lower city and up to the Monastery cost 15-25 JOD round trip. Camel rides for short distances at the Treasury and lower city cost 5-10 JOD. Animal welfare concerns about heavy tourist use have been raised; ethical travelers should consider walking and politely declining animal services where possible.
Are there alternatives to the Main Trail?
Yes, several. The Al-Khubtha Trail climbs to a high overlook of the Treasury. The High Place of Sacrifice trail climbs to the mountain summit south of the lower city. The 'back route' enters Petra from Little Petra in the north, avoiding the Siq entirely. The Jebel Haroun trail leads to Aaron's Tomb. The Wadi Mataha route explores the back canyons. These alternative trails are more demanding than the Main Trail and reward travelers spending multiple days at Petra.

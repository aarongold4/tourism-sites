Samuel's Fortress

The ramparts of a thousand-year-old capital — and the best panorama in town.

Crowning Ohrid's hill with three kilometers of walls and a crown of towers, Samuel's Fortress is the town's full stop — the place every lane eventually climbs to. Around the year 1000 this was the capital of Tsar Samuel's empire, which at its height ran from the Adriatic to the Black Sea, and the citadel he raised (on fortifications dating back to the 4th century BC — Livy was already mentioning a fort here in Roman times) remains the largest and best-preserved in the country.

The honest pitch

Be clear about what you're climbing for: the view, not the ruins. The interior is mostly open grass and excavation trenches; there is no museum, little signage and no shade. But the wall-walk delivers the single best panorama on the lake — the full sweep of red-roofed old town below, the harbor, thirty kilometers of blue to the Albanian shore, and Galičica's ridge rising to the east. On a clear day it is genuinely spectacular, and the entrance fee of about 120 MKD makes it one of Europe's cheapest great views.

The history under your feet

Samuel ruled what historians call the First Bulgarian Empire from Ohrid until Byzantine emperor Basil II — afterward nicknamed "the Bulgar-Slayer" — broke his army in 1014; the story goes that Basil blinded 14,000 prisoners, leaving one eye per hundred men to lead them home, and Samuel died of the sight of them. The fortress passed to Byzantium, then through Norman, Serbian and five centuries of Ottoman hands, each rebuilding on the last. The walls you walk are substantially Samuel's era, heavily restored in the 2000s — purists grumble about the restoration's polish; visitors get ramparts you can actually walk.

Doing it right

Come late afternoon: the climb is cooler, the light on the lake is at its best, and you can descend via Plaošnik — where the restored Church of St. Clement and the active archaeology of the old university site sit just below the walls — and continue down the pine path to Kaneo for sunset. That descent, fortress to cliff-church, is the best forty minutes of walking in North Macedonia. Bring water and a hat in summer; the top is exposed. Hours follow a roughly daytime pattern (shorter off-season) and the gate takes cash only.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Samuel's Fortress cost?

Around 120 MKD (roughly €2–3) for foreign visitors, paid in cash at the gate. Cheap by any standard — the view alone covers it.

Is the climb difficult?

It's a steady 15–20 minute uphill walk from the old town on cobbles and steps — sweaty in summer, manageable for most fitness levels. There is no vehicle access to the gate; the reward arrives all at once at the top.

Is there much inside the fortress?

Honestly, no — the interior is largely open ground with ongoing archaeology. You come for the walls themselves and the 360-degree panorama of town, lake and mountains. Budget 30–45 minutes.

Petra day tours from Amman

The 2026 operator landscape: budget group tours from $80 per person, small-group tours at $150-250, private tours from $300, and multi-day packages adding Wadi Rum or the Dead Sea. Here's how to choose and what to actually book.

The Petra-from-Amman tour market is well-developed, with dozens of operators running tours across a wide price and quality range. The basic structure is straightforward: an early morning pickup from your Amman hotel, a 3-hour drive south to Petra, 5-6 hours on the archaeological site, and a 3-hour drive back arriving in Amman by 19:30-20:00. The variation lives in what you get for the price — group size, transport quality, whether a guide is included, whether lunch is included, and the operator's overall service quality. This page covers the four main price tiers and how to choose between them.

The headline framing: tours are not the right answer for most travelers. If you have at least 2 nights in Jordan, independent travel (JETT bus or rental car) plus a 2-night Wadi Musa stay produces a dramatically better Petra experience at roughly half the cost. Tours make sense for genuinely time-constrained travelers, families and solo travelers who want logistics handled, and anyone uncomfortable with the cultural transition. If that's you, the section below covers what to look for.

The four price tiers

Budget group tours — $80-120 USD per person

Large coach tours with 15-30 passengers, basic shared transport, light commentary on the bus, no licensed guide on site at Petra. You're essentially paying for transport, the entrance ticket, and a driver who knows the route. Lunch is occasionally included but often extra. The Petra time is roughly 5 hours on site (10:00-15:00).

These work for travelers who want the cheapest possible logistics-handled option and don't mind a coach-tour atmosphere. They're standard for cruise-ship passengers doing Petra as a port excursion. The trade-off is meaningful: no on-site guide, larger groups slow everything down, and you'll be back at the bus by 15:30 whether you've seen what you want or not. Operators: most major Jordan tourism agencies offer this tier; the aggregators list dozens.

Small-group tours — $150-250 USD per person

The sweet spot for most tour-goers. Groups of 6-12 in a comfortable van or mini-coach, English-speaking driver, licensed guide on site at Petra (usually 3-4 hours of guided walking, plus another 2-3 hours of free time), lunch typically included at a Wadi Musa restaurant. Petra time runs 5-7 hours on site (10:00-16:00 or thereabouts).

The improved guide-to-traveler ratio means meaningfully better experience — your guide can answer questions, the walking pace adapts to the group's energy, and you'll see things the budget tours skim past. The trade-off versus budget: roughly 50-100% more expensive. Most aggregator-rated 4.5+ star Petra tours sit in this tier.

Private day tours — $200-500 USD per person for two travelers

Private vehicle, driver and/or guide, fully flexible pacing, custom itinerary, pickup time of your choosing. The price-per-person drops if you're a group of 3-4; the per-car cost is typically $350-700 for the day regardless of party size. Lunch usually included or arranged at a restaurant of your choice.

The right choice for: travelers who want the flexibility to start early (06:00 rather than 07:00), pace the day around their interests rather than the group's, take detours to Karak Castle or Wadi Musa town, and have the option of Petra by Night without scheduling conflicts. The trade-off is cost — a couple paying $600 for a private day tour could instead pay $200 total for a JETT bus round-trip plus 2 nights at a mid-range Wadi Musa hotel.

Multi-day tours — $400-900+ USD per person

The 2-3 day packages combining Petra with Wadi Rum, the Dead Sea, Jerash, or other major sites. Typical configurations:

  • 2-day Petra + Wadi Rum: $400-650 per person, includes one night at a Wadi Rum Bedouin camp
  • 3-day Petra + Wadi Rum + Dead Sea: $600-900 per person, includes hotel nights and meals
  • 4-day Jordan loop: $700-1,200 per person, adds Jerash and Amman city tour

These make sense for travelers who want the full Jordan experience without logistics work, particularly those with limited Arabic and a strong preference for "everything handled." For independent travelers, doing the same loop yourself runs roughly 40-50% cheaper.

The aggregator landscape

Three main booking platforms handle the Jordan tour market:

Viator (a TripAdvisor company). Largest catalog, strong review system tied to TripAdvisor's broader review database, easy cancellation (typically free up to 24 hours before departure), English-language customer support. Pricing is competitive across most operators. The platform's "Likely to sell out" indicator is genuinely useful for popular tours.

GetYourGuide. Similar catalog to Viator (most operators list on both), often cleaner interface, similarly strong review system, similarly easy cancellation. Sometimes slightly cheaper than Viator for the same tour due to different commission structures; sometimes slightly more expensive.

Direct booking with Jordan operators. Cheaper by 10-20% in most cases — you're cutting out the aggregator commission — but you lose the buyer protection (aggregator dispute resolution, free cancellation, review-based quality signal). Worth doing if you have a personal recommendation; risky if you're starting from scratch.

Our practical recommendation: Compare the same tour on both Viator and GetYourGuide before booking. Pricing differs by 5-15% in either direction for identical tours. Read the most recent 10-15 reviews on whichever platform shows the most. Book whichever is cheaper at the time, unless one has materially better recent reviews.

What to look for in a Petra tour

Signals of a quality tour:

  • "Licensed guide" specifically named (not just "driver/guide") — Jordan licenses Petra guides separately and you want a real one on site
  • Group size capped at 12 or under for small-group tours
  • 4+ hours minimum on site at Petra, ideally 5+
  • 06:30-07:00 pickup time — anything later and you arrive at Petra after the morning Treasury light is gone
  • Recent reviews (within 90 days) averaging 4.5+ stars
  • Entrance ticket included in the price (some tours quote prices excluding the 90 JOD non-accommodated Petra entry)
  • Explicit lunch arrangement — included at a named restaurant or specifically noted as not included
  • Air-conditioned vehicle — Jordan summer especially, the drive is unpleasant without AC

Red flags:

  • Group size not specified or "varies"
  • Less than 4 hours on site at Petra (means you can't reach the Monastery)
  • Recent reviews flagging "rushed schedule" or "had to leave before we wanted to"
  • Vague language about "Petra highlights" without specifying which monuments
  • Pickup time after 07:30
  • Entrance ticket pricing ambiguous or paid separately on site (small chance of being charged the 90 JOD non-accommodated rate vs the 50 JOD accommodated rate)

The independent-travel comparison

For honest comparison, here's the rough math on doing Petra independently from Amman over the same 1-2 day window:

  • JETT bus round-trip Amman-Petra: ~22 JOD ($31 USD)
  • Two nights at a mid-range Wadi Musa hotel: ~$120-200 total
  • Two-day Petra ticket (or Jordan Pass Explorer covering the same): $77-105 USD
  • Three meals per day (cheap-to-mid): ~$60 total
  • Total per person: ~$300-450

For two people doing the same trip:

  • Same hotel doubles as a couple (no marginal cost)
  • Same JETT bus tickets
  • Total per couple: ~$500-700, or $250-350 per person

Versus a small-group day tour at $150-250 per person, plus the 90 JOD non-accommodated Petra entry the day tour requires ($128 USD), totaling $278-378 per person for just one compressed day on site.

For most travelers, the independent option is meaningfully cheaper AND meaningfully better. The case for a tour is specifically about logistics tolerance, not value.

Frequently asked questions

How much do Petra day tours from Amman cost?
The 2026 market sits in three rough price tiers. Budget group tours run $80-120 USD per person — large groups (15-30 people), basic transport, light commentary. Small-group tours run $150-250 per person — 6-12 people, more flexible pacing, a licensed guide on site at Petra. Private day tours run $200-500 per person for two travelers — a private vehicle, driver and/or guide, fully customized pace. Multi-day tours combining Petra with Wadi Rum or the Dead Sea typically run $400-900 per person.
What's the difference between Viator, GetYourGuide, and booking directly?
Viator and GetYourGuide are tour aggregators — they don't run tours themselves, they list tours from third-party operators. Both have strong review systems, English-language support, and easy cancellation policies (typically free cancellation 24+ hours before departure). The same tour is often listed on both platforms at near-identical pricing. Booking directly with a Jordanian operator can be 10-20% cheaper but loses the aggregator buyer protection — useful if you have a personal recommendation or have used the operator before.
What time do these tours leave Amman?
Most day tours leave between 06:00 and 07:00 from Amman hotels. The drive south is 3 hours, putting you at Petra around 10:00 — slightly later than the 06:00 entry strategy independent travelers can manage, but better than the JETT bus which arrives at the same time. Tours typically depart Petra around 16:00, putting you back in Amman by 19:30-20:00. The total day is 13-14 hours.
Is a guide included?
Depends on the tier. Budget group tours typically include only the driver — you're left to explore Petra on your own (Bedouin guides can be hired on site for 50 JOD per 2 hours). Small-group and private tours typically include a licensed guide who walks Petra with you — meaningfully better if you want context for what you're seeing. The guide language is almost always English, with Spanish/French/German available on request from premium operators.
Should I take a tour or do it independently?
Take a tour if: you're short on time and want zero logistics, you don't speak Arabic, you're traveling solo and want company, or you specifically want a guide at Petra. Skip the tour if: you have at least 2 nights in Jordan (independent travel is cheaper and more flexible), you're traveling with a group of 3-4 (a hired driver for the day is cheaper than per-person tour pricing), or you specifically want to do Petra by Night (most day tours don't accommodate it). For most travelers, independent travel with 2 nights in Wadi Musa is the better choice.
What about multi-day Petra + Wadi Rum + Dead Sea tours?
These are the most common 2-3 day Jordan package on the aggregator platforms. Typical pricing: 2-day Petra + Wadi Rum from $400-650 USD per person, 3-day Petra + Wadi Rum + Dead Sea from $600-900 per person. The premium covers transport, accommodation, guides, and meals. They make sense for travelers who want one-week Jordan experience without logistics work. For travelers comfortable with independent travel, doing it yourself is dramatically cheaper.

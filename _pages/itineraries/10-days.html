10 Days in North Macedonia

The full national circle — mosques, monasteries, two national parks and the wine plain.

HomeItineraries10 Days in North Macedonia

Ten days turns North Macedonia into a circle: out through the painted mosques and monastery gorges of the west, the lake at the center, the consul city and glacial lakes of the south, home through the highest town in the Balkans and the vineyards. With a rental car it is one of Europe's great undiscovered road trips; total driving, under twelve hours spread across the whole loop.

Days 1–2 — Skopje + Matka

The standard capital opening: bazaar-and-bridges day, then Matka Canyon with the Vodno option. Collect the car on the morning of day 3.

Day 3 — Tetovo and the Radika gorge to Mavrovo

West to Tetovo's Painted Mosque (45 minutes), then south into Mavrovo National Park: the half-submerged church of St. Nicholas at the lake, and the day's centerpiece, Bigorski Monastery in the Radika gorge — its carved walnut iconostasis is the masterpiece of Macedonian woodwork. Night in Mavrovo or continue to Ohrid (the gorge road south is spectacular either way).

Days 4–6 — the lake, in full

Three Ohrid nights running the proven sequence: hill circuit and Kaneo sunset; the St. Naum boat day; then the choice day — Galičica's Magaro hike for the two-lakes view, or a Trpejca-and-beaches day for the indolent. Drivers can add the Galičica panoramic road over to Lake Prespa as a half-day.

Days 7–8 — Bitola and Pelister

Ninety minutes to Bitola: Širok Sokak, Heraclea's mosaics in afternoon light, boulevard dinner (day 7). Day 8 goes up: Pelister National Park — the trail to the two glacial "Eyes" beneath the 2,601 m summit, or a gentler forest loop among five-needle Molika pines — then back to Bitola or onward to Kruševo by evening.

Day 9 — Kruševo to the wine plain

Morning in Kruševo: the spaceship Makedonium, the 1903 republic's story, mountain air — and for the committed, a pre-booked tandem paraglide (€60–80) off one of Europe's best-value sites. Afternoon drive east to Tikveš: check into Popova Kula's tower hotel or a Kavadarci stay, tasting (€5–15) before dinner. The designated-driver math is why you sleep among the vines.

Day 10 — Stobi and home

The Roman city of Stobi sits beside the motorway ten minutes from the wineries — theatre, basilicas, baptistery mosaics, usually near-empty at opening time. Then the easy hour up the highway to Skopje: car back, last çevapi, fly out.

The extensions

With more days: exit at St. Naum into Albania (Tirana 2.5 hours, the Riviera beyond — confirm the rental's cross-border permission), or finish with a Prizren, Kosovo night from Skopje (green card coverage check, again). The loop also compresses to 8 days by cutting Pelister and merging the wine leg into the final drive — but at ten, nothing feels owed.

The driving, demystified

Total wheel time across the whole loop is under twelve hours, spread over ten days — no single leg exceeds about two and a half hours, and most run well under. Rentals start around €25–35/day for a compact; collect the car the morning of day 3 (you don't need it for the walkable Skopje–Matka opening) and return it on day 10 before flying out, keeping the rental clock to roughly eight days. The practical notes that matter here: headlights stay on by law day and night, motorway tolls are small and frequent (keep denars or a card), mountain roads are good but slow — plan 50 km/h averages off the motorway — and the Galičica and Radika gorge sections are spectacular enough to budget photo-stop time into. For Albania or Kosovo extensions, confirm the rental agreement and green-card cover those countries before crossing; many exclude Kosovo by default.

What ten days costs

Budget roughly €700–1,000 per person on the ground for the full loop at comfortable mid-range — lodging across seven distinct bases, all meals, the rental share (split between two it's modest), fuel, tolls, tastings and entries. The wine night among the vines and a Kruševo paraglide are the optional splurges; everything structural stays cheap. Solo drivers carry the whole car cost and land higher; pairs and groups of three or four make the loop one of Europe's great-value road trips, since the car cost divides while the per-night and per-meal prices stay tiny. Two travelers sharing typically run the ten days for well under €1,500 each, flights aside.

Compressing it to eight days

If ten is a stretch, the loop shortens gracefully to eight by cutting the Pelister day (days 7–8 become a single Bitola-and-Heraclea stop) and merging the wine leg into the final drive — Stobi and one Tikveš tasting on the way back to Skopje rather than an overnight among the vines. You lose the glacial-lakes hike and the unhurried cellar evening, but keep the loop's spine: the western mosques and monasteries, the full lake, the southern city and the Roman ruins. At ten days nothing feels owed; at eight it still feels like the whole country rather than a highlights reel.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a car for the 10-day loop?

Strongly recommended — Mavrovo, Bigorski, Kruševo and the wine country are the loop's soul and bus access to them ranges from thin to theoretical. Budget €25–35/day plus cheap fuel; the freedom repays it daily.

Can I extend into neighboring countries?

That's the loop's party trick: Ohrid sits an hour from Albania (cross at St. Naum toward the Riviera), Skopje two hours from Prizren in Kosovo, and the Greek border 90 minutes south. Ten days here converts into a 14-day Balkans run with one decision.

Which direction should I drive the loop?

Clockwise (west first: Tetovo → Mavrovo → Ohrid) front-loads mountains and saves the wine plain for the relaxed final leg — and puts the lake at the trip's center, where it belongs. Counter-clockwise works fine in reverse logic.

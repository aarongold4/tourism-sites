GETTING THERE

From Wadi Rum

The transport connections out of Wadi Rum — to Petra, Aqaba, Amman, or the Eilat border. Where to leave your car during the camp stay, what the JETT bus schedules look like, and how to handle the transition from desert camp back to the wider trip.

Last reviewed June 5, 2026 · 6-minute read · See our editorial standards

Wadi Rum sits at the southern end of Jordan, 60 km north of Aqaba and 100 km south of Petra. The transport connections out of Wadi Rum are straightforward — well-maintained highways, multiple service options, no major logistical complications — but worth understanding before you arrive at the camp. This page covers the major destinations, the transport options to each, and the practical details of handling the transition from Bedouin camp back to the rest of your trip.

The transit basics

Wadi Rum visits work as a wheel-and-spoke arrangement: the Wadi Rum Visitor Centre is the hub, the Bedouin camps inside the protected area are the spokes. All transport in and out of Wadi Rum passes through the Visitor Centre — your camp transfer (4WD vehicle, included in the camp package) takes you from the Visitor Centre to the camp on arrival and back on departure.

When you arrive at the Visitor Centre, you pay the entrance fee (5 JOD per person, included in the Jordan Pass), meet your camp's pickup vehicle, transfer your overnight bag, and leave the rest of your luggage and any vehicle in the Visitor Centre parking lot. The car stays parked while you're in the camp; you retrieve it when you transfer back.

When you depart Wadi Rum the next day (or however long you stay), the camp transfers you back to the Visitor Centre and from there you continue to your next destination by whichever transport option you've arranged.

To Petra (100 km north, 2 hours)

The most common Wadi Rum departure direction. The route is on the Desert Highway, well-maintained, well-signed in English. Options:

Your own rental car. The most flexible option. Drive from the Wadi Rum Visitor Centre back up the Desert Highway to the Petra (Wadi Musa) turnoff, then west to Wadi Musa. About 2 hours total. Optional stop at Shobak Castle on the way (45-minute detour, 90-minute visit, free with Jordan Pass).

JETT bus. JETT operates a daily Wadi Rum-Petra service, typically morning departure from the Visitor Centre. About 2 hours travel, around 22 JOD per person. Check the JETT website (jett.com.jo) for current schedules; the timing changes seasonally and isn't always perfectly aligned with camp checkout times.

Private transfer. Most Bedouin camps can arrange private transport to Petra for around 80-110 JOD for the car (1-3 passengers). The driver picks you up at the Visitor Centre and delivers you to your Petra hotel. The most comfortable option; about 2 hours travel.

Shared taxi (service). Operates from the Visitor Centre when sufficient passengers gather, typically morning. About 10-15 JOD per person. Slower and less comfortable than private options but the budget choice.

To Aqaba (60 km south, 1 hour)

The natural option if you're flying out of Aqaba King Hussein Airport (AQJ), crossing to Eilat via the Wadi Araba border, or adding a diving stop to your trip.

Driving: 60 km on the Desert Highway, about 1 hour. Direct and easy.

JETT bus: Daily Wadi Rum-Aqaba service, approximately 15-18 JOD per person. The bus stops at central Aqaba; from there you take a taxi to your hotel, the airport, or the border.

Private transfer: 50-70 JOD for the car. Slightly cheaper than the Petra direction because the distance is shorter.

Shared taxi: 5-8 JOD per person from the Visitor Centre to Aqaba.

To Amman (320 km north, 4 hours)

The long return option, typically for travelers heading back to Queen Alia airport (AMM) or continuing to the Dead Sea or northern Jordan destinations.

Driving: 320 km on the Desert Highway, about 4 hours non-stop. Optional stop at Petra/Shobak (adding 2-3 hours), or at the Dead Sea (turn west at the Karak intersection — adds about 1.5 hours but combines well with a half-day Dead Sea visit).

JETT bus: The Wadi Rum-Amman direct JETT service runs daily, around 22-25 JOD per person, about 5 hours including stops. Check current schedules at jett.com.jo.

Private transfer: 140-180 JOD for the car. Worth it for groups of 3-4 splitting the cost; less compelling for couples versus the JETT bus.

To the Eilat border (60 km southwest)

For travelers crossing back to Israel via the Wadi Araba / Yitzhak Rabin border crossing south of Aqaba.

The crossing is essentially at the southern edge of Aqaba — go to Aqaba via any of the options above, then take a short taxi (10-15 JOD, 15 minutes) from central Aqaba to the border. The Wadi Araba border is open Sun-Thu 06:30-20:00, Fri 08:00-20:00, closed Saturdays for Israeli Shabbat. See the from-Eilat page for full border-crossing logistics (the same process in reverse).

To the Dead Sea (350 km north, 4 hours via Desert Highway + Dead Sea Road)

For travelers including the Dead Sea on a Jordan loop after Wadi Rum. The most common arrangement: drive north on the Desert Highway, turn west at the Karak/Madaba intersection, descend to the Dead Sea via the Dead Sea Road. Total about 4 hours of driving.

Most travelers do this with a rental car since the public transport connections are awkward. Private transfer is possible but expensive ($150-200 USD for the car) given the distance.

Practical details

Timing your departure

Most Wadi Rum camps do sunrise tours, breakfast, and then a transfer back to the Visitor Centre between 09:00 and 10:00. From the Visitor Centre, you can typically be on the road by 10:30 — which works well for an early-afternoon arrival at Petra, lunchtime arrival in Aqaba, or evening arrival in Amman.

If you have an early flight or critical schedule, communicate with your camp the night before — earlier transfers are usually possible but require arranging in advance.

Luggage logistics

Take only what you need at the camp — typically a small overnight bag with toiletries, change of clothes, warm layers for the evening, and any photography gear. Leave the rest in your rental car at the Visitor Centre (the parking is secure) or coordinate with your hotel in the next destination to store luggage if you're returning to a hotel where you previously stayed.

Combining destinations

A common pattern: stay at Wadi Rum for 1-2 nights, then drive north to Petra OR south to Aqaba depending on what's next. For travelers doing a full Jordan loop, the typical sequence is Amman → Petra → Wadi Rum → Aqaba (or back to Amman via the Dead Sea). For travelers crossing to/from Israel, Wadi Rum fits naturally between Aqaba (border crossing) and Petra.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get from Wadi Rum to Petra?
100 km on the Desert Highway, about 2 hours by road. Options include: (1) your own rental car (most flexible, $0 incremental cost if you already have one), (2) a JETT bus connection (around 22 JOD per person each way, scheduled service that fits the camp transfer logistics), (3) a private taxi or transfer ($60-100 USD one-way, arranged through your camp or hotel), or (4) a shared taxi (10-15 JOD per person, lower comfort, available at the Wadi Rum Visitor Centre). For most travelers staying overnight in Wadi Rum, the camp transfer back to the Visitor Centre includes the option of continuing to Petra by various means.
Can I do Wadi Rum and Petra back-to-back in one day?
Technically yes, practically no for the full Wadi Rum experience. Some operators run day-trip Wadi Rum visits from Petra (Wadi Musa) — early morning departure, 4-6 hours in Wadi Rum (limited to a daytime jeep tour, no overnight), return to Petra by evening. This skips the parts of Wadi Rum that matter most (sunset, stargazing, the overnight Bedouin camp dinner). Most travelers should do at least one overnight in Wadi Rum if they're including it in the trip at all.
What about driving from Wadi Rum to Aqaba or back to Amman?
Wadi Rum to Aqaba is 60 km, about 1 hour on the Desert Highway. Wadi Rum to Amman is 320 km, about 4 hours on the Desert Highway. Both routes are well-maintained and well-signed. The Aqaba route is the natural option for travelers crossing the border to Eilat or continuing to dive sites; the Amman route is the natural option for travelers returning to Queen Alia airport or continuing to the Dead Sea/Jerash on a longer Jordan loop.
Where do I leave my car during the Wadi Rum stay?
At the Wadi Rum Visitor Centre parking lot, which is free for guests of the licensed Bedouin camps. You drive to the Visitor Centre, meet your camp's pickup vehicle (4WD, included in the camp package), transfer your overnight bag, and leave the rest of your luggage and the car at the Visitor Centre. The parking is secure (gated and monitored). When you return from the camp the next day, you retrieve your vehicle and continue your trip. Most camps confirm this logistics during the booking process.
Is there public transport from Wadi Rum?
Limited but functional. JETT bus operates one daily service between Wadi Rum and major destinations (typically Petra, Aqaba, sometimes Amman) with morning departures. The schedule changes seasonally; check current times when planning. Shared service taxis also operate from the Visitor Centre to nearby destinations, with departure times depending on when the taxi fills with passengers — typically morning is most reliable, mid-afternoon less so.

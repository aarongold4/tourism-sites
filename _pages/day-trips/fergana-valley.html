The Fergana Valley

Silk, ceramics and the old Kokand khanate — Uzbekistan's craft heartland, over the Kamchik Pass.

East of Tashkent, beyond the Kamchik Pass, lies the Fergana Valley — the fertile, densely populated cradle of Uzbek craft. This is where the country&apos;s famous silk ikat is woven by hand and its blue-glazed ceramics are turned and fired. It sees far fewer tourists than the Silk Road cities, which is much of its appeal.

Getting there from Tashkent

The valley is reached over the Kamchik Pass (around 2,267 m), and it is a long haul: roughly four to six hours by car, five to six hours by train (stopping at Kokand, Margilan and Andijan), or about a 50-minute domestic flight. Because of the distance, the Fergana Valley is best treated as an overnight trip, though very long single-day tours do exist.

The three craft towns

  • Kokand — the former khanate capital, with the lavish Khudoyar Khan Palace and the Jami Mosque.
  • Rishtan — the ceramics centre, famous for its ishkor blue-green glaze; visit a master potter&apos;s workshop such as the well-known Rustam Usmanov studio.
  • Margilan — the silk capital, home to the Yodgorlik factory where ikat is still made entirely by hand, plus the big Kumtepa Bazaar (busiest Thursdays and Sundays).

How to do it

Most visitors take a guided tour with a driver, which handles the pass and the scattered workshops in one or two days. If you prefer independence, trains run from Tashkent to Margilan and Kokand. Either way, plan an overnight in Fergana or Margilan rather than trying to round-trip it in a day. Booking links for tours will appear here once our partner programmes are live.

Frequently asked questions

How do you get to the Fergana Valley from Tashkent?

Over the Kamchik Pass — about four to six hours by car, five to six by train (via Kokand and Margilan), or a roughly 50-minute flight. The distance makes it an overnight trip rather than a true day trip.

What is the Fergana Valley known for?

Traditional crafts: handmade silk ikat woven at the Yodgorlik factory in Margilan, and the blue-glazed ceramics of Rishtan. Kokand adds the history of the old khanate, including the Khudoyar Khan Palace.

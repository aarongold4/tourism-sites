How they were built: settled and debated

'Nobody knows how the pyramids were built' is wrong twice — most of it is well understood, and the genuinely open parts are better questions than the fake ones. Here's the evidence, sorted honestly into settled, debated, and discovered-last-decade.

Last reviewed June 6, 2026◆9-minute read

Photo: kallerna · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons

Pyramid construction is the rare topic where popular culture insists on mystery while the evidence keeps answering. The honest state of knowledge: the materials, tools, transport, and organization are settled to a degree few ancient industries match; the precise ramp configurations remain genuinely debated; and the last decade added discoveries — a foreman's logbook, hidden voids — that working Egyptologists consider more exciting than any lost- civilization fantasy. Here's the whole picture in three tiers.

Settled: materials, tools, and transport

The stone: core blocks of local limestone — the quarries, with half-extracted blocks still attached, sit a few hundred meters south of the pyramids; fine white casing from Tura across the river (its delivery documented in Merer's logbook); granite for the chambers from Aswan, 800 kilometers upstream, barged down on the flood. The tools: copper chisels and saws (constantly re-sharpened — the smithies are in the workers' city), dolerite pounders for granite, wooden sledges, levers, ropes, and surveying kit — plumb bobs, sighting tools, water-leveling — sufficient, as the base's two-centimeter levelness proves. The moving: sledges over prepared causeways, with the famous Djehutihotep tomb scene showing the water-pouring trick that modern experiments confirm cuts hauling friction dramatically. None of this is speculative; it's excavated, depicted, or documented.

Settled: the organization

The deepest answer to "how" is administrative, and it's the best-evidenced part of all: a purpose-built town provisioning thousands of rotating workers with bread, beer, and remarkable quantities of beef; named crews competing and signing their work; a supply chain spanning the country; and middle managers filing reports — the full story in the builders' page. Twenty years of Khufu's reign, a workforce around 20,000-30,000 at peak, an average block placed every few working minutes: numbers that stop sounding impossible the moment the bakeries are in front of you.

Debated: the ramps

How blocks rose remains the legitimate open question, with serious candidates each fitting some evidence: a single great straight ramp (simple, but its volume rivals the pyramid's and no trace survives at Giza); spiral ramps wrapping the exterior (modest volume, but they obscure the corners surveyors needed); the internal ramp hypothesis (elegant for the upper courses, testable in principle, unproven); and pragmatic combinations — most working reconstructions stage a straight ramp for the bulk lower two-thirds, then something cleverer above. Ramp remnants at other pyramids and quarries confirm the technology; the specific Giza configuration is honestly unresolved, and "we don't know exactly which ramp" is a very different sentence from "we don't know how."

The new frontier: the voids

Since 2017, muon tomography — imaging the pyramid with cosmic-ray particles — has confirmed two genuine discoveries in the Great Pyramid: a corridor behind the north face's chevron blocks (visually confirmed by endoscope in 2023) and a large void above the Grand Gallery. The sober interpretations involve construction logic — stress-relieving spaces like the known relieving chambers, or remnants of the building process itself; the investigation is ongoing, peer-reviewed, and the best reminder that the real pyramid keeps producing real news. The manufactured mysteries have to invent theirs.

The proof in the failures

The strongest single argument that humans figured this out the human way stands at Dahshur: Meidum's collapse, the Bent Pyramid's mid-build correction, the Red Pyramid's success — a visible learning curve, in datable order, one generation before Giza. Perfected technology arriving from nowhere leaves no drafts. The drafts are standing in the desert, and you can climb inside the final exam.

Frequently asked questions

How long did the Great Pyramid take to build?

Around twenty years — Khufu's reign length, supported by dated graffiti and the logistics math. That implies setting an average block every few minutes of every working day for two decades, which is exactly the kind of number that sounds impossible until you've seen the workers' city that organized it.

How were the heavy granite beams moved?

Quarried at Aswan 800km south, shipped downriver on barges during the flood, hauled up from the harbor on sledges over prepared surfaces — water-slicked, per the famous tomb scene and modern experiments — and raised by ramp and lever. The King's Chamber's 50-80 ton beams are the proof of concept sitting in place.

What are the voids found inside the Great Pyramid?

Muon-detection surveys (2017-2023) confirmed a large void above the Grand Gallery and a corridor behind the north face's chevrons — real, peer-reviewed discoveries. Leading interpretations involve construction relieving structures; nobody serious claims treasure chambers, and investigation continues. It's the genuine frontier, as opposed to the manufactured ones.

Were the blocks concrete?

The geopolymer hypothesis — blocks cast in place rather than quarried — has been tested and rejected: the stones' fossils, density variations, and quarry-matching all indicate cut natural limestone, and the actual quarries with half-extracted blocks sit beside the pyramids. A fun idea the evidence declined.

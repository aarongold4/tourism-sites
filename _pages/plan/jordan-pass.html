The Jordan Pass: is it worth it?

The short answer is almost certainly yes — but the math depends on how long you're staying and which other sites you'll see. Here's the full breakdown.

Last reviewed April 18, 2026 · 6-minute read · See our editorial standards

The Jordan Pass is one of those rare tourism-board ideas that's straightforwardly a good deal for almost every traveler. It bundles the visa-on-arrival fee, Petra entrance, and over 40 other sites into a single pre-paid ticket. For anyone visiting Petra plus one other major site, it pays for itself with room to spare.

The catch — and there's only one — is that you have to buy it before you arrive in Jordan. Show up at the airport without a Jordan Pass and you'll pay the 40 JOD visa fee at the booth, after which buying the pass becomes pointless.

What you get

Three tiers, all priced in Jordanian Dinar:

  • Jordan Wanderer — 70 JOD. One-day entry to Petra plus visa waiver and 40+ other sites.
  • Jordan Explorer — 75 JOD. Two-day entry to Petra. Recommended for most travelers.
  • Jordan Expert — 80 JOD. Three-day entry to Petra. Worth it for serious hikers planning the Monastery, Al-Khubtha, and a sunrise visit.

The math

Standalone, the Petra entrance fee is 50 JOD for one day, 55 for two days, 60 for three days — but only if you stay overnight in Jordan. Day-trippers from Israel pay a punitive 90 JOD for one day, which is the single best reason to spend at least one night in Wadi Musa.

Add the 40 JOD visa, and an unbundled one-day visit costs 90 JOD. The 70 JOD Jordan Wanderer pass beats that by 20 JOD and adds free entry to Wadi Rum, Jerash, the Citadel in Amman, the Roman Theater, the Dead Sea museums, and dozens of other sites.

When the pass doesn't make sense

Two narrow cases. First, if you're entering Jordan from a country eligible for free visa on arrival, the visa savings disappear and you're just paying for site entries. Second, if you're skipping Petra entirely — which would be an unusual choice — the pass loses most of its value.

For everyone else, the answer is: buy the Explorer (75 JOD), give yourself two days at Petra, and consider the rest of the included sites a free upgrade to your trip.

Frequently asked questions

How much does the Jordan Pass cost?
There are three tiers: 70 JOD (one-day Petra entry), 75 JOD (two-day), and 80 JOD (three-day). All three include the visa-on-arrival fee (40 JOD) and entry to over 40 sites including Petra, Wadi Rum, Jerash, and the major Roman ruins.
Is the Jordan Pass worth it for a short trip?
Yes, if you're visiting Petra. The standalone Petra entrance is 50 JOD for one day, plus 40 JOD for the visa = 90 JOD. The 70 JOD pass saves 20 JOD and includes everything else free. The math fails only if you're skipping Petra entirely.
Do I need to book the Jordan Pass before I arrive?
Yes. The Jordan Pass must be purchased online before you land — it's used to waive the visa fee at the airport, and that waiver only happens if your name and passport are already in their system. Print the QR code; don't rely on phone screens at the border.

Batumi

Georgia's Black Sea second city is a subtropical resort, a casino boomtown, and a restored Belle Époque port all at once — five hours west of the capital and worth an overnight, not a day trip.

Last reviewed: June 7, 2026

Batumi is what happens when a Belle Époque Black Sea port collides with a twenty-first-century building boom and a subtropical climate. The result is gloriously incoherent: restored old-town squares and Art Nouveau facades sit beneath a skyline of architecturally deranged towers (one has a Ferris wheel embedded in it), the seaside Boulevard runs for kilometers past the pebble beach, and the whole thing is wrapped in palm trees and humidity. It is not a polished Mediterranean beach holiday and doesn\u2019t try to be — people come for the mash-up.

The highlights are easy to string together: walk the Boulevard and the beach, ride the Argo cable car for the bay panorama, watch the Ali and Nino kinetic statue slowly merge and part on the waterfront, and wander the restored Old Town and Europe Square. The real prize sits up the coast — the Batumi Botanical Garden, a world-class subtropical collection terraced above the sea, worth a half day on its own.

Making the trip work

This is an overnight, not a day trip — five hours each way rules out the round trip in a day. The double-decker train is the civilized way (book ahead in summer); flights cut it to under an hour. It pairs beautifully with a Kutaisi stop on the route, and travelers flying into Kutaisi can run the west first and return to Tbilisi by rail. Go in summer for the full resort tilt, shoulder seasons for calm; for how it fits a longer trip, the itineraries treat the coast as the natural extension once the capital is covered.

Past the Boulevard

Batumi rewards a little wandering beyond the beachfront. The restored Old Town around Europe Square and Piazza is genuinely pretty — Belle Époque facades, cafe tables, the astronomical clock — and a world away from the casino towers a few blocks over. Up the coast lies the headline day trip, the Batumi Botanical Garden, a world-class subtropical collection terraced above the sea and worth a half day on its own. Inland, the Adjara hills hide the Makhuntseti waterfall and an arched medieval “Queen Tamar” bridge, an easy marshrutka or taxi excursion, and the region's own wine and the distinctive Adjaran khachapuri — the boat with the egg — are best eaten where they were invented.

Set expectations for delightful incoherence rather than a polished resort. Batumi is a working Black Sea port, a building-boom skyline, and a restored old town all at once, the beach is pebble rather than sand, and the weather is subtropical — gloriously warm in summer, wet and moody in the shoulders. That mash-up is the appeal. Pair it with Kutaisi and the monasteries for a proper western loop, ride the double-decker train for the civilized way in and out, and treat it as the coastal chapter of a longer trip the itineraries build toward once the capital is covered.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get from Tbilisi to Batumi?

The modern double-decker train is the civilized choice — around five hours city-center to city-center, comfortable, scenic, and bookable through Georgian Railways. Flights cut it to under an hour, and marshrutkas and buses run the route cheaper but rougher. See our train guide for booking specifics.

Is Batumi worth the trip from Tbilisi?

As a day trip, no — five hours each way is too much. As an overnight or a two-to-three-day extension, yes, especially if you want the Black Sea, a change of pace, and Batumi’s distinct subtropical-resort-meets-architectural-fever-dream character. It pairs naturally with a Kutaisi stop on the way.

What is there to actually do?

Walk the seaside Boulevard and the pebble beach, ride the cable car for the bay view, wander the Old Town’s restored squares, see the moving Ali and Nino kinetic statue, and take in the skyline of gloriously strange towers. Beyond the city, the Batumi Botanical Garden up the coast is genuinely world-class.

When should I go?

Summer (June–September) for the beach-resort experience, when the city is at full tilt and busiest. Spring and autumn are mild and quieter. Winter is subtropical-damp and sleepy — the towers stay lit but the beach life shuts down. The botanical garden rewards any season.

Beach town or city break?

Both, awkwardly and charmingly. Batumi is a working Black Sea port and a casino-resort boomtown and a restored Belle Époque old town all at once, so set expectations for delightful incoherence rather than a polished beach holiday. People come for the mash-up, not for Mediterranean perfection.

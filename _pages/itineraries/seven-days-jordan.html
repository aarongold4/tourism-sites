ITINERARIES

Jordan in one week

The classic seven-day Jordan loop. Amman, Jerash, the King's Highway, Petra for two full days, Wadi Rum overnight, and the Dead Sea on the way back. Here's the day-by-day plan that works.

Last reviewed June 4, 2026 · 12-minute read · See our editorial standards

Seven days is the right amount of time for a first Jordan trip — enough to cover the four major destinations (Amman, Petra, Wadi Rum, Dead Sea) without rushing any of them, with one buffer day for the inevitable schedule slippage. The itinerary below assumes you've flown into Amman, have a rental car or hired driver, and have purchased a Jordan Pass Explorer (75 JOD) before arrival. The total budget for a couple traveling mid-range typically runs $1,800-2,500 USD per person excluding flights.

This page is the practical version: where to be each day, how to get there, where to sleep, what to eat, what to skip. It's not a sales pitch — Jordan justifies the trip on its own merits. The framing throughout: this is what we'd actually recommend to a friend doing this for the first time.

The route at a glance

  • Day 1: Arrive Amman. Citadel + Roman Theatre + downtown souks.
  • Day 2: Day trip to Jerash. Optional Ajloun Castle in the afternoon.
  • Day 3: Drive south via the King's Highway. Stop at Madaba, Mount Nebo, Karak Castle. Arrive Wadi Musa for dinner.
  • Day 4: Petra Day 1 — Siq, Treasury, Royal Tombs, Monastery climb. Optional Petra by Night evening.
  • Day 5: Petra Day 2 — High Place of Sacrifice or Al-Khubtha trail, then Little Petra.
  • Day 6: Drive south to Wadi Rum. Bedouin camp overnight — jeep tour, sunset, dinner, stargazing.
  • Day 7: Sunrise at Wadi Rum. Drive north via the Dead Sea — afternoon float, then continue to Amman or fly out from Queen Alia.

Day 1 — Amman arrival

Land at Queen Alia International Airport (AMM), 35 km south of central Amman. Pick up your rental car if applicable (most agencies have airport counters), or take an airport taxi to your hotel (22 JOD fixed rate, 30-40 minutes). Check into a hotel in Jabal Amman or downtown.

Afternoon: visit the Citadel (Jabal al-Qal'a) — the hilltop archaeological site with the Temple of Hercules, Umayyad Palace, and the panoramic view across the city. Allow 90 minutes. Walk or short taxi down to the Roman Theatre below for another 30 minutes. The two together give you the historical orientation of central Amman.

Evening: dinner downtown at one of the traditional restaurants (Hashem for street-food shawarma and falafel, Sufra for a sit-down traditional Jordanian meal, or one of the Rainbow Street places like Sufra Restaurant). Sleep in Amman.

Day 2 — Jerash day trip

Morning drive to Jerash, 50 km north of Amman (1 hour). The well-preserved Roman provincial city is one of the great archaeological sites in the region — Hadrian's Arch, the oval forum, the colonnaded cardo maximus, the South Theatre, the Temple of Artemis. Allow 3 hours minimum on site. Entry is free with Jordan Pass.

Optional afternoon: continue to Ajloun Castle (35 km from Jerash, 45 minutes) — the 12th-century Ayyubid fortress built by Saladin's nephew. Smaller and less visited than the Crusader castles further south, but a worthwhile add-on if you're enjoying the Roman/medieval history thread. Allow 90 minutes.

Return to Amman by 17:00. Dinner in Rainbow Street neighborhood, slower evening at one of the cafes. Sleep in Amman. See the Jerash page for the full visit detail.

Day 3 — King's Highway south

The day everything starts shifting south. Check out of your Amman hotel after breakfast and drive south on Route 35 (the King's Highway, not the faster Desert Highway). This is the scenic route — 5-6 hours total driving with stops, versus 3 hours on the Desert Highway. The trade-off is worthwhile for a first trip.

Stops along the King's Highway:

Madaba (30 km from Amman, 45 minutes). Visit St. George's Church to see the 6th-century mosaic floor map of the Holy Land — the oldest surviving map of the region, with Jerusalem clearly identifiable at the center. Free with Jordan Pass. 30-45 minutes.

Mount Nebo (10 km from Madaba). The traditional spot where Moses saw the Promised Land before dying. A small Byzantine-Crusader church, a panoramic viewpoint over the Jordan Valley, the Dead Sea visible to the west. Entry 3 JOD for foreign visitors (as of Nov 2026). 30-45 minutes.

Karak Castle (130 km from Mount Nebo, 2.5 hours). The major Crusader fortress in southern Jordan — 12th-century, built by Raynald of Châtillon, one of the more dramatic figures of the Crusades. The castle commands a hilltop with sweeping views over the Wadi Mujib gorge. Allow 90 minutes. Free with Jordan Pass.

Continue south to Wadi Musa (90 km from Karak, 90 minutes). Check into your Petra hotel — Mövenpick Resort, Petra Guest House, Petra Moon, or whatever you've booked. Early dinner, sleep early. The 06:00 wake-up tomorrow won't feel optional.

Day 4 — Petra Day 1

The marquee day. Standard Petra Day 1 from the two-day Petra itinerary:

  • 06:00 enter the gate, walk to the Siq
  • 06:20 walk the Siq
  • 06:50 Treasury in morning light
  • 07:20 Royal Tombs and Street of Facades
  • 08:15 Roman theatre and lower city
  • 09:00 begin Monastery climb
  • 10:00 Monastery (Ad-Deir)
  • 11:45 lunch at Basin Restaurant
  • 13:00-16:00 slow afternoon back through the lower city, Royal Tombs in afternoon light, Treasury walk-through, Siq return
  • 16:30 exit

Evening: if it's a Sunday through Thursday, Petra by Night (20:30-22:30, 30 JOD separate ticket, candle-lit Siq + Bedouin music + 3D projection on the Treasury). Otherwise, early dinner and sleep.

Day 5 — Petra Day 2

Slower start (07:00 entry rather than 06:00). Pick one of the two Day 2 options from the two-day itinerary:

Option A — Al-Khubtha trail. 3.5 km round trip up the eastern cliff to the famous "Treasury from above" viewpoint. 1.5-2 hours. Best done in late morning when the Treasury is in good light from above.

Option B — High Place of Sacrifice with Wadi Farasa descent. The longer, harder, more rewarding option. 3 km up to the Nabataean ritual altar, 90-minute descent through Wadi Farasa past the Lion Monument and Garden Temple. 3.5-4 hours total.

Both options out by 13:00. Lunch in Wadi Musa town. Afternoon: Little Petra (Siq al-Barid), 8 km north of Petra. Free entry, walk the slot canyon, climb to the painted Biclinium, visit the Beidha Neolithic site next door. 2-2.5 hours total. Return to Wadi Musa by 17:00.

Evening: pack for tomorrow's Wadi Rum overnight (you'll want a small bag with overnight essentials — the rest stays in the car at the Wadi Rum Visitor Centre). Dinner, early sleep.

Day 6 — Wadi Rum

Morning drive south to Wadi Rum, 100 km, about 2 hours. Stop at Shobak Castle on the way (45 minutes off the route, 90-minute visit, free with Jordan Pass) if you're interested — the smaller Crusader fortress, less impressive than Karak but in a more dramatic setting.

Arrive Wadi Rum Visitor Centre by midday. Pay the 5 JOD entrance fee (covered by Jordan Pass) and meet your Bedouin camp pickup — they'll transfer you by 4WD into the protected area to your camp (30-45 minutes off-road drive).

Standard Wadi Rum afternoon:

  • 14:00-16:00: settle in at camp, lunch if not eaten en route
  • 16:00-19:00: jeep tour — Lawrence's Spring, Khazali Canyon, Mushroom Rock, sunset at Um Sabatah viewpoint
  • 19:30: traditional Zarb dinner (meat and vegetables cooked in an underground sand oven) at camp
  • 20:30-22:00: evening around the fire, Bedouin tea, stargazing
  • 23:00: sleep in the Bedouin tent

See the Wadi Rum page for camp selection and the full experience detail. For most travelers in this itinerary, a mid-range camp ($80-150 USD/person) hits the right balance of comfort and authenticity.

Day 7 — Dead Sea and return

Sunrise option at Wadi Rum (worth doing — the desert at dawn is one of the highlights). Breakfast at camp, transfer back to the Visitor Centre by 09:00, retrieve your rental car, begin the drive north.

From Wadi Rum to the Dead Sea via the Desert Highway: about 350 km, 4 hours. Stop for fuel and lunch in Aqaba (1 hour from Wadi Rum) if you're a diver and want to add a snorkeling stop — Aqaba's South Beach has the easiest access to coral reefs.

Arrive at the Dead Sea by 14:00-15:00. Several beach options:

Public beach (Amman Beach or Dead Sea Spa Hotel beach). 25-30 JOD entry, basic facilities, but the floating experience is the same. The cheapest option.

Hotel day pass (Mövenpick Dead Sea, Hilton Dead Sea, Kempinski). 50-80 USD entry includes beach access, pool, lunch buffet, sometimes spa credit. The comfortable middle option.

Public swimming areas (Wadi Mujib, free). Less developed, more authentic, requires a short walk to reach the water. For travelers who don't want resort vibes.

Allow 2-3 hours at the Dead Sea. The floating experience is genuinely strange — water density so high that you cannot sink, with the Israeli coast visible across the water to the west. Mud treatment (the famous Dead Sea black mud) is available at all the resort beaches.

From the Dead Sea back to Amman (60 km, 1 hour) or directly to Queen Alia Airport (80 km, 90 minutes). Plan your departure flight accordingly — Dead Sea afternoon plus drive to airport works for evening flights but is tight for early evening departures.

Practical notes

Budget breakdown

  • Accommodation: Amman 2 nights ($150-200), Wadi Musa 3 nights ($210-450), Wadi Rum camp 1 night ($80-150 per person), Dead Sea optional 0-1 nights ($120-250). Total $440-900 for the week.
  • Transport: Rental car for 7 days ($210-315) + fuel ($60-90). Total $270-405.
  • Entrance fees: Jordan Pass Explorer 75 JOD ($105 USD) covers Petra 2 days + Jerash + most other sites + visa. Additional: Petra by Night 30 JOD ($42), Wadi Rum jeep tour included in camp fee.
  • Food: $25-45 per day per person for mix of hotel breakfasts and local lunches/dinners. Total $175-315.
  • Tours and extras: Wadi Rum camp included with accommodation, Dead Sea day pass $50-80 if using a resort, occasional guides $20-40.

Total for a couple traveling mid-range: $3,600-5,000 for the week excluding international flights, or roughly $1,800-2,500 per person.

What to pack

Good walking shoes (trail runners or light hikers, not dress sneakers) for Petra. Light layers — Jordan has a wide diurnal temperature range, particularly in spring and autumn. Sunscreen and a wide-brim hat — Petra and Wadi Rum are full-sun environments. A scarf or shawl for women visiting religious sites. A small daypack for Petra and Wadi Rum days. Bathing suit for the Dead Sea. Spare shoes for the Dead Sea (the rocks and salt crystals are sharp). Water bottle to refill.

What to skip if time is tight

If you have 5-6 days instead of 7: skip the King's Highway scenic route (take the Desert Highway from Amman directly to Petra in 3 hours), and skip Ajloun on the Jerash day. This compresses the trip without losing any of the top destinations.

If you have 4 days: skip Jerash entirely (or do it as a half-day from Amman if you must), and either skip the Dead Sea or compress it to a 90-minute swim stop on the return.

Frequently asked questions

Is one week really enough for Jordan?
Seven days is the sweet spot — enough time to do all four major destinations (Amman, Petra, Wadi Rum, Dead Sea) without rushing any of them, with some buffer for travel days and the inevitable changes of plan. Less than 5 days forces compromises on Petra (the most time-sensitive site). More than 10 days lets you add the desert castles, Aqaba diving, Dana Biosphere hiking, or a slower pace at each stop — useful but not essential for a first trip.
Should I rent a car or use public transport?
For a 7-day Jordan loop, rent a car. The flexibility — leaving Petra when you want, stopping at Karak Castle and Shobak on the King's Highway, reaching Wadi Rum on your own schedule — is worth the $30-45 USD/day rental cost. Jordan's roads are well-maintained and the major routes are well-signed in English. Public transport works for budget travelers tied to JETT bus schedules, but compresses the itinerary. Most travelers should rent.
Which way should I do the loop?
Counter-clockwise from Amman: Amman → north to Jerash → south via the King's Highway (Madaba, Mount Nebo, Karak) → Petra (2 days) → Wadi Rum (1-2 nights) → north on the Desert Highway, stopping at the Dead Sea → back to Amman. This ordering puts the high-energy walking days (Petra) earlier in the trip while you're fresh, and ends with the spa-like Dead Sea recovery.
What's the best month?
March-May or September-November. Spring (March-May) brings wildflowers in the desert and moderate temperatures (15-25°C). Autumn (September-November) brings similar conditions with potentially clearer light. Summer (June-August) is genuinely hot (35-40°C+ in the south) and uncomfortable for the walking-heavy Petra days. Winter (December-February) is mild but the Wadi Rum overnight gets cold (overnight lows around 5-8°C, occasionally near freezing).
How much budget should I plan?
For a couple traveling mid-range, plan $1,800-2,500 USD per person for a week, excluding flights. This breaks down roughly: accommodations $600-900 (mix of mid-range hotels and a Wadi Rum camp), transport $250-400 (rental car + fuel + JETT or transfers), entrance fees $150-200 (Jordan Pass covers most), food $200-300 (mix of hotel and local restaurants), tours and experiences $200-400 (Wadi Rum jeep tours, Dead Sea day pass, occasional guides), buffer $200-300. Budget travelers can do the same trip for $1,000-1,400 per person; luxury travelers easily $3,500-5,000+.
Should I buy a Jordan Pass for a 7-day trip?
Yes — almost certainly. The Jordan Pass Explorer (75 JOD) includes the visa-on-arrival fee plus two-day Petra entry plus 40+ other sites including Jerash, Wadi Rum, the Citadel, and most major attractions. The math works out heavily in favor of the pass for any trip including Petra. The Jordan Pass Expert (80 JOD) is the right tier if you're extending Petra to three days. Buy online before arrival at jordanpass.jo; the visa waiver only works if you stay at least two nights in Jordan, which a 7-day trip easily satisfies.

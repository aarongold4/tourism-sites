EGYPT TRAVEL

Money in Egypt, decoded

Egypt runs two economies in parallel — a card-first official one and a cash-and-small-bills informal one — and a smooth trip is mostly a matter of carrying the right instrument for each. Here's the whole system.

Last reviewed June 6, 2026◆6-minute read

Photo: V Manninen from Funland · CC BY 2.0 · Wikimedia Commons

The Egyptian pound (EGP) has had a turbulent decade — repeated devaluations have made Egypt remarkably inexpensive in hard currency while making any printed price stale on arrival — and the payments landscape has modernized fast on top of it: the antiquities authority went card-first, ride-hailing made urban transport cashless, and yet the street still runs on paper. The visitor's job isn't to pick a side; it's to carry both correctly.

The card economy

Visa and Mastercard now cover the official layer end to end: antiquities sites(card-first and increasingly card-only at the windows, with online purchase the smart default — the Giza system), GEM (online-only for foreigners), hotels, real restaurants, supermarkets, pharmacies, and the apps. Practicalities: bring two cards on different networks (portal payment systems are finicky — GEM's notoriously), enable travel use before flying, and when terminals offer to charge your home currency, decline — paying in EGP beats the machine's exchange rate every time.

The cash economy

Everything informal is cash: tips, street food, off-app taxis, bazaar purchases, the camel, water from vendors, restroom attendants. ATM strategy: withdraw EGP from bank-attached machines in the city (ubiquitous, modest per-withdrawal fees, better math than exchange counters), in medium amounts — large notes are a liability, not a convenience. The small-bills habit is the single highest-yield money skill in Egypt: break big notes at every hotel desk and supermarket, hoard the 5s through 50s, and arrive at the pyramids carrying denominations the informal economy can actually change (the kit).

Tipping: the system, not the gesture

Baksheesh is wage structure, not optional politeness — service workers are paid expecting it — so budget it as a line item: small notes for small services, 10% at unserviced restaurants, and the guide/driver benchmarks (~$10-15 and ~$5-8 per day respectively, handed directly in cash at day's end) that every tour page on this site repeats (why directly). The mindset shift that helps: it's a micro-payment system you participate in, briefly, as a guest — cheaper than any service charge at home, and smoother the less you fight it.

Budgeting the trip

Rough daily bands per person, excluding hotels: backpacker $20-35 (street food, metro, one site); standard $50-90 (restaurants, app rides, a site with interiors, tips); comfortable $120-200+ (guided days, the Mena lunch, the show). Frequently asked questions

Should I bring cash or use cards in Egypt?

Both, by economy: cards now dominate the official layer — antiquities sites are card-first or card-only, hotels and real restaurants take them happily — while the informal economy (tips, taxis off-app, bazaars, everything at the pyramids outside the ticket window) runs on cash. ATMs are everywhere in Cairo; withdraw EGP locally rather than importing it.

How much should I tip in Egypt?

Baksheesh is structural: small notes (5-20 EGP) for small services — bags carried, photos taken at tombs, restrooms attended; 10% where service isn't included at restaurants; roughly $10-15/day for a private guide and half for a driver, handed directly. Budget it as a real line item, around 5-10% of daily spend.

What's the deal with small bills?

Egypt runs chronically short of change, and 'no change' doubles as a rounding-up technique. The counter-habit: break large notes at every legitimate venue (hotels, supermarkets, card-paid restaurants asking for cashback), hoard 5s, 10s, and 20s like the currency they are, and never hand a 200 to a camel handler expecting 150 back.

Can I use US dollars in Egypt?

For the visa counter and occasionally big-ticket tourism, yes; for daily life, no — the country transacts in EGP, and street prices quoted in dollars are tourist prices. Bring some USD as the visa fee and a reserve; live in pounds.

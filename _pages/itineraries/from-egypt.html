ITINERARY

Petra from Egypt

Three main routes from Egypt to Petra: the Nuweiba-Aqaba ferry, the via-Israel land route, or direct flights from Cairo. The combined Egypt-Jordan trip is one of the most rewarding regional itineraries. Here's the practical breakdown.

Last reviewed June 5, 2026 · 7-minute read · See our editorial standards

Combining Petra with an Egypt trip is one of the standard regional travel patterns — Cairo's Pyramids, Luxor's temples, Aswan's monuments, and the Sinai Peninsula's destinations all pair naturally with Petra and Wadi Rum on a comprehensive Middle East itinerary. The geographic adjacency makes the combination logistically feasible (a 1-hour ferry separates Sinai from Jordan), and the cultural complementarity makes it richly rewarding — three of the world's most significant ancient civilizations (Pharaonic Egyptian, Nabataean, Roman-Byzantine) all reachable on a single coordinated trip.

This page covers the practical routing options for getting from Egypt to Petra, the trip-planning considerations for combined Egypt-Jordan itineraries, and the logistics of border crossings, visas, and timing. The framing throughout: a well-traveled route with established infrastructure, but with enough complexity that some advance planning is essential.

The three main routes

Route 1: Nuweiba-Aqaba ferry (recommended for Sinai-based trips)

The most direct route for travelers already in the Sinai Peninsula. The ferry crosses the Gulf of Aqaba between Nuweiba (eastern Sinai) and Aqaba (southern Jordan):

  • Distance: ~70 km across the Gulf of Aqaba
  • Fast ferry time: 1 hour (modern fast ferry, daily afternoon departures)
  • Regular ferry time: 3-4 hours (older vessel, daily departures on different schedule)
  • Cost: $80-110 USD per person for the fast ferry, $50-70 for the regular ferry
  • Total Sinai-to-Petra travel time: 5-7 hours including ferry, immigration, and onward driving from Aqaba to Petra

The fast ferry typically departs Nuweiba in early afternoon (often 14:00-15:00) and arrives Aqaba late afternoon. From Aqaba, the 130 km drive to Petra takes 2-2.5 hours; with afternoon ferry arrival, you typically reach Petra/Wadi Musa in the evening for overnight.

Schedule realities: The ferry schedule isn't always tightly maintained. Weather conditions can cancel sailings (occasional in winter, rare in summer); mechanical issues sometimes delay departures; schedule changes happen periodically. Plan for at least 1 buffer day in case of delays, confirm the departure schedule the morning of travel through your hotel, and don't book non-refundable Jordan accommodations dependent on exact ferry timing.

Tickets: Book at the Nuweiba port directly or through Sinai-based hotels and tour operators. Walk-up booking on the day works most of the time but isn't guaranteed for the fast ferry during peak season.

Route 2: Via Israel (multiple borders)

For travelers passing through Israel as part of the broader trip. The route involves multiple border crossings:

  • Sinai to Israel: Cross at the Taba border (eastern Sinai to Eilat)
  • Israel transit: Brief time in Eilat or onward Israeli travel
  • Israel to Jordan: Cross at the Wadi Araba border (Eilat to Aqaba)
  • Aqaba to Petra: Standard 130 km drive

Total travel time: 8-10 hours including multiple border procedures. This route adds complexity (more borders, more visas/fees, potential schedule disruption) but works for travelers who are already including Israel in their broader trip. Solo travelers without prior border experience often find this route stressful; tours that handle the logistics smoothly make it more manageable.

Border costs: Egypt exit tax (~$10), Sinai-only visa surrender if applicable, Israel entry (free for most nationalities, but check visa requirements for your passport), Israel border exit tax (109 NIS ~$30), Jordan visa at Wadi Araba (60 JOD ~$85). Total border fees: $120-150 per person across the two crossings.

Route 3: Direct flight Cairo to Amman or Aqaba

The fastest option, particularly for travelers starting in Cairo rather than Sinai:

  • Cairo → Amman: EgyptAir and Royal Jordanian fly daily, 1 hour flight time, $200-450 round-trip economy depending on booking timing
  • Cairo → Aqaba: Limited but available, similar pricing
  • From Amman to Petra: 235 km / 3-4 hours via JETT bus, rental car, or private transfer
  • From Aqaba to Petra: 130 km / 2-2.5 hours

Total Cairo-to-Petra travel time: 4-6 hours via Aqaba flight + onward drive; 5-7 hours via Amman flight + longer onward drive. The flight route is faster than the ferry route from Cairo, slower if you're already in Sinai.

Best when: You're starting in Cairo and don't have Sinai destinations included in the trip, or when time is a priority and the additional cost is acceptable. Combine with Cairo's main attractions (Pyramids, Egyptian Museum) for the first few days, then fly to Jordan for the Petra-Wadi Rum portion.

The combined Egypt-Jordan trip

For travelers wanting a comprehensive regional experience, the combined trip typically runs 10-14 days. A representative itinerary:

  • Days 1-3: Cairo — Pyramids, Sphinx, Egyptian Museum, Khan el-Khalili
  • Days 4-6: Luxor — Valley of Kings, Karnak Temple, Hatshepsut Temple, hot air balloon ride
  • Day 7: Aswan — Philae Temple, Nubian villages, optional Abu Simbel day trip
  • Day 8: Travel to Sharm el-Sheikh or Dahab (flight or overnight train)
  • Days 9-10: Sinai — Mount Sinai sunrise hike, St. Catherine's Monastery, Red Sea snorkeling
  • Day 11: Drive to Nuweiba, afternoon ferry to Aqaba, evening arrival Petra/Wadi Musa
  • Days 12-13: Two days at Petra
  • Day 14: Wadi Rum overnight, return flight from Aqaba or onward to Amman

Variations: drop Aswan to compress; add Wadi Rum night to extend; add Dead Sea after Wadi Rum for a fuller Jordan experience.

Practical considerations

Visas

Egypt: Most Western passport holders (US, EU, UK, Australia, Canada, etc.) can obtain a 30-day single-entry visa on arrival for $25 USD, paid in cash at the airport. Travelers entering only Sinai (south of Sharm el-Sheikh) sometimes use the simpler "Sinai-only" visa system that doesn't allow travel to mainland Egypt; check current rules with your specific entry point. Multi-entry visas are also available at slightly higher cost.

Jordan: Most Western passport holders can get visas on arrival. Standard tourist visa at airports is 40 JOD (~$56 USD); at the Wadi Araba land border the fee jumps to 60 JOD (~$85). The Jordan Pass (jordanpass.jo) bundles the visa fee with Petra entrance and other site admissions and waives the visa fee for 2+ night stays — often the best-value approach for tourists. Note that travelers entering via the Aqaba ferry terminal qualify for a visa fee waiver (visa-free entry) due to Aqaba's Special Economic Zone status, provided they stay at least one night in Aqaba.

The Aqaba ferry terminal visa waiver

A specific provision worth knowing: travelers arriving in Aqaba via the Nuweiba ferry can enter Jordan without paying the visa fee, due to Aqaba's Special Economic Zone (ASEZ) status. The waiver applies if you arrive via the Aqaba port (ferry or yacht) — not for travelers arriving at the airport or land borders. This makes the ferry route financially attractive: you save $85 on the Wadi Araba visa fee (which would apply if you crossed via Israel) and 56 USD on the standard airport visa fee.

The waiver requires you to stay at least one night in Aqaba (or transition through with documented onward travel). For Petra-bound travelers, spending the ferry-arrival night in Aqaba and continuing to Petra the next morning works well — many do it this way for the visa savings.

Currency

Egypt uses the Egyptian Pound (EGP); Jordan uses the Jordanian Dinar (JOD). The two currencies aren't interchangeable. ATMs at the Nuweiba port and Aqaba port dispense local currency using international cards. US dollars are widely accepted at both borders and ports.

Time zones

Egypt is at UTC+2 standard, UTC+3 daylight saving (Egypt observes DST). Jordan is at UTC+2 year-round (Jordan abolished DST in 2022). During summer months, Jordan is one hour behind Egypt; during winter months, the time zones match. Account for this when planning ferry connections — the schedule changes can be confusing during DST transitions.

Language

Arabic in both countries, with distinct dialect differences but mutual intelligibility in tourist contexts. English is widely understood at hotels, restaurants, and tour operations. French is occasionally useful in Sinai (older French tourism presence); not in Jordan.

Booking the trip

Three main approaches:

  • Independent travel: Book components separately (flights, ferries, hotels) using standard channels (airlines, Booking.com, etc.). Maximum flexibility, lowest cost, requires research effort.
  • Combined Egypt-Jordan tour package: Multi-day tour operators (Trafalgar, Intrepid Travel, Audley Travel, regional specialists) offer pre-packaged itineraries combining the major destinations. Premium pricing, full logistics handling.
  • Hybrid approach: Book independently for portions you're comfortable handling (international flights, major city hotels) and use tour operators for complex segments (the ferry crossing, the Sinai-to-Petra transition).

Frequently asked questions

How do I get from Egypt to Petra?
Three main routes. (1) The Nuweiba-Aqaba ferry: a 1-3 hour Red Sea crossing from the Sinai Peninsula to Aqaba, $80-110 USD per person, daily service when weather permits. From Aqaba, the standard 130 km drive to Petra. (2) Via Israel: cross from Sinai to Eilat via the Taba border, then Wadi Araba border into Jordan — adds complexity but works for travelers already passing through Israel. (3) Flying: direct flights from Cairo to Amman (1 hour) or Cairo to Aqaba (1 hour), then standard Jordan onward transport. Choose based on whether you're already in Sinai (ferry), passing through Israel (border route), or starting in Cairo (flight).
How long does the trip take?
Depends on the route. (1) Sinai ferry route: 5-7 hours total Sinai-to-Petra including ferry, immigration, and onward driving. (2) Israel route: 8-10 hours including multiple border crossings. (3) Flight route: 4-6 hours total Cairo-to-Petra including flight, immigration, and onward transport. The ferry route is the most direct if you're starting in Sinai or southern Egypt; the flight is faster if you're starting in Cairo or northern Egypt.
What about combining Petra with Egypt destinations?
Common pattern: Cairo → Pyramids → flight to Sharm el-Sheikh → drive to Nuweiba → ferry to Aqaba → drive to Petra → Wadi Rum → optional onward to Israel/Jordan/return. The combined Egypt-Jordan trip is one of the standard regional itineraries, typically 10-14 days total. Major tour operators sell packages combining the headline Egyptian sites (Pyramids, Luxor, Aswan) with the major Jordanian destinations (Petra, Wadi Rum, Dead Sea, Jerash).
Is the Nuweiba-Aqaba ferry reliable?
Generally yes, with caveats. The fast ferry runs daily (typically afternoon departure from Nuweiba, evening departure from Aqaba, journey time ~1 hour). The regular slow ferry also runs daily on a different schedule (3-4 hours). Schedule disruptions happen — weather cancellations, mechanical issues, occasional schedule changes. Book in advance through your hotel or tour operator, plan for at least 1 buffer day in case of delays, and confirm departure the morning of your travel. Tickets cost $80-110 USD per person for the fast ferry.
What about visas?
Most nationalities can get visas on arrival for both Egypt and Jordan, but check specific requirements before traveling. Egypt typically issues 30-day single-entry visas at the airport ($25 USD); travelers entering via Sinai under the Sinai-only visa regime get a shorter visa not valid for the rest of Egypt. Jordan issues visas at the Aqaba ferry terminal (40 JOD / ~$56 USD) or land borders (60 JOD); the Jordan Pass waives this fee for 2+ night stays. For travelers crossing through Israel, additional Israeli border procedures apply.

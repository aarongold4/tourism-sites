Four Seasons at The First Residence

Cairo's west-bank luxury flagship: Nile views, a marble-and-boutiques mall attached, restaurants on a moored riverboat, and Four Seasons service in its classical register. What it is — and the one thing its Giza address doesn't mean.

Last reviewed June 6, 2026◆6-minute read

Photo: Radosław Botev · CC BY 3.0 pl · Wikimedia Commons

Start with the address, because it misleads productively: the First Residence stands in Giza — the governorate — on the Nile's west bank beside the zoological gardens, which puts it a 20-35 minute run from the plateau, roughly half the downtown departure. What it is not is a pyramid hotel: the monuments are 9-10 kilometers off, visible from some upper floors as distant silhouettes. The product here is different — Cairo's quieter luxury flagship, facing the river and the gardens, attached to the city's most polished mall — and judged as that product, it's excellent.

The property

A tower of roughly 270 rooms and suites in the First Mall complex, built in the Four Seasons' pre-contemporary idiom: classical, ornate, marble-and-chintz rather than the brand's newer minimalism — dated to some eyes, beautifully maintained to most, and consistent with the lobby's famous flower displays. Floor-to-ceiling windows are the rooms' signature; the categories that matter face the Nile (the buy) or the gardens and city, with the higher floors adding the distant-pyramids bonus. Service draws the property's most consistent praise across years of reviews — the anticipatory, name-remembering register the brand trades on — and it's the half of the rate that survives any quibble about decor.

Eating, the mall, and the boat

The food program is unusually deep for a city hotel: Lebanese at the Nile-facing Aura, the brasserie, the tea lounge — and, the property's party trick, the First Nile Boat moored across the corniche, a stationary riverboat housing its Mediterranean and pan-Asian rooms with panoramic water views. The attached First Mall (think Rolex-and-Bulgari retail under marble) functions as the hotel's climate-controlled annex; a pharmacy and the practical city sit minutes away. The fourth-floor heated pool with its cabanas, plus the full spa, complete the resort layer. One honest flag from the review record: night noise reaches some rooms — street and zoo on one flank, the Nile-bank restaurant boats on the other — so light sleepers should request high and river-facing.

Position, for a pyramid trip

The west-bank address halves the dawn pyramid run versus downtown — a real convenience on plateau morning (the schedule it serves) — while costing reach on everything east: Tahrir's museum, Islamic Cairo, and the bazaar all sit across the river, where the sister property at Nile Plaza (Garden City) holds the location advantage. That's the real decision between the two Four Seasons, and it's an itinerary question: monument- and-calm trips lean First Residence, city-immersion trips lean Nile Plaza. Either way, the pyramid-window experience remains a separate purchase — the Mena House or a Sphinx-gate rooftop for a night does what no city tower can.

The verdict

The First Residence is the strongest version of a specific choice: full international luxury on the quiet bank, with the river out the window and the plateau a short morning run away. Book a Nile-view room, high floor; pair it with a pyramid-side night if the monument window matters; and if your trip is museums-and-medina first, price the Garden City alternatives in the central guide before committing. Rates run the city's full five-star band — roughly $250-450 standard by season — with the usual October-April peak (the cluster's booking rules).

Room categories, decoded for this property

The First Residence's inventory rewards specificity at booking. The base categories face the city or gardens — comfortable, viewless in the ways that matter here. The Nile-view rooms are the property's actual product: the floor-to-ceiling windows earning their reputation, with higher floors adding the distant-pyramids bonus on clear days. Suites scale the same geography upward, the corner formats taking river and city at once. The booking craft mirrors the cluster's universal rule: the view category in writing, the high floor requested at booking and re-confirmed by message, and — this property's specific note — the river-facing side doubles as the quiet-side request, since the documented night noise lives on the street-and-zoo flank. Rates swing the full seasonal band (peak October-April, soft summers), and the brand's direct-booking perks occasionally beat the platforms at this tier — price both.

A stay in practice: the two-day shape

What the property does best, choreographed: arrive to the famous lobby flowers and a riverside afternoon — the fourth-floor pool's cabanas, tea in the lounge, dinner aboard the First Nile Boat with the water lit around you. Pyramid morning from here is the property's structural advantage — the 20-35 minute run beats every downtown departure (that math) — back by early afternoon for the spa's recovery shift. Evening two crosses to the east bank's restaurants or stays for Aura's Lebanese with the Nile out the glass. The First Mall handles any retail therapy without leaving climate control, and the concierge desk arranges the southern-day driver at the usual marked-up-but-flawless hotel rate. It's a rhythm the classical decor suits — unhurried, polished, river-paced.

The alternatives, priced against it

The comparison set for this rate band: the Mena House trades the Nile for the pyramid window at similar money — the choice is literally which view you came for; the Nile Plaza sister trades west-bank calm for Garden City's walkable-to-Tahrir position in a more contemporary register; and the corniche towers of the central guide deliver the river view with deeper sightseeing convenience at often gentler rates. The First Residence wins the brief that matches it: full international luxury, quiet bank, easy plateau mornings, the mall and the boat as house amenities. Travelers whose brief reads differently have, on this page's honest accounting, better-fitting spends.

Who this property is exactly right for

Closing the review with the fit test, since at this rate the fit is the product: the First Residence suits the traveler pairing serious comfort with a monument-first itinerary — the plateau and southern mornings launch better from the west bank than from anywhere downtown — and the temperament that prefers a hotel to be a retreat rather than a base camp: the mall, the boat, the pool, and the lounge make staying in a complete evening. It suits return visitors who have done the east bank's sights and now want the river and the calm. It does not suit first-timers optimizing museum-and- medina walking (Nile Plaza or Garden City win that brief), pyramid-window romantics (the Mena House owns that product outright), or anyone allergic to classical decor. Priced against its true competitive set rather than its address, it is what it has quietly been for decades: Cairo's most complete luxury retreat, on the bank the monuments happen to share.

Frequently asked questions

Can you see the pyramids from the Four Seasons First Residence?

From some higher rooms and suites, yes — as a skyline silhouette in the distance, not the filled-window view the term 'pyramid view' means at Giza-side hotels. The property's signature views are the Nile and the city; treat any pyramid sighting as a bonus, and buy the Mena House if the pyramid window is the point.

First Residence or Nile Plaza — which Four Seasons?

Nile Plaza (Garden City) sits closer to Tahrir, the museum, and the central sights, in a more contemporary register; First Residence (Giza side, by the zoo) offers the quieter, more classical property with marginally easier pyramid runs. Sightseeing-first travelers usually optimize with Nile Plaza; travelers prioritizing calm, the mall, and the west-bank position pick First Residence.

How far is it from the pyramids?

About 9-10 kilometers — a 20-35 minute morning run, roughly half the distance of the downtown departure. Convenient, but be clear: this is a city luxury hotel on the Giza side of the river, not a pyramid-adjacent property.

What does it cost?

Standard rooms commonly run in the $250-450 band by season, Nile-view categories and suites above — Cairo's full international-luxury pricing. The seasonal swing (peak October-April, softer summers) applies here as across the city's five-star tier.

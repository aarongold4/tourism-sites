HOTEL AREA

Town centre

The commercial heart of Wadi Musa. Mid-range hotels at $40-90 per night, the most authentic restaurant scene in the area, and a real Jordanian town atmosphere rather than a tourist enclave. Here's what staying in town centre actually involves.

Last reviewed June 5, 2026 · 6-minute read · See our editorial standards

Wadi Musa town centre is the right area for travelers prioritizing value over view, authentic Jordanian atmosphere over chain-hotel polish, and a working-town character over the manicured tourist enclaves of the Tayba ridge and Visitor Centre strip. The area has the highest concentration of budget-to-mid-range hotels in the Petra zone, the most diverse and most authentically Jordanian restaurant scene, and the broadest range of practical services (ATMs, pharmacies, grocery shops, internet cafés, repair services) of any neighborhood serving the site.

The trade-off is the 2-3 km distance to the Petra Visitor Centre — close enough for active travelers to walk in good weather, far enough that most visitors use hotel shuttles or taxis. For budget-conscious travelers, multi-night visitors looking for cultural texture, and travelers who specifically value engaging with Jordanian daily life, the town centre is the better choice than the higher-priced alternatives.

What the town centre actually is

Wadi Musa is a Jordanian town of approximately 17,000-20,000 residents whose economy has become substantially tourism-dependent over the past four decades but whose daily life retains its pre-tourism character. The town centre — the dense commercial and residential core — clusters around the main mosque, the central commercial street (Wadi Musa Main Street), and the small public square where the JETT bus stops and local mini-buses gather. The area is densely built with multi-story residential buildings, small shops at street level, restaurants and cafés mixed among the commercial space, and the practical services that any working Jordanian town needs.

The town centre sits at the western edge of Wadi Musa, with the Petra Visitor Centre at the eastern edge — the main commercial street runs essentially east-west between them along the natural valley floor. The Tayba ridge rises to the north of the main street; the residential neighborhoods extend south. As you move east from the town centre along the main street, you encounter the transition zone (mid-range hotels, restaurants, tourist-services), then the Visitor Centre strip itself (chain hotels, the Visitor Centre, the entrance to Petra).

Hotels in the town centre

Upper mid-range (4-star, $70-130 USD per night)

Petra Palace Hotel. The largest and most prominent 4-star property in the town centre. About 200 rooms, full amenity package (pool, restaurant, bar, spa, fitness room), traditional architecture mixed with contemporary additions. Popular with mid-range tour groups. Rates 70-130 USD per night depending on season; weekly group rates often lower. Walking distance to multiple restaurants and shops.

Mid-range (3-star, $45-90 USD per night)

Cleopatra Hotel. The most popular mid-range hotel in the town centre. Around 80 rooms, comfortable 3-star quality, reliable Wi-Fi, restaurant on-site with widely-praised food, helpful English-speaking staff. Walking distance to most town centre services. 45-85 USD per night; books up early in high season.

Sunrise Hotel. Smaller 3-star option, family-run, slightly older facilities but well-maintained. Strong value at the lower end of the mid-range. 40-70 USD per night.

La Maison Hotel. Boutique-style 3-star with character-driven decor and personalized service. Smaller property (about 35 rooms). 50-80 USD per night.

Budget (2-star and guesthouse, $20-50 USD per night)

Hostels. Cleopetra Hostel and similar backpacker-oriented properties with dorm beds at 8-15 USD per night and basic private rooms at 25-40 USD.

Family-run guesthouses. Small properties (5-15 rooms) operated by Wadi Musa families, often with home-cooked breakfast included. Variable quality but generally comfortable; 25-45 USD per night.

The budget cluster around the town centre is the most affordable accommodation zone in the entire Petra area. Travelers prioritizing low pricing can find very acceptable rooms at $30-40 per night.

The restaurant scene

The town centre has the highest concentration of restaurants in the Petra area and the broadest price range:

Street food (1-4 JOD). Shawarma stands (chicken or beef wrapped in flatbread with tahini, vegetables, pickles), falafel shops (deep-fried chickpea fritters in pita), juice bars (fresh-squeezed orange, pomegranate, lemon-mint). The shawarma at Al-Wadi Restaurant on the main street is locally famous.

Local restaurants (5-12 JOD per person). Traditional Jordanian places serving mansaf (the national dish — lamb on rice with fermented yogurt sauce), maqluba (upside-down spiced rice and meat), mixed grilled meats, traditional mezze (hummus, baba ghanouj, tabbouleh, fattoush). Beit al-Barakah, Petra Restaurant, and several family-run places along the main street.

Mid-range and tourist-oriented (12-25 JOD per person). Larger restaurants targeting tour groups and international travelers, with broader menus including pizza, pasta, and Western-style options alongside Jordanian dishes. Cleopatra Restaurant (in the hotel), several restaurants along the upper main street.

The town centre restaurant scene is the most authentically Jordanian in the area. For travelers wanting to actually eat Jordanian food during their Petra stay, the town centre is the right zone.

Practical services

The town centre has the practical infrastructure that the resort areas don't:

  • ATMs. Multiple bank ATMs on the main street, all accepting international cards. See ATMs and cards.
  • Pharmacies. Several full-service pharmacies, English-speaking pharmacists, most common medications available without prescription.
  • Grocery shops. Local supermarkets and corner stores for snacks, water, basic supplies.
  • Mobile/SIM services. Zain, Orange, and Umniah all have stores in the town centre. See SIM cards.
  • Souvenir shops. Higher concentration than the Visitor Centre area, more variety, generally better prices through negotiation.
  • Tour operators. Multiple offices book Petra tours, Wadi Rum trips, day tours, and broader Jordan packages.

Getting to Petra from the town centre

Three options:

Walking. 15-25 minutes on the main road heading east. Mostly downhill on the way to the Visitor Centre, mostly uphill on the return. Feasible for active travelers in good weather; tiring at the end of a long Petra day in summer heat.

Hotel shuttle. Most mid-range and 4-star hotels run shuttles on roughly hourly schedules during entry windows. Free for guests; coordinate with the front desk. Less frequent than the Tayba ridge hotel shuttles but adequate for one entry per day.

Taxi. 3-5 JOD one-way to the Visitor Centre, available from the main street stand or summoned by hotel front desk. Fastest option and well within budget for occasional use.

Who it's best for

The town centre works particularly well for:

  • Budget-conscious travelers. The biggest price savings versus other zones. A 3-night stay at a town centre 3-star at $60/night saves $400-600 versus a Tayba ridge 5-star at $250/night.
  • Travelers wanting cultural immersion. The Jordanian town character is real and accessible.
  • Backpackers and budget multi-week travelers. The hostel and budget options here are the only ones in the immediate Petra area.
  • Multi-night visits with restaurant exploration goals. The variety of restaurants rewards staying multiple nights and trying different establishments.

Less ideal for:

  • Maximum-luxury seekers. The town centre tops out at solid 4-star; the 5-star options are on Tayba ridge.
  • View-driven preferences. No mountain-facing positioning advantages.
  • Multi-entry-per-day Petra plans. The shuttle dependency adds friction for travelers wanting to return mid-day.
  • Quiet-atmosphere preferences. The town centre is genuinely a working town — morning calls to prayer, daytime commercial activity, evening street life.

Frequently asked questions

What's the Wadi Musa town centre?
The commercial heart of the town that serves Petra — about 2-3 km west of the Petra Visitor Centre, centered around the main mosque, the central commercial street, and the bus station. The town centre has the highest concentration of restaurants, small shops, ATMs, pharmacies, and budget-to-mid-range hotels. It's a working Jordanian town that happens to have a major tourist attraction adjacent to it; the daily life feels Jordanian rather than touristic.
Is the town centre a good place to stay?
Yes for travelers prioritizing value, character, and walking access to local restaurants. The town centre offers 4-star and below hotels at significantly lower prices than the Tayba ridge or Visitor Centre areas — typically $40-90 USD per night for solid mid-range options. The trade-off is no direct Petra walking access (you'll need a shuttle, taxi, or 15-20 minute downhill walk) and less view-oriented atmosphere.
How far is the town centre from the Petra Visitor Centre?
About 2-3 km depending on your specific hotel. The route is mostly downhill on the way to Petra (going east, descending toward the Visitor Centre) and uphill on the return. A 15-20 minute walk is feasible for active travelers in good weather; most use the hotel shuttle or a 3-5 JOD taxi. Local mini-buses run the main town street toward the Visitor Centre area for 0.5-1 JOD, though the schedule isn't tourist-friendly.
What's the food and dining scene like?
The most authentic in the Petra area. The town centre has dozens of restaurants ranging from basic local shawarma stands (2-3 JOD for a meal) to mid-range traditional Jordanian restaurants (10-15 JOD per person for a substantial dinner with mezze, mains, and tea). The Cleopatra Hotel restaurant, Beit al-Barakah, and several family-run places along the main street are reliable. The chain hotel restaurants on Tayba ridge and at the Visitor Centre are higher-end but less distinctive.
Which town centre hotels should I look at?
Several options at different price points. Petra Palace Hotel (4-star, $70-110/night) anchors the top of the town centre range. Cleopatra Hotel ($45-75/night) is the most popular mid-range option. Sunrise Hotel and La Maison Hotel offer modest 3-star options at $40-70/night. Multiple guesthouses and family-run small hotels at $25-50/night fill the budget end. The town centre is the right zone for budget-conscious mid-range visitors.

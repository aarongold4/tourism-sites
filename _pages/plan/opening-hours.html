Petra opening hours

Open daily at 06:00 year-round. Last entry between 16:00 and 18:00 depending on season. Petra by Night runs Sun-Thu evenings. Here's the practical schedule and the timing decisions that shape your visit.

Last reviewed June 5, 2026 · 5-minute read · See our editorial standards

Petra is open every day of the year, no exceptions for holidays, weekends, or religious occasions. The 06:00 daily opening makes Petra unusually accommodating among major archaeological sites — most comparable sites open at 09:00 or 10:00 and miss the best morning light entirely. Understanding the practical timing of Petra's hours (and how those hours shape the visitor experience) is one of the small decisions that makes the difference between a good visit and a great one.

This page covers the daily schedule, the seasonal variations, the Petra by Night timing, and the practical recommendations for when to actually arrive based on what you want to see. The framing throughout: the official hours and the experiential hours are different things, and the practical visit is about the latter.

The official daily schedule

Summer (April-October)

  • Site opening: 06:00
  • Last entry: approximately 18:00
  • Gate closure: 19:00

Summer days are long; the site itself stays usable through sunset (around 19:30-20:00 depending on the specific month). Most visitors are out by 17:30-18:00 because the long walk back from the lower city or the Monastery takes 45-60 minutes and you want to be at the Visitor Centre before full dark.

Winter (November-March)

  • Site opening: 06:00
  • Last entry: approximately 16:00
  • Gate closure: 17:00

Winter days are shorter; the site closes earlier. Useful walking time is roughly 07:00 (sunrise) to 16:00 (last hour of comfortable light). The shorter day is a real constraint — winter visitors should arrive at 06:00 entry to get the full experience.

Tickets and entry

Tickets are sold at the Visitor Centre starting at 06:00. Skip the line by buying your Jordan Pass online before arrival (jordanpass.jo) — the pass-holder line is separate and shorter than the cash ticket line. The Petra Pass is the entry permit that comes with the Jordan Pass; bring the printed PDF or have the QR code available on your phone.

The experiential hours

The official hours aren't the same as the practical visiting hours. Three time windows shape the actual experience:

The early morning window (06:00-08:30)

The single most important window. Before about 08:30, the Treasury plaza is essentially empty — the tour groups arriving from Amman don't reach Petra until 09:00-10:00, and the morning sun lights the Treasury facade from the east directly. The combination of soft light, moderate crowds, and cool temperatures makes the first 2.5 hours after opening the best time of day to be at Petra.

This is why serious travelers always do the 06:00 entry. The schedule difference between "arrive at 06:00" and "arrive at 09:00" isn't 3 hours — it's whether you see Petra at its best or whether you see it through other people's heads.

The middle of the day (10:00-15:00)

The busy hours. Tour groups have arrived, the Treasury plaza fills up, the Royal Tombs area gets congested, and the lower city has substantial foot traffic. The middle of the day is a fine time to walk the colonnaded street, visit the lower-city monuments, and do the Monastery climb — but the headline experience of the Treasury is less rewarding than at the bookend hours.

Summer in particular is uncomfortable mid-day — temperatures 35-40°C with no shade on most paths, direct sun on the carved monuments. Many travelers take a long lunch break (12:00-14:00 at the Basin Restaurant) to avoid the worst heat.

The late afternoon (15:30-17:30)

The second-best window after early morning. The tour groups depart starting around 15:00-15:30; the site begins to empty out; the afternoon light hits the Royal Tombs and the upper-city carved facades from the west, which produces different (and arguably better) photographs than morning light. By 16:30 the Treasury plaza is moderately empty again, giving the second daily opportunity for the high-quality Treasury experience.

This is the window for Petra Day 2 travelers — the morning is dedicated to either the Al-Khubtha trail or High Place, and the late afternoon picks up the headline monuments at the second-best light.

Petra by Night schedule

The candle-lit evening experience runs on a specific schedule:

  • Days: Sunday, Monday, Wednesday, and Thursday nights only.
  • Hours: 20:30 to 22:30.
  • Ticket: 30 JOD per person, separate from the daytime entry and from the Jordan Pass.
  • Purchase: At the Visitor Centre on the day of the show or the day before.

Petra by Night doesn't run on Tuesday, Friday, or Saturday — a frequent point of confusion for travelers who arrive expecting to add it to any evening. If your dates are tight and you want to do Petra by Night, schedule your visit so Day 1 falls on Sun, Mon, Wed, or Thu.

The performance includes a candle-lit walk through the Siq (about 25 minutes), a 30-minute musical performance at the Treasury plaza (Bedouin musicians playing traditional instruments, sometimes accompanied by storytelling in Arabic and English), a 3D projection show on the Treasury facade (about 15 minutes), and the return walk through the Siq. Total experience time is about 2 hours.

Holiday and Friday operations

Petra operates on its normal schedule every day of the year:

  • Friday (Muslim holy day): Open as normal. Some peripheral services (donkey rides, certain Bedouin tea stalls) may have fewer operators, but the core site is fully accessible.
  • Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha: Open as normal. Wadi Musa town may have some businesses closed for the holiday, but Petra itself runs.
  • Ramadan (March-April 2026): Open as normal. Restaurants in Wadi Musa town may have modified hours; some don't serve lunch during the daylight fast. The Petra site itself isn't affected.
  • Christmas and Easter: Open as normal. Mostly relevant for Christian travelers; doesn't change Petra operations.
  • Yom Kippur and Jewish holidays: Open as normal. Worth noting because the Wadi Araba border crossing to Eilat closes for Yom Kippur and the eves of major Jewish holidays — affects travelers, doesn't affect Petra itself.

The Visitor Centre and museum

The Petra Visitor Centre is open during the same hours as the main site (06:00-19:00 summer, 06:00-17:00 winter). The on-site Petra Museum at the Visitor Centre is free to enter and is open the same hours.

The museum is worth 30-45 minutes either at the start of your visit (for context) or at the end (to consolidate what you've seen). Exhibits cover:

  • Nabataean history and the broader kingdom context
  • The major monuments with architectural detail
  • The carbonized papyrus scrolls from the Petra Church
  • Water management infrastructure
  • Religious art and material culture
  • The Bedouin community history including the 1985 resettlement

Recommended schedule

A standard Petra Day 1 timing:

  • 05:30: Wake at your Wadi Musa hotel.
  • 06:00: Enter the gate, begin the Siq walk.
  • 06:50: Treasury in morning light.
  • 08:00: Royal Tombs and Roman theatre.
  • 09:00-12:00: Monastery climb and descent.
  • 12:00-13:30: Lunch at Basin Restaurant.
  • 13:30-16:30: Lower city monuments, Royal Tombs in afternoon light, Treasury walk-through.
  • 16:30: Exit before tour group congestion.
  • 20:30 (if Sun-Thu): Petra by Night.

Frequently asked questions

What time does Petra open?
06:00 daily, year-round. The site is open every day of the year including holidays. The 06:00 opening is well before sunrise in winter and right around sunrise in summer — the actual usable visiting starts when there's enough light to see, typically 06:15-06:30 in winter and 05:30-06:00 in summer. The early opening matters: the Treasury catches the best morning light in the first 90 minutes after sunrise, which is why serious travelers always do the 06:00 entry.
What time does it close?
Last entry typically 16:00-18:00 depending on the season, with the gates closing fully around 18:00-19:00. Specifically: summer (April-October) last entry around 18:00, gates close 19:00. Winter (November-March) last entry around 16:00, gates close 17:00. The site itself empties out by sunset regardless of the official closing time — once it's dark, walking the trails becomes unsafe, so most visitors are out by 17:30-18:00 even in summer.
Is Petra open on holidays and Fridays?
Yes, every day of the year including Fridays, Eid holidays, Christmas, Ramadan, and Yom Kippur. The schedule doesn't change for religious holidays. The only exception is the rare partial closure for major political events or security situations — extremely rare and announced in advance. Friday operations are sometimes lighter (some peripheral services like donkey rides may have fewer operators), but the core archaeological site remains fully open.
When does Petra by Night run?
Sunday, Monday, Wednesday, and Thursday nights only — not every day. The performance runs from approximately 20:30 to 22:30. Tickets are 30 JOD per person (not included in the Jordan Pass), purchased at the Visitor Centre on the day of the show or the day before. Sold out is rare but possible during peak season (March-May, September-November); buying tickets in the afternoon is safer than waiting until evening.
What about the Visitor Centre and museum?
The Visitor Centre is open from 06:00 to 19:00 (summer) or 06:00 to 17:00 (winter), matching the main gate hours. The on-site Petra Museum at the Visitor Centre is open during the same hours and is free to enter (no ticket required). The museum is a useful 30-45 minute stop at the start or end of your visit — exhibits cover Nabataean history, the major monuments, the carbonized papyrus scrolls from the Petra Church, and water management infrastructure.

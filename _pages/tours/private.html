Private tours

Georgia's quiet bargain: guiding rates low enough that 'a private historian for the morning' is a midrange decision, not a luxury one. Here's how the market works and when it beats every alternative.

The arithmetic is the argument. Because professional guiding in Georgia prices far below Western European norms, the premium for private over group narrows to almost nothing for parties of three or four — and even couples often find the half-day private city walk costing in the range of a good dinner. What that buys is structural, not cosmetic: your pace, your questions answered in paragraphs instead of caption-length, routes bent around your knees or your lens, and the freedom to stop in the courtyard that catches you instead of the one on the schedule.

The market has three shapes. Private city guides work the Old Town and beyond on foot. Driver-guides extend the model to the day-trip routes, narrating the Military Highway instead of just driving it. Plain drivers — including the fixed-price app model where the fare is locked before departure — strip the interpretation and the cost: the right call for travelers who've done the reading and want wheels, not lectures. All three run on WhatsApp, all three should be confirmed in writing, and all three reward checking two or three quotes, because pricing is personal in the most literal sense.

Vetting in ten minutes

Read for named, repeated praise — "ask for Nino" appearing across a dozen reviews outweighs any star average. Confirm the language honestly (ask a question in it), fix the route and hours before money moves, and treat reluctance to put terms in a message as the red flag it is. For city walks, book the morning; for photography, negotiate the golden hours explicitly; for day trips, agree the stop list — the difference between "Kazbegi" and "Kazbegi with Ananuri, the Friendship Monument, and an hour at Gergeti" is the product. Designing the day

Private touring is worth the most when you actually use the freedom it buys, so come to the conversation with a shape in mind. For a city day, name your priorities — photography at golden hour, a slower pace around the Old Town's slopes, a deep dive on architecture or Soviet history — and let the guide route around them rather than running the default loop. For a day trip, the stop list is the product: “Kazbegi” and “Kazbegi with Ananuri, the Friendship Monument, an hour at Gergeti, and the Truso valley if the weather holds” are very different days at a similar price, so spell it out and agree it in writing before departure. The same logic extends to combining regions a group tour would never attempt — Mtskheta plus Gori plus a winery, say — in a single bespoke loop.

A few mechanics keep it smooth. Confirm the language honestly by asking a question in it, fix the hours and price in a WhatsApp message you can point back to, and clarify what the quote includes — fuel and the driver's lunch on long days, site entries, the Gergeti 4x4. Tipping isn't obligatory but is appreciated; the more valuable thank-you is a named, specific review on the platform you booked through, which is career fuel in this market. Frequently asked questions

What does a private guide cost in Tbilisi?

Low by Western standards — the structural fact this whole page rests on. Half-day city guiding routinely costs less than two seats on a premium group tour in Western Europe, and full-day driver-guides for out-of-town routes split painlessly between three or four people. Exact quotes vary by guide, language, and season; get two or three before booking.

Guide, driver, or driver-guide?

In the city: a guide on foot — vehicles are a liability in the Old Town. For day trips: a driver-guide if you want interpretation en route, or a plain driver (including fixed-price app services like GoTrip) if you’d rather pocket the difference and read our route guides instead. The driver-guide hybrid is where Georgia’s value is most extreme.

How do I find someone good?

Three channels: marketplace private listings (easy recourse, named-guide reviews), operator websites (often cheaper, same guides), and referral — ask your hotel or your free-tour guide, who freelances privately more often than not. Whichever channel, confirm language, route, hours, and price in writing (WhatsApp counts) before the day.

When is private clearly worth it?

Photography (golden-hour pacing no group allows), mobility considerations (the Old Town’s slopes can be routed around, not through), special interests (architecture, Soviet history, wine science), families with young kids, and any group of three-plus — at which point private often beats group on raw price anyway.

Should I tip a private guide?

Not obligatory, appreciated, and increasingly common: round up or add roughly ten percent for a day well run. The better gift is the review — named, specific, and on the platform you booked through; it’s career fuel in this market.

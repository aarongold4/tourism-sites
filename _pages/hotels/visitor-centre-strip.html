HOTEL AREA

Visitor Centre strip

The walking-distance hotel cluster. The properties directly adjacent to the Petra Visitor Centre at the eastern edge of Wadi Musa, where the 5-minute walk to the entrance becomes the primary value. Here's what staying right at the gate actually involves.

Last reviewed June 5, 2026 · 6-minute read · See our editorial standards

The Visitor Centre strip is where you stay at Petra when convenience to the entrance is the primary criterion. The cluster of hotels directly adjacent to the Petra Visitor Centre — roughly a 200-meter zone at the eastern edge of Wadi Musa — offers the shortest possible walking access to Petra of any hotel area. The Mövenpick Resort Petra is the anchor 5-star property; the Petra Guest House sits literally attached to the Visitor Centre complex; the Petra Moon and several other mid-range options share the immediate area. For travelers prioritizing the in-and-out logistics over view-driven atmosphere or budget pricing, the strip is the right choice.

This page is the area overview — what staying on the Visitor Centre strip means, what the trade-offs are, and how to choose between the specific hotels. The framing throughout: convenience-driven choice for travelers who plan to spend serious time inside Petra rather than at the hotel.

The walking-distance advantage

The defining feature of the strip is the proximity. From the Mövenpick Resort Petra lobby to the Petra Visitor Centre entrance gate is about a 3-minute walk, downhill. From the Petra Guest House — which shares a wall with the Visitor Centre complex — the walk is essentially zero. The Petra Moon and other strip hotels are within 5-10 minutes of the gate.

In practical terms, this changes several aspects of a Petra visit:

Dawn entries are frictionless. Wake at 05:30, eat a quick breakfast (or take it from the room), walk to the gate at 05:55, be inside at 06:00 when the gates open. No shuttle coordination, no taxi negotiation, no schedule dependency. For travelers doing dawn entries on multiple days, this is a real quality-of-life improvement.

Mid-day rest is feasible. Returning to your room for 1-2 hours during the hot midday window (typically 12:00-14:00) is realistic when the hotel is a 5-minute walk away. You can rest, change clothes, eat a quick meal in your room, and re-enter Petra for the afternoon — without losing the half-day to round-trip transport.

Petra by Night is easy. The 20:30 show requires being at the entrance at 20:00 for ticket pickup and the candle-lit Siq walk. From the strip, this is a 5-minute walk after dinner. From Tayba ridge or town centre, it requires arranging transport. The strip's convenience here is significant for travelers doing Petra by Night.

Late-afternoon flexibility. If you want to stay in Petra until close (17:00 in winter, 18:00-19:00 in summer), you can — without worrying about catching the last hotel shuttle back. Walk out, walk home.

The strip hotels

Mövenpick Resort Petra (5-star)

The anchor luxury property of the strip. Approximately 180 rooms, full international 5-star service via the Mövenpick (now Accor) brand. The hotel is positioned directly across the street from the Visitor Centre — essentially the shortest possible walk to the entrance for a 5-star property. Comprehensive amenities (pool, spa, multiple restaurants, business center, fitness room), traditional Nabataean-inspired architecture mixed with contemporary additions. Pricing 200-320 USD per night for standard rooms; suites and upper categories range higher.

Best for travelers who want 5-star service combined with maximum entrance proximity. The trade-off versus the Mövenpick Nabatean Castle on Tayba ridge: less view, more convenience.

Petra Guest House (4-star)

The closest hotel to the actual Petra entrance — the property is part of the same building complex as the Visitor Centre. About 50 rooms, comfortable 4-star quality, traditional architecture using elements that match the Visitor Centre's design language. The proximity is unique; the Cave Bar inside the property is locally famous (occupies an actual ancient cave incorporated into the hotel structure). Pricing 80-150 USD per night.

Best for travelers who want maximum entrance proximity at a reasonable price point. The 4-star quality is genuine; the property is well-maintained and pleasant.

Petra Moon Hotel (3-4 star)

About 80 rooms, comfortable 3.5-star quality, family-friendly atmosphere. 5-7 minute walk to the Visitor Centre entrance. Reliable Wi-Fi, restaurant on-site, helpful English-speaking staff. The Petra Moon is one of the most consistently positive-reviewed mid-range hotels in the Petra area. Pricing 60-110 USD per night.

Best value option for the walking-distance zone. Strong choice for travelers wanting strip convenience without 5-star pricing.

Edom Hotel and other mid-range options

Several other 3-star properties share the strip area at similar 50-100 USD pricing. Quality varies; check recent reviews before booking. The category overall provides walking-distance access at moderate prices, with trade-offs in amenity depth and finish quality.

The trade-offs versus other areas

Versus Tayba ridge

The strip trades view and atmosphere for convenience. Tayba ridge offers dramatic positioning with panoramic mountain views; the strip offers practical proximity to the entrance. For travelers planning multi-entry days or Petra by Night, the strip's convenience is significant. For travelers prioritizing the room-and-property experience over the in-Petra time, Tayba ridge often wins.

Versus the town centre

The strip trades pricing and dining variety for convenience. Town centre hotels run $40-90 per night; comparable-quality strip hotels run $60-150. The town centre has dramatically more restaurant options, more authentic Jordanian atmosphere, and more practical services (ATMs, pharmacies, shops). The strip has the entrance proximity that no other area offers. For travelers doing dawn entries and multi-entry days, the strip is worth the premium; for budget-conscious or culture-focused travelers, town centre is the better choice.

Dining on the strip

The strip has limited dining variety compared to the town centre, with most options being hotel-attached:

Hotel restaurants. The Mövenpick Resort, Petra Guest House, and Petra Moon all have on-site restaurants serving breakfast through dinner. Quality is consistent with the hotel tier; pricing is at the upper end of local norms.

The Cave Bar. Inside the Petra Guest House, occupying an actual ancient cave structure. Limited food menu but distinctive setting; popular for drinks rather than full meals.

The Basin Restaurant (inside Petra). A 30-40 minute walk from the strip hotels — relevant for lunch during Petra visits but not for dinner.

Tourist restaurants along the approach road. A few mid-range restaurants between the strip and the town centre proper. Variable quality; do brief research before committing.

Travelers staying on the strip who want serious dining variety typically take taxis (3-5 JOD one-way) to town centre restaurants for evening meals.

Who it's best for

The Visitor Centre strip works particularly well for:

  • Multi-entry-per-day Petra visitors. The convenience of returning to the room mid-day or doing dawn + afternoon entries is most valuable here.
  • Petra by Night travelers. The walking access to the 20:30 show is the cleanest of any area.
  • Single-night Petra stops. If you're just stopping for one overnight and need to enter Petra both evening and morning, the strip's convenience compounds well.
  • Travelers with mobility constraints. The shorter walk to the entrance matters more for travelers who tire easily or have walking limitations.
  • Solo travelers and small groups prioritizing in-Petra time over hotel experience. Maximum efficiency for those treating the hotel as a base camp rather than a destination.

Less ideal for:

  • View-driven travelers. The valley-floor position means no panoramic mountain views — go to Tayba ridge for those.
  • Budget-conscious travelers. Strip pricing is meaningfully higher than town centre alternatives.
  • Multi-night visits emphasizing on-property experience. The strip's amenity depth is moderate; longer stays often reward the larger ridge properties.
  • Travelers wanting deep cultural immersion. The strip is essentially a tourist enclave; the town centre offers more daily-life engagement.

Frequently asked questions

What's the Visitor Centre strip?
The cluster of hotels and tourist-services properties directly adjacent to the Petra Visitor Centre at the eastern edge of Wadi Musa. This is the walking-distance zone — hotels here are within 5-10 minutes' walk of the Visitor Centre and the entrance to Petra. The Mövenpick Resort Petra is the anchor property; the Petra Guest House, Petra Moon Hotel, and several other mid-range options share the immediate area.
Why stay at the Visitor Centre strip?
Convenience. The 5-minute walk to the entrance is the principal value proposition — you can do dawn entries without taxi coordination, return to your room for mid-day rest if needed, and walk to Petra by Night without arranging transport. For multi-day visits or travelers planning multiple Petra entries per day, the convenience compounds significantly compared to Tayba ridge or town centre alternatives.
What does it cost?
Wide range. The Mövenpick Resort Petra runs $200-320 per night at 5-star pricing. The Petra Guest House is $80-150 per night for 4-star service. Mid-range options (Petra Moon, Edom Hotel) run $60-110 per night. The strip is more expensive on average than the town centre but less expensive than the Tayba ridge 5-stars at the upper end.
What are the trade-offs?
Two main ones. (1) Less view-driven atmosphere — the strip is at valley-floor elevation rather than on the ridge, so the dramatic Petra-mountain views that Tayba ridge offers aren't available here. (2) Less restaurant variety — the strip has hotel restaurants and a few tourist-oriented places but lacks the depth of the town centre dining scene. Some travelers find the area less interesting to walk around during downtime than the working town.
Which Visitor Centre strip hotels should I consider?
Three main tiers. (1) Mövenpick Resort Petra for full 5-star service with maximum convenience. (2) Petra Guest House for solid 4-star at the entrance — the property is directly attached to the Visitor Centre complex with the shortest possible walk. (3) Petra Moon Hotel and similar mid-range options ($60-110/night) for value with continued walking access. See individual hotel pages for detailed reviews.

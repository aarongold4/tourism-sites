Review

Stamba Hotel

A Soviet publishing house reborn as the hotel that announced Tbilisi to the design world: 42 theatrical rooms above a lobby that functions as the city's living room.

Last reviewed: June 7, 2026

The building is the thesis. Stamba occupies a 1950s printing and publishing house in Vera, and the conversion — by the Adjara Group, opened in 2018 — kept the brutalist bones honest: raw concrete columns, a five-story atrium where the presses ran, and industrial scale softened by jungle-dense planting, sculptural furniture, and bookshelves by the wall. The ground floor operates as semi-public theater — Café Stamba's all-day room, the speakeasy-adjacent bar, a chocolaterie — and the result is that rare hotel that anchors its neighborhood's social life rather than extracting from it.

The 42 rooms and suites commit to the bit: generous footprints, super-king beds that dominate guest reviews, gilded freestanding tubs, La Marzocco machines, McIntosh sound (vinyl in some categories), and huge windows onto the atrium or the gardens shared with Rooms Hotel next door. Published rates as of our last review run from the high $100s toward $300 by category and season — Tbilisi's top of market, and well under what this product costs anywhere west of here. Breakfast is served at Rooms next door, a quirk of the shared campus.

The honest ledger

For: the most photogenic interiors in the Caucasus, service that recent guests consistently rate among the city's best, a Vera address that puts the wine bars and cafes at your door, and an atmosphere that makes staying in feel like participating. Against: no full spa (gym access runs through the sister property), moody lighting that divides reviewers, and scene-hotel energy that suits people who like their lobby lively. Book it for a design-led city trip, an anniversary, or any stay where the hotel is part of the itinerary; book elsewhere in Vera if you want the neighborhood without the theater. Either way, come for coffee — the atrium is a sight in its own right, and our two-day plan routes past it accordingly.

Booking it smart

A few specifics turn a Stamba stay from good to seamless. Room categories vary more than the rate alone suggests: the atrium-facing rooms trade outside light for the theatrical interior view, the garden-facing ones reverse the deal, and the higher tiers add the freestanding brass tubs and vinyl players that the reviews rhapsodize about — worth scanning the category descriptions rather than booking blind. The moody, low-light design that defines the place divides guests, so if you need a bright room to function in the morning, factor that in. Breakfast is served next door at Rooms, a quirk of the shared campus rather than an oversight, and wellness amenities are limited — there's no full spa, with gym access via the sister property — so spa-seekers should size that expectation now.

Compare the direct rate against the marketplaces before committing; refundable rates are widely available, and design-tier rooms move fast on the autumn and late-spring weekends that crowd the whole city. Whether or not you stay, treat the ground floor as a public attraction: the five-story atrium, Café Stamba, the chocolaterie, and the bar function as semi-public theater, and a coffee there is the cheapest ticket to one of the region's best interiors. Basing in Vera puts the wine bars and cafes at the door; the itineraries route past the building for exactly that reason.

Frequently asked questions

What does Stamba cost?

As of our last review, published rates commonly run from the high $100s to around $300 per night depending on room category and season — destination pricing for Tbilisi, midrange pricing for what an equivalent design landmark charges in Western Europe. Refundable rates are widely available; compare the direct rate against the marketplaces before booking.

What are the rooms actually like?

Large, theatrical, and consistent with the building: super-king beds guests rave about, freestanding brass bathtubs, La Marzocco espresso machines, McIntosh hi-fi (vinyl players in some categories), and windows onto the atrium or the shared gardens. Lighting runs moody by design — a recurring review note that’s either ambiance or annoyance depending on you.

Does it have a spa or pool?

No full spa — wellness amenities are limited, with gym access via the sister property and the in-room bathtubs doing the soaking duty. Travelers prioritizing a resort-style spa floor should size that expectation now; this is a design-and-scene hotel, not a wellness retreat.

Where exactly is it?

Kostava Street in Vera — the creative quarter’s main artery, sharing a garden block with Rooms Hotel next door. Rustaveli and the funicular are short walks; the Old Town is 20–30 minutes on foot downhill or a quick Bolt.

Is it worth visiting without staying?

Emphatically — that’s half its role in the city. The lobby, Café Stamba, the chocolaterie, and the five-story atrium operate as public theater; coffee there is the cheapest ticket to one of the region’s best interiors.

ITINERARIES

Petra itineraries

Day-by-day plans for every kind of Petra trip — a focused 48 hours, a one-week Jordan loop with Wadi Rum and the Dead Sea, or the cross-border combinations with Israel and Egypt. Pick the one that fits the days you actually have.

Last reviewed April 28, 2026 · 14-minute read · See our editorial standards

The most useful planning fact about Petra is that it sits roughly 3 hours south of Amman, 2 hours north of Aqaba, and 2 hours from Wadi Rum — which means almost everyone visiting it does so as part of a larger Jordan loop, not as a standalone trip. That changes the planning math considerably. A Petra itinerary isn't really "how many days at Petra"; it's "how do I sequence Petra into Jordan's four big destinations (Amman, Petra, Wadi Rum, Dead Sea) without driving the same road twice."

This page is the index to the day-by-day plans we recommend, organized by total trip length. The shortest viable itinerary is two days focused only on Petra; the longest reasonable one is two weeks covering everything Jordan has to offer plus a Sinai or Israel extension. Most travelers land in the 5–10 day window, and most of those should be using some version of the classic loop: Amman → Dead Sea → Petra → Wadi Rum → Aqaba or back to Amman. The variations are in how long you spend at each stop and whether you take the King's Highway or the Desert Highway between them.

If you're trying to decide how long Petra itself deserves, the realistic answer is two days. One day works only as a compressed visit; three days is for hikers or photographers. The links below explain what each duration actually buys you, with hour-by-hour day plans where it helps.

Multi-destination Jordan itineraries

Most travelers who come to Petra come to see Jordan more broadly. The good news: the country is small (about the size of Portugal), the roads are excellent, and the four major destinations — Amman, the Dead Sea, Petra, and Wadi Rum — form a natural loop that you can do in either direction. The bad news: the loop is a real driving commitment. A one-week Jordan trip involves around 1,000 km of driving over five travel days, and rushed itineraries (less than 5 days total) sacrifice more than they save.

The two main route decisions are the Desert Highway versus the King's Highway between Amman and Petra (covered in the FAQ above), and whether to overnight in Aqaba on the way back or skip it. Most visitors who aren't divers find Aqaba skippable. Wadi Rum is non-negotiable — the Bedouin camp overnight under stars is one of the trip's high points, and only works if you actually stay.

Cross-border itineraries

A meaningful share of Petra visitors arrive from Israel or Egypt rather than flying directly into Jordan. Both are workable, both add real complexity, and both deserve their own dedicated planning. The Israel route uses the Wadi Araba (Yitzhak Rabin) border crossing near Eilat — easy in theory, slow in practice, with the 130 JOD in combined exit/entry fees that catches travelers off guard. The Egypt route uses the Taba crossing between Eilat and Sinai and typically routes via a domestic flight Sharm el-Sheikh → Cairo.

The most important thing to know about cross-border itineraries: budget half a day per crossing. Border crossings here move at the speed of bureaucracy, and a Petra day trip from Eilat that promises to "get you back by sunset" usually means you'll be eating dinner at 22:00, not 19:00. If you have the flexibility, overnighting in Jordan (and using the Jordan Pass entrance discount) makes the math dramatically better.

The standard two-day Petra plan

For the impatient: if you're planning two days at Petra and want the shape of the itinerary in three paragraphs, here it is. This is the plan we'd recommend to a friend.

Day 1. Enter the site at 06:00 — yes, you should set an alarm — and walk the Siq to the Treasury in the cool morning light, with the canyon largely to yourself. Continue through the Street of Facades to the Royal Tombs (climb up briefly to the Urn Tomb terrace for the view back over the lower city). Visit the Roman theatre and the colonnaded street. Lunch at the Basin Restaurant or pack your own. In the afternoon, climb the 800 steps to the Monastery — start by 14:00 to have it in the better afternoon light. Walk back through the lower city in golden hour, with the Treasury empty again as the day-trip crowds have left. If it's Sunday through Thursday, take a long break in Wadi Musa, then return for Petra by Night at 20:30.

Day 2. Re-enter the site at 06:30. Today is for the trails you skipped — either the Al-Khubtha trail up to the famous Treasury viewpoint, or the High Place of Sacrifice with the Wadi Farasa loop down past the Garden Temple. Either trail is 3–4 hours including viewing time. Come down for lunch, then spend the afternoon at Little Petra (a 15-minute taxi ride) or revisit the Royal Tombs in the better light. Out by 16:00, dinner in Wadi Musa, departure the next morning.

The full hour-by-hour version, with timing for sunrise, when each restaurant opens, and which trail entry points get crowded when, is on the two-day itinerary page.

What to do once you've picked an itinerary

Most travelers, once they've settled on dates and days, are deciding between three transport options for the actual Petra portion. The fastest is a guided tour (no logistics, but less flexibility). The cheapest is the JETT bus from Amman (round-trip transport for around $28 USD). The most flexible is a self-driven rental car, which we cover on the getting there pillar with route notes for the Desert Highway and the King's Highway.

Whichever you pick, you'll want a Jordan Pass — it pays for itself the moment you land. Book your Wadi Musa hotel 2–3 months ahead for spring or autumn dates. And if Petra is your first stop after a long international flight, the smart play is to start with a low-key day in Amman rather than driving south immediately.

Frequently asked questions

How many days should I spend at Petra specifically?
Two days is the right answer for most visitors. One day works if you start at 06:00 and accept missing either the Monastery hike or the high-place trails. Three days is for serious hikers, photographers, or anyone wanting a leisurely visit with time for Little Petra. Petra in less than a full day is genuinely not worth the trip — the Treasury alone takes 90 minutes to do justice once you account for the Siq walk, and the lower city and Royal Tombs are another half-day.
What's the best Jordan itinerary that includes Petra?
For one week, the classic loop is Amman (1 day) → Dead Sea (1 night) → Petra (2 days) → Wadi Rum (1 night) → Aqaba or back to Amman (1 day). This hits the four major destinations with enough breathing room and minimal backtracking. For 10 days, add Jerash and the Decapolis cities in the north (an easy day trip from Amman), Karak Castle on the King's Highway between the Dead Sea and Petra, and a second night in Wadi Rum. We have a full one-week itinerary breakdown linked below.
Can I combine Petra with Israel or Egypt?
Yes, both work. The Eilat border crossing (Wadi Araba / Yitzhak Rabin Crossing) makes a Petra + Israel combo straightforward — a common itinerary is Tel Aviv → Jerusalem → Eilat → Petra → Wadi Rum → back to Eilat. Petra + Egypt typically routes through the Taba crossing between Eilat and Sinai, then on to Cairo by domestic flight. Both add significant border-crossing logistics (allow half a day for each crossing, both directions) and a separate Jordan visa unless you have a Jordan Pass — see our visa page for the details.
Is the King's Highway worth taking instead of the Desert Highway?
For a one-time visitor with a rental car, yes. The Desert Highway is a 3-hour direct drive from Amman to Petra. The King's Highway is 5–6 hours but passes through Madaba (Byzantine mosaics), Mount Nebo (Moses's vantage point over the Promised Land), Karak Castle (Crusader fortress), and the Dana Biosphere Reserve. It's a one-day journey worth taking in itself. Most organized tours use the Desert Highway for time reasons; renting a car is the easiest way to do the King's Highway properly.
How do Petra by Night and the sunrise tours fit into an itinerary?
Petra by Night runs Sunday through Thursday at 20:30 (it was expanded from three nights a week in late 2025) and only happens if you're staying overnight in Wadi Musa. It is a separate 30 JOD ticket, not included in the Jordan Pass. If you're in Petra for two days, the standard sequencing is Day 1 at the site, evening Petra by Night, Day 2 back at the site for the Monastery or the trails you skipped. Sunrise visits (06:00 entry, Treasury before the crowds) are a no-brainer if you're overnighting nearby — and the reason every multi-day itinerary on this site assumes a Wadi Musa hotel.
What about a Petra-only weekend?
A 2-night Petra-only trip works well if you're flying directly into Amman or Aqaba and have nothing else on your list. Two days at the site, one night Petra by Night, no other destinations to compete for time. The math works out: roughly 12–14 hours of actual exploration over two days, plus transport. The catch is the visa-on-arrival quirk — you need to stay at least two nights in Jordan to make the Jordan Pass visa waiver valid, so a Petra weekend hits the floor exactly.

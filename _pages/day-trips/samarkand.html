Samarkand from Tashkent

The Registan, Gur-e-Amir and Shah-i-Zinda — two hours from Tashkent by high-speed train.

Samarkand is the reason many people come to Uzbekistan at all, and it is the most popular thing to do from Tashkent. The Registan — three colossal tiled madrasas facing a single square — is one of the great sights of the Islamic world, and the city adds the Gur-e-Amir mausoleum of Timur, the Shah-i-Zinda avenue of tombs, and the Bibi-Khanym mosque.

Getting there: the Afrosiyob train

The Afrosiyob high-speed train is the way to go. It covers the roughly 300 km in about two hours and arrives near the centre of Samarkand. Since February 2026 an economy seat costs about 311,000 som (roughly 26 US dollars), with business around 455,000 som and VIP around 709,000 som. The catch: it is popular and sells out, so book as far ahead as you can — tickets typically open about 45 days before travel.

  • Take an early train to maximise your day if you are not staying overnight.
  • Second (economy) class is perfectly comfortable for a two-hour ride.
  • If the Afrosiyob is sold out, the slower Sharq train covers the same route for less and is easier to book.
  • See getting around for reaching Tashkent&apos;s stations.

Day trip or overnight?

A long day trip works — early train out, late train back gives you six or seven hours on the ground, enough for the Registan, Gur-e-Amir and Shah-i-Zinda. But Samarkand rewards an overnight, especially to see the Registan lit after dark. If you have the nights, build it into a wider Silk Road loop with Bukhara.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the train from Tashkent to Samarkand?

About two hours each way on the Afrosiyob high-speed train, which is the fastest and most comfortable option. Slower trains such as the Sharq take around three hours.

How much does the Tashkent–Samarkand train cost?

Economy class on the Afrosiyob is about 311,000 som (roughly 26 US dollars) one way as of 2026, with business and VIP costing more. Book ahead — tickets open around 45 days before departure and sell out in peak season.

Can you do Samarkand as a day trip from Tashkent?

Yes. With an early departure and an evening return you get six to seven hours in the city — enough for the main sights. Staying a night is better if you can, mainly to see the Registan illuminated after dark.

JORDAN

Wadi Rum

Jordan's most spectacular desert — 720 square kilometers of sandstone and granite rock formations rising from red sand, where Lawrence of Arabia campaigned and where The Martian and Dune were filmed. Here's how to do it right.

Last reviewed April 28, 2026 · 10-minute read · See our editorial standards

Wadi Rum is the second great destination of a Jordan trip — the experiential counterweight to Petra's archaeological intensity. Where Petra is a high-energy walking trip through carved monuments, Wadi Rum is a seated experience: jeep tours through massive rock formations, sunset viewpoints, Bedouin camp dinners around an open fire, and one of the best stargazing nights anywhere in the Middle East. For most travelers, combining the two — Petra first, Wadi Rum after — produces the strongest version of a Jordan trip.

This page covers the practical version of Wadi Rum: how to get there, how long to spend, choosing a Bedouin camp, what jeep tours actually involve, current pricing, and how to make the connections with Petra and Aqaba work. The Lawrence and Mars filming history adds cultural color, but the real value of Wadi Rum is the desert itself.

What Wadi Rum actually is

Wadi Rum is a protected desert area in southern Jordan, covering approximately 720 square kilometers. The defining geological feature is the rock formations — enormous sandstone and granite monoliths rising vertically from a red-sand desert floor, sometimes 500-700 meters tall. The largest is Jebel Umm ad-Dami, the highest peak in Jordan at 1,854 meters. The area was inhabited continuously from at least the Neolithic period; Nabataean inscriptions and rock art from various pre-Islamic periods appear throughout the landscape.

The modern Wadi Rum tourism economy is built around the local Zalabia Bedouin tribe, who administer the protected area jointly with the Jordanian government. Bedouin families run the camps, the jeep tours, the camel treks, and most of the on-the-ground services. The area is UNESCO World Heritage-listed since 2011 for both its natural and cultural significance.

How to get to Wadi Rum

Wadi Rum is most commonly reached from Petra (100 km north, 1.5-2 hours), Aqaba (60 km south, 1 hour), or Amman (320 km north, 4 hours). The entry point is the Wadi Rum Visitor Centre on the western edge of the protected area, just off Route 47 from the Desert Highway.

From Petra: JETT bus daily (~22 JOD, ~17:00 departure, arrives 18:30). Camp-arranged transfer (30-50 JOD per car). Rental car (1.5-2 hours direct on Desert Highway).

From Aqaba: Shared taxi or service car (~15 JOD per person, fills slowly). Private taxi (35-50 JOD for the car). Rental car (1 hour direct).

From Amman: JETT bus runs to Aqaba; transfer at Aqaba to a Wadi Rum shuttle. Total journey 5-6 hours. Direct rental car drive is 4 hours on the Desert Highway. Most travelers reach Wadi Rum via Petra rather than directly from Amman.

At the Wadi Rum Visitor Centre, you'll pay a 5 JOD entrance fee (covered by the Jordan Pass), or your camp pickup will handle it. From the Visitor Centre, you transfer to a Bedouin 4WD vehicle for the drive into the protected area to your camp — typical 30-45 minutes of off-road driving.

Choosing a Bedouin camp

The camp choice is the single biggest decision for a Wadi Rum trip. The three rough tiers:

Basic Bedouin camps ($30-60 USD per person per night). Standard tents on a sand floor, shared bathrooms, communal dining, includes the basic jeep tour and dinner/breakfast. Authentic but rustic. Examples: Bedouin Whispers, Wadi Rum Bedouin Camp.

Mid-range camps ($80-150 USD per person per night). Private tents with proper beds, attached bathrooms in newer camps, better dinners, more comfortable common areas. The sweet spot for most travelers. Examples: Memories Aicha, Wadi Rum Sky Tours camp.

Luxury "bubble" camps ($200-500+ USD per person per night). Transparent-roof dome tents for stargazing from your bed, full bathrooms, restaurant-quality meals, often with hot tubs and pools. The Instagram version of Wadi Rum. Examples: Sun City Camp, Magic Camp, Wadi Rum Night Luxury Camp.

For most travelers, the mid-range tier produces the best balance of comfort and authentic experience. The luxury camps are remarkable but expensive (often more than a Petra hotel); the basic camps are authentic but uncomfortable for less experienced campers.

The typical Wadi Rum experience

A standard one-night camp stay runs like this:

  • 14:00-16:00: Arrive at the Visitor Centre, meet your camp's pickup vehicle, transfer 30-45 minutes into the desert to the camp.
  • 16:00-19:00: Jeep tour through the major rock formations. Standard stops: Lawrence's Spring (a small water source associated with T.E. Lawrence), Khazali Canyon (Nabataean petroglyphs on the canyon walls), the Mushroom Rock (an iconic eroded formation), one or two of the natural rock bridges, sunset at a high viewpoint (Um Sabatah is a common choice).
  • 19:30: Dinner at camp. The traditional Zarb — meat (chicken, lamb) and vegetables slow-cooked in a sand-buried oven — is the typical preparation. Eaten communally with other camp guests around low tables.
  • 20:30-22:00: Evening around the fire. Bedouin tea, sometimes traditional music or storytelling. Stargazing if the moon allows.
  • 23:00: Sleep in a Bedouin tent. Rugs and thick blankets, no electricity in basic camps, comfortable beds in mid-range and luxury camps.
  • 06:30 (next morning): Sunrise. Most camps offer an optional early jeep tour or a walk to a viewpoint to watch the light.
  • 08:00: Breakfast at camp.
  • 09:00-10:00: Transfer back to the Visitor Centre and onward to your next destination.

A two-night stay adds: a longer jeep tour day with more remote formations, optional camel trek, optional rock climbing or hiking, longer time at camp, the second sunset and sunrise, and the option of a hot-air balloon flight on the second morning (one of the truly memorable Wadi Rum experiences if you can afford it — $170-250 USD per person).

The Lawrence and filming history

Wadi Rum's international fame comes from two sources: T.E. Lawrence's Arab Revolt campaigns of 1916-1918, documented in his "Seven Pillars of Wisdom" and dramatized in David Lean's 1962 film "Lawrence of Arabia"; and more recently, the use of Wadi Rum as a Mars stand-in for "The Martian" (2015) and several scenes in "Dune" (2021) and "Dune: Part Two" (2024).

The Lawrence connection is real but interpreted loosely — the named "Seven Pillars of Wisdom" rock formation visible from the Visitor Centre was named after Lawrence's book, not the other way around. Lawrence's actual campaigns through the area are interpreted by local Bedouin guides at the major rock formations; some of the connections are well-documented historically, others are storytelling embellishment.

The Mars filming connection is more recent and more straightforward: the red sand, the dramatic rock formations, and the comparatively low budget of filming in Jordan made Wadi Rum the obvious choice for both productions. Tours sometimes point out specific filming locations from "The Martian" — the rover scenes, the habitat exterior shots — though much of "The Martian" was filmed in nearby valleys rather than at the recognized tourist sites.

When to visit Wadi Rum

The best months are March-May and September-November — moderate temperatures (20-28°C daytime, 8-15°C overnight), reduced wind, comfortable for both jeep tours and outdoor camp evenings. December-February can be genuinely cold overnight (sometimes near freezing in January), though clear skies make for excellent stargazing if you can handle the cold. June-August is hot (35-40°C daytime) but tolerable inside air-conditioned jeeps and after sunset.

Avoid Easter week, Christmas/New Year week, and major Israeli/Jewish holidays — Wadi Rum gets busy with visitors and camp prices spike. Mid-week stays in shoulder months produce the quietest, best-value experience.

Combining with Petra

The classic Jordan trip combines Petra (2 days) and Wadi Rum (1 night), typically: arrive Wadi Musa, do Petra for two days, transfer south to Wadi Rum on the morning of day 3, overnight in a camp, continue to Aqaba or back to Amman on day 4.

For travelers extending to four days: Petra 2 days, Wadi Rum 2 nights. The extra Wadi Rum night enables the balloon flight, the longer jeep tour, and the proper afternoon at camp. Significantly better experience for the marginal cost.

Frequently asked questions

Where is Wadi Rum?
About 100 km south of Petra and 60 km north of Aqaba, in the south of Jordan. It's a 720-square-kilometer protected desert area characterized by enormous sandstone and granite rock formations rising from red sand. The main entry point is the Wadi Rum Visitor Centre, where most jeep tours and camp transfers depart.
How many days do I need at Wadi Rum?
One night is the minimum for the real Wadi Rum experience — afternoon jeep tour, sunset, Bedouin camp dinner, stargazing, sunrise the next morning. Two nights is meaningfully better: a full day of activities (longer jeep tour, camel trek, hike), the second sunset, the second morning. Three+ nights is for travelers who specifically want hiking, rock climbing, or extended desert immersion. For most travelers combining Wadi Rum with Petra, one night is the right starting point.
How do I get to Wadi Rum from Petra?
Three options. JETT bus runs the route daily (~22 JOD, 1.5-2 hours, 17:00 typical departure from Petra). Most Wadi Rum camps will arrange transfer for guests (30-50 JOD per car, pickup at your Wadi Musa hotel). Rental car or private taxi (similar pricing, more flexibility). The camp transfer is usually the cleanest option — call your booked camp 1-2 days ahead to arrange.
How much does a Wadi Rum Bedouin camp cost?
Wide range. Basic Bedouin camps start at $30-60 USD per person per night including dinner and breakfast — standard tents, shared bathrooms, communal dining. Mid-range camps run $80-150 USD/night — private tents with proper beds, attached bathrooms in newer camps, better dinners. Luxury "bubble" camps with transparent dome roofs for stargazing from your bed run $200-500+ USD/night. For most travelers, mid-range is the right tier.
What's the typical Wadi Rum experience?
Arrive at camp in late afternoon, take a 3-4 hour jeep tour through the major rock formations (Lawrence's Spring, Khazali Canyon, the Mushroom Rock, sunset at one of the high viewpoints), return to camp for traditional Zarb dinner (meat and vegetables cooked in an underground sand oven), evening around the fire, sleep in a Bedouin tent. Optional add-ons: sunrise jeep tour, camel ride, hot-air balloon flight, rock climbing.
Is Wadi Rum safe?
Yes. The area is administered jointly with the local Zalabia Bedouin tribe and tourism is well-established. Safety concerns are environmental (sun exposure, dehydration on jeep tours and hikes, occasional flash floods during winter storms) rather than security-related. Solo travelers including women travel Wadi Rum routinely. Cellular coverage is patchy inside the protected area but the major camps have Wi-Fi.

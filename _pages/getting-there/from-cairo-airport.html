GETTING THERE

From Cairo airport

CAI and the pyramids bookend Greater Cairo — 45 kilometers and anywhere from 50 minutes to two-plus hours apart. The transport choice is simple once you've decided what an arrival gauntlet is worth to you.

Last reviewed June 6, 2026◆6-minute read

Photo: Radosław Botev · CC BY 3.0 pl · Wikimedia Commons

Cairo International sits on the city's northeastern edge; the pyramids stand on its southwestern one. Everything between is Cairo, which makes this less a transfer than a crossing — and makes time of day the biggest variable in your plan. The good news: the options are few, clear, and all of them work. Here they are in the order most travelers should consider them.

Ride-hailing: the default

Uber and Careem both blanket the airport and the city. Fares to Giza or central Cairo run a fraction of the terminal taxi quotes — order-of-magnitude, a long crossing on the apps costs what a short hop costs in Western cities — with the price fixed in the app and no negotiation. The only craft is the pickup: app cars collect from designated zones a short walk from arrivals, the walk is lined with freelance "assistance," and the move is the same as everywhere in Giza-bound travel — follow your app's pin, decline cheerfully, keep moving. Have an Egyptian SIM or roaming data working before you exit customs (airport SIM counters are decent value), since the apps are your transport layer for the whole trip (the full ride-hailing guide).

Pre-arranged transfers: the soft landing

Every hotel offers an airport pickup, and after a red-eye it's the one time the markup buys something real: a name sign past customs, a known price agreed in writing, zero gauntlet. Expect several times the app fare — still modest in absolute terms — and book it when you book the room, confirming the price includes parking and waiting time. Tour operators bundle the same service into arrival-day packages. The calculus: first time in Egypt, night arrival, family in tow — transfer; seasoned traveler, daytime arrival — apps.

The terminal taxi layer: know it to skip it

The arrivals hall and curb host a persistent ecosystem of taxi brokers quoting flat rates that start at multiples of the app fare and descend with resistance. There's no safety issue — it's the same wallet game as the plateau's (same counters) — but there's also no reason to play: the white official taxis with meters exist but meter discipline is unreliable, and the apps render the whole negotiation obsolete.

Routing and timing

Going straight to the pyramids: set the destination to the main visitors' center entrance or your pyramid-side hotel, and prefer a morning arrival slot — the crossing at 6-7am is the 50-minute version, and it deposits you in the golden window. Going to a central hotel first (most itineraries): Downtown, Garden City, and Zamalek are 30-60 minutes depending on hour. Evening arrivals should simply expect the long version and stop checking the ETA. The public-transport option exists — an airport bus or ride to the metro, then the system's pennies-cheap lines — but with luggage after a flight it's an enthusiast's economy; the honest math is in the city-side guides.

Terminal by terminal: the arrival walkthrough

CAI's terminals share a layout logic worth previewing. Off the jetbridge, the sequence is visa bank counters (if buying on arrival — exact USD, before the immigration hall), immigration, baggage, customs, and then the arrivals concourse where the meeting boards, SIM counters, ATMs, and the freelance "assistance" ecosystem all share floor space. The moves in order: visa first if needed (the current process), data second (the carrier booths sell tourist eSIM/SIM bundles at fair prices — five minutes that powers everything after), cash third (the bank-attached ATMs in arrivals beat any exchange counter — withdraw EGP, break a big note at the kiosk immediately), and transport last, by whichever plan you pre-made. Pre-booked transfer: find your name board inside the hall and verify the hotel/booking name back. App ride: follow the signage to the designated pickup zone, decline escorts, match the plate. The whole sequence runs 45-90 minutes from gate to highway at normal hours; red-eyes at the visa-queue peaks run longer and argue for the e-visa's skip.

Departure day: the run in reverse

The return leg has its own arithmetic. International departures want you at CAI 3 hours out (2.5 as the floor), and the crossing gets the time-of-day treatment in reverse: evening flights fight the 4-9pm peak, so a 7pm departure from a Giza-side hotel means rolling at 3:00-3:30, not 4:30. The app works fine airport-bound (no pickup-zone complications — any curb loads); pre-arranged cars earn their premium for pre-dawn flights, when you want a confirmed vehicle rather than an open app at 3am. Inside, security runs at the terminal entrance (before check-in — budget the extra queue), currency rules cap exported EGP (spend it down or reconvert), and the corridor-night strategy exists for exactly the flights this paragraph makes sound stressful. Last practical note: keep the small EGP bills to the end — the trolley, the porter, and the final coffees all run on them, and the duty-free will not break a 200 for a water.

The crossing, in summary

The airport page's residue: apps for the confident, transfers for the tired, never the terminal taxi quotes; data working before the doors; the crossing budgeted at an hour empty and two-plus at the peak in either direction; and the layover ambitions checked against the honest arithmetic before any pyramid promises get made to the family. Cairo's airport rewards exactly one thing — a plan made before landing — and punishes exactly one thing, which is its absence.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the drive from Cairo airport to the pyramids?

The route crosses the full width of Greater Cairo — about 45 kilometers. Empty roads (early morning, late night): 50-60 minutes. Daytime norm: 75-90. Evening peak: two hours and beyond. Whatever your plan, the departure hour is part of the route.

Does Uber work at Cairo airport?

Yes — Uber and Careem both serve CAI, at fares that undercut the terminal taxi offers several times over. The friction is the pickup: app pickup points sit a short walk from the terminals, and you'll be offered 'help' the whole way. Follow the app's pin, not the helpers.

Should I just book a hotel transfer?

After a long-haul arrival, it's the one time the premium earns itself: a name sign, a known price, no gauntlet. Expect several times the app fare. For confident travelers and daytime arrivals, ride-hailing does the same trip for a fraction.

Can you go straight from a layover to the pyramids?

With enough layover, yes — the working math is 8+ hours of layover for a worthwhile visit, accounting for immigration, the crossing each way, and the airport's return buffers. The dedicated layover plan in the itineraries cluster runs the numbers honestly.

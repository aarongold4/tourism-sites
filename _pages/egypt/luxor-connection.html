EGYPT TRAVEL

Adding Luxor and the south

Cairo gives you the Old Kingdom; Luxor, 650 kilometers upriver, gives you the New — Karnak, the Valley of the Kings, the painted tombs. Here's how the classic pairing connects, and the honest cases for and against.

Last reviewed June 6, 2026◆6-minute read

Photo: V Manninen from Funland · CC BY 2.0 · Wikimedia Commons

This site's depth is Giza and Cairo — that's the point of it — but most readers planning a pyramid trip are really planning an Egypt trip, and the Luxor question arrives immediately. The short version: the pairing is the classic for good reason (the pyramids are the Old Kingdom's statement, Luxor's tombs and temples are the New Kingdom's, and together they're the whole arc), the connection is easy, and the only real variable is time.

The three connections

Fly (the default): Cairo-Luxor runs about an hour, multiple times daily, at fares that are modest by international standards — the answer for anyone on a week or less. Book the early flight and you're at Karnak by mid-morning. The sleeper train (the classic): roughly ten overnight hours in private two-berth cabins, dinner and breakfast included, arriving with the day intact — the budget-romantic choice, booked ahead in season. The Nile cruise (the destination): the 3-4 night Luxor-Aswan boats don't connect Cairo at all (you fly to meet them); what they offer is the south by water — Edfu and Kom Ombo arriving at the gangplank, the river as the trip. Choose it as an experience, not a transfer.

What Luxor actually is

Ancient Thebes, in two halves the river divides: the East Bank of the living — Karnak, the largest religious complex ever built, and Luxor Temple glowing at night — and the West Bank of the dead: the Valley of the Kings' painted royal tombs (the color Giza's bare chambers never had), Hatshepsut's terraces, the nobles' tombs, the Colossi. Two full days covers both banks' essentials; the ticketing runs the same card-first, tiered, frequently-revised system as Giza, so the verify-near-your-date habit travels with you.

The week that works

The canonical seven days: days 1-3 Cairo — the two-day pyramid program plus a city day (the old cities); day 4 fly south, East Bank afternoon, Luxor Temple at night; day 5 West Bank at opening (its own 7am rule — the tombs heat fast); day 6 second-pass favorites or the Dendera day trip; day 7 fly home via Cairo. Swap days 4-6 for a cruise and the week becomes nine days. With five days or fewer: do Cairo properly and bank Luxor for the return trip — a rushed Egypt serves neither kingdom.

Frequently asked questions

How do you get from Cairo to Luxor?

Three real options: the one-hour flight (the time-efficient default, frequent and cheap by international standards), the overnight sleeper train (the romantic budget classic, ~10 hours, private cabins), or the day train (long, scenic, cheapest). Driving is neither permitted in practice for tourists on most desert routes nor worth it.

How many days does Luxor need?

Two full days minimum: one for the West Bank (Valley of the Kings, Hatshepsut, the tombs), one for the East (Karnak, Luxor Temple). Three breathes; cruise itineraries stretch the same content along the river to Aswan over 3-4 nights.

Is the Nile cruise worth it?

As transport, it's slow; as a trip, it's beloved for a reason — temples arriving by water, the river's rhythm, sunset from the deck. The honest framing: a 3-4 night Luxor-Aswan cruise is a destination in itself that happens to include the sites. Time-poor travelers fly and stay in hotels; time-rich ones cruise.

Cairo or Luxor — if I must choose?

Different answers for different travelers: Cairo (with Giza, Saqqara, GEM) is the Old Kingdom, the pyramids, and a living megacity; Luxor is the New Kingdom — the painted tombs and the great temples — in a small, manageable river town. First-time, single-city trips usually choose Cairo; tomb-and-temple obsessives choose Luxor.

Giza opening hours

The plateau runs a short day — roughly 7 to 4 — and the difference between its first hour and its fourth is the difference between a great visit and a hot, crowded one. Hours shift in Ramadan; check the review date on this page.

Last reviewed June 6, 2026◆5-minute read

Giza keeps shorter hours than most visitors expect. The plateau is a daytime archaeological site, not an attraction with evening programming (the Sound and Light Show excepted), and its day is compressed enough that arrival time is the single most consequential planning decision you'll make. Here is the current schedule and how to use it.

Standard hours

Daily, 7:00 AM to 4:00 PM, year-round, with no weekly closing day. Ticket sales and entry stop ahead of closing — plan on roughly the final hour being entry-closed — and staff begin sweeping visitors toward the gates as 4:00 approaches. Some seasons have run extended summer hours; treat anything past 4:00 PM as a bonus to verify on the official ticketing platform, not a plan.

The pyramid interiors operate within the site day, with the capacity-limited Great Pyramid allocation (about 150 entries daily — see entrance fees) frequently exhausted well before afternoon in peak season. If the interior is on your list, it is effectively a morning activity.

Ramadan hours

During Ramadan the schedule compresses: opening moves to 8:00 AM and last entry to 3:30 PM, with the site winding down earlier as staff break their fast at sunset approaches. Ramadan follows the lunar calendar and moves about eleven days earlier each year — in 2026 it fell across February and March — so check whether your dates collide before fixing plans. Visiting during Ramadan is entirely workable and the plateau is often quieter; just build your day around the shifted window.

The shape of the day

7:00-9:00 AM — the golden window. Soft light, temperatures at their daily low, tour buses not yet arrived, interior tickets available. Everything written on this site about Giza assumes you'll try to be here for it.

9:30 AM-1:30 PM — peak load. The bus fleets land mid-morning and the plateau carries its maximum crowd through midday under vertical sun. Photography flattens, queues form at the interiors, and the tout density peaks with the visitor density (the routines are decoded in the tout playbook).

2:00-4:00 PM — the second window. Crowds thin as the buses leave, light warms again, and the Sphinx — which faces east — silhouettes against the lowering sun. The trade-offs: interior tickets may be gone, summer heat is at its stored maximum, and the closing sweep limits how long you can linger.

Saqqara and Dahshur, for combined days

The day-trip sites run similar daytime schedules — roughly 8:00 AM to 4:00 PM as their standard pattern, with the same Ramadan compression logic. The classic three-site day (covered in our itineraries cluster) works precisely because of these aligned windows: Giza at opening, Saqqara late morning, Dahshur early afternoon — or the reverse order to take Giza in the quieter final window. Verify each site's current hours on the official platform when booking, as restoration work occasionally adjusts access.

The verification habit

Egypt adjusts site hours more often than most countries — seasonally, for Ramadan, around major events, and occasionally without much notice. The official source is the Ministry's ticketing platform at egymonuments.com, which lists current hours alongside ticket sales. This page is re-reviewed on a rolling basis (date above), but the night before your visit, thirty seconds on the official site is cheap insurance.

Hours across the wider circuit

The plateau's clock anchors this page, but trips run on the whole region's hours, so the comparative picture: Saqqara, Memphis, and Dahshur keep similar daylight windows (roughly 8 to 4-5 with last entry an hour before close, Ramadan-compressed like everything), which is why the southern day starts almost as early as the Giza one. The Grand Egyptian Museum is the schedule's great exception — open into the evening, last entry well before close — making it the region's one major after-dark option and the natural second half of any plateau day. The Tahrir museum runs conventional museum hours; the Sound and Light show owns the post-closing plateau evenings in rotating language slots. The meta-rule across all of them, one last time: hours in Egypt are policies, not facts — verified at the official platforms near your date, carried here with a review date, and worth thirty seconds of checking against any plan that depends on a closing time.

Frequently asked questions

What time should I arrive at the pyramids?

At opening — 7:00 AM in the standard schedule. The first 90 minutes deliver the trip's best conditions on every axis at once: softest light, coolest temperatures, thinnest crowds, and the freshest allocation of capacity-limited Great Pyramid interior tickets.

Is Giza open every day?

Yes — the plateau operates daily, year-round, including Fridays, weekends, and public holidays. Hours shift during Ramadan but the site does not close.

Can you visit the pyramids at night?

Not on a general ticket — the plateau clears in the late afternoon. Evening access happens through the Sound and Light Show, which seats visitors in the viewing area facing the floodlit Sphinx and pyramids, covered in our tours cluster.

What is the last entry time?

Ticket sales and entry stop before the posted closing time — typically about an hour prior in the standard schedule, and at 3:30 PM during Ramadan. Arriving for a "quick look" 45 minutes before close is a plan that regularly fails at the ticket window.

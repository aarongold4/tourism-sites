GETTING THERE

Shared taxi (service)

Jordan's budget transport workhorse. Shared sedans and minivans running between major cities at 5-12 JOD per person, departing when full rather than on schedules. Slower than JETT bus, cheaper than private taxi, and the way most Jordanians actually move between cities.

Last reviewed June 5, 2026 · 5-minute read · See our editorial standards

Shared taxis (called "service" in local usage, pronounced "serveece" from the French servis) are Jordan's primary intercity transport mode for ordinary Jordanians. The system fills the gap between expensive private taxis and the limited JETT bus network — multi-passenger vehicles running specific routes between cities, departing when they fill rather than on fixed schedules, charging per-passenger rates that are dramatically lower than private transport.

For tourists, shared taxis are the budget option for inter-city travel. They're slower, less comfortable, and require more flexibility than JETT buses, but they cost roughly half as much and provide a more authentic experience of Jordanian daily transport. This page covers how the system actually works, the practical considerations, and when shared taxis make sense for tourist travel.

How shared taxis work

The basic system:

Departure stations. Each city has specific gathering points where shared taxis assemble — typically a bus station or designated area near the city centre. Drivers wait at these stations for passengers to fill the vehicle (typically 4-7 people depending on the vehicle size — older Mercedes sedans seat 4, larger minivans seat 6-7).

Fill-and-go departures. The vehicle departs when full, not on a schedule. This means the wait time varies — popular routes during high-demand windows fill in 15-30 minutes; less popular routes can take 1-2 hours to fill. Morning is the most reliable time; afternoons see longer waits.

Fixed routes. Each shared taxi follows a defined route between two cities, with potential intermediate stops to pick up or drop off passengers along the way. The route is typically the most direct road between the destinations; drivers don't deviate significantly for individual passengers.

Per-passenger pricing. Each passenger pays a fixed per-seat rate, paid in cash to the driver (typically when boarding or when arriving at the destination). The rates are standardized; drivers don't negotiate fares with passengers.

Routes and pricing

The major routes serving Petra-area tourist destinations:

Amman to Petra (Wadi Musa)

  • Departure: South Bus Station (Mahatat al-Janub / Wahdat) in southern Amman.
  • Vehicle: Typically older Mercedes sedans (4 passengers) or minivans (6-7).
  • Cost: 7-12 JOD per person.
  • Time: 3-4 hours including wait and minor stops.
  • Best timing: Morning (06:00-10:00) when vehicles fill most reliably. Afternoon departures are possible but with longer waits.

Aqaba to Petra (Wadi Musa)

  • Departure: Central bus station in Aqaba.
  • Cost: 5-8 JOD per person.
  • Time: 1.5-2 hours.
  • Best timing: Morning departures most reliable; the Aqaba-Petra route is well-traveled.

Petra (Wadi Musa) to Wadi Rum

  • Departure: Informally from near the main mosque in Wadi Musa town centre.
  • Cost: 7-12 JOD per person.
  • Time: 2 hours.
  • Best timing: Morning. The route has lower passenger volume than the Petra-Aqaba leg; afternoons can see substantial waits.

Wadi Rum to Aqaba

  • Departure: Wadi Rum Visitor Centre.
  • Cost: 5-8 JOD per person.
  • Time: 1 hour.
  • Best timing: Morning, coordinated with the camp transfer schedules.

Practical considerations

The wait time variable

The "departs when full" model means your total travel time is unpredictable. A vehicle that's already 5 of 6 seats full when you arrive might depart in 5 minutes; a vehicle starting from empty might take an hour to fill. For travelers with fixed appointments or schedule constraints, this unpredictability is the biggest disadvantage of shared taxis versus JETT buses.

Strategies that help: Travel during morning peak (06:00-10:00) when vehicles fill faster. Be willing to wait or to pay for empty seats to leave sooner (some drivers will depart if you offer to cover the unfilled seats — typically 15-25 JOD additional total for the vehicle). Ask station staff or other passengers about typical wait times for your route.

Comfort and amenities

Shared taxis are functional rather than comfortable. Typical conditions:

  • Vehicles are usually older Mercedes 240Ds, Hyundai Sonatas, or Toyota Hiaces. Maintenance varies.
  • Air conditioning is sometimes present but not universally — older vehicles may have it broken or absent.
  • Seats are close together; tall passengers may be uncomfortable for the longer rides.
  • Luggage rides in the trunk or on roof racks; large items may not fit. Standard travel-size backpacks work fine.
  • Driver may stop for fuel, prayer breaks, or food along the route, adding to total travel time.

Communication

Most shared taxi drivers have basic English — enough to handle the transaction (where you're going, what it costs) but not extensive conversation. Other passengers vary in English ability. The journey is mostly silent or in Arabic; travelers comfortable with their own thoughts or audio entertainment have no problem.

Payment

Cash only. Have small bills available (5 JOD and 10 JOD notes are most useful). Drivers don't carry significant change; large bills (50 JOD notes) may be returned or refused. ATMs are widely available in Amman and Aqaba departure stations.

Safety considerations

Shared taxis operate under government regulation with licensed drivers. The basic safety profile is comparable to other public transport. Standard precautions apply:

  • Keep valuables (passport, wallet, electronics) on your person rather than in luggage
  • Don't display large amounts of cash when paying
  • Trust your instincts about specific drivers or vehicles — you can wait for the next one if something feels wrong
  • Female solo travelers may experience occasional unwanted attention; sitting next to other women when possible reduces this

Shared taxi vs alternatives

vs JETT bus

JETT runs modern coaches on fixed schedules — more comfortable (air conditioning, reserved seats), more predictable timing, slightly higher cost (~10-11 JOD Amman to Petra vs 7-12 JOD shared taxi). For travelers prioritizing comfort and schedule certainty, JETT wins. For travelers prioritizing cost and flexibility, shared taxi wins. The pricing difference is modest, so JETT is often the better default choice for tourist travel.

vs private taxi

Private taxis charge per-vehicle rates (75-100 JOD Amman to Petra) that are dramatically higher than shared per-passenger rates. The convenience is real (departure on your schedule, direct route, comfortable solo or couple experience) but the cost differential is large. For solo travelers and couples on budgets, shared taxi is the much-better-value option.

vs hired driver

A hired driver for a full day (60-100 JOD) covers multiple destinations and waits between stops. Shared taxis cover single direction at a time and require independent logistics for waiting/return transport. Hired drivers make sense for multi-stop days; shared taxis make sense for direct city-to-city transport.

When shared taxi makes sense

Best fit for:

  • Budget-conscious travelers maximizing cost savings on inter-city transport
  • Solo travelers and couples without significant luggage
  • Travelers with flexible schedules who can wait for vehicles to fill
  • Travelers wanting an authentic experience of Jordanian daily transport
  • Multi-week travelers in the region accustomed to local transport modes
  • Backpackers and similar budget profiles where every 5-10 JOD saved matters

Less suitable for:

  • Travelers with fixed appointments or tight schedules
  • Travelers with substantial luggage
  • Comfort-prioritized travel (especially in summer heat or winter cold)
  • Older travelers or those with mobility considerations
  • Travelers nervous about navigating local systems independently

Frequently asked questions

What's a shared taxi in Jordan?
A shared transport service called 'service' (pronounced 'serveece' from the French) where passengers split the cost of a multi-passenger taxi or minivan running between specific destinations. The vehicles depart when full (4-7 passengers depending on size) rather than on fixed schedules, follow standard routes between major cities, and stop for passengers along the way. Shared taxis fill the niche between expensive private taxis and infrequent public buses — slower than private but dramatically cheaper.
What does shared taxi to Petra cost?
Approximately 7-12 JOD per person from Amman South Bus Station to Wadi Musa, 5-8 JOD from Aqaba to Wadi Musa. The rate is per passenger, not per vehicle — the driver collects from each passenger as they board. The journey is 3-4 hours from Amman and 1.5-2 hours from Aqaba, somewhat longer than direct driving because the driver waits for the vehicle to fill before departing and may stop for additional passengers along the way.
Where do I catch a shared taxi?
From specific bus stations and informal gathering points. From Amman, the South Bus Station (Mahatat al-Janub, also called Wahdat) is the departure point for southbound shared taxis to Petra, Wadi Rum, and Aqaba. From Aqaba, the central bus station handles shared taxis to most Jordanian destinations. From Petra (Wadi Musa), the gathering point is informally near the main mosque in town centre. Most stations operate roughly 06:00-18:00 with morning departures most reliable.
Is shared taxi safe?
Generally yes. Shared taxis are a normal everyday transport mode used by Jordanians for inter-city travel; they operate under government regulation with licensed drivers and registered vehicles. Standards aren't luxurious — vehicles are typically older sedans or minivans, air conditioning is variable, and you're traveling with strangers. But the safety profile is comparable to other public transport. Travelers should follow standard precautions (keep valuables secure, don't display large amounts of cash).
Should I use shared taxi or JETT bus?
Depends on priorities. JETT bus is more comfortable (modern coaches, air conditioning, reserved seats), runs on fixed schedules (so you can plan around timing), and costs slightly more (~10-11 JOD Amman to Petra). Shared taxi is cheaper, more flexible on departure timing (whenever the vehicle fills rather than waiting for next scheduled bus), and provides a more authentic experience of Jordanian daily life. For comfort-prioritized travel, JETT. For budget-prioritized travel or for travelers who specifically want the local experience, shared taxi.

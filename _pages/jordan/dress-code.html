JORDAN

What to wear in Jordan

Conservative but tolerant — Jordan is comfortable with Western tourist dress in most contexts, but a few situations (mosques, downtown souks, rural areas) reward modest choices. Here's the practical guide by situation.

Last reviewed June 5, 2026 · 6-minute read · See our editorial standards

Jordan's dress code question gets more attention than it deserves. The country is conservative by Western standards but extremely tolerant of tourist dress by Middle Eastern standards — visitors in shorts and t-shirts walking through Petra or Amman attract no negative attention. The few situations that reward more conservative choices (mosques, the deep downtown souks, rural villages) are well-defined and easy to plan around. The practical framing: dress comfortably for the climate and activity, with a few specific accommodations at specific moments.

The general rule

Jordan is conservative in private and traditional spaces, comfortable with Western dress in tourist and modern spaces.

Conservative situations (cover shoulders, knees, and avoid tight clothing):

  • Religious sites (mosques, active churches)
  • Downtown Amman souks (Al-Balad)
  • Rural Bedouin villages outside tourist areas
  • Local markets in smaller cities (Karak, Madaba town centers)

Relaxed situations (Western dress is fine):

  • Petra archaeological site and Wadi Musa
  • Wadi Rum (camp areas and jeep tours)
  • Modern Amman neighborhoods (Jabal Amman, Abdoun, Rainbow Street)
  • International hotel restaurants and bars
  • Resort properties and Aqaba beach areas

For women

Works everywhere: T-shirts, light long-sleeved tops, knee-length or longer skirts and dresses, long pants, leggings under tunics, comfortable walking shoes, a lightweight scarf for mosque visits. A loose linen or cotton long-sleeved shirt is the most versatile single item.

Works in most situations but not all: Sleeveless tops, mid-thigh shorts, mini-skirts. Fine at resort properties and modern Amman neighborhoods. Less appropriate in downtown Amman souks, rural areas, or religious site approaches. Carrying a light scarf or wrap solves most situations.

Required at mosques: Hair covered with a scarf, shoulders covered, legs covered to ankles, no tight clothing, no shoes inside. Major Amman mosques (King Abdullah I Mosque, King Hussein Mosque) provide robes for women visitors who haven't dressed appropriately.

At churches (St. George's in Madaba, Mount Nebo): less strict. Covering shoulders is appreciated, but hair covering isn't required and modest shorts are usually acceptable.

At Petra: Hiking-practical clothing — performance fabrics, sun-protective long sleeves, hiking pants or running shorts, sturdy footwear. Yoga pants, athletic shorts, and sports tops all work without attracting negative attention.

At Wadi Rum: Bedouin camps are comfortable with Western dress. T-shirts, long pants (the desert is cooler at night), light layers. A scarf is useful for sun protection and dust during jeep tours.

At resorts and beaches: Standard Western swimwear is fine at the Dead Sea and Aqaba resort beaches. At public beaches, one-piece swimwear feels more contextually appropriate. Cover up with a sarong when walking to the bar/restaurant area.

For men

Works everywhere: T-shirts, polo shirts, short-sleeved button-downs, long pants, knee-length shorts in tourist contexts, hiking pants, walking shoes. Linen or lightweight cotton works for the climate.

Works in most situations but not all: Mid-thigh shorts — fine at Petra, Wadi Rum, and resort areas; less appropriate in downtown Amman souks or for visiting mosques.

What to avoid: Tank tops or sleeveless shirts outside resort pool areas. Very short shorts (above mid-thigh). Shirts with offensive imagery. Beach attire (swim trunks, no shirt) anywhere except the actual beach or pool.

At religious sites: Mosques — long pants required, no shorts, modest shirt with sleeves, shoes removed. Churches — long pants preferred, knee-length shorts usually acceptable.

At Petra and Wadi Rum: Long-sleeved sun-protective shirts work better than t-shirts for full-day exposure. Lightweight long pants beat shorts for trail wear. Sun hat, solid hiking shoes.

Specific situations

Ramadan (March-April in 2026): Slightly more modest dress is appreciated everywhere outside resort properties. Avoid eating, drinking, or smoking in public during daylight hours regardless of dress — this matters more than clothing for cultural respect.

Downtown Amman souks: Working markets, not tourist-oriented. Modest dress (covered shoulders and knees) is appropriate. Photography of people requires permission.

Bedouin camp evenings: Warm layers are essential — Wadi Rum gets genuinely cold at night, particularly October-March. Long pants, fleece or light jacket, closed-toe shoes for walking around camp.

Border crossings: No dress code, but modest dress speeds things slightly. Long pants and a collared shirt is a sensible choice.

Hiking trails (Monastery, Al-Khubtha, Jebel Haroun): Pure practicality. Sun-protective long sleeves are the single most important item. Brimmed hat. Trail shoes with grip.

What to pack — the minimal wardrobe

For a 7-day Jordan trip:

For women: 3-4 t-shirts or light tops, 1-2 long-sleeved sun-protective shirts, 2 pairs of long pants or hiking pants, 1 pair of leggings, 1 modest skirt or dress (optional), 1 lightweight scarf, 1 sun hat, trail shoes, sandals, swimwear, 1 light jacket for Wadi Rum evenings.

For men: 3-4 t-shirts, 1-2 long-sleeved sun-protective shirts, 2 pairs of long pants, 1 pair of knee-length shorts (optional), 1 sun hat, trail shoes, sandals, swimwear, 1 light jacket.

Most travelers pack more than they wear. Jordan trips don't require elaborate wardrobes — comfort, sun protection, and a single modest-dress option cover all situations.

Frequently asked questions

Is Jordan strict about dress code?
Conservative-but-tolerant is the right framing. Jordan is a Muslim-majority country with traditional values, but the tourism industry is well-established and Jordanians are accustomed to seeing visitors in Western clothing. Modesty is appreciated and signals respect; rigid adherence to traditional dress isn't expected. Dress comfortably and modestly, with attention to specific situations (religious sites, downtown souks, rural areas).
What should women wear?
For general sightseeing — t-shirts, long pants or knee-length skirts, comfortable walking shoes. Avoid shorts above mid-thigh and sleeveless tops in downtown Amman souks or rural areas (fine at resorts and in tourist zones). Carry a lightweight scarf for mosque visits. At Petra and Wadi Rum, hiking pants and t-shirts are completely appropriate.
What should men wear?
Less restrictive than for women. Long pants or knee-length shorts are fine in most situations. T-shirts and short-sleeved button-downs work everywhere. Skip tank tops and sleeveless shirts in conservative areas. At religious sites, long pants are required.
What about visiting a mosque?
Both genders need to cover. Women: hair covered with a scarf, shoulders covered, legs covered to ankles, no tight clothing. Men: long pants, no shorts, modest shirt with sleeves. Both remove shoes at entrance. Major Amman mosques provide robes for visitors who haven't dressed appropriately.
What's appropriate at Petra specifically?
Hiking-practical clothing — t-shirts, hiking pants or comfortable shorts, sturdy walking shoes, sun hat. Modesty matters less than at religious sites or in working markets. The main practical considerations are sun protection and footwear. Women wearing leggings, sports shorts, or athletic wear get no negative attention at Petra.
What about beach and swimwear?
At resort beaches (Mövenpick Dead Sea, Hilton, Marriott, Kempinski) and Aqaba resort areas, regular Western swimwear is fine. At public beaches and more conservative areas, modest swimwear (one-piece for women, knee-length swim shorts for men) feels more comfortable. Cover up with a sarong or t-shirt when leaving the beach.

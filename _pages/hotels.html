HOTELS

Where to stay in Petra

Wadi Musa is the town outside Petra, and where you sleep matters more than most travelers realize. The right hotel saves you 40 JOD on entry, an hour of taxi rides, and the difference between a 06:00 arrival at the Treasury and a 10:30 one.

Last reviewed April 28, 2026 · 12-minute read · See our editorial standards

Almost every guide to Petra treats accommodation as an afterthought — stay somewhere in Wadi Musa, the advice usually goes. That advice is technically correct and practically useless. Wadi Musa is a small town, but where you sleep within it determines two things that genuinely affect your trip: how easily you can get inside the gate at 06:00 (the only time of day the Treasury is yours), and whether you pay the 50 JOD or the 90 JOD entrance fee. Wadi Musa hotels also vary wildly in quality at a given price point — there are $80 hotels here that feel like $40 hotels, and $40 ones that punch well above their weight.

This page is the honest version. We've broken Wadi Musa down by area (Visitor Centre strip, town centre, Tayba ridge), pulled out the properties that consistently deliver at each tier, and flagged the ones that look good in photos but disappoint in person. Each area and individual hotel review links to its own page with the practical specifics — walk time to the gate, breakfast notes, the WiFi situation, and what we'd actually book versus what we'd recommend skipping.

By budget

The other useful way to think about Wadi Musa hotels is by price tier. The town runs the full range from $25 hostels to $300+ resort suites, but the value clusters at specific price points. Knowing where those clusters sit is the easiest way to filter the field — and to avoid the dead zone in the $90–$120 range where prices climb but quality doesn't.

Under $50 USD per night. Hostels, guesthouses, and budget hotels in the town centre. Rooms are simple, breakfast is often a roll and instant coffee, but the operators are usually welcoming and the value is real. Best for solo travelers and backpackers who care about price more than comfort.

$60–90 USD per night. The mid-range sweet spot. Hotels like Petra Moon Hotel and Petra Canyon Hotel deliver clean rooms, real breakfast, and walking distance to either the gate or the town centre. This is where most travelers should look first.

$120–180 USD per night. Mid-luxury. The Petra Guest House (managed by Crowne Plaza, the second-closest hotel to the gate) and the better 4-star properties on the Tayba ridge sit here. You're paying for either location or view, sometimes both. Worth it for the Petra Guest House specifically because of the 5-minute walk.

$200+ USD per night. Luxury. The Mövenpick Resort Petra at the gate ($180–260), the Petra Marriott on the ridge ($200–320), and the Mövenpick Nabatean Castle (further out, $220–300). All three are 5-star properties; the Mövenpick at the gate is the only one where the premium buys you something Petra-specific (the 50-meter walk to the entrance).

The hotels worth a dedicated review

A handful of properties in Wadi Musa get the volume of bookings and the volume of confusion that they merit individual deep-dive reviews. The Mövenpick Resort Petra and the Petra Guest House are the two closest hotels to the gate and the most-searched options on the site. The Petra Marriott is the most-searched luxury option but the location confusion (it's not at the gate, despite what some photographs suggest) sends a lot of travelers down the wrong path. The Petra Moon Hotel is the most-booked mid-range option and worth its own review because the value is genuinely above what the photos communicate.

Frequently asked questions

Should I stay at Petra (Wadi Musa) or do a day trip?
Stay at least one night in Wadi Musa if you can possibly manage it. The two reasons are real. First, the Petra entrance fee is 50 JOD for visitors who stay overnight in Jordan and 90 JOD for visitors who don't — a 40 JOD per-person savings that more than covers a budget hotel room. Second, overnight stays let you enter the site at 06:00 when it is genuinely yours, instead of arriving at 10:30 with the day-trip buses.
What's the best area to stay near Petra?
Three useful areas. The Petra Visitor Centre area (the strip immediately outside the main gate) is unbeatable for convenience — you can walk to the gate in 2–5 minutes and you'll be inside before the first tour buses arrive. The town centre of Wadi Musa, about 1–2 km uphill from the gate, is where the budget options and the local restaurants concentrate. The Tayba area further out is where the luxury hotels sit on a ridge with sweeping views but a 10-minute taxi ride to the gate.
How much do hotels in Petra cost?
A wide range. Hostels and basic guesthouses in Wadi Musa town start around $25–45 USD per night in low season and $40–70 USD in high season. Mid-range hotels with breakfast and a pool run $80–150 USD per night. The Mövenpick Resort Petra at the main gate is typically $180–260 USD per night and the Petra Marriott on the ridge is $200–320 USD. Prices spike during Easter, Christmas/New Year, and the spring and autumn shoulder peaks — book those 2–3 months ahead.
Is the Mövenpick Resort Petra worth the premium price?
For most travelers, yes — but only because of the location. The Mövenpick sits 50 meters from the Petra Visitor Centre, which means you can wake up, eat breakfast on the terrace, and be inside the gate at 06:00 with no logistics. The hotel itself is a solid 5-star but not extraordinary; you're paying for the address. If you can't justify the premium, the Petra Guest House (Crowne Plaza-managed) is the second-closest property to the gate and roughly half the price.
What about Bedouin camps and unique accommodations?
They're a legitimately different experience and worth considering for one of your two nights. Most of the camps are in Wadi Rum (a 1.5–2 hour drive south), not at Petra itself — Wadi Rum is where the sleep-under-the-stars desert experience lives. Around Petra, the closest equivalent is Little Petra Bedouin Camp, near the Siq al-Barid entrance. A common itinerary that works well: one or two nights in Wadi Musa to do Petra, one night in a Wadi Rum camp on the way south.
When should I book my Petra hotel?
For spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) high season, book 2–3 months ahead. The best-located hotels — Mövenpick, Petra Guest House, Petra Marriott — sell out for Easter and the autumn shoulder peak. For summer and winter, 2–4 weeks ahead is usually sufficient. The cheapest rates almost always come from booking direct or through Booking.com with the free-cancellation flexibility, which lets you re-price if rates drop closer to your dates.

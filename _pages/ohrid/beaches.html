Lake Ohrid Beaches

Twenty-meter visibility, gravel coves and a fishing village they call the Macedonian Saint-Tropez.

HomeLake OhridLake Ohrid Beaches

Lake Ohrid's swimming is unlike anywhere else in the Balkans: water filtered through underground springs to a clarity that regularly exceeds twenty meters, over three hundred meters deep at the center, and ringed by mountains on every horizon. The "beaches" are mostly gravel coves and platforms rather than sand — the price of that transparency — and the best of them are strung down the eastern shore between Ohrid town and St. Naum.

In and around town

You can swim within ten minutes of the old town. The Kaneo platforms below the famous church are the iconic dip — ladders off the rocks, tavernas at your back, the cliff walk home. East of the harbor, the town beaches along the boulevard are convenient but busiest; they suit a quick cool-off more than a beach day. Water quality is good throughout — the lake is one of Europe's cleanest.

The eastern shore run

The shore road south is effectively a beach menu. Lagadin and Peštani have the organized lidos — loungers, bars, watersports in season. Gradište, by the campsite, is the young crowd's pick with beach bars and the lake's best summer energy. Then comes Trpejca: a white-pebble crescent beneath a tumbling fishing village, turquoise shallows, taverna trout at the waterline — the "Macedonian Saint-Tropez" tag oversells the glamour and undersells the beauty. It is the lake's loveliest swim, and arriving before noon in August matters.

St. Naum and the far south

At the southern tip, the St. Naum beaches add the surreal backdrop of the springs — the water nearby runs noticeably colder where the lake is being born — plus loungers in season (around 140 MKD) and the monastery for shade-break culture. Across the border, the Albanian shore continues the same water at even lower prices, an easy extension for those with wheels and a passport.

Practical notes

Bring water shoes (gravel), cash for loungers and beach bars, and sun cover — shade is scarce on the natural coves. The local bus down the shore road serves every beach named here, making a no-car beach crawl entirely doable: swim Trpejca in the morning, lunch in Peštani, finish at Gradište. The season runs roughly mid-June to mid-September; outside it you'll have the coves to yourself and the lake will remind you it's fed by mountain springs.

The beaches, sorted by what you want

Lake Ohrid's shore offers very different beach days. In Ohrid town, small pebble and concrete-platform beaches sit right below the old town — convenient, walkable, busy. The Kaneo coves under the church are the prettiest near the center. Heading south down the eastern shore, the resort strip around Lagadin, Gradište and Ljubaništa brings longer pebble beaches, loungers, beach bars and the campsites, with the water cleaner and clearer the further from town you go. The St. Naum beaches at the far south are the best swimming on the lake — clear, cold, spring-fed — and pair with the monastery. On the western shore, Struga has its own town beaches where the Black Drim leaves the lake.

Getting to them, and when

The eastern-shore beaches are strung along the Ohrid–St. Naum road, served by the same cheap shore buses and easily reached by car, taxi or even the harbor boats. Town beaches need no transport at all. Swimming season is roughly June through September, peaking in July–August when the water is warmest (low-to-mid 20s°C) — but that's also when the resort strip is busiest with regional holidaymakers, so go early or pick a quieter southern cove. The lake is exceptionally clean (it's a UNESCO-protected ancient lake), the water clarity is remarkable, and unlike the sea there's no salt, tide or jellyfish — just cold, clear, fresh water against a mountain backdrop.

Frequently asked questions

When is Lake Ohrid warm enough to swim?

Comfortably from mid-June to mid-September, peaking around 24°C in late July–August. The lake is spring-fed and deep, so it stays brisker than the air suggests — and the clarity makes even a cold dip worth it.

Are Lake Ohrid beaches sandy?

Mostly no — expect fine gravel and pebbles, which is exactly why the water stays so clear. Water shoes earn their keep. A few organized beaches truck in sand; St. Naum has the most natural sandy patches.

Which is the best beach on Lake Ohrid?

For scenery, Trpejca's crescent bay. For convenience, the Kaneo platforms minutes from the old town. For a full beach day with facilities, Gradište or the lidos toward Lagadin. For combining with sightseeing, St. Naum.

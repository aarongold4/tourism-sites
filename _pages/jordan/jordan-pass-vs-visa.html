JORDAN PASS

Jordan Pass vs paying the visa fee

The decision is simpler than it looks. If you're visiting Petra and staying at least 2 nights, the Jordan Pass almost always wins. If you're transiting Jordan for under 2 nights or skipping Petra entirely, pay the visa separately. Here's the math.

Last reviewed April 28, 2026 · 6-minute read · See our editorial standards

The Jordan Pass versus visa decision is one of the most frequent questions for first-time Jordan visitors, and the math is simpler than the various tourism sites make it appear. The break-even point is essentially: are you visiting Petra plus anything else, and staying at least 2 nights? If yes, the Jordan Pass wins on cost. If no, paying the visa fee separately is cheaper. This page works through the specific numbers so you can make the decision with confidence.

The headline math

The basic comparison, in JOD, for a single adult traveler:

Paying separately

  • Jordan visa-on-arrival: 40 JOD (airports), 60 JOD (Wadi Araba border)
  • Petra entrance (overnight visitors): 50 JOD (1 day), 55 JOD (2 days), 60 JOD (3 days)
  • Total minimum cost (visa + 1-day Petra): 90 JOD
  • Total maximum cost (Wadi Araba + 3-day Petra): 120 JOD

With the Jordan Pass

  • Wanderer (1-day Petra): 70 JOD
  • Explorer (2-day Petra): 75 JOD
  • Expert (3-day Petra): 80 JOD
  • All tiers include the visa fee waiver (saving 40-60 JOD)
  • All tiers include entry to 40+ other attractions

The savings

For a typical airport-arriving traveler visiting Petra for 2 days:

  • Separately: 40 JOD visa + 55 JOD 2-day Petra = 95 JOD
  • Jordan Pass Explorer: 75 JOD
  • Savings: 20 JOD (~$28 USD)

For a Wadi Araba border crosser visiting Petra for 2 days:

  • Separately: 60 JOD visa + 55 JOD 2-day Petra = 115 JOD
  • Jordan Pass Explorer: 75 JOD
  • Savings: 40 JOD (~$56 USD)

For a 3-day Petra visit with the Expert tier, savings rise by another 5 JOD per traveler. For a family of four with two adults plus two children (kids under 12 are free at Petra), savings compound proportionally — typically 80-120 JOD per family at airport arrival, more at Wadi Araba.

The Jordan Pass also includes other attractions

Beyond the visa waiver and Petra entry, the Jordan Pass includes free entry to 40+ other attractions across Jordan. For travelers doing the standard Jordan loop, the realistic additional value is:

  • Jerash: 10 JOD entry, included free
  • Amman Citadel: 3 JOD entry, included free
  • Wadi Rum protected area entrance: 5 JOD, included free
  • Mt Nebo: 3 JOD, included free
  • Madaba Archaeological Museum: 3 JOD, included free
  • Karak Castle: 3 JOD, included free
  • Ajloun Castle: 3 JOD, included free
  • And several dozen more smaller sites

A traveler doing the full Jordan loop will typically use 4-6 of these, adding another 15-25 JOD of value on top of the Petra and visa savings. The total practical value of an Explorer or Expert tier Pass, for a comprehensive Jordan trip, often exceeds 100 JOD against a 75-80 JOD purchase price.

When paying separately is better

Three specific scenarios where the Jordan Pass doesn't pay off:

1. You're staying fewer than 2 nights. The visa fee waiver only applies for stays of 2 nights or longer (verified at exit). If your trip is a short layover or a quick day trip, the Pass becomes essentially a Petra ticket with bundled extras you may not use.

2. You're not visiting Petra at all. If your trip focuses on Wadi Rum, the Dead Sea, or Amman without Petra, the Pass cost (70-80 JOD) outweighs the typical attractions you'd visit. In that case, pay the 40 JOD visa and the individual attraction fees as you go.

3. Your nationality requires pre-approval e-visa. The Jordan Pass only waives the visa fee, not the underlying eligibility requirement. Travelers whose nationalities require pre-approved e-visas (some Asian and African countries) still need to apply through the Ministry of Interior portal, and the Pass doesn't change that.

Wadi Araba special rule

One important exception: at the Wadi Araba border crossing only, the visa fee is automatically waived if you stay at least 2 nights in Jordan and exit from the same Wadi Araba border — even without a Jordan Pass. This is an official policy of the Aqaba Special Economic Zone and is documented on the jordanpass.jo FAQ.

In practice, this rarely changes the math: you'd still buy the Jordan Pass to get Petra entry covered, which is the larger cost. But for travelers who specifically aren't visiting Petra and are crossing only at Wadi Araba round-trip with 2+ nights in Aqaba, the visa fee can be avoided without buying the Pass.

Which tier to buy

The Jordan Pass comes in three tiers, differing only in how many days of Petra entry are included:

  • Jordan Wanderer (70 JOD): 1-day Petra entry. For travelers doing a compressed Petra visit.
  • Jordan Explorer (75 JOD): 2-day Petra entry. The sweet spot for most visitors.
  • Jordan Expert (80 JOD): 3-day Petra entry. For travelers who want extended Petra time.

The marginal cost between tiers is just 5 JOD each — significantly cheaper than buying additional Petra entry days separately. For most travelers, Explorer is the right choice: two days at Petra is the recommended visit length (see the two-day itinerary), and Explorer is just 5 JOD more than Wanderer for the substantial benefit of being able to return on day two.

Practical purchase steps

  1. Confirm your trip dates and Petra duration. Lock in whether you want 1, 2, or 3 days at Petra.
  2. Visit jordanpass.jo — the official government website, the only authorized seller.
  3. Select your tier (Wanderer, Explorer, or Expert) and complete the purchase.
  4. Pay online with a credit or debit card.
  5. Save the QR code sent to your email — print it or have it ready on your phone.
  6. Show the QR code at Jordanian immigration on arrival for the visa waiver.
  7. Show the QR code at Petra and other attractions for free entry.

The Pass is valid for 12 months from purchase and expires 14 days after first use, so don't buy it months in advance unless you're confident about your travel dates. For most travelers, purchasing 3-7 days before departure is the right timing.

The decision tree

Quick summary for the impatient:

  • Visiting Petra + staying 2+ nights → Buy Jordan Pass Explorer (75 JOD)
  • Visiting Petra for 1 day only + staying 2+ nights → Buy Jordan Pass Wanderer (70 JOD)
  • Hiker/photographer doing 3 days at Petra + 2+ nights → Buy Jordan Pass Expert (80 JOD)
  • Day trip from Eilat (less than 2 nights) → Pay visa + Petra separately (130 JOD total)
  • Visiting Jordan without Petra → Pay visa separately (40 JOD)

Frequently asked questions

Should I buy the Jordan Pass or just pay the visa separately?
For travelers staying at least 2 nights and planning to visit Petra, the Jordan Pass is almost always cheaper. The Pass costs 70-80 JOD and includes both the visa fee (otherwise 40 JOD) and Petra entry (otherwise 50-60 JOD for the 1-3 day ticket). The combination saves 20-30 JOD per person and adds 40+ other attractions. For travelers staying fewer than 2 nights or not visiting Petra at all, paying the visa fee separately is cheaper.
What's the exact break-even point?
Roughly 65 JOD of combined visa + attractions. If you're paying 40 JOD for the visa and 50+ JOD for Petra entry, the Jordan Pass at 70 JOD already saves you money. Each additional attraction included in the Pass (Jerash, Wadi Rum protected area, Amman Citadel, Mt Nebo, etc.) adds further value at no marginal cost. The break-even is essentially at "I'm visiting Petra plus anything else."
Which Jordan Pass tier should I choose?
Match the tier to your Petra visit duration: Wanderer (70 JOD) for a one-day Petra visit, Explorer (75 JOD) for two days, Expert (80 JOD) for three days. The marginal cost between tiers is just 5 JOD each, while a two-day Petra ticket bought separately is 5 JOD more than the one-day. For most travelers, Explorer (two-day Petra) hits the sweet spot — the right amount of time at Petra and the highest practical value.
Can I share a Jordan Pass with my family?
No — each Pass is single-use, non-transferable, and tied to the passport-holder. Children under 12 are typically free at Petra entry whether or not the accompanying adult uses a Jordan Pass; the same applies at most other Pass attractions. For older children (12-17) and adults, each person needs their own Pass.
What if I change my plans and only stay one night?
The visa fee waiver does NOT apply — you'll be charged the 40 JOD visa fee at your exit border. The Jordan Pass becomes effectively a (slightly discounted) Petra-plus-attractions bundle rather than a real saving. The 2-night minimum is verified electronically at exit. If your trip changes after you've purchased the Pass, you'll still get the attraction benefits but not the visa waiver.
When should I buy the Jordan Pass?
Before you travel — the Pass must be purchased online before arrival in Jordan for the visa waiver to apply. Buy 3-7 days before departure once you've confirmed your trip dates and Jordan stay length. The Pass is valid for 12 months from purchase but expires 14 days after first use, so don't buy it months in advance unless you're sure of your dates.

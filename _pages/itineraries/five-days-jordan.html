ITINERARIES

Jordan in five days

The focused Jordan trip. Two full days at Petra, one night in Wadi Rum, and a bonus half-day at the Dead Sea or Jerash — without the slower pace of a full week. Here's the day-by-day plan that makes it work.

Last reviewed June 5, 2026 · 11-minute read · See our editorial standards

Five days is the sweet spot for travelers who want a real Jordan experience but can't justify a full week. It's enough time to do Petra and Wadi Rum properly — the two genuine must-sees — plus one bonus destination, with a buffer day for travel. What you give up versus a 7-day trip is depth at Amman and the slower King's Highway scenic route. What you gain is a more focused trip that respects your time and budget.

This itinerary assumes you've flown into Amman (or Aqaba — see the alternative arrival note below), have a rental car or private driver, and have a Jordan Pass Explorer (75 JOD) which covers the visa-on-arrival fee plus two-day Petra entry plus most major attractions. Total budget for a couple traveling mid-range runs $1,400-1,900 USD per person excluding international flights.

The route at a glance

  • Day 1: Arrive Amman or Aqaba. Half-day acclimation, dinner, sleep.
  • Day 2: Drive to Petra (3 hours via Desert Highway). Afternoon orientation walk. Optional Petra by Night.
  • Day 3: Petra Day 1 — Siq, Treasury, Royal Tombs, lower city, Monastery climb.
  • Day 4: Petra Day 2 morning (Al-Khubtha or High Place), drive to Wadi Rum afternoon, overnight in Bedouin camp.
  • Day 5: Sunrise at Wadi Rum. Drive north. Optional half-day at the Dead Sea or Jerash. Fly out from Amman.

Day 1 — Arrive Amman

Land at Queen Alia International Airport (AMM), 35 km south of central Amman. Pick up your rental car if applicable, or take a fixed-rate airport taxi (22 JOD, 30-40 minutes). Check into your hotel — Jabal Amman is the best neighborhood for first-timers, with walking access to Rainbow Street restaurants and a short taxi to downtown.

Afternoon options depending on flight arrival time:

  • Morning arrival: Citadel + Roman Theatre + downtown souks (4-5 hours total). See the Amman page for detail.
  • Afternoon arrival: Lighter — corniche walk, Rainbow Street cafes, early dinner.
  • Late evening arrival: Skip directly to hotel sleep. You\'ll be driving south tomorrow.

Dinner at a Rainbow Street restaurant (Sufra for traditional, Beit Sitti for cooking-class experience) or at one of the downtown places (Hashem for street-food classics). Sleep early — Day 2 has a long drive.

Day 2 — Drive south + Petra orientation

Morning: check out of your Amman hotel by 09:00. Drive south on the Desert Highway (Route 15) — 235 km, about 3 hours non-stop. This is the fast route; the King's Highway scenic alternative adds 2-3 hours and is generally skipped in a 5-day itinerary.

Arrive Wadi Musa by 12:30-13:00. Check into your Petra hotel — Petra Moon Hotel ($70-140) for mid-range value, Mövenpick Resort ($180-400) for at-the-gate convenience, Petra Guest House ($110-160) for the middle ground.

Afternoon: a deliberate orientation walk rather than a full Petra day. Enter Petra around 14:00 (with two days remaining on your ticket, this counts as Day 1 of 2). Walk the Siq, see the Treasury, walk to the Royal Tombs and the lower city. Turn around by 16:30 and walk back. You\'re not climbing the Monastery or doing trails today — that\'s tomorrow. The point is to orient yourself with the site layout in the better afternoon light, save energy, and avoid the early-morning Day 3 wake-up feeling like a punishment.

Evening: dinner in Wadi Musa town. If your Day 2 is Sunday through Thursday, this is when to do Petra by Night (20:30-22:30, 30 JOD separate ticket). Otherwise, early dinner and an early sleep.

Day 3 — Petra Day 1 (the deep visit)

The marquee day. Set an alarm for 05:30 — the early start is non-negotiable for the Treasury experience.

  • 06:00: Enter the gate, walk the Siq
  • 06:50: Treasury in morning light (the plaza is empty)
  • 07:20: Royal Tombs and Street of Facades
  • 08:15: Roman theatre and lower city
  • 09:00: Begin Monastery climb (800 steps, 45-60 minutes)
  • 10:00: Monastery — tea at the cafe, walk to the viewpoint
  • 11:00: Descend to the lower city
  • 11:45: Lunch at Basin Restaurant
  • 13:00-16:30: Slow afternoon walking back — Royal Tombs in afternoon light, Treasury walk-through, Siq return

Evening: dinner in Wadi Musa, early sleep. Pack a small overnight bag for tomorrow's Wadi Rum stay (leave the rest of your luggage in the car or hotel storage). See the two-day Petra itinerary for hour-by-hour detail.

Day 4 — Petra Day 2 morning + Wadi Rum

The transition day. Morning at Petra, afternoon transit to Wadi Rum.

07:00 enter Petra for Day 2. Pick one of the two trail options:

Al-Khubtha trail (recommended for first-timers). 3.5 km round trip up to the Treasury viewpoint from above. 1.5-2 hours total. The famous Treasury-from-above photograph is taken here. Best done in late morning when the Treasury is in shadow with surrounding rock catching light. See the Al-Khubtha trail page.

High Place of Sacrifice with Wadi Farasa descent (harder, more rewarding). 3.5-4 hours total. Climbs to a Nabataean ritual altar on Jebel Madbah, descends through a less-visited canyon past the Lion Monument and Garden Temple. For travelers comfortable with longer hikes.

Both options out by 11:30-12:30. Lunch in Wadi Musa town. Pack your overnight bag for Wadi Rum.

14:00: depart Wadi Musa for Wadi Rum — 100 km, about 2 hours via the Desert Highway. Optional stop at Shobak Castle on the way (45-minute detour, 90-minute visit, free with Jordan Pass) for travelers interested in Crusader-era history.

16:00: Arrive at the Wadi Rum Visitor Centre. Pay the 5 JOD entrance fee (covered by Jordan Pass), meet your camp's pickup vehicle, transfer to the camp by 4WD (30-45 minutes into the desert).

16:30-19:00: Jeep tour through the major rock formations — Lawrence's Spring, Khazali Canyon petroglyphs, the Mushroom Rock, sunset at one of the high viewpoints (Um Sabatah is a common choice).

19:30: Traditional Zarb dinner at camp (meat and vegetables cooked in an underground sand oven). Evening around the fire, Bedouin tea, stargazing. Sleep in a Bedouin tent.

For most travelers, a mid-range camp ($80-150 USD per person, private tent with proper bed and bathroom) is the right tier. See the Wadi Rum page for camp selection.

Day 5 — Wadi Rum sunrise + return north

The final day. Sunrise at Wadi Rum is genuinely worth waking up for — the desert at dawn is one of the trip's strongest visual moments.

06:00-08:00: Sunrise, optional sunrise jeep tour or short hike, breakfast at camp.

09:00: Transfer back to the Visitor Centre. Retrieve your rental car. Begin the drive north.

The Day 5 route depends on your flight time and which bonus destination you want:

Option A — Dead Sea bonus stop (for early evening flights from Amman). Drive from Wadi Rum north on the Desert Highway to the Dead Sea (4 hours direct, or stop in Aqaba for lunch en route adding 1 hour). Arrive Dead Sea by 14:00-15:00. Half-day at a resort with day pass ($50-80 USD per person including beach, pool, lunch). Continue to Queen Alia airport (90 minutes from Dead Sea) for evening flights.

Option B — Jerash bonus stop (for late-evening or next-day departures). Drive north from Wadi Rum to Amman (4 hours via Desert Highway), continue 50 km further north to Jerash for an afternoon visit (2-3 hours on site). Return to Amman or Queen Alia airport. Better for travelers who want Roman archaeology in addition to Petra's Nabataean architecture.

Option C — Skip the bonus and go directly to the airport. For travelers with mid-day departure flights, drive Wadi Rum to AQJ Aqaba airport (1 hour) for European routes, or Wadi Rum to Amman Queen Alia (4-5 hours) for international routes. No bonus stop.

Alternative arrival: Aqaba instead of Amman

For European travelers, flying into Aqaba's King Hussein International Airport (AQJ) instead of Amman flips the itinerary direction. The route becomes: Day 1 arrive Aqaba, Day 2 drive north to Petra (2 hours), Days 3 and 4 same as above, Day 5 return south to Aqaba via Wadi Rum for departure flight. This saves about 6 hours of total ground driving and works particularly well for budget European routes (Wizz Air, Ryanair).

The Day 5 bonus stop changes too — without the Amman return, the Dead Sea is no longer a natural addition. Substitute: an extra dive at Aqaba\'s Cedar Pride wreck (35-50 JOD per dive), an extra half-day at Wadi Rum (sunrise balloon flight, $170-250 USD per person), or a beach afternoon at one of the Aqaba resort day passes.

See the Aqaba page for the AQJ-arrival logistics.

Budget breakdown

For a couple traveling mid-range, total for 5 days runs roughly $2,800-3,800 between two people excluding international flights — or $1,400-1,900 per person.

  • Accommodation: Amman 1 night ($80-130), Wadi Musa 3 nights ($210-450), Wadi Rum camp 1 night ($160-300 for two). Total $450-880.
  • Transport: Rental car 5 days ($150-225) + fuel ($50-70). Total $200-295.
  • Entry fees: Jordan Pass Explorer 75 JOD per person ($105). Petra by Night optional 30 JOD ($42). Total $200-260 for two.
  • Food: $30-50 per day per person. Total $300-500 for two over 5 days.
  • Wadi Rum jeep tour: Usually included in camp fee.
  • Bonus stop: Dead Sea day pass $100-160 for two, or Jerash entry free with Jordan Pass.
  • Tips and miscellaneous: $200-300 buffer.

The 5-day vs 7-day decision

If you have the choice and the time, 7 days produces a meaningfully better trip — adds a full Amman day, the scenic King's Highway with stops at Madaba, Mount Nebo, and Karak Castle, a second Wadi Rum night for the balloon flight, and a slower pace throughout. See Jordan in one week for the full version.

That said, 5 days delivers the essential Jordan experience without compromise on the two destinations that matter most. For travelers using vacation days carefully, 5 days is genuinely sufficient. The trade-off is real but acceptable.

Frequently asked questions

Is five days enough for Jordan?
Yes, if you focus and accept some trade-offs. Five days covers the two highest-priority destinations (Petra and Wadi Rum) with proper attention, plus a half-day at one bonus destination (typically the Dead Sea or Jerash). What you give up versus a 7-day trip: a full Amman exploration, the slower King's Highway scenic route, the second Wadi Rum night, and a Dead Sea overnight. For travelers whose primary interest is Petra plus the desert experience, 5 days is genuinely sufficient.
Should I fly into Amman or Aqaba for a 5-day trip?
For European travelers, Aqaba is often the smarter choice — flying into AQJ saves about 6 hours of total ground transport (3 hours each way to and from Amman). For North American travelers, you'll typically fly through Amman regardless because direct flights to Aqaba from outside Europe are limited. Either way, see whether you can do an open-jaw ticket (in via one airport, out via the other) — this lets you do the Jordan loop linearly without backtracking.
Can I do this without a rental car?
Yes, but with compromises. JETT bus connects Amman → Petra → Wadi Rum → Aqaba on a fixed daily schedule, which works for the linear southbound version of the trip but limits your flexibility. Private transfers between the same destinations cost $80-120 USD per leg, which adds up over the trip. Most 5-day travelers benefit from either renting a car (~$150-200 total for the trip) or hiring a private driver for the full trip (~$400-600 USD for the whole 5 days, often a better deal for groups of 3-4).
What should I skip if my flight times compress this to 4 days?
Skip the Dead Sea or the Jerash visit, not the Petra or Wadi Rum portions. A 4-day version: arrive day 1 in Amman, drive south to Petra (use Desert Highway, fastest), 2 full days at Petra, drive to Wadi Rum on day 3 afternoon, overnight in Bedouin camp, depart day 4 morning from Wadi Rum to Aqaba or back to Amman. Compresses but covers the must-sees.
How much should I budget?
For a couple traveling mid-range, $1,400-1,900 USD per person for the 5 days excluding international flights. This breaks down: accommodation $450-650, transport $200-300, Jordan Pass and entrance fees $110-130, food $150-225, Wadi Rum camp $80-150 per person, miscellaneous and tours $200-300. Budget travelers can do the same trip for $850-1,200 per person; luxury travelers easily $2,800-4,000+.
Should I do Petra by Night in a 5-day trip?
If your Petra Day 1 falls on a Sunday through Thursday, yes — slot it in between Day 1 and Day 2 of Petra. The candle-lit Siq walk and Treasury plaza performance is a genuinely distinctive 90 minutes that doesn't happen anywhere else. 30 JOD separate ticket (not included in Jordan Pass). For travelers whose schedule doesn't overlap with Sun-Thu nights, no real loss — the daytime experience is the main event.

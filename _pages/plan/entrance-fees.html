ENTRANCE FEES

Petra entrance fees explained

50, 55, or 60 JOD for one, two, or three days — if you're staying overnight in Jordan. 90 JOD if you're not. This is the single most important fee distinction at Petra and the one most often missed.

Last reviewed April 28, 2026 · 6-minute read · See our editorial standards

Petra has the most complicated entrance fee structure of any major Middle Eastern archaeological site, and the complication is intentional: the Petra Development and Tourism Region Authority has built incentives directly into the pricing to encourage overnight stays in Wadi Musa rather than day trips from Israel. The result is a four-tier price structure that catches a meaningful share of first-time visitors off guard, especially those crossing from Eilat for a day visit.

This page walks through what you actually pay, why the system works the way it does, and the simplest ways to avoid paying more than you need to.

The current 2026 rates

For visitors staying overnight in Jordan (the typical case):

  • One-day ticket: 50 JOD (~$70 USD)
  • Two-day ticket: 55 JOD (~$78 USD)
  • Three-day ticket: 60 JOD (~$85 USD)

For visitors not staying overnight in Jordan (mostly day-trippers from Eilat):

  • One-day ticket: 90 JOD (~$128 USD)

Children under 12 are free when accompanied by a paying adult. The Petra Visitor Centre accepts cash (JOD) and credit cards. There is no charge for parking at the Visitor Centre.

The 90 JOD trap, explained

The 40 JOD difference between the overnight rate (50 JOD) and the non-accommodated rate (90 JOD) is the single most impactful pricing decision at Petra. The mechanic: when you arrive at the ticket booth, you'll be asked whether you're staying overnight in Jordan. If you say yes, you'll pay the lower rate; the staff may ask to see proof (a hotel booking confirmation, typically). If you say no — or if you're obviously a day-tripper from Eilat — you'll pay the higher rate.

The policy exists because Petra is one of Jordan's largest sources of tourism revenue, and the government has every incentive to encourage visitors to spend nights in Jordan rather than day-tripping in and out across the Israeli border. The 40 JOD difference reliably covers a budget Wadi Musa hotel for two travelers ($55–80 USD/night for a basic room with breakfast), which means the math nearly always favors overnighting unless you specifically cannot stay for logistical reasons.

The Jordan Pass: better than buying separately

For almost any traveler doing both the visa-on-arrival and Petra, the Jordan Pass is the cheaper option. The pass bundles your visa fee (normally 40 JOD) plus Petra entry plus 40+ other sites at three tier prices:

  • Jordan Wanderer (70 JOD): includes one-day Petra entry
  • Jordan Explorer (75 JOD): includes two-day Petra entry
  • Jordan Expert (80 JOD): includes three-day Petra entry

The math: a one-day Petra visit with a Jordan Wanderer pass costs 70 JOD total, versus 50 JOD Petra + 40 JOD visa = 90 JOD paid separately. The pass saves you 20 JOD. The two-day Explorer (75 JOD versus 55 + 40 = 95 JOD separately) saves you 20 JOD. The three-day Expert (80 versus 60 + 40 = 100 JOD separately) saves another 20 JOD. The savings compound further when you visit the other sites the pass includes — Jerash, the Citadel in Amman, Wadi Rum, the Roman theatre. For any multi-stop Jordan trip, the Jordan Pass is mathematically the right choice.

The single catch: the visa waiver only applies if you stay at least two nights in Jordan. One-night transits don't qualify, and the visa fee gets charged at the airport regardless. Petra-only weekend trips need to be three days / two nights minimum for the Jordan Pass math to work.

What the ticket includes

The standard Petra entrance ticket covers entry to the Petra Archaeological Park for the number of days printed on it. You get unlimited entries and exits during opening hours (06:00 to ~17:30 in summer, 06:00 to ~16:00 in winter). The ticket includes:

  • Access to the entire archaeological park: the Siq, the Treasury, the lower city, the Royal Tombs, the Monastery trail, the High Place of Sacrifice, the Wadi Farasa route, Little Petra (separate entrance), and all marked trails
  • Access to the Petra Archaeological Museum at the Visitor Centre
  • Free shuttle from the Visitor Centre to the Siq entrance (a 5-minute walk, but available if you need it)

The ticket does NOT include:

  • Petra by Night — separate 30 JOD ticket, available only at the Visitor Centre on the day of the event
  • Guides — licensed Petra guides start at 50 JOD for a 2-hour group tour
  • Horse or donkey rides — independently operated, negotiate prices on the spot (typical: 5 JOD for the short horse ride from the entrance to the Siq; 15–20 JOD for the donkey ride to the Monastery)

The decision tree

If you're flying into Jordan and staying 2+ nights: Buy the Jordan Pass before you arrive. It saves money on every dimension and is the simplest option.

If you're flying into Jordan and staying 1 night (Petra-only weekend): Skip the Jordan Pass (the visa waiver requires 2 nights minimum) and pay separately — 40 JOD visa + 50 JOD one-day Petra = 90 JOD per person.

If you're crossing from Israel for a day trip: Pay the 90 JOD non-accommodated rate. Or — better — extend to one night in Wadi Musa, pay the 50 JOD overnight rate, and end up ahead overall.

If you're combining Jordan with Israel for 5+ days: Get the Jordan Pass, stay 2+ nights in Jordan (Petra + Wadi Rum or Amman), use the visa waiver. This is the cleanest math of any cross-border itinerary.

Frequently asked questions

How much is Petra entrance?
For visitors staying overnight in Jordan, the standard rates are: 50 JOD for a one-day ticket, 55 JOD for a two-day ticket, 60 JOD for a three-day ticket. For visitors not staying overnight in Jordan (typically day-trippers from Israel via the Eilat border), the one-day ticket is 90 JOD — almost double. This is the single most important fee fact about Petra and the one most often missed in trip planning.
What's the 90 JOD trap?
The Petra authority charges different rates depending on whether you're staying overnight in Jordan or not. To pay the lower 50 JOD rate, you must show evidence (a hotel booking, ideally) that you have stayed at least one night in Jordan. Day-trippers from Eilat who cross the border for a single day visit pay 90 JOD. The difference — 40 JOD per person — typically exceeds the cost of a budget Wadi Musa hotel for a couple, which is why we strongly recommend overnighting in Jordan even for short visits.
Are children free?
Children under 12 are free with the Petra entrance ticket when accompanied by a paying adult. The non-accommodated 90 JOD rate also includes free entry for accompanying children under 12. Petra by Night has a separate ticket policy: free for children under 10.
Does the Jordan Pass include Petra?
Yes. The Jordan Pass bundles your visa-on-arrival fee (40 JOD) plus entry to Petra and 40+ other sites at a single bundle price of 70, 75, or 80 JOD depending on tier (Jordan Wanderer, Jordan Explorer, or Jordan Expert, corresponding to 1, 2, or 3 days at Petra). For any traveler doing both the visa and Petra, the Jordan Pass pays for itself the moment you land. Critical caveat: the visa waiver only applies if you stay at least two nights in Jordan.
Can I pay by credit card?
Yes, at the Visitor Centre ticket booth. Cash (Jordanian Dinars) is also accepted. US dollars and euros are not accepted at the ticket booth — exchange your money in advance or use the ATM at the Visitor Centre. If you have a Jordan Pass, you don't need to pay anything at the ticket booth — the pass acts as your entry ticket and you scan it at the gate.
Where do I buy tickets?
On-site at the Petra Visitor Centre, on the day of your visit. Tickets are also available online at petrapass.pdtra.gov.jo, but pre-purchasing doesn't give you a meaningful advantage — there is rarely a queue at the ticket booth, and the gate security check is the same regardless. The exception is during the Easter and autumn shoulder peaks, when occasional queues can form; in those windows, the small convenience of arriving with a Jordan Pass already in hand is worth the small effort to buy it before your trip.

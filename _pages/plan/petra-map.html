The Petra map

The site is essentially a six-kilometer east-west line, with the Visitor Centre at one end and the Monastery at the other. Here's the schematic, the distances, and the walking times that actually matter for planning your day.

Last reviewed April 28, 2026 · 5-minute read · See our editorial standards

Petra is geographically simpler than its scale suggests. The site is essentially a six-kilometer east-west line through the mountains, with one entry point (the Visitor Centre at the east), one canyon (the Siq), one main archaeological zone (the lower city), and one major climb (the Monastery, at the western end). Once you understand the basic axis, the site stops being confusing — and you can make informed decisions about what to fit into a one-day or two-day visit.

This page covers the schematic layout, the walking distances, and the practical routing decisions. For the hour-by-hour itinerary that makes use of these distances, see the two-day itinerary.

The schematic

Schematic only — not to scale. The Petra Visitor Centre paper map (free with your entrance ticket) shows accurate scale and topography.

The east-west axis

From the Visitor Centre, the main path runs west: 800 meters of paved path through the outer site (with the Djinn Blocks and Obelisk Tomb on the way), then 1.2 kilometers through the Siq, emerging at the Treasury plaza 2 kilometers west of the entrance. From the Treasury, the path continues west through the Outer Siq, past the Street of Facades and the Royal Tombs on the right, the Roman theatre on the left, and into the lower city. The Basin Restaurant — the only formal restaurant inside the site — sits 3.5 kilometers from the Visitor Centre, at the foot of the Monastery trail.

The major detours

Three significant climbs branch off this main axis:

The Monastery. The trailhead is behind the Basin Restaurant, 3.5 km from the entrance. The 800-step climb takes 45–60 minutes to ascend, 30–45 minutes to descend. The Monastery itself sits at the top of this climb, roughly 1.5 km of horizontal distance northwest of the Basin Restaurant. Total round-trip walking time from the Basin: 2 to 2.5 hours.

The High Place of Sacrifice. The trailhead is on the right side of the main path just past the Roman theatre, about 2.5 km from the Visitor Centre. The 600-step climb takes 45 minutes to an hour up. From the top, the recommended descent is the Wadi Farasa route, which exits the cliff face back to the colonnaded street rather than retracing the climb. Total loop time: 3 to 4 hours.

The Al-Khubtha trail. The trailhead is signposted past the Royal Tombs on the northeastern cliff. The trail climbs to a Bedouin-run viewpoint directly above the Treasury — this is where the famous "Treasury from above" photographs are taken. 1.5 to 2 hours round trip.

Walking distances and times

Approximate distances from the Visitor Centre, with walking times at an unhurried pace:

  • Siq entrance: 0.8 km, 10 minutes
  • Treasury: 2 km, 30 minutes
  • Royal Tombs: 2.4 km, 40 minutes
  • Roman theatre: 2.7 km, 45 minutes
  • Great Temple: 3.2 km, 55 minutes
  • Basin Restaurant: 3.5 km, 60 minutes
  • Monastery: ~5.5 km via Basin + 800 steps, 2 to 2.5 hours from Visitor Centre
  • High Place of Sacrifice (summit): ~3.5 km via main path + 600 steps, 90 minutes from Visitor Centre

A complete one-day visit covers roughly 12 to 14 km total. A complete two-day visit covers 18 to 22 km across the two days.

The vertical axis

Worth noting that Petra has substantial elevation differences beyond the east-west path. The Visitor Centre sits at about 1,050 meters above sea level. The Treasury is at 920 meters (the Siq descends about 130 meters from the entrance). The lower city is at about 880 meters. The Monastery sits at about 1,050 meters (the same elevation as the Visitor Centre, but reached by an 800-step climb from the lower city). The High Place of Sacrifice is at about 1,025 meters.

The practical implication: most of the walking from the entrance is downhill (which makes the morning walk easier than the afternoon walk back). The Monastery climb is the equivalent of climbing back up from the lower city to the original entrance elevation, plus a bit. The mental model of "downhill into the canyon, climb back up to leave" is roughly accurate.

Where to download a real map

Free paper map. Included with your Petra ticket at the Visitor Centre. Basic but adequate for the main routes.

PDTRA official PDF map. Available at the official Visit Petra website. More detail than the paper map.

Google Maps and Maps.me. Both have reasonable Petra coverage including the main trails and many minor monuments. Download the area offline before entering — cell coverage inside Petra is patchy, particularly on the trails.

Sunny Maps Petra trail map. The most detailed third-party map, sold in Wadi Musa shops and hotels for around 10 JOD. Worth it for serious hikers planning the back routes or Jebel Haroun.

Frequently asked questions

How big is Petra?
The Petra Archaeological Park covers 264 square kilometers in total, but the meaningful visitable area is roughly a 6-kilometer line from the Visitor Centre at the east, through the Siq, past the Treasury, through the lower city, and up to the Monastery at the far west. A typical adult walking visit covers 8 to 12 kilometers depending on whether you climb the Monastery and high-place trails.
Is there a map I can download?
The Petra Visitor Centre hands out a free paper map with your ticket — basic but useful for the main routes. The PDTRA (Petra Development and Tourism Region Authority) publishes a more detailed PDF map on its website. Google Maps and Maps.me both have decent Petra coverage; download offline before entering since cell coverage is patchy. For serious hikers, the Sunny Maps Petra trail map (purchasable in Wadi Musa for 10 JOD) is the most detailed option.
Where exactly is the Treasury in relation to the Visitor Centre?
The Treasury sits 2 kilometers west of the Visitor Centre via the Siq. The walk takes 30-45 minutes: 800 meters of paved path to the Siq entrance, then 1.2 kilometers through the canyon to the Treasury plaza. The path is one-way in practice — you exit through the same Siq you entered.
How far is the Monastery from the Treasury?
The Monastery is about 3 kilometers from the Treasury, accessed via the lower city. The standard route: Treasury → Outer Siq → Street of Facades → Royal Tombs area → lower city colonnaded street → Basin Restaurant → 800-step climb to the Monastery. The total walk from the Visitor Centre to the Monastery is 5 to 6 kilometers, taking 2.5 to 3 hours with the climb.
Where are the Royal Tombs?
On the eastern cliff face of the lower city, just past the end of the Outer Siq. From the Treasury, walk west about 400 meters and the four monumental facades come into view on your right. The carved steps up to the Urn Tomb terrace are the access point. Easy to find — the row of tomb facades is visible from a long distance.
Where is Little Petra in relation to the main site?
Little Petra is 8 kilometers north of the main Petra Visitor Centre, accessed by a separate road. There's no walking route within the archaeological park that connects them; you need a taxi or rental car (15 minutes by road). The back-route trail from Little Petra into the main Petra site exits at the Monastery, but it's a 6 km hike used by experienced walkers, not a standard tourist connection.

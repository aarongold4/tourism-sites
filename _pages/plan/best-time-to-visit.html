WHEN TO GO

Best time to visit Petra

Petra is open year-round, and the experience changes more by month than most travelers expect. The short answer: October is the consensus best month. Here's the month-by-month long answer.

Last reviewed April 28, 2026 · 9-minute read · See our editorial standards

The short answer: October. Daytime highs of 24–28°C (75–82°F), nights cool enough to need a light jacket, almost no rain, the dust of summer has settled, and the autumn shoulder-season crowds are present but not overwhelming. If you can only pick one month to visit Petra and nothing else constrains your timing, pick October — early October if you can manage it. April is a close second.

The longer answer is that Petra sits at 850 meters of elevation in southern Jordan's mountainous desert, which produces wider daily temperature swings than most travelers expect (40–50°F between dawn and afternoon is normal) and a genuine four-season climate compressed into a small region. Summer is brutally hot. Winter is genuinely cold. Spring and autumn are the consensus best — and the most expensive, most crowded times to visit. There's a real case to be made for the off-season too: winter Petra is underrated, summer Petra is hostile but viable if you plan around the heat, and Ramadan is its own quiet rhythm.

This page breaks the year down by month, with what each one is actually like to visit. Read straight through, or skip to the month you're considering.

The high-confidence answer

For most travelers, the right months to visit Petra are March through May and September through November. Within that window, October has the most consistent weather and the longest stretch of comfortable touring days. April is just behind, with the bonus of spring wildflowers in the desert.

The months to avoid if you have the choice are July and August (extreme heat, genuinely dangerous on the trails after mid-morning) and December through early February if you mind cold mornings and the small risk of rain. Neither is strictly "bad" — both have their own appeal — but they require more planning to do well.

Month by month

January

The coldest month. Daytime highs around 13–15°C (55–59°F), overnight lows near freezing, occasional rain (5 days on average with measurable precipitation, ~17mm total — the wettest month of the year). Petra is dramatically quiet. Hotel rates are at their lowest. The light is low and dramatic, ideal for photography. Bring a fleece, a light waterproof, and a hat for the wind in the canyon. Petra by Night runs on a reduced schedule and is occasionally cancelled for cold or rain.

February

Similar to January but slightly warmer (daytime highs 15–17°C / 59–62°F) and with the wildflowers just starting to appear in the surrounding desert. February is the second-quietest month and one of the best for travelers who prioritize having the site to themselves. The risk: this is when Ramadan falls in some years (depending on the lunar calendar), and during Ramadan many Wadi Musa restaurants and some hotel services run reduced hours.

March

The beginning of the spring shoulder peak. Daytime highs climb to 19–21°C (66–70°F), with desert wildflowers (the red anemones around Petra are particularly striking) in their best showing. Crowds build through the month, and prices follow. Late March is when Easter typically falls (variable by year) and Easter week is the single most crowded period of the year. If you can avoid Easter week itself, late March is excellent.

April

The first of the two consensus best months. Daytime highs of 23–25°C (73–77°F), nights cool but comfortable, virtually no rain, the wildflower bloom at its peak. April is when most professional Petra photography is shot. The trade-off is that this is also one of the busiest months, with European school holidays, Easter, and the spring peak overlapping. Book hotels 2–3 months ahead.

May

Still excellent, with rising temperatures (highs 27–29°C / 81–84°F by month's end) and the end of the spring crowds. Late May can be warm enough on the Monastery climb to feel like a stretch. Hotel rates start to drop in the back half of the month as the season transitions toward summer.

June

The transition month into summer. Highs reach 30–32°C (86–90°F), and the trails are noticeably harder than in May. The site is quieter than the spring peak but not yet empty. June is the last reasonable month for active hiking (Monastery, Al-Khubtha, the high places); from July onward, the trails are doable but require predawn starts.

July

Hot. Daytime highs of 32–34°C (90–93°F), UV index of 12 (extreme), no measurable rainfall, and the site is mostly empty of European and American tourists. Hotels are 30–40% cheaper than autumn. The Treasury and Siq remain doable in the morning, but the Monastery climb after 10 AM is genuinely dangerous. If you go in July, plan to be inside the gate by 06:00 and out by noon.

August

The hottest month. Highs of 33–35°C (91–95°F), occasionally higher. The site is at its emptiest of the year. Cruise ships skip Aqaba almost entirely. The advice for July applies more strongly: very early start, very early finish, lots of water, and accept that the Monastery is for tomorrow at sunrise rather than today at noon.

September

The transition month into autumn. Heat eases meaningfully in the second half: early September is still summer-like, but by mid-month the highs drop to 28–30°C (82–86°F) and the trails become comfortable again. Crowds start to build but haven't hit the October peak. A good month for travelers who want shoulder-season conditions without the highest prices.

October

The single best month. Daytime highs of 24–28°C (75–82°F), nights cool, rain effectively zero, longer daylight than spring (~11 hours), and the air is at its clearest. Crowds are real — the autumn shoulder peak runs from late September through early November — but the weather is the most consistent of any month in the year. Hotel rates are at their highest along with April. If you can pick a single month for Petra, this is it.

November

The tail of the autumn shoulder. Highs drop into the upper teens to low 20s (66–72°F), nights are cold, and the first rain of the season arrives toward the end of the month. Late November is one of the best value periods of the year — weather still excellent, crowds noticeably reduced, hotel rates back to mid-season levels.

December

Winter. Daytime highs of 14–16°C (57–61°F), nights below freezing, the small chance of rain or even brief snow flurries (genuinely rare but it has happened). The site is at its quietest of any month except August. Hotel rates are at their lowest of the year. The Christmas/New Year week is an exception — the brief Christmas peak draws Christian pilgrims and brings crowds back briefly. December outside that week is excellent for travelers who don't mind cold mornings.

The two-day-Petra-visit timing answer

For travelers planning a two-day visit specifically — the recommended duration for most people — the right answer is to time your trip so that you have two consecutive days inside the spring or autumn shoulder windows. April, May, September, October, and November are the months where two days inside the site produces the most reliable weather. Avoid scheduling the second day for a Friday in those months (Friday is the day of rest in Jordan and crowds shift to Saturday/Sunday) and avoid Easter week, the autumn shoulder peak weekends, and Jordanian school holidays if you want the quietest experience.

Frequently asked questions

What is the absolute best month to visit Petra?
October. It's consistently rated the best single month: daytime highs around 24–28°C (75–82°F), nighttime lows around 13–16°C (55–60°F), virtually no rainfall, longer daylight than spring, and the autumn shoulder peak crowds haven't fully built yet. April is a very close second — slightly cooler, the desert wildflowers are in bloom, but the Easter peak compresses crowds.
Should I avoid summer?
For most travelers, yes. Petra in July and August sees daytime highs of 33°C+ (91°F+) with a UV index of 12 (extreme), and the canyon and trail surfaces radiate the heat back at you. The Monastery climb in these conditions is genuinely dangerous after 10 AM. That said, summer has two advantages: hotels are 30–50% cheaper than autumn, and the site is much emptier. If you go in summer, plan to be inside the gate at 06:00 and out by 12:00.
Is winter a bad time to visit?
No — it's the underrated season. December and January are genuinely cold (daytime highs 13–15°C / 55–59°F, nights near freezing, occasional rain, very rare light snow) but the site is dramatically less crowded, hotel rates are at their lowest, and the lower light angle is excellent for photography. The catch: Petra by Night runs less reliably in winter, and a few of the Bedouin tea stalls along the trails close. Bring a fleece, a light waterproof, and be ready for cold mornings.
When are the biggest crowds?
Three peaks: Easter week (whenever it falls — major Christian pilgrimage from Europe and Latin America), the autumn shoulder from late September through early November (the consensus best weather), and the spring shoulder from late March through May. The cruise season also matters: large ships dock in Aqaba from October through April and disgorge day-trippers who arrive at the Treasury around 10:30 AM. Visit on a Tuesday or Thursday in low season for the emptiest experience.
When is Ramadan, and does it affect a visit?
Ramadan dates shift each year (in 2026 it falls roughly mid-February to mid-March; in 2027, early February to early March). During Ramadan, daytime hours are quieter, many restaurants in Wadi Musa close until sunset, and Petra by Night runs on adjusted hours or is suspended. The site itself is fully accessible. Ramadan can actually be a good time to visit if you don't mind eating dinner late — the iftar meal at sunset is a genuine cultural experience, and crowds are lower.
Does the weather affect what you can see?
Mostly no — Petra is open in all weather, the site is mostly outdoors, and rain is rare in any month. The single exception is the Siq, which is a slot canyon and the only access point to the lower city. Petra has been closed temporarily during rare flash-flood events (the most recent serious incident was in 2018). If heavy rain is forecast on your visit day — check it in the morning — assume the site may close for a few hours and have a backup plan for Wadi Musa exploration.

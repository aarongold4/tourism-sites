ITINERARIES

Petra in two days

The recommended duration for most travelers — and the version of Petra worth flying internationally for. Day 1 the headline monuments and the Monastery. Day 2 the high-place trails, Little Petra, and the museum. Optionally Petra by Night in between.

Last reviewed April 28, 2026 · 11-minute read · See our editorial standards

Two days is the right answer for most travelers visiting Petra. One day works as a compressed version but skips real attractions; three days is for hikers, photographers, and travelers who specifically want a leisurely visit. Two days gets you the headline monuments, both major climbs (Monastery and one of the high-place trails), Petra by Night if it falls on the right night, Little Petra, the museum, and enough time to actually slow down at the monuments rather than rushing past them.

This itinerary assumes you're staying two nights in Wadi Musa with a two-day Petra ticket (55 JOD) or Jordan Pass Explorer (75 JOD including visa). The hour-by-hour plan below is what we'd recommend to a friend visiting Petra for the first time. Day 1 covers the obvious: the Siq, the Treasury, the Royal Tombs, the lower city, and the Monastery. Day 2 covers the rewarding-but-skippable: a major trail, Little Petra, and the parts of the site you didn't have time for on Day 1.

Day 1 — The headline visit

06:00 — Enter at the gate

Set an alarm. The gate opens at 06:00 and the first 90 minutes inside the site are the only time of day the Treasury is genuinely yours. Walk the 800 meters from the Visitor Centre to the Siq entrance, past the Djinn Blocks and the Obelisk Tomb.

06:20 — Walk the Siq

The 1.2-kilometer canyon walk takes 20–30 minutes. Take it slowly — the Nabataean water channels on the walls, the camel-trader reliefs, and the carved votive niches all reward a closer look. The Siq in early morning is in deep shadow, which makes the Treasury reveal at the end more dramatic.

06:50 — The Treasury, in morning light

The Treasury is in direct east-facing sunlight at this hour. The plaza is largely empty. Spend 30 minutes here — photographs, a quiet look at the facade, a sense of the scale that the photographs never quite capture.

07:20 — Street of Facades and Royal Tombs

Continue past the Treasury into the Outer Siq. The Street of Facades runs along the right side; the Royal Tombs are carved into the eastern cliff on the same side. Climb up to the Urn Tomb terrace, enter the Urn Tomb (look for the 446 CE Christian inscription inside), walk along the row of four tombs. The view back over the lower city from the Urn Tomb terrace is one of the best in Petra. Allow 45 minutes.

08:15 — Roman theatre and lower city

Descend to the Roman theatre — carved directly into the cliff, 4,000-seat capacity, the lower rows accessible. Continue along the colonnaded street past the Great Temple on the left to Qasr al-Bint at the far end. Allow 45 minutes.

09:00 — Begin the Monastery climb

The trailhead is behind the Basin Restaurant. Allow 45–60 minutes for the climb at a steady pace. Three Bedouin tea stalls along the way for water and rest. The Lion Triclinium about two-thirds of the way up is worth a five-minute detour.

10:00 — The Monastery

The Monastery is at the top of the climb. Take tea at the cafe across the terrace (one of the great views in Petra), walk past it along the marked path to the viewpoint for the panorama west toward Wadi Araba. Allow 45 minutes.

11:00 — Descend

30–45 minutes back down to the Basin.

11:45 — Lunch at the Basin Restaurant

The buffet is around 18–20 JOD per person. Take your time — you've earned it. The afternoon will involve more walking, so a real meal helps.

13:00 — Walk back through the lower city

Take the slightly longer route — climb to the upper terrace of the Great Temple for a different angle on the lower city, visit the Byzantine Church to see the surviving mosaic floors, look at the Petra Pool and Garden Complex (recently excavated, partially reconstructed).

14:30 — Royal Tombs in afternoon light

The tombs face west and the afternoon sun brings out their rose-red color in a way the morning shadow never can. This is the visit's photography highlight if you've been pacing the day correctly.

15:30 — Treasury walk-through

The Treasury in late afternoon is in shadow but the plaza is quieter than at any other point in the daytime. Spend 20 minutes — different light, different feel.

16:00 — Slow Siq walk back

You've already noticed the obvious features on the morning walk; on the return walk, look for what you missed. Allow 45 minutes.

16:45 — Exit

Back at the Visitor Centre. If you want, the Petra Archaeological Museum is open until 17:00 — a 30-minute walk-through to see carved sculptures and artifacts.

Evening — Petra by Night (Sun–Thu)

If your Day 1 falls on a Sunday through Thursday, return to the Visitor Centre by 20:15 for Petra by Night. The candle-lit Siq walk to the Treasury (over 2,000 candles), traditional Bedouin music at the plaza, and a 3D projection show on the facade. Tickets are 30 JOD, separate from your daytime ticket. The whole experience runs 20:30 to 22:30. See the Petra by Night page for details.

Day 2 — The deeper visit

06:30 — Enter the site again

A slightly later start than Day 1 — you've earned it, and the Day 2 trails benefit less from the absolute earliest entry. The trade-off is that the Siq and Treasury will be quieter than mid-morning but not as empty as 06:00. Walk down to the trailhead of your chosen Day 2 climb.

The Day 2 choice

Two options. Both work. Pick one based on what you want from the second day.

Option A — Al-Khubtha trail to the Treasury viewpoint. The trail starts past the Royal Tombs (signposted from the main path). 3.5 km round trip up the eastern cliff to a Bedouin-run viewpoint directly above the Treasury. The famous "Treasury from above" photograph is taken here. 1.5 to 2 hours round trip. Best done in late morning when the Treasury is in good light from above.

Option B — High Place of Sacrifice with Wadi Farasa descent. The trail starts on the right side of the main path just past the Roman theatre. 3 km up to the Nabataean ritual altar on Jebel Madbah, then a 90-minute descent through Wadi Farasa past the Lion Monument and the Garden Temple. Total 3.5 to 4 hours. The harder, longer, and arguably more rewarding choice. See the High Place page for details.

We slightly favor Option B for travelers comfortable with longer hikes — the Wadi Farasa loop passes monuments most visitors never see. Option A is the better choice if your priority is the iconic Treasury-from-above photograph.

13:00 — Lunch in Wadi Musa

Exit Petra around 12:30, taxi back to Wadi Musa town, lunch at a local restaurant — Al-Wadi, My Mom's Recipe, or any of the Tourism Street places. The break gives you energy for the afternoon and the local food is genuinely good. Allow 90 minutes.

15:00 — Little Petra

Taxi or short drive to Little Petra (Siq al-Barid), 8 km north of Petra. Free entry. The small slot canyon, the painted Biclinium ceiling (bring a flashlight or use your phone), the Neolithic site at Beidha next door. Allow 90 minutes total. See the Little Petra page.

17:00 — Back to Wadi Musa

Take your time getting back. Dinner in Wadi Musa, early sleep — if you have a Day 3 planned (Wadi Rum, Aqaba, or back to Amman), you'll thank yourself.

What you've now seen

In two days, you've covered the Siq, Treasury, Royal Tombs, Roman theatre, colonnaded street, Great Temple, Byzantine Church, Monastery, either the Al-Khubtha trail or the High Place of Sacrifice with Wadi Farasa loop, Little Petra, and the Beidha Neolithic site. You've experienced the site in morning shadow, midday heat, late-afternoon golden hour, and possibly candle-lit night. You've climbed at least one of the two major trails. You've eaten at the Basin Restaurant. You've had Bedouin tea on the Monastery terrace.

That's the version of Petra most travelers remember as transformative.

Frequently asked questions

Why is two days the right answer for Petra?
Three reasons. First, two days lets you do the Monastery without rushing — the climb takes 2.5–3 hours round trip and seriously cuts into a one-day visit. Second, it lets you experience Petra in different light: morning shadow in the Siq, afternoon sun on the Royal Tombs, golden-hour walk back from the Monastery. Third, the second day at any major archaeological site is qualitatively different from the first — you stop trying to see everything and start actually noticing things.
Do I need to buy a two-day ticket?
Yes. The Petra two-day ticket is 55 JOD for visitors staying overnight in Jordan (just 5 JOD more than the one-day ticket). The Jordan Pass Explorer (75 JOD) includes the two-day Petra ticket plus your visa-on-arrival fee. Either is dramatically cheaper than buying one-day tickets on consecutive days. Make sure to specify "two days" at the ticket booth — the staff will adjust the ticket accordingly.
What should I do on each day?
Day 1: Siq, Treasury, Royal Tombs, lower city, Monastery climb in the afternoon. Day 2: Either the Al-Khubtha trail (Treasury viewpoint from above) or the High Place of Sacrifice with Wadi Farasa loop, plus Little Petra and the museum in the afternoon. The order matters — doing the Monastery on Day 1 means you're fresh for it, and the Day 2 trails benefit from understanding the site layout you absorbed on Day 1.
Should I fit in Petra by Night?
If your visit overlaps with a Sunday through Thursday and you have the energy for an evening walk after a full day on site, yes. Petra by Night is a candle-lit Siq experience (over 2,000 candles) plus a Bedouin music performance and 3D projection show at the Treasury. The walk back through the Siq in candlelight is the part most visitors remember. Tickets are 30 JOD separate from the daytime ticket. Best slotted between Day 1 and Day 2 — late dinner in Wadi Musa, then over to the Visitor Centre by 20:15.
What if it rains?
Rain in Wadi Musa is rare (typically only December through March, ~17mm in the wettest month). Light rain is no obstacle to a Petra visit — the site is open in all weather. Heavy rain is a different matter: the Siq is a slot canyon and has flooded historically (most recently in November 2018, with fatalities). The site authorities will close the Siq if heavy rain is forecast. If you wake to heavy rain, call your hotel desk or check the Petra Authority's social feeds before heading down to the gate.
Can I extend to three days from a two-day plan?
Yes, easily. The Petra three-day ticket is 60 JOD (5 JOD more than the two-day), and the Jordan Pass Expert is 80 JOD (5 JOD more than Explorer). A third day lets you add Jebel Haroun (full-day strenuous hike), Wadi Farasa as a separate destination, the back route from Little Petra to the Monastery, or simply a slower revisit of your favorite monuments.

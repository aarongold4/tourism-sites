Getting There

From Kutaisi Airport (KUT)

Georgia's budget-flight gateway sits in a different city entirely — three and a half hours from the capital, with a transfer economy purpose-built around the arrivals board.

Last reviewed: June 7, 2026

Kutaisi's airport exists because low-cost carriers — Wizz Air above all — built their Georgian operation there, and a complete transfer ecosystem grew around the timetable: coach companies sell seats online matched to specific flights, stage outside arrivals, wait out delays, and run the roughly four-hour highway leg to central Tbilisi drop-offs. The system's maturity is the point — this is not a figure-it-out-on-arrival airport but a book-it-with-the-flight one, and done that way it's among the smoothest budget pipelines in the region.

The decision is arithmetic plus honesty about your own stamina. Add the transfer fare and four hours to the low-cost ticket and compare against a direct TBS fare for your dates; early-booked budget routes from Europe usually still win on money, and lose on the experience of finishing a 6 a.m. flight with a highway afternoon. Families and short-trip travelers should price TBS seriously; long-trip and budget-first travelers barely notice the coach by hour two of Georgian scenery.

The smarter routings

Two upgrades beat the straight transfer. First, break the journey: a night in Kutaisi for Gelati, Prometheus Cave, and the canyons turns dead transit into a chapter — our Georgia cluster makes the case and the counter-case. Second, route the loop: fly into KUT, go west to Batumi first, and return to Tbilisi by the Batumi train, arriving rested instead of road-worn. Either way, the itineraries cluster absorbs the arrival-day reality: plan a gentle first evening, not a 9 a.m. walking tour.

The budget-flight reality

Kutaisi (KUT) exists on most travelers' radar for one reason: it's the hub for Wizz Air and the other low-cost carriers linking Georgia to Europe, often at fares that make the longer transfer worth it. The catch is geography — Kutaisi is roughly 230 km west of Tbilisi, a three-and-a-half to four-hour run — so the airport experience is really a transfer-planning exercise. Coach companies (Georgian Bus is the established one) schedule departures to meet arriving flights and sell seats online in advance; book yours when you book the flight, because capacity is matched to planes and a missed coach means a long wait or an expensive taxi.

The smarter play, if your schedule allows, is to refuse to treat Kutaisi as dead transfer time. A daytime arrival with energy to spare turns the stopover into Gelati Monastery and a canyon before you ever reach the capital; a brutal 4 a.m. budget-flight landing argues for transferring straight through and sleeping. Either way, confirm whether your coach terminates at Tbilisi's Station Square or elsewhere, keep small lari for the fare if buying onboard, and build a buffer — the road is good but long, and stacking a tight onward plan on top of a four-hour transfer is how trips start badly. The train is the civilized way back east once you've seen the west.

Frequently asked questions

How far is Kutaisi Airport from Tbilisi, really?

Roughly three and a half to four hours by road, about 230 kilometers west. The airport is named for Kutaisi but serves the whole country’s budget-flight market — treat it as “Georgia West” rather than a Tbilisi airport with a long driveway.

How does the coach transfer work?

Dedicated transfer companies time coach departures to flight arrivals and sell seats online in advance — book when you book the flight, show the ticket, board outside arrivals. Coaches run to central Tbilisi drop-offs; the system is mature and survives delayed flights by design, since the coach waits for its flight.

Is the budget flight + coach actually worth it?

Run the math honestly: low-cost fare plus transfer cost plus four hours versus a direct TBS fare. For many European routes the total still wins clearly, especially booked early; for others, TBS at a sale fare beats the journey. The non-monetary factor: arriving in Tbilisi at midnight after a four-hour coach is a rough first chapter — weight comfort, not just lari.

Can I make Kutaisi a stop instead of a transfer?

The smart version of this airport: a night or two for Gelati Monastery, Prometheus Cave, and the canyons converts the transfer into a destination — our Kutaisi page weighs it properly. Westbound trips can also pair the airport with Batumi rather than backtracking east.

What about arriving by train from Kutaisi?

Rail links exist but the coach ecosystem is built around the flights and beats it on simplicity. Trains matter more on the Tbilisi–Batumi axis — see the train page.

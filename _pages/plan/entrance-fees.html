Giza entrance fees, tier by tier

One general ticket gets you onto the plateau; everything inside a pyramid costs extra, on a tiered system that confuses almost every first-time visitor. Here's the whole structure at current prices — and the prices change often, which is why this page carries a review date.

Last reviewed June 6, 2026◆9-minute read

Photo: kallerna · CC BY-SA 3.0 · Wikimedia Commons

The single most common point of confusion at Giza is the assumption that one ticket covers everything. It doesn't, and it isn't meant to. The system is tiered: a general admission ticket covers the plateau itself — walking among all three pyramids, the panoramic viewpoint, and the Sphinx enclosure — while each pyramid interior, and a couple of exceptional tombs, require separate tickets on top. Understanding the tiers before you arrive turns a chaotic gate experience into a two-minute transaction.

A second thing to understand: Egypt revises these prices frequently, usually upward, sometimes more than once a year. The figures below were verified against the Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities' official ticketing platform and current on-the-ground reports as of the review date at the top of this page. If that date is more than a few months old, treat the numbers as close rather than exact.

The general admission ticket — 700 EGP

Adult: 700 EGP (roughly $14-15 USD). Student with valid ISIC card: 350 EGP. This is the ticket everyone needs, and for most visitors it's genuinely most of the experience. It covers the full plateau: the exteriors of the Great Pyramid of Khufu, the Pyramid of Khafre, and the Pyramid of Menkaure; the queens' pyramids and the surrounding cemetery fields; the panoramic desert viewpoint where the classic all-pyramids photograph happens; and the Sphinx with its enclosure and the Valley Temple of Khafre beside it.

What it explicitly does not cover, per the Ministry's own ticket terms: the interior of any pyramid, the Tomb of Meresankh III, and the Workers' Cemetery. Those are the add-on tiers below.

The Great Pyramid interior — 1,500 EGP

Adult: 1,500 EGP (roughly $30 USD), with reduced child pricing. This is the headline add-on and the most expensive single ticket on the plateau — more than double the general admission. It buys you the climb through the Great Pyramid's ascending passage and Grand Gallery to the King's Chamber, where Khufu's empty granite sarcophagus sits in a bare, hot, extraordinary room at the heart of the last surviving Wonder of the Ancient World.

Two practical realities govern this ticket. First, daily capacity is limited — on the order of 150 visitors per day — and in peak season (October through April) the allocation can be gone early. If the interior is a priority, buy online in advance or arrive at opening. Second, the climb is physically real: the ascending passage runs at well under standing height, the Grand Gallery is steep, and the interior is hot and airless year-round. Claustrophobia, knee problems, or heat sensitivity are all legitimate reasons to skip it — and the dedicated Great Pyramid guide in our Things to See cluster makes the honest case for and against.

The second pyramid — the rotation

Roughly 200-280 EGP depending on which pyramid is open. The Ministry rotates interior access between the Pyramid of Khafre and the Pyramid of Menkaure — partly for conservation, partly around restoration work — so which one you can enter, and at exactly what price, varies by season. Recent pricing has put Menkaure at around 200 EGP and Khafre at around 280 EGP when each is open; at times only one of the two sells interior tickets at all.

Here's the quietly useful fact: the second-pyramid interior is, for many visitors, the smarter purchase than the Great Pyramid. The passages are similar in character, the chambers comparably atmospheric, the ticket is a fraction of the price, and there's no capacity scramble. If your budget or your knees allow only one interior, it's a genuine toss-up — the Great Pyramid for the name and the Grand Gallery, the second pyramid for value and calm.

The tomb tickets

Tomb of Meresankh III: around 200 EGP. A Fourth Dynasty queen's tomb (listed as "Mars Ankh" on the official ticketing site, a transliteration quirk) with some of the finest preserved relief carving and painted statuary on the plateau. It takes fifteen minutes and is the best small purchase at Giza — if you're not continuing to the tombs of Luxor or Saqqara, it's close to essential.

Workers' Cemetery: around 700 EGP. The burial ground of the pyramid-building crews, south of the main pyramid field — historically fascinating (it's a cornerstone of the evidence that paid laborers, not slaves, built the pyramids), but a specialist purchase at this price. Our History cluster covers what the workers' cemetery proved without the ticket.

How and where to buy

Online, in advance: the Ministry's official platform at egymonuments.com sells dated tickets for the plateau and the interiors. This is the right move in peak season, especially for the capacity-limited Great Pyramid interior.

At the gate: tickets are sold at the main visitors' center entrance (the Mena House side), with card payment the standard — Egypt has been phasing cash out of its major sites. Don't rely on paying cash at the window; do carry small cash for everything outside the ticketed perimeter, where the camel economy runs on it.

One warning that belongs here as much as in the scams guide: the only ticket sellers are the official windows, machines, and website. Anyone approaching you outside the gate offering tickets, "guide-inclusive tickets," or claiming the ticket office is closed is running a routine. The ticket office is not closed.

What's changed recently

Two structural changes are worth knowing even if you read nothing else. First, private vehicles were banned from the plateau in 2025 and replaced with a free hop-on hop-off shuttle system running from the visitors' center around the main sites — a genuine improvement that ended decades of vehicular chaos. Second, payment has gone card-first across Egypt's major antiquities sites. Both changes have made the official layer of a Giza visit notably smoother; the informal layer outside the gates is unchanged, and that's covered in the tout playbook.

Budgeting it all

A reasonable per-person ticket budget: 700 EGP minimum (plateau only, which is a complete visit), around 900-1,000 EGP for the smart mid-tier (plateau + second pyramid interior + Meresankh III), or around 2,400 EGP (~$48 USD) for everything a first-timer plausibly wants (plateau + Great Pyramid + Meresankh III). Saqqara and Dahshur tickets are separate and covered in their own guides.

Frequently asked questions

Can I pay cash at the Giza ticket office?

Egypt has been phasing cash out of its major antiquities sites — card payment is now the standard at official ticket points, and online purchase through the Ministry's egymonuments.com platform is fully supported. Bring a Visa or Mastercard; keep small cash for tips, drinks, and the informal economy outside the gates, where cards buy you nothing.

Do Great Pyramid interior tickets sell out?

Yes, regularly, in peak season (October-April). Daily interior admissions are capped at roughly 150 visitors, and on busy days the allocation goes early. If the interior matters to you, buy online in advance or be at the ticket point at opening. There is no reliable way to get one at noon in December.

Is the Sphinx included in the general ticket?

Yes. The general plateau ticket includes the Sphinx enclosure and viewing area. You cannot enter the Sphinx itself — nobody can — so there is no separate Sphinx ticket to worry about.

Are there student or child discounts?

Students with a valid international student card (ISIC) pay half price on most tickets, including general admission at 350 EGP. Young children typically enter free, and reduced child pricing applies on interior tickets. Bring the physical card — screenshots are routinely refused.

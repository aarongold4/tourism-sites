GETTING THERE

The King's Highway

One of the oldest continuously used trade routes in the world, and Jordan's most spectacular drive. 280 km from Amman south to Petra, with Madaba mosaics, the Wadi Mujib canyon, and two major Crusader castles along the way. Here's how to do the route right.

Last reviewed June 5, 2026 · 10-minute read · See our editorial standards

The King's Highway is Jordan's most rewarding road trip — and one of the most rewarding road trips in the Middle East. The route runs 280 km from Amman south to the Petra area, with major stops at Madaba, Mount Nebo, the Wadi Mujib canyon crossing, Karak Castle, Shobak Castle, and Dana Biosphere Reserve. The drive itself is part of the experience: switchbacking through mountain terrain, descending 1,000+ meters into a canyon and climbing back out, passing through villages that haven't changed much in centuries. For travelers willing to make the King's Highway a full day rather than a transit route, it\'s the right choice.

This page covers what to expect, where to stop, how long each segment takes, and how the King's Highway fits into a wider Jordan trip. The framing throughout: treat this as a sightseeing day with destination stops, not as a way to get from Point A to Point B faster.

The basic decision: King's Highway or Desert Highway?

Jordan has two routes between Amman and the south:

The Desert Highway (Route 15). 235 km, 3 hours non-stop. Divided highway, well-signed, easy driving. This is what the JETT bus and most tours take. Best for travelers prioritizing speed.

The King\'s Highway (Route 35). 280 km, 5-6 hours with stops. Two-lane road with switchbacks, dramatic terrain, multiple major sightseeing stops. Best for travelers who want the journey to be part of the destination.

Our recommendation for most travelers with enough time: take the King\'s Highway one direction (southbound on arrival, with fresh energy for the stops) and the Desert Highway the other (faster return for the trip home or onward to Wadi Rum). This pattern gets you the scenic experience without the time penalty of doing both directions slowly.

The major stops

Madaba (30 km from Amman, 45 minutes)

The first major stop. Madaba is best known for the 6th-century mosaic floor map of the Holy Land at St. George\'s Church — the oldest surviving map of the region, with Jerusalem clearly identifiable at the center. The map is genuinely remarkable: over 2 million pieces of colored stone, depicting the cities, rivers, and major landmarks of the eastern Mediterranean as a Byzantine cartographer understood them in 542-570 CE.

St. George\'s Church is in central Madaba, free entry with Jordan Pass, open to visitors except during services. Beyond the map, Madaba has several other surviving Byzantine mosaics (the Madaba Archaeological Park, the Church of the Apostles) that justify a longer visit for travelers interested in early Christian art. For most travelers, 45-60 minutes at St. George\'s Church is enough.

Mount Nebo (10 km from Madaba)

The traditional site where Moses saw the Promised Land before dying (Deuteronomy 34:1-5). A small Byzantine-era memorial church (rebuilt by the Franciscans in the 20th century) sits at the summit, with surviving 6th-century mosaic floors visible inside. The viewpoint at the summit looks west across the Jordan Valley — on clear days you can see Jericho, the Dead Sea, and the hills of Jerusalem.

Entry is 3 JOD for foreign visitors, paid at the gate (as of Nov 2026). Allow 30-45 minutes. The view is the main draw; the church interior is modest. Worth combining with Madaba as a single morning stop.

Wadi Mujib (60 km from Madaba)

The dramatic canyon crossing — Jordan\'s "Grand Canyon," a 1,000-meter deep gorge that the King\'s Highway descends into and climbs out of via switchbacks. There\'s no formal stopping point for the crossing itself, but several pullouts on both rims offer photograph opportunities. The descent and ascent each take about 20-30 minutes of slow driving.

The adjacent Wadi Mujib Biosphere Reserve (off the King\'s Highway, requires a separate access road) offers serious canyon hiking — the Siq Trail is the most famous, a 2-hour wet-canyon hike through the wadi itself. Worth a half-day for travelers specifically interested in adventure hiking; skip if you\'re focused on the archaeological route.

Karak (140 km from Amman, 90 minutes)

The major Crusader castle in southern Jordan — 12th-century, built by Pagan the Butler in 1142 CE and expanded by successive Crusader and later Mamluk holders. The castle commands a hilltop with sweeping views over the Wadi Mujib gorge to the north and the Wadi Araba to the west. The most historically significant occupant was Raynald of Châtillon, the controversial Crusader lord whose attacks on Muslim caravans precipitated Saladin\'s campaign that ended the Latin Kingdom of Jerusalem in 1187.

The castle is partly ruined, partly intact, with several levels of underground passages accessible (bring a flashlight or use your phone). Allow 90 minutes minimum, 2 hours for a thorough visit. Free entry with Jordan Pass. The small museum at the entrance is worth 15 minutes.

The town of Karak around the castle is modest — a small Jordanian city with a working market, several decent restaurants for lunch (Kir Heres Restaurant is the long-time tourist favorite), and basic services. If you started in Amman at 09:00 and made the Madaba/Mount Nebo stops, you\'ll likely be in Karak for lunch around 13:00-14:00.

Dana Biosphere Reserve (70 km from Karak)

Jordan\'s largest nature reserve, covering 320 square kilometers of varied terrain — mountains, canyons, sandstone formations, and a unique combination of Mediterranean, Saharan, and Arabian biogeography. The village of Dana (perched on the canyon rim) and the Feynan ecolodge at the bottom of the canyon are the two main visitor bases.

For King's Highway travelers, Dana is best visited as either a quick photograph stop (the canyon view from the village is dramatic) or as a 1-2 night detour for serious hiking. A full Dana experience involves at least one canyon hike, ideally with overnight at Feynan (an off-grid lodge accessible only by walking or 4WD, well-reviewed for travelers who specifically want a wilderness experience). For travelers passing through, the canyon viewpoint stops at Dana village and a coffee at the Dana Guesthouse are the typical visit.

Shobak Castle (180 km from Amman, 30 km from Petra)

The smaller and less-visited Crusader castle, predating Karak by a few years (built 1115 CE by King Baldwin I of Jerusalem). The castle sits on a more isolated hilltop than Karak, with similar layout but less infrastructure for visitors. Many travelers skip it; for travelers interested in Crusader history, it complements Karak well.

Free entry with Jordan Pass, allow 60-90 minutes. The site is partly ruined — you walk among the broken stone structures rather than through fully intact rooms. The view from the castle is the strongest reason to visit; the position commanded the southern approach to the Crusader Outremer.

The realistic day plan

A standard King\'s Highway Amman-to-Petra day:

  • 09:00: Depart Amman south on Route 35.
  • 10:00-11:30: Madaba — St. George\'s Church and the mosaic map.
  • 11:45-12:30: Mount Nebo — the viewpoint and church.
  • 12:30-13:45: Drive south through Wadi Mujib. Brief photograph stops at the rim viewpoints.
  • 14:00-15:30: Karak Castle and lunch in Karak town.
  • 15:30-16:30: Drive south past Tafilah toward Petra. Brief Dana viewpoint stop if energy permits.
  • 16:30-17:30: Shobak Castle (optional 60-minute visit).
  • 17:30-18:30: Final drive south to Wadi Musa. Check into your Petra hotel by 19:00.

This is a full day with reasonable pacing. Compressed versions (skipping Mount Nebo, Dana, or Shobak) are possible but increasingly resemble the Desert Highway in feel — the value of the King\'s Highway is in not rushing it.

Practical notes

Driving conditions

The road is well-maintained and well-signed in English and Arabic. Two-lane throughout, with occasional widening at viewpoints. Speed limits are typically 60-80 km/h on the open sections, slower through villages. Fuel stations are spaced every 30-50 km but vary in quality — refuel when convenient rather than waiting until you need to.

The Wadi Mujib crossing is the most demanding section: 25-30 minutes of switchback descent followed by 25-30 minutes of switchback ascent on the other side. Use lower gears on the descent to avoid overheating brakes. Don\'t pass slow trucks on blind curves (you\'ll see Jordanians do this; don\'t copy them).

Time of day

Morning departures from Amman work best — you reach Madaba in good light, the Wadi Mujib crossing in the middle of the day when visibility is best, and Karak Castle in the afternoon. Avoid driving the King\'s Highway after dark — the road has minimal lighting, animals (camels, goats) cross unexpectedly, and the switchback sections are harder to drive at night.

The Dana detour

For travelers wanting the Dana experience, the route can be extended to include an overnight in Dana village or at Feynan Ecolodge. Add half a day for either option. The Wadi Dana hike (12 km point-to-point from Dana village down to Feynan) is a strong day-long adventure for travelers willing to make the time. Feynan Ecolodge is well-reviewed but books out 2-3 months ahead in peak season.

Where the King's Highway fits

The King\'s Highway works best on the southbound leg of a Jordan trip — when you have fresh energy for the stops and aren\'t trying to make a flight. Pair it with the Desert Highway for the return (or onward to Wadi Rum), so you experience the scenic route once and don\'t pay the time penalty twice.

For 5-day Jordan trips, the King\'s Highway is usually the first thing cut due to time constraints — the Desert Highway gets you to Petra in time for a meaningful Day 2 visit. For 7+ day trips, the King\'s Highway becomes a Day 2 or Day 3 stop in its own right. See Jordan in one week for the typical positioning.

Frequently asked questions

What is the King's Highway?
Route 35, the scenic alternative to the Desert Highway running between Amman and Aqaba. The name refers to one of the oldest continuously used trade routes in the world — mentioned in the Hebrew Bible (Numbers 20:17), used by the Nabataeans for the incense trade, and now a two-lane modern road through some of Jordan's most dramatic terrain. The route passes Madaba, Mount Nebo, Wadi Mujib (Jordan's "Grand Canyon"), Karak Castle, Shobak Castle, and Dana Biosphere Reserve before joining the Desert Highway near Petra.
How long does the King's Highway drive take?
Amman to Petra via the King's Highway is 280 km and roughly 5-6 hours with stops, versus 235 km and 3 hours via the Desert Highway. The difference matters: if you treat the King's Highway as a transit route, it's slow. If you treat it as a day-long sightseeing route with planned stops at Madaba, Mount Nebo, Wadi Mujib, and Karak, it becomes a full day of its own — not a transit.
Should I take the King's Highway or the Desert Highway?
Take the King's Highway one direction (southbound on arrival, with fresh energy for the stops), and the Desert Highway the other (faster return). This is what most experienced Jordan travelers recommend. If you have to pick only one, the King's Highway is the more memorable choice but requires significantly more time and ideally a rental car or hired driver for the flexibility.
Is the King's Highway road safe to drive?
Yes, with caveats. The road is paved and well-maintained, but it's a two-lane mountain highway with frequent switchbacks, occasional slow trucks, and elevation changes that affect both visibility and braking. Drive defensively, particularly near Wadi Mujib where the road descends 1,000+ meters into the canyon and climbs back out the other side. Avoid driving the King's Highway in heavy rain (rare, mostly December-February) — the steeper sections can have rockfall.
Can I do the King's Highway without a car?
Difficult. JETT bus service runs only on the Desert Highway between Amman and Petra; no scheduled service follows the King's Highway. Shared taxis sometimes run partial segments (Amman to Madaba, Madaba to Karak), but stitching together a full Amman-to-Petra King's Highway journey on public transport requires multiple connections and an awkward day. Two practical alternatives: rent a car, or hire a private driver for the day ($150-200 USD including stops). The driver option is often the better choice — they know the route and can point out features you'd miss as a self-driver.
What's the best stop to skip if time is tight?
Skip Mount Nebo if you must. The view across the Jordan Valley is nice but not unique, and the small museum and church are quick visits. Madaba (for the mosaic map at St. George's Church) and Karak Castle are the two genuine highlights. Wadi Mujib doesn't require a stop — you experience it as a dramatic driving section. Shobak Castle is a worthwhile shorter stop near the end of the route if you have time.

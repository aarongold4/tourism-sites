Petra photography tours

Petra is one of the most photographed archaeological sites in the world — the Treasury facade has been on the cover of more travel magazines, in the opening credits of more films, and in the portfolios of more professional photographers than almost any single architectural subject on Earth. For photographers visiting Petra specifically to make images, the difference between a standard sightseeing visit and a photography-optimized visit is significant: the timing, the viewpoints, the willingness to wait for light, and the equipment considerations all matter in ways that don't matter for general visits.

This page covers photography tours and photography-focused independent visits — what specialized tours include, how to plan an independent photography visit, and the practical details (best light, equipment, permits) that matter for image-making at Petra. The framing throughout: a niche but well-served category for travelers with specific photography goals.

The best-light timing

Petra rewards a precise understanding of when each monument catches its best light. The major windows:

The Treasury at dawn (06:30-08:30). The Treasury faces roughly east, so direct morning sunlight illuminates the facade during the first two hours after sunrise. The contrast between the lit facade and the still-shadowed Siq behind creates the iconic image that most photographers come for. Tour groups don't typically arrive at the Treasury until 09:00-10:00; photographers entering at 06:00 have the plaza essentially to themselves for the prime light window.

The Al-Khubtha overlook (08:00-11:00). The hidden viewpoint above the Treasury, accessed via the Al-Khubtha trail from behind the Royal Tombs. The overlook provides the famous high-angle view down onto the Treasury plaza. Morning light is best because the sun hits both the Treasury facade and the surrounding cliffs from the same direction; afternoon light produces strong backlighting that's harder to work with. The climb takes 45-60 minutes; plan to be on the trail by 07:00 for the best mid-morning position.

The Royal Tombs (mid-morning and late afternoon). The carved facades face roughly west, so they catch direct light in late afternoon (15:00-17:00 in winter, 16:00-18:00 in summer). The mid-morning light also works for the upper portions of the facades. Sunset light on the Urn Tomb and Palace Tomb is particularly dramatic.

The Monastery (late afternoon). The Monastery faces roughly east-southeast, so it catches both morning and afternoon light at different angles. Late afternoon (15:00-17:00 winter, 16:00-18:00 summer) produces the warmest light on the facade and the dramatic shadows that bring out the carved detail. The Monastery climb takes 60-90 minutes; plan ascent to start around 14:00-15:00 for late-afternoon light at the top.

The High Place of Sacrifice (any clear day, golden hour). The summit provides panoramic views over the entire Petra basin. Sunrise from the High Place is exceptional but requires being at the summit by 05:45 (climbing in pre-dawn light); sunset works equally well with easier logistics.

What a photography tour includes

Standard photography tour package elements:

  • Dawn entry coordination. The guide handles the 05:30-06:00 pickup from your hotel, ticket processing at the Visitor Centre, and entry at the moment the gates open. No standing in line, no logistics friction during the time-sensitive morning window.
  • Optimized routing. The day's path through Petra is structured around best-light timing for each major subject rather than around standard sightseeing flow. This usually means visiting monuments out of the conventional order — e.g., starting at the Treasury, then climbing to Al-Khubtha overlook, then the lower city in mid-day, then the Monastery for late-afternoon light.
  • Specialized guide knowledge. Photography guides know the lesser-known viewpoints, the back routes that produce different angles on famous monuments, and the technical considerations (light direction, contrast challenges, composition framings) that matter for image-making.
  • Time for image-making. Standard tours move at sightseeing pace; photography tours allow extended time at specific viewpoints (30+ minutes for tripod setup, multiple exposure brackets, waiting for light). The pacing is fundamentally different.
  • Optional technical support. Some photography tours include basic technical guidance for less-experienced photographers — exposure settings for high-contrast conditions, composition principles for the major subjects, post-processing recommendations.

Tour pricing tiers

1-day photography tour, shared (2-6 photographers). $200-300 USD per person. The standard tier for visitors who want photography-optimized timing but are happy sharing a guide with other photographers. Best value for couples or small groups of photographer friends.

1-day photography tour, private. $400-600 USD per person for 1-2 people. Premium pricing for fully customized timing and itinerary. Best for serious photographers with specific image goals.

Multi-day photography tour. $500-1,500+ per person for 3-5 day Petra-focused trips, sometimes extending to Wadi Rum and other Jordanian destinations with photography emphasis at each location. The right format for travelers building substantial photography portfolios.

Workshop photography tours. $3,000-5,000+ per person for 5-10 day intensive programs with name-brand instructors, structured around teaching photography skills as well as making images. Booked through specialized photography workshop companies (LensProToGo, Photo Tour Trekkers, etc.) and individual photographer-instructors.

DIY photography visits

Independent photography is entirely feasible at Petra and saves the tour premium. The key planning considerations:

Multi-day stay. A single-day photography visit is severely time-constrained. Photography-focused visits work best with at least 2 days at Petra — Day 1 for Treasury dawn + Al-Khubtha + Royal Tombs afternoon, Day 2 for Monastery + lower city + sunset locations.

Hotel choice. Stay at a walking-distance hotel (Visitor Centre strip) for the dawn entry convenience. The Mövenpick Resort Petra, Petra Guest House, and Petra Moon Hotel all support the 05:30 wake / 06:00 entry pattern without shuttle dependency.

Equipment considerations. A tripod is essential for low-light interior shots (the Treasury and Monastery interiors) and for the dawn-light bracketed exposures. A telephoto lens (24-105mm or 24-200mm covers most subjects) provides the working range; the wider end captures monumental architecture, the longer end isolates detail. A wide-angle (16-35mm or equivalent) captures the Siq narrows and the High Place panoramas. ND filters help with long-exposure motion blur of crowds.

Backup batteries and storage. Plan for 8-10 hour days of active shooting in temperature extremes. Petra's daytime temperatures range from cool winter mornings (3-8°C) to hot summer afternoons (35-40°C); both affect battery life. Carry 2-3 spare batteries minimum.

The hidden routes. The Al-Khubtha trail is the major hidden-viewpoint route and is well-marked. Less-known viewpoints (the back paths above the Monastery, the trails to Jebel Haroun, the high overlooks on the south side of the valley) require some scouting. A licensed Bedouin guide for one day ($90-150) can show you these routes efficiently if you don't want to scout independently.

Drone and permit considerations

Drones. Drone use within Petra is restricted and generally not permitted without specific Civil Aviation Authority permits. The site is a UNESCO World Heritage Site with cultural protection considerations, and drone use has been increasingly restricted. Photographers planning drone footage should research current permit requirements well before traveling — informal drone use at the site risks confiscation and potential fines.

Commercial photography permits. Commercial photography (for publication, commercial advertising, or paid editorial use) technically requires permits from the Jordan Department of Antiquities. In practice, enforcement is lax for individual photographers; most travel-photography Petra images appear in publications without formal commercial permits. For high-stakes commercial shoots (advertising campaigns, film production), formal permits are essential and require advance application.

Tripods inside Petra. Permitted without restriction. Be considerate of other visitors when setting up at popular spots — the Treasury plaza can get crowded enough that tripod setups disrupt other people's experience. Photographers who set up well before tour groups arrive (06:00-08:00 window) avoid this problem.

The Al-Khubtha trail

The single most important photography route at Petra. The Al-Khubtha trail climbs from behind the Royal Tombs up the cliff face to the overlook above the Treasury — providing the famous high-angle view down onto the Treasury plaza that's almost impossible to capture from any other position. The route takes 45-60 minutes one-way of moderate climbing on a well-marked trail.

The overlook itself sits on a flat platform with safety barriers, comfortable for tripod setup and extended shooting. Multiple positions along the platform give different angle options. Morning light (08:00-10:30) is the prime window; the view holds up through midday but with flatter light.

See the dedicated Al-Khubtha trail page for the full hiking detail.

Where to book photography tours

Specialized operators handle photography tours better than general Jordan operators:

  • Petra Photographers. Local Jordanian operators specializing in photography-focused visits, with guides who are themselves photographers. Direct booking through their websites.
  • Workshop-style operators. Specialized photography tour companies (Wild Frontiers, Steppes Travel, photography-specific operators) for multi-day workshop formats with instructor-led learning.
  • General operators with photography add-ons. Most major Jordan tour operators can adjust standard tours to add photography emphasis on request — useful for travelers who want photography focus but in a hybrid format with general sightseeing.

For most photographers, booking 1-3 weeks ahead of arrival is sufficient. Premium and workshop tours require longer lead times (4-12 weeks).

Frequently asked questions

What's a Petra photography tour?
A tour optimized for photographers — typically structured around the best-light hours at each monument, with timing flexibility that standard tours don't allow. A photography tour might enter at 06:00 for the Treasury dawn light, climb to the Al-Khubtha viewpoint by mid-morning, do the Monastery in late afternoon for warm light on the facade, and end with sunset at a chosen viewpoint. The format trades sightseeing breadth for image-making depth.
What does a photography tour cost?
$200-400 USD per person for a 1-day photography-focused tour with a specialized guide. Multi-day photography tours run $500-1,500+ per person depending on duration and accommodation tier. Workshop-style photography tours with name-brand instructors (Steve McCurry-style intensive trips) run $3,000-5,000+ per person for week-long programs. The category is niche but well-served — multiple operators offer photography-specific options.
Should I hire a photography guide or do it independently?
Depends on your photography experience and goals. Specialized photography guides know the precise best-light timing for each monument, the lesser-known viewpoints (Al-Khubtha trail, the high paths above the Monastery, the back routes to the Treasury overlook), and can troubleshoot technical issues. For travelers who want to maximize their photography output during a one-shot Petra visit, hiring a photography guide is worth the premium. For travelers with sufficient time to scout independently or who prioritize the photography process over guided efficiency, DIY works fine.
What's the best time of day for photographing the Treasury?
The 06:30-08:30 window during the first two hours after sunrise. The eastern face of the Treasury catches direct morning light from this angle, illuminating the carved facade dramatically against the still-dark Siq behind. The window is brief — by 09:00 the light has moved off the lower portions, by 10:00 it's overhead-flat. The same window applies year-round though the precise timing shifts slightly with seasonal sunrise variation. Photographers on dedicated photography trips arrange to be inside Petra at 06:00 to claim position in the Treasury plaza before tour groups arrive.
Can I bring professional photography equipment into Petra?
Yes — Petra has no general restrictions on professional photography equipment for personal use. Tripods, telephoto lenses, drones (with restrictions — see below), and similar gear are all permitted. Drones require specific Civil Aviation Authority permits and are not permitted within Petra without prior authorization; commercial photography for publication or commercial use technically requires additional permits but enforcement is lax for individual photographers. Inside Petra, be respectful of other visitors when setting up tripods at popular spots.

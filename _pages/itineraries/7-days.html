7 Days in North Macedonia

The spine plus the second tier — Bitola's boulevard, Roman mosaics and a mountain or a vineyard.

HomeItineraries7 Days in North Macedonia

Seven days is the country done justice: both anchors at full depth, the best of the second tier, and one day that bends to your taste — up a mountain or into a wine cellar. The route runs as an open triangle (Skopje → Ohrid → Bitola → Skopje), buses throughout, car optional.

Days 1–2 — Skopje and Matka

Run the 5-day itinerary's opening exactly: city day one (bazaar, bridges, a museum, Debar Maalo), Matka Canyon day two with the Vodno sunset option. Two Skopje nights.

Days 3–4 — Ohrid's essentials

Morning bus to the lake; afternoon hill circuit ending at Kaneo for sunset (day 3); the St. Naum boat day with springs, swim and golden-hour return (day 4). Ohrid nights one and two.

Day 5 — the choice day

Two excellent answers, one decision:

  • The mountain: Galičica National Park — taxi to the trailhead and the ascent of Magaro (2,254 m) for the country's signature panorama, Lakes Ohrid and Prespa at once. A solid 5–6 hour outing; legs required, technical skills not. Reward: Trpejca swim on the way down.
  • The vineyard (drivers): loop east to Tikveš — the Stobi ruins first, then tastings at Stobi Winery or Popova Kula (€5–15), returning via the lake road. Designated driver non-negotiable; the wines will test the policy.

Third Ohrid night either way.

Day 6 — Bitola via Heraclea

The 90-minute bus to Bitola runs over the Galičica shoulder — sit left for lake views. Drop bags, walk Širok Sokak end to end (the consul-era façades and the country's best café crawl), then taxi to Heraclea Lyncestis for the late-afternoon light on Philip II's city and its peacock mosaics. Dinner back on the boulevard. Bitola night.

Day 7 — the return, upgraded

Direct buses run Bitola–Skopje (~2.5–3 hours) for the simple exit. Drivers upgrade it: north via Kruševo — the Makedonium, the highest town in the Balkans, possibly a tandem paraglide if booked ahead — or via Prilep and the Treskavec turnoff, reaching Skopje by evening. Fly out next morning, or same-night if your flight is late.

Tuning

Swap the Bitola night for a second choice-day if cities outrank ruins for you; in winter, replace day 5 with a Mavrovo detour on the western route instead. The structure holds: two bases, one satellite, no day spent only in transit.

Where you sleep, night by night

The week's lodging splits cleanly: nights 1–2 in central Skopje (anything within ten minutes of the Stone Bridge, €40–70), nights 3–5 at Lake Ohrid (old town for atmosphere or the boulevard for flat-walking convenience, €40–80 in season), and night 6 in Bitola (small hotels off Širok Sokak, €30–50). Night 7 lands back in Skopje or at whichever end your flight uses. Only the Ohrid block carries real summer booking pressure — reserve those three nights weeks ahead for July–August; the rest take days-ahead booking comfortably. Keep the one-night stops light on luggage gymnastics: bags barely move on this route.

What a week costs

At comfortable mid-range, budget roughly €450–650 per person on the ground for seven days — accommodation share, all meals, intercity buses, the Matka and St. Naum outings, and one choice-day activity (the Galičica hike is nearly free; a wine day or paraglide adds €15–80). Backpackers running hostels, bakery-and-bazaar meals and buses bring it under €300; the comfortable ceiling, with the best rooms and a private boat hour, still rarely passes €900. Car renters add €175–245 for the week plus cheap fuel, repaid in the western-arc and wine-country access buses can't match. As everywhere here, organized day tours (€40–60 each) are the only line item that can quietly double a budget — mix one or two into a self-organized week and the famous cheapness holds.

Running it in reverse, and in winter

The triangle is symmetric: fly into Ohrid, run the lake days first, take in Bitola mid-trip and finish in Skopje — ideal if an open-jaw fare (OHD in, SKP out) is cheaper, or if you simply prefer ending in the capital's energy. In winter the week reshapes around snow: keep both Skopje days, compress Ohrid to its atmospheric essentials, and convert the choice day plus the Bitola leg into a Mavrovo ski stop on the western route, or a Bitola-and-Pelister snowshoe day if the slopes are thin. The May–October version is the route at its fullest; the winter version trades the lake's warmth for empty sites and cheap ski passes.

Frequently asked questions

What does a week add over five days?

Bitola — the elegant second city with the Heraclea Lyncestis ruins — plus one "choice day": a Galičica National Park hike with the two-lakes view, or a Tikveš wine day for drivers. The week turns a highlights trip into a country trip.

Do I need a car for the 7-day version?

No — buses connect Skopje, Ohrid and Bitola frequently, and the hike option works from Ohrid with a taxi to the trailhead. A car only becomes valuable if you pick the wine day or want Kruševo's Makedonium en route.

Where do I sleep each night?

The clean split: nights 1–2 Skopje, nights 3–5 Ohrid, night 6 Bitola, night 7 back in Skopje or at the airport-friendly end of your route. One-night stands stay minimal; bags barely move.

STANDARDS

Editorial standards

The rules this site holds itself to, in public, so you can hold it to them too.

Last reviewed June 6, 2026

Accuracy over volume. Pages publish when they're verified, not when a content calendar says so. Operational facts — prices, hours, ticket tiers, access rules — are checked against official sources (the Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities' platforms first) and current on-the-ground reports before publication, and re-checked on a rolling basis afterward. Where sources conflict, the page says so and states the range rather than picking the convenient number.

Review dates mean something. Every content page carries a "last reviewed" date in its header and footer. That date is updated only when the page's facts have actually been re-verified — not refreshed cosmetically to look current. If a date looks stale, treat the page's prices as approximate and tell us via the contact page.

Recommendations precede links. Where this site recommends a tour, hotel, or booking platform, the recommendation is made on merit first. Affiliate relationships never determine what gets recommended; they only determine whether a recommendation carries a tracked link. Where a recommended option has no affiliate program, it is recommended anyway, without one. The full mechanics are in the affiliate disclosure.

Honesty includes the negative. Reviews on this site state what's wrong as plainly as what's right — the room categories that don't deliver, the show that's dated, the tickets that aren't worth it for some visitors. A guide that can't say "skip this" can't be trusted saying "book this."

No fabricated personas. Content is written in a single editorial voice. We don't invent author biographies, composite "local experts," or staged review identities. History content follows the scholarly consensus, flags genuine debates as debates, and treats pseudo-history as what the evidence shows it to be.

What these standards are for. Travel content fails its readers in predictable ways — stale prices presented as current, enthusiasm purchased and disguised, confident answers to questions the writer never checked — and every rule on this page exists as a specific countermeasure to one of those failures. The standards are public so that the countermeasures are checkable: a reader who catches this site violating its own rules has caught something real, and the contact page is where that catch becomes a fix.

Review cadence, stated. The rotation behind the dates: money-and-access pages (fees, hours, visas, transport benchmarks) are checked most frequently and whenever credible reports of change surface; recommendation pages on each significant market shift; history pages annually or on publication of new findings. A date alone doesn't guarantee freshness — Egypt can change a price the morning after a review — which is why the official platforms get the final word on every page and readers get standing credit in the next paragraph.

Corrections welcomed and applied. Reader corrections are verified and applied promptly with updated review dates. We'd rather be corrected than wrong, and the contact page exists primarily for exactly that.

How a page gets made. The pipeline, so the standards above are checkable against it: a page begins as a question travelers actually ask; the operational facts are pulled from official sources and tested against recent on-the-ground reports; conflicts get recorded as ranges with the conflict named; the draft is written to answer the question rather than to fill a word count; internal links are added where a claim is carried by another page; the page ships with its review date; and it re-enters the verification rotation thereafter. Pages about money and access (fees, hours, visas, tickets) sit on the fastest rotation; history pages, whose facts move at the speed of excavation seasons, on the slowest.

AI assistance, disclosed. This site uses modern writing and research tools, including AI assistance, in drafting and maintenance — and holds the output to the same standard regardless of how a sentence was produced: facts verified against sources, prices dated, claims attributable. The standard is the accountability of the published page, not the provenance of the keystrokes; the corrections policy above applies with equal force either way.

Advertising boundaries. Display advertising, where present, runs on informational pages and is suppressed on pages whose primary content is booking recommendations, so that editorial and commercial incentives stay visibly separate. Sponsored placements presented as editorial content are not accepted at any price, and advertisers receive no notice of, and no influence over, what gets written.

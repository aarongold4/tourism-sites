Berovo

Pine air, a quiet lake and the best cheese in the country — the east's escape hatch.

HomeDestinationsBerovo

Berovo asks a different question from the rest of this list — not "what is there to see?" but "what is there to breathe?" The answer, locals will tell you with medical confidence, is some of the cleanest, most oxygen-rich air in the Balkans, courtesy of the Maleševo mountains' vast pine forests at a gentle 800–900 meters. The town has built a quiet wellness identity on it, and the formula works.

The lake and the forest

Berovo Lake, a few kilometers above town among the pines, is the center of gravity: a small reservoir ringed by walking paths, picnic shores and a handful of resort hotels where the agenda is firmly horizontal. Marked trails fan into the Maleševo hills — easy half-day loops, mushroom and berry territory in season, and longer routes toward the ridge for the ambitious. Winter brings snowshoe quiet rather than ski infrastructure.

The table

Berovo's fame at national level is edible: Berovsko sirenje, the white brined sheep's cheese that Macedonians name-check the way the French do appellations, and Maleševo honey from the meadow-and-pine mix. Buy both from producers or the market, and order the local lamb and mountain potatoes at the kafanas — highland cooking, unembarrassed by butter.

Monastic footnote

The 19th-century St. Archangel Michael monastery on the town's edge keeps a working sisterhood and a pretty courtyard — a gentle hour, in keeping with everything else here.

The honest case

Berovo is the one destination in this cluster that requires intent: nearly three hours east, away from every other route in the country. That is precisely its product. Come for two slow nights with a book; pair it, if continuing, with Strumica's waterfalls or a Bulgarian border crossing. Travelers chasing sights should spend the time at the lake or in the mountains west — and travelers who know the difference between a trip and a rest will understand Berovo immediately.

Frequently asked questions

Why do people go to Berovo?

Air and ease: the Maleševo highlands' pine forests are credited with some of the cleanest air in the Balkans, and the town pairs them with a calm lake, walking trails, spa-leaning hotels and the country's most celebrated cheese and honey. It's a wellness weekend, not a sight-list.

How do I get to Berovo?

It's the country's far east — about 2.5–3 hours from Skopje by car or bus (via Štip or Strumica connections). The remoteness is the feature; spontaneous visits favor drivers.

What should I buy in Berovo?

Berovsko sirenje — the white brined cheese with protected reputation — and Maleševo honey, both from local producers and the weekend market. They travel; your bag will smell wonderful.

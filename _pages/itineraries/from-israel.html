Petra from Israel

One of the most common ways travelers reach Petra — and one of the most commonly under-planned. Here's the realistic version: which border to use, how long to spend, what it costs, and why the day-trip math almost never works out the way travelers expect.

Last reviewed June 4, 2026 · 11-minute read · See our editorial standards

A significant share of Petra visitors come via Israel — either as an extension to an Israel-focused trip, or as a combined Israel-Jordan loop. The logistics are well-established but unintuitive: border crossings work differently than the airport entries most travelers are used to, the fees are higher than expected (around 130 JOD per person each way), Saturday closures catch out unwary travelers, and the day-trip math from Eilat almost never works out the way it looks at first glance. Getting this trip right requires understanding the trade-offs upfront.

This page covers the practical version: which border crossing to use, how long the trip actually takes, what it costs, and the itinerary that produces the best Petra experience for travelers based in Israel. The framing throughout: a 2-3 night Jordan visit is dramatically better than a day trip, and the cost difference is smaller than most travelers expect.

The three border crossings

Israel and Jordan share three operational border crossings. For Petra trips, only one is genuinely practical, but it's worth understanding all three.

Wadi Araba / Yitzhak Rabin (near Eilat) — the right choice for Petra

The southernmost crossing, 5 km north of central Eilat on the Israeli side, connecting directly to Aqaba on the Jordanian side. From the Jordanian side, Petra is 130 km north (2 hours by road). This is the crossing for any Petra-focused trip from Israel. Hours: Sun-Thu 06:30-20:00, Fri 08:00-20:00, closed Saturday. Combined fees: roughly 130 JOD per person each way (110 NIS Israeli exit fee + 60 JOD Jordanian visa, unless you have a Jordan Pass).

See getting to Petra from Eilat for the full border-crossing logistics, hours, and fee detail.

Allenby / King Hussein (near Jericho/Jerusalem) — for Jerusalem-based trips

The middle crossing, connecting Israeli-controlled Jericho to Jordanian-controlled territory near Amman. The crossing takes 2-3 hours each way due to higher security screening. Costs roughly the same as Wadi Araba. From the Jordanian side, you're 4-5 hours from Petra by road.

This crossing makes sense for travelers based in Jerusalem who want to do a 4-5 day Jordan trip covering Amman, Petra, and possibly Wadi Rum, with the option of flying out of Amman rather than returning to Israel. For pure Petra trips, Wadi Araba is faster and cheaper.

Jordan River / Sheikh Hussein (north, near Beit She'an)

The northernmost crossing, primarily used by travelers based in northern Israel who want to visit Amman. Puts you closer to Amman than to Petra (3-hour drive south to Petra from this crossing). Rarely the right choice for Petra-focused trips.

The day-trip vs overnight decision

The single most important decision for Petra-from-Israel travelers, and one where the easy answer is usually wrong.

The day-trip math

A typical Eilat-to-Petra day trip:

  • 05:30: Wake at Eilat hotel, taxi to border
  • 06:30-08:00: Border crossing (90 minutes total)
  • 08:00-10:00: Taxi from border to Petra (2 hours)
  • 10:00-15:00: 5 hours at Petra (paying 90 JOD non-accommodated entry rate)
  • 15:00-17:00: Taxi back to border
  • 17:00-19:00: Border crossing return (varies, 90-120 minutes)
  • 19:30: Back at Eilat hotel

14 hours door-to-door for 5 hours on site. You skip the Monastery (no time for the 2.5-3 hour climb), the morning Treasury light (you don't arrive until mid-morning), the Royal Tombs in afternoon light, and Petra by Night entirely. You pay the higher 90 JOD non-accommodated entry fee. If anything goes wrong — long border queue, traffic, a flat tire — you risk missing the 20:00 border closure.

Day-trip total cost per person: roughly $300-450 USD (border fees + 90 JOD entrance + transport + tour operator if using one).

The overnight math

A 2-night Wadi Musa stay with a Jordan Pass:

  • Day 1: Cross border in late morning, arrive Wadi Musa by 14:00, check into hotel, optional Petra by Night that evening
  • Day 2: Full Petra Day 1 — Siq, Treasury, Royal Tombs, Monastery
  • Day 3: Full Petra Day 2 — Al-Khubtha trail or High Place, Little Petra, return to border by 17:00, back in Eilat by 19:30

Overnight total cost per person: roughly $500-700 USD (border fees one-way + Jordan Pass Explorer 75 JOD which includes the visa waiver and 2-day Petra entry + 2 nights mid-range hotel + transport + meals).

For an extra $200 per person, you get: 12 hours at Petra instead of 5, the Monastery climb, Petra by Night, morning light at the Treasury, evening dinner at a real Jordanian restaurant, and a much smaller risk of border-closure stress. The math heavily favors the overnight version for nearly every traveler.

The recommended 3-day itinerary

Day 1 — Border crossing and arrival

Morning: taxi from your Eilat hotel to the Wadi Araba border (10-15 minutes, ~50 NIS). The border opens at 06:30 Sun-Thu, 08:00 Friday — go early to avoid the morning rush. Allow 90 minutes for the full crossing (exit Israel, walk no-man's-land, enter Jordan with Jordan Pass for visa-waived entry).

Onward to Petra from the Jordanian side: shared taxi (25-35 JOD per person, fills as travelers emerge) or private taxi (80-100 JOD for the car). Arrive Wadi Musa by 12:00-13:00. Check into your hotel (we recommend Petra Moon for mid-range value, Mövenpick or Petra Guest House for closer-to-gate convenience).

Afternoon: rest, lunch in Wadi Musa town. If your day falls on Sun-Thu and you have the energy, Petra by Night this evening (20:30-22:30, 30 JOD, candle-lit Siq + Bedouin music + 3D projection on the Treasury). Otherwise, early dinner and sleep.

Day 2 — Petra Day 1

Standard Petra Day 1 from the two-day Petra itinerary:

  • 06:00 enter the gate, walk the Siq
  • 06:50 Treasury in morning light
  • 07:20 Royal Tombs and Street of Facades
  • 08:15 Roman theatre and lower city
  • 09:00-12:00 Monastery climb and descent
  • 12:00 lunch at Basin Restaurant
  • 13:00-16:30 slow afternoon — Royal Tombs in afternoon light, Treasury walk-through, Siq return

Evening: dinner in Wadi Musa, early sleep.

Day 3 — Petra Day 2 and return

Morning: lighter Petra Day 2 — pick either the Al-Khubtha trail (3.5 km up to the Treasury viewpoint, 1.5-2 hours) or the High Place of Sacrifice with Wadi Farasa descent (3.5-4 hours). Both options out by 12:00-13:00.

Lunch in Wadi Musa. Afternoon: depart Wadi Musa by 14:00, taxi back to the border (2 hours), border crossing return (90 minutes). Back in Eilat by 19:00.

If you'd like to add Wadi Rum, this is when to do it: spend Day 3 morning at Petra, drive south to Wadi Rum for an overnight camp stay (1 hour from Petra), then cross at Aqaba/Eilat on Day 4. Adds approximately $150-250 per person but transforms the trip.

The expanded 4-5 day version with Wadi Rum

For travelers who can extend to 4-5 days, adding Wadi Rum is the single best upgrade. The Wadi Rum overnight produces some of the most memorable hours of any Jordan trip — Bedouin camp dinner, sunset jeep tour, stargazing on a moonless night, sunrise from the desert floor. Combined with Petra, it's the version of Jordan that justifies the cross-border logistics.

The 4-day sequence: Day 1 cross border + arrive Wadi Musa, Days 2-3 Petra, Day 4 morning Petra-to-Wadi Rum + camp overnight, Day 5 morning Wadi Rum + cross at Aqaba back to Eilat. See Petra + Wadi Rum for the combined detail.

Tour operators that handle the trip

For travelers who want the whole thing organized, several Israeli and Jordanian tour operators run Petra packages from Eilat:

1-day Petra tours from Eilat: $180-280 USD per person. Pickup at your Eilat hotel, handle the border crossing (you still pay the government fees yourself), Jordanian guide at Petra, lunch included, return to Eilat by evening. The compressed version — same trade-offs as DIY day trips, but smoother logistics.

2-day Petra tours from Eilat: $350-550 USD per person. Adds one night in a Wadi Musa hotel, two days at Petra. The right tier if you want zero logistics planning.

3-day Petra + Wadi Rum tours from Eilat: $500-800 USD per person. Adds the Wadi Rum overnight. Worth the premium if you want a complete experience handed to you.

For independent travelers comfortable with general international travel, doing the trip without a tour saves roughly 30-50% on the equivalent itinerary. For first-timers worried about the border crossing, the tour premium is defensible.

Practical notes

Currency

Bring some US dollars or euros for border fees if you don't want to deal with shekel-to-dinar exchange. Both Israeli and Jordanian borders accept USD, EUR, and credit cards for fee payment. On the Jordanian side, ATMs are available at the border and in major cities — withdraw 200-300 JOD for the trip (taxis, restaurants, smaller purchases). Major hotels and restaurants accept credit cards.

Visas

Most Western nationalities (US, UK, EU, Canada, Australia, Japan) get visa-on-arrival at Jordanian borders. The standard land-border visa is 60 JOD per person (higher than the 40 JOD airport visa). The Jordan Pass waives this fee entirely if you stay 2+ nights — which any 2- or 3-day Petra trip easily satisfies. Buy the Jordan Pass online before arrival at jordanpass.jo.

Connectivity

Israeli SIM cards don't work in Jordan and vice versa. Options: international roaming (expensive but works seamlessly), a local Jordanian SIM (10-20 JOD for a tourist eSIM with data, available at Wadi Musa hotels), or relying on hotel WiFi. Most Wadi Musa hotels and restaurants have decent WiFi.

What to bring across the border

A small overnight bag for the Wadi Musa stay. Leave the rest of your luggage at your Eilat hotel — most hotels store luggage free for guests crossing into Jordan and returning. Bring: passport, Jordan Pass printout, comfortable walking shoes, water bottle, basic toiletries, change of clothes for 2 days. Most travelers significantly over-pack for the Jordan portion.

Frequently asked questions

What's the best way to do Petra from Israel?
Cross the Wadi Araba / Yitzhak Rabin border (the southernmost of the three Israel-Jordan crossings, near Eilat), stay at least two nights in Jordan so you qualify for the Jordan Pass visa waiver, and treat Petra as a 2- or 3-day visit rather than a day trip. Day trips from Eilat are possible but expensive (130 JOD in border fees plus 90 JOD non-accommodated Petra entry) and give you only 4-5 hours on site. Overnighting in Wadi Musa is dramatically better experience for similar total cost when you factor the Jordan Pass.
Which border crossing should I use?
Three options. The Wadi Araba crossing (near Eilat) is the right choice for Petra-focused trips — it puts you 2 hours from Petra by road. The Allenby/King Hussein crossing (near Jericho/Jerusalem) works for travelers based in Jerusalem who want to include Petra in a longer Israel trip, but it requires a longer drive south to Petra (4-5 hours). The Jordan River/Sheikh Hussein crossing (north, near Beit She'an) is rarely the right choice for Petra trips — it puts you closest to Amman, then a 3-hour drive south to Petra.
How much does the whole trip cost?
For a 3-day version (1 night in Petra, 2 days at the site, return to Israel): roughly $500-700 USD per person including border fees, Jordan Pass, mid-range Wadi Musa hotel, transport from the border, and meals. For a 4-5 day version that adds Wadi Rum: $750-1,100 per person. Significantly cheaper than the all-in $300-500 per person of a one-day organized tour from Eilat — and you actually see Petra rather than rushing it.
Do I need a guide or tour operator?
No, but tours can simplify first-time crossings. Independent travelers handle the border crossing themselves, arrange Jordanian transport (shared or private taxi to Petra), book hotels online, and follow the standard Petra itinerary — all completely feasible for anyone with general travel experience. Tour operators (typically $250-450 per person for 2-day packages) handle the border logistics and provide a guide at Petra, useful for first-timers who want zero planning. Most repeat visitors travel independently.
Can I rent a car in Israel and drive to Petra?
No. Almost no Israeli rental car companies allow their vehicles to cross the border into Jordan, and the few that do charge substantial cross-border fees ($80-150 per day). The practical workflow: drive to the border, park in the Israeli lot (~50 NIS/day), walk across the crossing, take a Jordanian taxi or arrange pickup on the other side. For travelers wanting a rental car in Jordan, rent on the Jordanian side at Aqaba airport or in Wadi Musa.
What about Shabbat closures?
The Wadi Araba border is closed all day Saturday for Israeli Shabbat — this catches a meaningful share of travelers who plan a Saturday Petra trip. The border is also closed on Yom Kippur, the eves of Rosh Hashanah, Passover, and Sukkot. Always check both governments' official sources before traveling, and plan to arrive at the crossing at least 60 minutes before the 20:00 closure. Friday afternoon (08:00 opening) crossings work but have higher queue volume.

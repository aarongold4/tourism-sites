Petra tours from Aqaba

The 130 km between Aqaba and Petra makes day trips possible but compressed. Tours from Aqaba range from 12-hour day excursions to multi-day combined Petra and Wadi Rum packages. Here's the practical breakdown.

Aqaba is the natural launching point for Petra tours for several traveler categories: cruise passengers whose ships dock at the Aqaba port, divers based in Aqaba combining underwater days with Petra excursions, travelers crossing from Eilat via the Wadi Araba border who use Aqaba as the entry point to Jordan, and budget travelers flying into Aqaba King Hussein Airport (AQJ) on lower-cost European routes. For all these profiles, organized tours from Aqaba provide an efficient way to add Petra to the trip without the logistics of independent travel from a base further away.

This page covers what Aqaba-origin tours actually involve: day trip formats, multi-day packages combining Petra with Wadi Rum, cruise shore excursion arrangements, and the practical question of whether organized tours or DIY travel works better from this starting point. The framing throughout: efficient option for time-constrained travelers, less compelling for travelers with flexibility to spend multiple nights in Wadi Musa.

The basic geography

Aqaba sits at the northern tip of the Gulf of Aqaba, the eastern arm of the Red Sea. Petra is 130 km north on the Desert Highway — about 2-2.5 hours of driving depending on traffic and stops. The route passes through dramatic desert landscape, with the road climbing steadily from sea level at Aqaba to about 1,100 meters at Wadi Musa.

The driving is straightforward — modern divided highway, well-maintained, well-signed in English. The route bypasses Wadi Rum (which is to the east of the Desert Highway, accessed via a paved spur road from the south); travelers wanting to combine Petra with Wadi Rum on the same trip take the spur road to Wadi Rum and either backtrack to the Desert Highway or take cross-country routes (the latter requiring 4WD).

Day trip tours

Standard format

A typical Aqaba-to-Petra day tour:

  • 06:30-07:00: Pickup from your Aqaba hotel or cruise terminal.
  • 07:00-09:30: Drive to Petra (130 km, ~2.5 hours including a brief rest stop).
  • 09:30-10:00: Arrive at Petra Visitor Centre, ticket processing, meet your licensed guide.
  • 10:00-12:30: Guided visit through Petra — Siq, Treasury, Royal Tombs, Roman theatre, descent into the lower city.
  • 12:30-13:30: Lunch (typically at the Basin Restaurant inside Petra or at a Wadi Musa restaurant near the entrance).
  • 13:30-15:30: Optional Monastery climb OR continued lower-city exploration (Qasr al-Bint, the Great Temple, colonnaded street), depending on group preferences and energy levels.
  • 15:30-16:00: Return walk to the Visitor Centre.
  • 16:00-18:30: Drive back to Aqaba.
  • 19:00-19:30: Drop-off at hotel or cruise terminal.

Total: about 12-13 hours door-to-door, with 4-6 hours actually inside Petra.

What it costs

Day tour pricing varies significantly:

  • Shared group tour: $80-130 USD per person. Group of 6-12 in a minivan or small bus, fixed itinerary, guided in-site visit, lunch included, Petra entrance fee usually included.
  • Cruise line shore excursion: $150-220 USD per person. Same basic format but with ship-coordinated logistics and the cruise line's guarantee of return-to-ship timing. Premium pricing reflects the convenience.
  • Private day tour: $150-250 USD per person for 1-2 people (price decreases for larger groups). Private vehicle, dedicated guide, flexibility on itinerary timing and inclusions.
  • Luxury day tour: $300-500 USD per person. Premium vehicle, senior-level guide, gourmet lunch arrangements, sometimes including helicopter or other premium upgrades.

The compressed-day reality

The honest assessment: a single day at Petra is not enough time for the site. The 4-6 hour visiting window of a day tour covers the highlights (Treasury, Royal Tombs, theatre, either Monastery or lower city) but doesn't allow for the slower pace, the second-best-light afternoon return to the Treasury, the High Place of Sacrifice climb, the Al-Khubtha trail to the Treasury overlook, or the contemplative engagement with monuments that Petra rewards. For travelers with flexibility, 2-3 days in Petra is meaningfully better than a single day tour.

The day tour format makes sense when you don't have the flexibility — cruise passengers with fixed ship schedules, business travelers with one free day in Aqaba, travelers building Petra into broader Egypt-Jordan-Israel itineraries with limited Jordan time. For these profiles, a day tour from Aqaba is better than not visiting Petra at all.

Cruise shore excursions

Aqaba is a regular stop on Mediterranean cruise itineraries that include the Red Sea, Gulf of Aqaba, and Egyptian destinations. Cruise lines typically offer Petra day tours as their flagship Aqaba shore excursion.

Cruise line tours. Booked through the ship's shore excursion office, $150-220 USD per person. Pros: cruise line guarantees you'll be back on the ship in time (if the tour is delayed, the ship waits). Cons: highest pricing, larger group sizes (often 25-40 people), less flexibility.

Independent operators. Booked through Viator, GetYourGuide, or directly with Jordanian operators, $80-130 USD per person. Pros: significantly lower price, smaller groups, more flexible. Cons: cruise line does NOT guarantee ship hold for these tours — if you're delayed, the ship leaves without you.

The independent option is usually fine in practice — operators serving cruise passengers understand the schedule criticality and build buffer time into their itineraries. The risk is real but small; travelers with risk tolerance often choose independent operators for the cost savings. Travelers with low risk tolerance or essential ship-bound schedules should consider the cruise line option.

Multi-day tours combining Petra and Wadi Rum

For Aqaba travelers with flexibility to commit more than one day, combined Petra + Wadi Rum tours are the more rewarding format:

2-day Petra and Wadi Rum

Day 1: Aqaba pickup, drive to Wadi Rum (1 hour), 4WD desert tour and overnight Bedouin camp. Day 2: Sunrise tour and breakfast, drive to Petra (2 hours), full afternoon at Petra (4-5 hours), drop-off at Wadi Musa hotel for the night.

Pricing: 180-280 USD per person depending on camp tier (basic Bedouin tent vs luxury bubble dome).

3-day Petra and Wadi Rum

Day 1: Wadi Rum jeep tour and overnight Bedouin camp. Day 2: Wadi Rum sunrise, transfer to Petra, afternoon at Petra, overnight in Wadi Musa. Day 3: Second Petra day (morning at the Treasury, Royal Tombs, Monastery climb, etc.), return to Aqaba in late afternoon.

Pricing: 280-450 USD per person. The 3-day format gives Petra the time it deserves while keeping the logistics manageable.

4-5 day full Jordan tours

Adding the Dead Sea, Jerash, and/or Amman to the itinerary. Pricing 450-800+ USD per person depending on length, accommodation tier, and inclusions.

Practical considerations

Jordan Pass usage

The Jordan Pass (which combines visa fee waiver with Petra entrance) requires a 2-night minimum stay in Jordan to qualify for the visa waiver benefit. Day-trip travelers from Aqaba — particularly cruise passengers — don't typically meet this requirement. For day trips, you pay the standard Petra entrance fee separately (50 JOD for 1-day non-accommodated visits, or 90 JOD for true day-trippers technically). Tour packages usually include the entrance fee; verify before booking.

See the Jordan Pass page for the full breakdown.

Border crossings

Travelers crossing from Eilat via the Wadi Araba border to reach Aqaba should account for border crossing time (typically 30-60 minutes for the crossing itself plus transit). The crossing is open Sunday through Thursday 06:30-20:00, Friday 08:00-20:00, closed Saturdays. Plan tours from Aqaba assuming the border timing.

Aqaba accommodation

Travelers staying multiple nights in Aqaba have multiple hotel options at the Tala Bay resort cluster (Mövenpick Tala Bay, InterContinental Aqaba), central Aqaba (Kempinski Aqaba, multiple mid-range options), and budget properties throughout the city. Tour pickups from any Aqaba hotel are standard; specify your hotel name when booking.

Where to book

Standard channels apply:

  • Viator and GetYourGuide: Convenient online booking, transparent pricing, customer reviews. Best for first-time bookers.
  • Direct booking with Jordanian operators: Sometimes 15-25% cheaper, more customization flexibility. Requires direct communication.
  • Your Aqaba hotel: Most hotels can book Petra day tours through their preferred operators, sometimes at competitive rates.
  • Cruise line shore excursions: For cruise passengers prioritizing the ship-hold guarantee.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a Petra day tour from Aqaba take?
About 12-13 hours door-to-door. The drive from Aqaba to Petra is 130 km / 2-2.5 hours each way on the Desert Highway. Inside Petra, the tour typically provides 4-5 hours of visiting time — enough for the Treasury, Royal Tombs, and either the Monastery climb OR the lower-city monuments, but not both. Most day tours depart Aqaba at 06:30-07:00 and return by 19:30-20:30. The single-day format is workable but compressed — Petra rewards more time than a day tour allows.
Can I do Petra as a day trip from a cruise ship in Aqaba?
Yes — this is a common arrangement. Cruise lines docking at Aqaba's port typically offer Petra day tours as shore excursions, and independent operators offer competitive alternatives at lower prices ($80-130 USD per person vs cruise line's $150-220). The tours are timed to ship schedules with morning departure and afternoon return. Whether shore excursion or independent, build in margin for unforeseen delays — being late back to the ship is a serious problem.
What about a Petra and Wadi Rum combined tour from Aqaba?
Possible as a 2-day or 3-day tour. The most common arrangement: Day 1 morning depart Aqaba, drive to Wadi Rum (1 hour), 4WD desert experience and overnight Bedouin camp. Day 2 morning sunrise tour and breakfast, drive to Petra (2 hours), full afternoon at Petra, overnight in Wadi Musa. Day 3 morning final Petra visit and return to Aqaba. Pricing 250-450 USD per person depending on accommodation tier and group size.
Should I take a tour or DIY from Aqaba?
Tour is usually better for day trips, DIY is better for multi-day trips. The 12+ hour day-trip rhythm benefits from a driver handling all logistics; navigating it yourself with rental car or public transport while keeping to a single-day schedule is stressful. For multi-day trips where you're staying overnight in Wadi Musa anyway, the flexibility of self-directed travel becomes more valuable than the convenience of tour logistics.
What does an Aqaba-Petra tour include?
Typical inclusions: round-trip transport (air-conditioned vehicle, English-speaking driver), Petra entrance fee (50 JOD for non-Jordan-Pass visits — note: Jordan Pass not generally usable for day visits from Aqaba since the 2-night minimum requirement applies), licensed guide for in-site interpretation, lunch (usually at the Basin Restaurant or a Wadi Musa restaurant), bottled water, and pickup/drop-off at your Aqaba hotel or cruise terminal. Check whether the entrance fee is included separately or in the package price.

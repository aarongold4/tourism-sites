GETTING THERE

Taxi and private driver

Jordan's taxi infrastructure is reliable, English-friendly in tourist contexts, and reasonably priced. Hiring a driver for a half-day or full-day is one of the most underrated ways to handle Jordan logistics — flexible like a rental car, but with someone else doing the driving.

Last reviewed June 5, 2026 · 5-minute read · See our editorial standards

Taxis and private drivers occupy useful middle ground in Jordan transport — more flexible than fixed-schedule JETT buses, less commitment than renting a car, less expensive than full tour packages. For specific traveler profiles (Amman-based visitors making day trips to nearby destinations, multi-day Petra travelers who don't want to drive themselves, travelers connecting between Petra and Wadi Rum without rental cars), taxis and hired drivers solve the logistics problem cleanly.

This page covers the practical use of taxis and drivers in Jordan: how Amman taxis work, what longer-distance taxi rides cost, how to hire drivers by the day, and the practical considerations that affect the experience. The framing throughout: a flexible, underrated category of Jordan transport that fits between rental cars and organized tours.

Amman taxis

Amman has an extensive taxi system. Yellow-painted taxis (the official livery) operate throughout the city; private cars (white taxis) also work as taxis with slightly different pricing rules. Both are reliable and widely available.

Metered taxis

Amman taxis are required by law to use meters. The base fare starts at 0.25 JOD when the meter starts; the rate accumulates based on time and distance traveled. A typical 5-10 km ride within Amman runs 2-4 JOD on the meter.

Always demand the meter. Say "al-ad-dad min fadhlak" (the meter please) or simply "meter" — most drivers understand the English word. If a driver refuses to use the meter, get out and find another taxi. The meter-refusal is a sign of an unscrupulous driver who will likely overcharge.

Tipping

Tips are appreciated but not required for short rides. Rounding up to the nearest JOD or 0.5 JOD is standard. For longer rides or particularly helpful service, 0.5-1 JOD tip is appropriate.

Careem (the Uber-equivalent)

Careem is the dominant Middle Eastern rideshare app (owned by Uber, operating under the Careem brand in the region). In Amman, Careem provides reliable service with fixed app-quoted pricing, no negotiation needed, payment by credit card through the app. Pricing is typically similar to or slightly lower than metered taxis.

How to use: Download the Careem app, register with your phone number (international SIMs work fine), order a ride from your current GPS location to your destination. App is in English; drivers are local but generally have at least basic English.

Careem works well for travelers who prefer the app convenience over street-hailing. The fixed pricing eliminates concerns about meter manipulation or rate negotiation.

Airport taxis

Queen Alia International Airport (Amman) has organized taxi service from a dedicated desk in the arrivals hall. Fixed fares to standard destinations:

  • Amman center: 22 JOD fixed fare (~$31 USD). Approximately 45 km / 45 minutes depending on traffic.
  • Petra (Wadi Musa): 75-100 JOD (~$105-140 USD). Approximately 235 km / 3-4 hours.
  • Dead Sea: 35-45 JOD (~$50-65 USD). Approximately 60 km / 1 hour.
  • Aqaba: 100-130 JOD (~$140-180 USD). Approximately 330 km / 4 hours.

The fixed-fare desk is the easiest option for arriving travelers. Direct negotiation with individual drivers outside the airport sometimes produces lower rates (especially for longer routes like Petra) but requires confidence in negotiation and risk tolerance for rate disputes.

Aqaba King Hussein International Airport (AQJ) has a smaller taxi operation with similar fixed-fare structure for major destinations.

Hiring a private driver

For multi-stop trips, multi-day exploration, or travelers wanting full flexibility without driving themselves, hiring a private driver is often the right choice.

What it costs

  • Half-day (4-5 hours): 30-50 JOD (~$42-70 USD). Best for single-direction transfers with some flexibility (e.g., airport pickup + brief Amman tour + drop-off at hotel).
  • Full day (8-10 hours): 60-100 JOD (~$85-140 USD). The standard format for serious exploration days. Includes multi-stop sightseeing, longer-distance transit between cities, or combined airport pickup + onward travel.
  • Multi-day: 50-90 JOD per day for 3-7 day arrangements. Reduced from single-day rates because the driver doesn't deadhead back to base each day.

What's included: Driver, vehicle, fuel, insurance, driver's meals. Standard exclusions: tolls, parking fees, entrance fees to attractions, driver's overnight accommodation (for multi-day trips — typically arranged at lower-tier hotels at $20-40 per night that the driver pays for separately).

Vehicle types

Sedan (Toyota Corolla, Hyundai Elantra, similar): Comfortable for 1-2 passengers with luggage. Standard 60-80 JOD/day pricing.

SUV (Toyota Land Cruiser, Hyundai Tucson): Better for 3-4 passengers, more luggage capacity, more comfortable on rougher roads. 80-120 JOD/day.

Van (Toyota Hiace, Mercedes Vito): 5-8 passengers, full luggage capacity. 100-150 JOD/day.

How to hire

Multiple channels:

  • Your hotel. Most hotels can arrange drivers through their preferred providers. Reliable, slightly higher pricing than direct booking but with the hotel's quality endorsement.
  • Direct booking with operators. Jordanian tour and transport operators (search "Jordan transport" or "Jordan private driver") arrange driver hire directly. Best rates and most flexibility but requires research and direct communication.
  • Airport drivers. Some travelers connect with drivers at the airport and arrange continued service for their broader Jordan trip. Workable for travelers comfortable with direct negotiation but variable quality.
  • Recommendations from other travelers. The travel forums (TripAdvisor, Reddit r/Jordan, Lonely Planet Thorn Tree archive) often have driver recommendations from recent visitors. The personalized referrals are valuable when they're current.

Petra-area taxis

Wadi Musa (the town serving Petra) has a smaller, less formal taxi system than Amman. Practical details:

How to find a taxi: Stand in front of any hotel and the front desk can call one; the main town centre has informal taxi stands where drivers wait for passengers; cars pass by regularly on the main street and can be hailed.

Pricing: No meters; rates are negotiated before departure. Standard Wadi Musa local trips:

  • Hotel to Visitor Centre: 3-5 JOD
  • Visitor Centre to Tayba ridge: 5-8 JOD
  • Wadi Musa to Little Petra: 10-15 JOD round trip including waiting time
  • Wadi Musa to Wadi Rum: 50-70 JOD one-way

Negotiation: Ask the rate before getting in. Drivers in Wadi Musa generally quote fair rates by local standards. Hotel front desks can verify reasonable rates if you're unsure.

The case for hiring a driver vs. renting a car

For multi-day Jordan trips, the rental-car-vs-hired-driver decision is one of the principal logistics questions. The trade-offs:

Hire a driver if:

  • You're nervous about driving in unfamiliar conditions (especially Amman city driving)
  • You want to focus on the scenery rather than the road
  • The convenience of someone else handling parking, fuel, and navigation outweighs the cost premium
  • You're traveling solo or as a couple — the per-person cost is competitive
  • You want a local resource for restaurant recommendations, cultural context, and informal guiding

Rent a car if:

  • You want maximum flexibility and spontaneous-stop capability
  • You're comfortable driving in unfamiliar countries
  • You're traveling with 3-4 people and the per-person cost of rental is much lower than a driver
  • You prefer to drive yourself and consider the driving part of the experience
  • You want to minimize ongoing daily cost commitments (driver costs accumulate daily, rental car cost is more front-loaded)

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between a taxi and a private driver?
A taxi is a metered or fixed-fare vehicle you hire for a single trip (e.g., airport to hotel, hotel to Petra entrance, Petra to Wadi Rum). A private driver is hired for longer periods — typically a half-day or full day — for multiple stops, broader exploration, or longer-distance routes. Pricing reflects this: taxis charge by the trip (5-25 JOD for typical tourist routes), private drivers charge by the day (50-100 JOD for a full day in southern Jordan).
How much does a taxi from Amman airport to Petra cost?
Approximately 75-100 JOD (~$105-140 USD) for the 235 km route, taking about 3-4 hours. The airport has a fixed-fare taxi desk in the arrivals hall — paying through this desk avoids negotiation but the rates are at the upper end. Direct negotiation with drivers outside the airport sometimes produces lower rates (60-85 JOD) but requires confidence in negotiation. Most travelers find the fixed-fare convenience worth the modest premium.
Can I just hire a taxi for the whole Petra visit?
Yes — hiring a taxi or private driver for half-day or full-day periods is common and effective. A full day driver in southern Jordan typically runs 60-90 JOD, covering airport pickup, transit between Petra and Wadi Rum, multi-stop sightseeing, and similar combined needs. The driver waits while you're in Petra and continues with your itinerary. For travelers who don't want to rent a car but need more flexibility than JETT bus provides, hiring a driver is the natural intermediate option.
Is Uber/Careem available?
Yes in Amman, no in Petra. Careem (the major Middle Eastern rideshare app, owned by Uber) operates in Amman with reliable service and fixed-app pricing. In smaller cities and tourist areas (including Wadi Musa), the rideshare apps don't have meaningful coverage; traditional taxis are the only option. Aqaba has limited Careem service. For Amman-based travelers, Careem is generally cheaper and more reliable than street taxis; for travelers based in Petra or Wadi Rum, hotel-arranged taxis are the standard approach.
Should I use the meter or negotiate?
In Amman, demand the meter ("al-ad-dad" in Arabic). Most taxis have meters and are required by law to use them; refusal to use the meter is a sign of an unscrupulous driver. In Wadi Musa and other tourist areas, meters are uncommon and rides are fixed-fare with rates negotiated before departure. For tourists, the safe approach is to ask the price upfront and confirm before getting in — most drivers in tourist areas quote fair rates by local standards.

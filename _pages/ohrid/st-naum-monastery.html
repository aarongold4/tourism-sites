St. Naum Monastery

A 10th-century monastery, glass-clear springs and resident peacocks at the lake's southern tip.

If Kaneo is Ohrid's postcard, St. Naum is its day out. At the lake's southern tip, 29 kilometers from town and within sight of Albania, the monastery founded by St. Naum in 905 combines everything the region does well in one compact site: a fresco-dense Byzantine church holding the saint's tomb, gardens patrolled by an entitled flock of peacocks, beaches, tavernas — and beside it all, the springs of the Black Drim, a lagoon so transparent that rowing across it feels like low-altitude flight.

Getting there is half the point

The classic approach is by boat from Ohrid harbor — about an hour and a half down the eastern shore, often calling at the Bay of Bones museum en route. Walk-up boats at the pier run around €15–25 return at the going rate (organized tours with guides and entries included cost more); the morning departure with a late-afternoon return gives you four-plus hours on site, which is right. The budget route is the local bus down the shore road (cheap and quick, with lake views most of the way), and drivers can do it in 40 minutes with paid parking (€1–2) at the gate. A nice hybrid: boat down, bus back.

At the monastery

The church of St. Naum of Ohrid is small and astonishingly painted — virtually every surface carries frescoes, mostly 16th–18th century over the 10th-century bones. The saint's tomb sits in a side chamber; local tradition says that if you press an ear to it you can hear his heartbeat, and you will see a steady queue of believers testing it. Entry to the interior is around €2.50; the grounds, the lake terraces and the peacocks — who scream, pose and own the place — are free.

The springs

Behind the monastery, dozens of underwater springs feed a lagoon where the Black Drim river is born before it dives through the lake on its way to the Adriatic. Rowboat trips (roughly €5 a head, or around 600 MKD per boat — gather a group and split it) glide over water so clear the plants below look pinned under glass; late spring adds water lilies. A free walking trail behind the small church of St. Petka parallels the springs if the boats feel steep, though the trees hide much of the view — the rowboat is the experience.

Making a day of it

Arrive before noon for quiet, do the church first while tour groups are still at sea, then the springs, then lunch at the terraced restaurants over the water (pricey by Macedonian standards, irresistible by location). Swim off the beaches, then catch the afternoon boat home — the return across open water, with Galičica's wall of mountains turning gold, finishes the day properly. Two hours covers the highlights; a full day is better spent.

The wider site: springs, beaches and the Bay of Bones

St. Naum is more than its church. The headland holds terraced restaurants over the water, peacocks on the lawns, a couple of the lake's best swimming beaches just north, and the famous spring lagoon where the Black Drim is born. Most boat itineraries from Ohrid pause at the Bay of Bones on the way — a reconstructed prehistoric stilt-village museum built out over the water on the original pile foundations, well worth the half-hour and a separate small entry. Together they make St. Naum a comfortable half- to full-day rather than a single monastery stop: church and springs in the morning, lunch over the lake, a swim, then the golden-hour cruise home.

When to go, and the Albania view

The site is at its best in late spring and September — water lilies on the springs, swimmable lake, long light, manageable crowds. July and August are beautiful but busy, and the terraced restaurants fill at lunch. From the headland you're looking across the lake's southern end into Albania, whose mountains wall the far shore; the border runs just minutes south of the monastery, which is why the boat and shore-road both stop here rather than continuing. Winter strips the place back to a quiet, atmospheric monastery with the restaurants shuttered and the boats reduced — lovely in its own austere way, but the springs-and-swim version needs warmth.

Frequently asked questions

How much does St. Naum cost to visit?

The grounds are free to wander; the church interior charges a small fee of around €2.50 (about 150 MKD), parking costs €1–2, and the springs rowboat runs roughly €5 per person or around 600 MKD per boat depending on the boatman and season — prices drift, so carry small cash.

How do I get to St. Naum from Ohrid?

Three ways: the boat from Ohrid harbor (the scenic choice, around 1.5 hours each way, walk-up trips from roughly €15–25 return), the local bus down the eastern shore (cheapest, ~45 minutes), or a 40-minute drive/taxi. Many travelers boat one way and bus back.

Can you swim at St. Naum?

Yes — there are two pleasant beaches by the complex with loungers for hire in season, and the water near the springs is brilliantly clear and noticeably cold. Bring swimwear; it rounds the trip into a full day.

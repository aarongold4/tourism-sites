GETTING THERE

How to get to Petra

Petra sits 3 hours south of Amman, 2 hours north of Aqaba, and across one of the Middle East's more interesting borders from Israel. Here's every route in, with current 2026 prices and the trade-offs between bus, car, taxi, and tour.

Last reviewed April 28, 2026 · 11-minute read · See our editorial standards

The basics of getting to Petra are simpler than most travelers expect, but the cheapest, fastest, and most flexible options are rarely the same thing. The JETT bus is the cheapest way from Amman by a wide margin (about $14 USD one-way), but it runs once a day and gives you exactly four hours flexibility before the return departure. A rental car costs more but lets you sleep in Wadi Musa, do Petra by Night, and detour via Karak Castle on the way back. A tour costs more again but takes the logistics off your hands entirely.

This page is the index to those routes, organized by origin. Each origin gets its own dedicated page with operator details, current pricing, and the timing quirks (the JETT bus's single daily departure, the Wadi Araba border crossing's Saturday Shabbat closure, the Aqaba bus schedule that shifts during Ramadan). If you only need the headline answer: most travelers from Amman should take a guided day tour or stay overnight in Wadi Musa with the JETT bus; most travelers from Aqaba should take a shared taxi or short JETT bus; and most travelers from Israel should overnight in Jordan rather than try to day-trip across the border.

The four-bus-stop answer

For travelers who want the shortest possible version of the transport answer, here it is. Petra has, effectively, four ways in.

The JETT bus is the cheapest. $14–17 USD one-way from Amman, $22 from Aqaba, $22 from Wadi Rum. One departure per day in each direction on the Amman route. Reserved seating, comfortable, air-conditioned. Book at jett.com.jo a day or two ahead. The single biggest constraint: if you miss the 17:00 return from Petra, you're sleeping in Wadi Musa whether you planned to or not.

The rental car is the most flexible. $30–45 USD per day for an economy car. Roads are excellent and Petra has free parking at the Visitor Centre. The Desert Highway is the fast direct route; the King's Highway via Karak and Madaba is the scenic alternative that adds 2–3 hours but turns a transit day into a real travel day. International Driving Permits are technically required and almost never checked.

A private taxi or hired driver is the easiest. 70–100 JOD ($100–140 USD) one-way from Amman, 35–50 JOD from Aqaba. Hotels can arrange these in 30 minutes. For groups of three or four travelers, the math often works out better than a JETT bus plus the inconvenience of the once-daily schedule. For day trips with multiple stops, hiring a driver for the day (around 150–180 JOD) is often the smartest single decision.

A guided tour bundles everything. $80–400 USD per person from Amman, depending on group size and inclusions. Door-to-door, guide on site, no logistics. We compare specific operators on the Petra tours from Amman page.

Once you've arrived

The Petra Visitor Centre is in Wadi Musa, the town immediately above the entrance. From the Visitor Centre, you walk to the gate (5 minutes), then 1.5 kilometers through the Siq before the Treasury comes into view. There's no internal transport inside Petra itself — the entire site is walked on foot, except for the optional donkey or horse-drawn carriage rides (we generally recommend skipping these on welfare grounds and because the walking is genuinely the point).

If you're staying overnight, your Wadi Musa hotel is almost certainly within walking distance of the Visitor Centre or a short cheap taxi away. If you're day-tripping, your transport out leaves from the same spot you arrived: the JETT bus stop at the Mövenpick, the Wadi Musa minibus rank for local services, or wherever your tour driver parked. Plan to be back at the pickup point at least 15 minutes before departure — the walk back from the lower city to the gate alone takes 45 minutes.

Frequently asked questions

What's the fastest way to get from Amman to Petra?
A private car or rental car via the Desert Highway, which is roughly 235 km and takes about 3 hours non-stop. Tours and the JETT bus run the same route and take the same time, plus stops. The King's Highway via Madaba and Karak is more scenic and a worthwhile journey in its own right, but adds 2–3 hours.
How much does the JETT bus from Amman to Petra cost?
Roughly 10–11 JOD ($14–17 USD) one-way, or around 20 JOD round-trip. There's one direct departure per day in each direction: leaving Amman's Abdali station at 06:30 (arrives Petra around 10:00) and leaving Petra at 17:00 (arrives Amman around 20:30). Book at jett.com.jo at least 1–2 days ahead in spring and autumn high season, when the single daily bus regularly sells out.
Is the JETT bus or a tour better value for Petra?
The JETT bus wins on price by a wide margin — $14–17 one-way versus $80+ for a budget group tour. The tour wins on convenience: door-to-door from your Amman hotel, guide on site, no risk of missing the 17:00 return. For solo travelers and confident independent travelers, the bus is the clear value choice. For first-timers worried about logistics, language, or the visa process at the border, the tour is worth the premium.
Can I rent a car and drive to Petra?
Yes, and it's the most flexible option. Rental car prices in Jordan start around $30–45 USD per day for an economy car. Roads to Petra are excellent — the Desert Highway is a divided highway most of the way, well-signed in English and Arabic. Parking at the Visitor Centre is free. The main caveat is driving culture: expect aggressive lane-changes around Amman and to be flashed at by oncoming cars warning you of speed cameras or police. International Driving Permits are technically required but rarely checked at rental counters.
How do I get to Petra from Aqaba?
Three options. JETT runs a once-daily bus from Aqaba to Petra (around 22 JOD, 2 hours), departing Aqaba mid-morning. A shared taxi or "service" from Aqaba bus station runs more frequently but on no fixed schedule (around 8–10 JOD per person, departs when full). A private taxi is around 35–50 JOD one-way for the car. Aqaba is closer to Petra than Amman (2 hours versus 3) and increasingly the better entry point if your flights work — King Hussein International handles direct flights from London, Istanbul, and several EU cities.
How do I get to Petra from Israel (Eilat)?
Cross the Wadi Araba/Yitzhak Rabin border just north of Eilat (open Sun–Thu 06:30–20:00, Fri 08:00–20:00, closed Saturdays during Shabbat and Jewish holidays). Israeli exit fee is roughly 110 NIS, Jordanian visa is 60 JOD (or free with a Jordan Pass, but the Jordan Pass requires you to stay at least two nights in Jordan to qualify for the visa waiver). On the Jordanian side, take a taxi to Aqaba (~30 minutes, around 25 JOD) and then bus or taxi to Petra. Or arrange a Jordanian driver to meet you at the border — far less hassle, around 80–100 JOD direct to Petra.

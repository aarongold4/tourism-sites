HOTELS

Best mid-range hotels in Petra

The $70-150 USD price tier is where most Wadi Musa travelers should look first. Walking distance to the gate, included breakfast, comfortable rooms — without the luxury hotel premium. Here are the consistently best options.

Last reviewed April 28, 2026 · 7-minute read · See our editorial standards

The mid-range price tier in Wadi Musa runs roughly $70-150 USD per night, and it's where most travelers find the best value-for-money in the town. Properties at this price point are typically 3-4 star family-run or small chain hotels within walking distance of the Petra gate, with included breakfast and comfortable (if not luxurious) rooms. The luxury options ($200-400/night) offer service polish and resort amenities but the experiential gap from mid-range isn't as large as the price gap.

This page covers the four most consistently recommended mid-range options, the trade-offs between them, and what to look for when booking. The framing throughout: for most travelers, mid-range is the right choice — luxury is a budget upgrade rather than a meaningful experience upgrade for the Petra-specific use case.

The four most-recommended options

Petra Moon Hotel. 5-minute walk to the Petra gate, family-run, rooftop terrace. The most consistently recommended mid-range option in Wadi Musa. $70-140 USD per night. Full review →

Petra Canyon Hotel. About 10 minutes from the gate, closer to Wadi Musa town centre. Modern furnishings, good Wi-Fi, reliable hot water. Slightly cheaper than Petra Moon at $60-110 USD per night.

Sella Hotel. On Tourism Street, about 8 minutes from the gate. Older property than Petra Moon but renovated rooms, consistent reviews, good value at $70-110 USD per night. The kind of hotel that doesn't excite but reliably delivers.

P Quattro Relax Hotel. Slightly further from the gate (about 15-minute walk or 5-minute taxi), more modern decor than the older options. Often available on Booking.com at discounted rates ($60-100 USD). The compromise on location is real but the property is comfortable.

What to look for in a mid-range Wadi Musa hotel

The features that actually matter at this price tier:

Walking distance to the gate. Under 10 minutes is the right target. The 06:00 Treasury entry is one of the strongest arguments for staying in Wadi Musa at all, and walking distance to the gate makes that practical. Hotels 20+ minutes uphill require taxis (3-5 JOD each way, multiple times daily) that erase the price savings.

Included breakfast. Should be a full Jordanian buffet, not just continental pastries. Look for reviews specifically mentioning eggs, hummus, labneh, fresh bread, and fruit. Cold-cuts and instant coffee are warning signs.

Reliable hot water and Wi-Fi. Both are sometimes inconsistent at lower price tiers in Wadi Musa. Recent reviews mentioning these specifically (positively or negatively) are reliable indicators.

Air conditioning that works. Wadi Musa gets genuinely hot in summer (35°C+ in June-August). Check recent summer reviews for AC mentions.

Honest representation in photos. Booking platform photos can be misleading. Recent guest photos in reviews (where available) are more accurate.

What you don't get at mid-range

Worth knowing what you're giving up vs the luxury options:

  • Pool. Most mid-range Wadi Musa hotels don't have one, or have small/seasonal ones. The Mövenpick and Marriott pools are real amenities.
  • 24-hour reception. Family-run mid-range hotels often have limited overnight staffing — call ahead if you're arriving after 22:00.
  • Multiple restaurant options. Mid-range hotels typically have one in-house restaurant plus breakfast. The luxury options have 3-4.
  • Spa, fitness centre, business amenities. Generally absent.
  • Consistent international-brand service training. Family-run service is warmer but more variable.

For most travelers, none of these are dealbreakers. For 1-2 night Petra-focused stays, the mid-range tier delivers everything that matters at meaningfully lower cost.

The booking strategy

Three useful tactics for mid-range Wadi Musa hotel booking:

1. Use Booking.com or Hotels.com with free cancellation. Rates fluctuate in the 30-60 days before peak season. Free-cancellation booking lets you rebook if rates drop. The small markup over direct-booking is worth the flexibility.

2. Filter by "review score 8.5+". The Wadi Musa mid-range market has some properties that look fine on photos but disappoint in person. Recent guest reviews filter these out.

3. Look at photos from guests, not hotel-provided photos. The hotel-supplied images on Booking.com are often the most flattering possible representation. Guest-uploaded photos show the real condition.

Frequently asked questions

What's the mid-range price tier in Wadi Musa?
Roughly $60-150 USD per night in 2026, depending on season. The sweet spot for most travelers sits around $80-120 USD per night for a comfortable hotel within walking distance of the Petra gate. Below $60 typically means budget hostel territory; above $150 means you're in the luxury price range without quite the luxury experience.
Should I stay near the Visitor Centre or in Wadi Musa town?
Visitor Centre area is more expensive but lets you walk to the gate in under 10 minutes — meaningful for 06:00 Treasury entry. Wadi Musa town is 1-2 km uphill, cheaper, and closer to the local restaurants. For most mid-range travelers, the Visitor Centre area or properties within 500m of the gate (like Petra Moon Hotel) offer the best balance. The further uphill you go, the more you'll spend on taxis.
Which mid-range hotels are most consistently recommended?
Petra Moon Hotel (5-minute walk to gate, rooftop terrace, family-run), Petra Canyon Hotel (closer to town centre, mid-range comfort), Sella Hotel (Tourism Street, reliable for the price), and P Quattro Relax Hotel (slightly further from gate, modern decor, often discounted). All four sit in the $70-130 USD range and have consistent 4+ star averages across booking platforms.
Is breakfast included at mid-range Wadi Musa hotels?
Almost always — and it matters more than you'd think. Most Wadi Musa mid-range hotels include a generous Jordanian breakfast (eggs, hummus, labneh, fresh bread, fruit, coffee) that sets you up for a full Petra day. Skipping breakfast and eating in Petra adds 8-15 JOD per person at the Basin Restaurant or the Bedouin tea stalls. The included breakfast is often the single best amenity in the mid-range price tier.
When should I book my mid-range Petra hotel?
For shoulder and peak seasons (March-May and September-November), book 1-2 months ahead — the best-located mid-range options sell out for Easter, the autumn peak, and major holidays. For summer and winter, 2-3 weeks ahead is fine. Booking through Booking.com or Hotels.com with free-cancellation flexibility is the safest play; rates sometimes drop in the 30-60 days before peak season as inventory clears, and the flex policy lets you re-book at the lower rate.

Itineraries

Three days in Tbilisi

The format most trips should book: the complete two-day city, then a third day pointed at the mountains, the wine valley, or the ancient capital — chosen by temperament and forecast.

Last reviewed: June 7, 2026

Days one and two run the complete city plan — right bank, bath, and golden-hour Narikala first; Sameba, the Dry Bridge, and Mtatsminda's sunset second. Day three leaves town, and the page you're reading is really a decision guide for which direction.

North — Kazbegi. The Military Highway, Ananuri, the Jvari Pass, and Gergeti Trinity against the glacier: Georgia's defining image at the price of a twelve-hour day and a weather lottery. Book it when the forecast is clear and your legs have one more climb in them. East — Kakheti. Sighnaghi's walls, family cellars, amber wine from buried qvevri, and a supra-shaped lunch: the pleasure vote, and the right one for food-led trips — note the transport math on that page, since tastings rule out self-drive. West — Mtskheta, alone as a gentle half-day or stacked with Uplistsikhe and Gori into the history day: lowest effort, earliest return, and the strongest companion to everything the city days taught you.

Whichever direction, the mechanics rhyme: early pickup (coffee accordingly), window seat on the right side going north, cash for site extras like the Gergeti 4x4, layers for altitude, and a final-evening plan that tolerates a 20:00 return. The day trips overview carries the format decision — group, private driver, or independent — that turns the chosen direction into a booked plan.

The winter variant

November through March, invert the order: book Kazbegi for day two, keep day three's city plan in your pocket, and let a pass-closure cancellation swap the days at zero cost — the tour operators decide by the prior evening, and Kakheti or Mtskheta absorb the re-route happily since neither cares about snow. The city half of the plan, meanwhile, improves in winter: the lanes empty, the baths reach their full meaning, and the supra moves indoors where it was always headed.

Choosing your day trip

Three days is where Tbilisi stops being only a city break, and the third day's day trip is the decision that defines the trip. Drama and mountains? Kazbegi — the Gergeti church under a 5,000-meter peak — is the unforgettable one, but it's a long day and weather-dependent, so in winter book it early in the stay so a closed pass can slide to another day. Wine and gentler country? Kakheti trades the peaks for cellars, the hilltop town of Sighnaghi, and the qvevri wine at its source. History on a half-day? Uplistsikhe and Gori pack a cave city and the Stalin museum into less time, leaving an afternoon back in town.

However you spend it, structure the three days so the city builds to the escape: the compact core and a sunset on day one, the far bank and a second viewpoint on day two, the day trip on day three when you've found your feet and your favorite breakfast. Leave the last evening soft — a long supra-style dinner or a final soak — rather than racing one more sight. And if three days only makes you want more, that's the cue to read beyond the day trips, where Svaneti, the coast, and the western monasteries turn a long weekend into a country.

Frequently asked questions

Which day trip should the third day take?

Kazbegi for spectacle, Kakheti for pleasure, Mtskheta-plus-Uplistsikhe for history — the choice is temperament, not quality. The tiebreakers: clear forecast strongly favors Kazbegi (the mountain hides in cloud); wine-led trips favor Kakheti; tight energy budgets favor Mtskheta’s half-day. Our day trips overview compares all four routes properly.

Should the day trip go on day two or three?

Day three, normally — the city days build the context that makes the excursion land, and tour pickups are early. The exception is winter Kazbegi: book it for day two so a weather cancellation can slide to day three instead of off the calendar.

What time do the day trips return?

Kazbegi and Kakheti group tours land back between 19:00 and 21:00 — plan a low-key final dinner near your hotel rather than a reservation you’ll sweat. Mtskheta-based days return with the whole evening intact, which is the quiet argument for it on departure-eve.

Can three days work with a late-night or dawn flight?

Yes — Tbilisi’s many small-hours departures pair naturally with this plan: run day three’s excursion, dinner, the bath if you skipped it, and head to TBS late. Confirm hotel luggage storage and see our airport page for the night-transfer picture.

How does this extend to four or five days?

Take the second big day trip (Kazbegi and Kakheti are different enough to justify both), and give the spare half-day to Vera’s cafes or the botanical garden. Past five days, stop extending and start moving — the Georgia cluster covers Batumi, Kutaisi, and where a week-plus trip should point.

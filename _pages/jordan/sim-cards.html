JORDAN

SIM cards and mobile data

Three carriers, three approaches. The Zain/Orange/Umniah tourist SIM packages, the increasingly viable eSIM alternative, and the practical coverage situation at Petra and Wadi Rum. Here's what actually works.

Last reviewed June 5, 2026 · 6-minute read · See our editorial standards

Mobile connectivity in Jordan is generally excellent — better than most travelers expect. The country has three competing carriers running modern 4G networks (5G is rolling out gradually in 2026), coverage in major cities and tourist destinations is essentially universal, and tourist SIM packages are widely available at reasonable prices. For travelers staying longer than 2-3 days, the math heavily favors a local SIM or eSIM over international roaming.

The three local carriers

Zain — the largest carrier by network coverage and the safest choice for travelers prioritizing reliability in remote areas. Strongest signal in Wadi Rum (including at most Bedouin camps), best coverage along Desert Highway and King's Highway driving routes. Tourist packages start around 10 JOD for 5 GB / 7 days.

Orange — strong network in cities and major tourist destinations, sometimes faster data speeds than Zain in central Amman. Coverage outside cities is acceptable but thinner. Tourist packages 10-15 JOD for 5-10 GB.

Umniah — smaller carrier with the cheapest base prices but the most limited network coverage. Fine for travelers spending time in Amman and Petra, less reliable for Wadi Rum and remote areas.

The eSIM option

For travelers on modern phones (iPhone XS or later, recent Android flagships), eSIM is increasingly the best option for short trips. Three reputable providers sell Jordan-specific eSIM data plans:

  • Airalo — the most popular. 1 GB / 7 days for $5, 5 GB / 30 days for $13, 10 GB / 30 days for $22.
  • Holafly — unlimited data plans. 5 days unlimited for $19, 10 days for $34.
  • Nomad — competitive pricing similar to Airalo.

The eSIM advantage: scan a QR code before arrival, your data plan activates the moment you turn on phone roaming in Jordan, your home number stays active for incoming SMS (useful for 2FA codes). The downside: data-only — you don't get a Jordanian phone number, so you can't be called by Jordanian numbers without messaging apps.

Where to buy a physical SIM

Queen Alia airport. Zain, Orange, and Umniah all have kiosks immediately past customs. Bring your passport — required for SIM registration in Jordan. Process takes 10-20 minutes; you walk away with an active SIM. Prices 10-20% higher than in-town stores but the convenience is significant.

Aqaba airport (AQJ). Zain and Orange kiosks in the arrivals area. Same essential process as Queen Alia.

Official carrier stores. In Amman, Petra, and Aqaba. Better prices and more package options. Worth visiting if you want a specific plan or are staying multiple weeks.

Convenience stores. Many small shops sell pre-paid SIMs. Cheap but the language barrier can be frustrating.

What you need to register

Jordanian law requires SIM card registration with passport identification. Bring your passport (the kiosk staff scans it), your phone (so they can install and activate the SIM), and payment (most kiosks accept cash and international cards). Total time: 10-15 minutes.

Coverage by destination

Amman: Excellent across all three carriers. Full 4G everywhere. 5G rolling out in 2026 on Zain and Orange.

Petra: Excellent in Wadi Musa town. Inside the Petra site itself, 4G works for most of the main routes (Siq, Treasury, Royal Tombs, colonnaded street), with signal weakening in deep canyons and at the Monastery. Coverage at the High Place and on the Al-Khubtha trail is intermittent.

Wadi Rum: The main constraint. The Visitor Centre has good coverage on all carriers. Inside the protected area, signal varies dramatically by camp — some have strong Zain signal, others have essentially no service. Most camps have Wi-Fi at common areas which fills the gap. Don't plan critical online work from a Wadi Rum camp.

Aqaba: Excellent in the city center, around the south beach resorts, and along the corniche.

King's Highway and remote driving routes: Mostly good but with gaps. Zain has the most consistent coverage along Route 35; Orange and Umniah have spotty signal in some sections.

Practical tips

  • Download offline maps before arrival. Google Maps offline regions prevent any coverage gap from becoming a navigation problem.
  • Use WhatsApp for everything. Jordanians use WhatsApp universally — hotel confirmations, taxi arrangements, and tour operator communications all happen on WhatsApp.
  • Keep your home SIM as backup. If you're using a physical Jordanian SIM, keep your home SIM somewhere safe for the return home.

Frequently asked questions

Should I buy a Jordanian SIM?
For trips longer than 2-3 days, yes. A tourist SIM with 10-20 GB of data costs 10-25 JOD ($14-35 USD) and saves you the cost of international roaming. For shorter trips and travelers on cheap European roaming plans, your existing SIM may be fine. eSIM options (Airalo, Holafly) are an increasingly good middle ground.
Which carrier is best?
Three carriers operate in Jordan: Zain (largest network, most reliable in remote areas), Orange (good coverage in cities, sometimes faster data), and Umniah (smaller network, typically cheaper). For travelers prioritizing coverage at Petra and Wadi Rum, Zain is the safest choice.
Where do I buy?
Three good options. (1) Queen Alia airport — Zain, Orange, and Umniah all have kiosks past customs. Convenient, marginally higher prices. (2) Official carrier stores in Amman or major tourist destinations — best prices, more friction. (3) Convenience stores throughout Jordan — quick and cheap but the staff may not speak English. The airport is the right call for most travelers.
What about eSIMs?
Increasingly the best option for short trips. Airalo: 5 GB / 30 days for $13. Holafly: unlimited 5 days for $19. Nomad: similar to Airalo. Advantages: scan a QR code before arrival, instant activation, your home number stays active. Downside: data-only (no Jordanian phone number for local calls).
Coverage at Petra and Wadi Rum?
Petra and Wadi Musa town have full 4G coverage on all three networks — usable for most of the main archaeological site, weaker in the deep canyons. Wadi Rum is patchier — the Visitor Centre has good coverage, but inside the protected area, signal varies by camp. Most camps have Wi-Fi at the common areas.
Will my US/EU phone work in Jordan?
Almost certainly yes. Jordan uses GSM networks on the standard 900/1800 MHz bands plus 4G LTE bands compatible with most US, European, and Asian phones. The main concern is carrier lock and roaming charges — check that your phone is unlocked before traveling.

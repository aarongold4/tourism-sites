ITINERARIES

Petra in one day

The compressed visit. Start at 06:00, walk steadily, and accept that you'll skip about a third of what Petra has to offer. Here's the hour-by-hour plan that gets you the best one-day version possible.

Last reviewed April 28, 2026 · 9-minute read · See our editorial standards

A one-day Petra visit works as a curated rather than comprehensive trip. The site is too large to do justice in eight or nine hours of walking, and the most rewarding parts (the Monastery, the high-place trails, Petra by Night) require either elevation or the right time of day. What one day can give you is the headline experience: the Siq, the Treasury, the Royal Tombs, the lower city, the Roman theatre, and one of the two major climbs. That's enough to understand why Petra justifies the trip and to leave wanting to come back.

This itinerary assumes you're staying overnight in Wadi Musa (the only way to make 06:00 entry work) and that you've already purchased a Jordan Pass or your standard 50 JOD Petra ticket. If you're day-tripping from Amman or Eilat, see the relevant transport pages — the schedule below will compress further. Otherwise, here's the hour-by-hour plan.

05:30 — Wake

Eat the breakfast your hotel left out (most Wadi Musa hotels can arrange a 05:30 breakfast box if you ask the night before), fill two water bottles, and walk or short-taxi to the Petra Visitor Centre. Have your Jordan Pass or printed ticket ready. The gate opens at 06:00.

06:00 — Enter, walk to the Siq

The gate opens at 06:00. Walk the 800 meters from the Visitor Centre down to the Siq entrance. Pass the Djinn Blocks (three large carved Nabataean cubic monuments on the right) and the Obelisk Tomb (an early Nabataean tomb facade on the left). Most travelers walk through these without stopping; they're worth two minutes of attention each.

06:20 — The Siq

Enter the Siq. The 1.2-kilometer slot canyon walk takes 20–30 minutes at an unhurried pace, but consider going slower — look at the Nabataean water channels carved into the walls on both sides, the carved camel-and-trader reliefs about halfway through (right side and left side, separately), and the small votive niches that once held figures of Nabataean gods. The Siq in early morning is in deep shadow, which makes the Treasury reveal at the end more dramatic. See the Siq page for what to look for in detail.

06:50 — The Treasury

The canyon opens onto the Treasury plaza. The Treasury is in direct morning sunlight, facing east — this is the only time of day the facade is genuinely illuminated rather than in shadow. Take photographs. The plaza is mostly empty until around 09:00. Spend 30 minutes here.

07:20 — Street of Facades and Royal Tombs

Continue past the Treasury into the Outer Siq, where the Street of Facades stretches along the right side — a row of carved Nabataean tomb facades, smaller than the Treasury but in the same style. The path curves left and opens onto the lower city. The Royal Tombs are carved into the eastern cliff face on your right — four monumental facades (Urn, Silk, Corinthian, Palace), visible from below.

Climb the carved steps up to the Urn Tomb terrace. Walk along the row of tombs, enter the Urn Tomb (look for Bishop Jason's 446 CE inscription on the inside back wall — evidence of the tomb's Byzantine conversion to a church). The view from the Urn Tomb terrace back over the lower city is one of the best in Petra. Allow 45 minutes total.

08:15 — Roman theatre and colonnaded street

Descend from the Royal Tombs and walk to the Roman theatre, carved directly into the cliff face. The lower seats are accessible; you can sit briefly and imagine the 4,000 spectators it once held. Continue along the colonnaded street, lined with reconstructed Roman columns, past the Great Temple on your left and Qasr al-Bint at the far end. Allow 45 minutes.

09:00 — Begin the Monastery climb

The trailhead is behind the Basin Restaurant. The 800-step climb to the Monastery takes 45–60 minutes at a steady pace. There are three Bedouin tea stalls along the way — rest at the first one if you need it. The trail is steep in places but doable for anyone with reasonable fitness. About two-thirds of the way up, the trail passes the Lion Triclinium (a small rock-cut chamber with two carved lions); worth a five-minute detour.

10:00 — The Monastery

The Monastery (Ad-Deir) emerges suddenly to your right as you reach the top. The facade is 48 meters wide and 47 meters tall, larger than the Treasury and arguably more impressive in person. The cafe across the terrace from the facade serves tea (drink one) with one of the great views in Petra. Walk past the cafe along the marked path to the viewpoint, about 10 minutes further, for the panorama out across the western desert. Spend 45 minutes here total.

11:00 — Descend the Monastery trail

The descent takes 30–45 minutes. Faster than the climb up, but watch your footing — the steps are uneven and polished smooth by centuries of foot traffic.

11:45 — Lunch at the Basin Restaurant

The Basin Restaurant sits at the foot of the Monastery trail, in the lower city. The buffet is around 18–20 JOD per person, with reasonable Jordanian food and shaded outdoor seating. Take your time — you've earned it, and the afternoon walk back is easier with a real meal in you. Allow 60–90 minutes.

13:00 — Walk back through the lower city

From the Basin, walk back along the colonnaded street. You can either retrace the morning route exactly, or take a short detour to the Great Temple (climb to the upper terrace for a different angle on the lower city) and the small Byzantine Church with surviving mosaic floors. Both add about 20 minutes each.

14:30 — Royal Tombs in afternoon light

The Royal Tombs face west and are illuminated by the afternoon sun — this is when the rose-red color really earns its name. Walk back along the terrace, photographing each of the four tombs in their better light. This is the visit's photography highlight if you haven't been hurrying.

15:15 — Treasury walk-through, again

By mid-afternoon, the Treasury is in shadow and the morning crowds have thinned dramatically. This is the quieter version of the Treasury — fewer tour groups, softer light, more contemplative. Spend 20 minutes here.

15:45 — The Siq walk back

The 1.2-kilometer Siq walk takes 30 minutes at an unhurried pace. By late afternoon, the canyon catches the western light differently than it did in the morning — slanting rather than overhead. Take a different lens to your photographs than you used in the morning.

16:15 — Exit

You exit through the Visitor Centre. The Petra Archaeological Museum is open until 17:00 if you want a final 30 minutes with the carved sculptures and artifacts pulled from the excavations. If you're tired (you should be — you've walked roughly 12 km on uneven ground), skip the museum and head back to your hotel for an early dinner.

What you skipped

The honest version of this itinerary: you skipped Al-Khubtha (the trail to the famous Treasury viewpoint), the High Place of Sacrifice with the Wadi Farasa loop, Little Petra, Petra by Night, the museum (probably), and the back routes. You also didn't have time to slow down at any single monument. A two-day visit fixes most of this. If you have a second day available, go to the two-day itinerary instead — the gains are substantial.

Frequently asked questions

Is one day really enough for Petra?
Barely. One day works only if you start at 06:00, walk at a steady pace, and accept that you'll skip either the Monastery climb or the high-place trails. You will not have time for Petra by Night, Al-Khubtha, Little Petra, or the museum. For travelers on a tight Jordan loop who specifically chose to compress Petra to a day, the one-day plan below makes the best of it. If you can possibly stretch to two days, do — see the two-day itinerary instead.
What time should I arrive at the gate?
06:00. Set an alarm. The site officially opens at sunrise — typically around 06:30 in winter and 05:30 in summer — and the Treasury is genuinely yours for the first 90 minutes after you enter. By 09:30 the first tour buses arrive from Amman and the plaza fills up. By 10:30 it's the Treasury photo crowd in full force. Every minute you can buy yourself before 09:30 is worth ten minutes after 10:30.
Should I do the Monastery or the High Place of Sacrifice?
In a one-day visit, Monastery. The Monastery is the larger monument (48 meters wide versus the Treasury's 40), the climb is part of the experience, and the cafe at the top with tea looking at the facade is one of the great moments of any Petra trip. The High Place is excellent but the descent route through Wadi Farasa, which makes it most rewarding, requires more time than a one-day visit allows. Save the High Place for your second visit (you'll come back).
Where should I have lunch?
The Basin Restaurant at the foot of the Monastery trail is the only formal restaurant inside the site. It's a buffet for around 18–20 JOD per person, with reasonable Jordanian food and a great location for resting before the Monastery climb. The alternative is to pack a sandwich from your Amman or Wadi Musa hotel and eat at one of the Bedouin tea stalls partway up the trail. Both work; the Basin is more comfortable, the trail option is cheaper.
Where do I exit at the end of the day?
The same way you came in — back through the lower city, past the Treasury, and through the Siq to the Visitor Centre. The walk takes about 90 minutes from the Monastery trailhead. This means you need to start your final descent by 14:30 if you want to be out by 16:00 (the realistic time for the last Treasury walk-through with thinning crowds and good late-afternoon light). Allow extra time on a hot day or if you're tired from the Monastery climb.
Can I do this as a day trip from Amman or Eilat?
Technically yes; it compresses further. A day trip from Amman gives you 5–6 hours on site (10:00 to 16:00 typical schedule), which is enough for the Siq, Treasury, Royal Tombs from below, and either a quick Monastery attempt or the lower city — not both. A day trip from Eilat gives you 4–5 hours on site after the border crossing and additional drive. Both work but produce a partial Petra experience. The "real" one-day itinerary below assumes you're staying overnight in Wadi Musa.

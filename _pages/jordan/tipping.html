JORDAN

Tipping in Jordan

Expected but at lower rates than US norms. The practical guide — restaurant 10%, hotel staff small amounts, Petra and Bedouin guides via day-end cash, taxi rounding. Here's what each situation actually requires.

Last reviewed June 5, 2026 · 6-minute read · See our editorial standards

Tipping in Jordan sits somewhere between the heavy US tipping culture and the very modest tipping of some European countries. Service workers — restaurant servers, hotel staff, guides, drivers — genuinely depend on tip income, but the percentages and amounts are lower than American visitors typically expect. The right framing: tip thoughtfully and consistently at standard rates rather than either overcompensating or stiffing entirely.

Restaurants

Standard: 10% of the pre-tax bill. Some upscale restaurants automatically add a 10% "service charge" to the bill — check before you tip again. If you see "service charge" on the bill, you've already tipped at standard rates; an additional 1-2 JOD for excellent service is appreciated but not required.

For casual and mid-range restaurants without an added service charge, leave 10% in cash. Round up to convenient numbers — a 27 JOD bill becomes 30 JOD. Street food and quick-service places (Hashem in downtown Amman, falafel shops): no tip expected, though rounding up to the nearest dinar is appreciated.

Hotels

Bellhops: 1-2 JOD per bag carried. The bellhop who takes your bags to the room: 2-3 JOD total.

Housekeeping: 1-2 JOD per night. Leave on the pillow on departure day, or distribute daily.

Concierge: No tip for routine help. For substantial help (booking a Petra tour, arranging a driver): 3-5 JOD. For major help (resolving problems): 5-10 JOD.

Restaurant and bar staff: Same 10% as any restaurant. Cash tips reach the staff more reliably than charged tips.

Spa therapists at Dead Sea resorts: 10% on the treatment cost.

Petra guides

Licensed Petra guides charge 50-80 JOD for a half-day, 80-150 JOD for a full day. A 10% tip is standard for good work — 5-15 JOD depending on day length. Tip in cash at the end of the visit.

Bedouin guides on trails typically charge 60-100 JOD for the day. A 10-15 JOD tip on top is appropriate. These are often longer, more physical days than the licensed-guide work, and the Bedouin guides have specialized local knowledge.

Bedouin shopkeepers and donkey owners: No tipping required beyond what you pay for the service. If you want to acknowledge warm service, an extra 1-2 JOD is appreciated.

Drivers

Yellow taxis: Round up to convenient numbers. A 4.50 JOD fare becomes 5 JOD. No additional percentage tip needed. Insist on the meter ("addad") when you get in.

Uber and Careem: Fares are set by the app; you can add a tip through the app after the ride (10-15% if you choose). Drivers prefer cash tips over app tips.

Airport transfers and one-way private transfers: 5-10 JOD per leg. The Queen Alia to Amman transfer (22 JOD fixed fare): 5 JOD tip. The Amman to Petra private transfer (80-100 JOD): 10 JOD tip.

Multi-day hired drivers: If you've hired a driver for a multi-day tour (typical pricing 80-150 JOD per day), tip 10-15 JOD per day at the end of the trip. A 4-day Jordan trip warrants a 50-60 JOD tip on top of the agreed daily rate.

JETT bus and public transport: No tipping. The fare covers the service.

Wadi Rum Bedouin camps

Wadi Rum camps involve multiple staff over a 1-2 night stay, and tipping conventions are different from hotels:

  • Jeep tour driver/guide: 5-10 JOD at the end of the tour, in cash, directly to the driver.
  • Camp host who handles meals and arrangements: 5-10 JOD on departure.
  • Optional sunrise guide: 3-5 JOD if you do the early morning tour.
  • Special services (balloon flight, camel trek): 5-10 JOD on top of the service cost.

Many camps have a tip jar at the common area for general staff gratuities. Total tipping at a 2-night Wadi Rum stay: 15-30 JOD per person, on top of the camp fee.

Other situations

Mosque or church visits: Small donation expected at religious sites (1-5 JOD). The donation supports building maintenance.

Bathroom attendants: 25-50 piasters in small coins is standard if there's a tip jar.

Cooking class instructors (Beit Sitti, Petra Kitchen): 5-10 JOD per person tip is appreciated at the end of the class.

Petra by Night musician: The Bedouin musician performing at the Treasury plaza traditionally accepts tips after the performance. 1-3 JOD is appropriate.

When NOT to tip

  • JETT bus drivers and public transport
  • Government workers at sites (ticket booths, security)
  • Hotel front-desk reception for routine check-in
  • Tour group leaders on large organized tours where gratuities are pooled and collected separately at the end

Budgeting for tips

For a 7-day Jordan trip with the typical mix of activities (Petra guide, Bedouin camp, restaurant meals, hotel stays, private transfers), budget roughly 70-120 JOD per person for total trip tipping. Keep small bills (1 and 5 JOD notes) in your daypack at all times — the single most common tipping mistake is having only large bills when small tips are needed.

Frequently asked questions

Is tipping expected in Jordan?
Yes, but at lower percentages than US tipping culture. Jordan is a service-economy country where tips genuinely matter to people's incomes. The right framing: tipping is expected and appreciated but not punitive if you miss occasional small situations. Standard rates run roughly half of US norms.
How much at restaurants?
10% is the standard. Some upscale restaurants automatically add a 10% service charge — if you see this on the bill, additional tipping isn't required. For mid-range and casual restaurants without an added service charge, leave 10% in cash. Round up to convenient numbers.
How much for Petra guides?
Licensed Petra guides charge 50-80 JOD for a half-day. A 10% tip (5-10 JOD) is standard for good service. For Bedouin guides on longer trails (Jebel Haroun, High Place + Wadi Farasa), 10-15 JOD tip on top of the 60-100 JOD day fee. Tip in cash at the end of the day.
What about drivers?
Yellow taxis: round up to convenient numbers — a 4.50 JOD fare becomes 5 JOD. Private transfers (airport-hotel, Amman-Petra): 5-10 JOD per leg. Multi-day hired drivers: 10-15 JOD per day on top of the agreed daily rate.
Hotel staff?
Bellhops: 1-2 JOD per bag. Housekeeping: 1-2 JOD per night (leave on the pillow). Concierge for substantial help: 3-5 JOD. Spa therapists: 10% on the treatment cost.
Bedouin camps in Wadi Rum?
Jeep tour driver/guide: 5-10 JOD. Camp host who handles meals: 5-10 JOD. Optional sunrise guide: 3-5 JOD. Total tipping at a 2-night Wadi Rum stay: 15-30 JOD per person.

Georgia Travel

Kutaisi

Georgia's third city is two things at once: the budget-flight gateway most travelers transfer straight through, and an ancient western capital whose monasteries and canyons reward anyone who lingers.

Last reviewed: June 7, 2026

Kutaisi has been a capital before — of the kingdom of Colchis, of the medieval Georgian kingdom — and that deep history sits lightly on a pleasant, low-key modern city most visitors only glimpse from a transfer coach. The reason to stop is just outside town. Gelati Monastery, the UNESCO-listed twelfth-century foundation of King David the Builder, holds some of the finest medieval mosaics and frescoes in the Caucasus, and the hilltop Bagrati Cathedral presides over the city itself. Beyond them lie the dramatic karst of Prometheus Cave and the Martvili and Okatse canyons — the green, watery west at its most cinematic.

For most travelers Kutaisi arrives as logistics: the budget-flight gateway, with coaches timed to flights for the roughly four-hour run to Tbilisi. The question this page really answers is whether to treat it as a corridor or a chapter — and the answer turns on your arrival time and your appetite for monasteries and gorges.

Stop or pass through?

Land mid-day with time and energy, and a Kutaisi night converts dead transfer hours into Gelati and a canyon — one full day does the monasteries plus one gorge, two days does it all unhurried. Land at 3 a.m. on a cheap flight, and the honest move is to transfer straight to the capital and sleep. Either way it pairs naturally with Batumi and the coast for a western loop, with the train carrying you back east to Tbilisi rested. A base for the green west

Treated as a base rather than a corridor, Kutaisi unlocks the lushest corner of the country. Gelati Monastery is the essential: a UNESCO-listed twelfth-century foundation of David the Builder, its main church lined with mosaics and frescoes that rank among the finest medieval art in the Caucasus, with the king's own grave at the gate. The hilltop Bagrati Cathedral presides over the city, and just out of town the karst landscape opens into Prometheus Cave, with its lit galleries and underground river, and the Martvili and Okatse canyons, where boardwalks and boat rides thread emerald gorges. The colorful central market and the green Bagrati hill fill any spare hours.

The practical rhythm is simple: one full day does Gelati, Bagrati, and one cave or canyon; two days does the monasteries and the gorges unhurried; beyond that, move on. For most travelers Kutaisi arrives as the budget-flight gateway, and the smart move is to convert the transfer into a stop rather than fight through it — a daytime arrival with energy buys Gelati before you ever reach the capital. It loops naturally with Batumi and the coast, with the train carrying you back east, and slots into a longer Georgia trip as the western chapter the itineraries sketch out.

Frequently asked questions

Is Kutaisi worth stopping in, or just an airport?

Both questions have a yes. Most travelers meet Kutaisi only as the budget-flight gateway and transfer straight to Tbilisi — but a night or two unlocks Gelati Monastery, Prometheus Cave, the canyons, and an ancient, low-key city that rewards the unhurried. If your schedule allows, breaking the journey here beats treating it as a corridor.

What are the must-sees?

Gelati Monastery — a UNESCO-listed twelfth-century complex with extraordinary mosaics and frescoes — is the headline. Add the Bagrati Cathedral above the city, the dramatic Prometheus Cave and Martvili and Okatse canyons nearby, and the colorful central market. The monasteries and canyons are the reason to linger; the city itself is pleasant rather than spectacular.

How does the airport transfer work?

Coach companies time departures to flight arrivals and sell seats online; the ride to central Tbilisi is roughly three and a half to four hours. Book it with your flight. If you’re stopping in Kutaisi first, taxis and local transport cover the short hops to Gelati and the caves.

Kutaisi or straight to Tbilisi?

If you land mid-day with energy and time, a Kutaisi night turns dead transfer time into Gelati and a canyon. If you land at 3 a.m. on a budget flight, transfer straight to Tbilisi and sleep. The deciding factors are your arrival time, your total days, and whether medieval monasteries and gorges are your thing.

How long do I need?

One full day covers Gelati, Bagrati, and either Prometheus Cave or a canyon. Two days lets you do the monasteries and the gorges without rushing. More than that and you’re better off moving on to Batumi and the coast or back to the capital.

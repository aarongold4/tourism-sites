Getting There

Bolt & taxis

Bolt didn't just enter the Tbilisi taxi market — it replaced it, for visitors especially. Here's how to use it well, and where the old overcharge economy still lurks.

Last reviewed: June 7, 2026

The before-times are worth one paragraph because they explain the present: Tbilisi street taxis ran unmetered, fares were negotiations, and visitors funded the gap between the local price and the optimistic one. Ride-hailing ended that — fares pre-agreed in the app, routes tracked, drivers named — and Bolt won the market so completely that "take a Bolt" is simply how the city says taxi. For visitors the value stack is total: no haggling, no language friction, no change games, and prices low enough that crossing the city on a whim is a non-decision.

Use it like a local: card attached before travel, pickup pins placed where cars can actually stop (the Old Town's stair-lanes defeat them — walk to the nearest real street), and a second app (Yandex Go) in your pocket for the ten-second price comparison locals run reflexively. Surge hours exist — rain plus rush, post-event crowds — and the app shows you the damage before you accept, which is the entire civilizational upgrade in one screen.

Where the old economy survives

The overcharge traps cluster exactly where you'd guess: the airport curb, the station forecourt, and the bath district late at night, wherever a tired tourist might accept a quoted "fixed price" that runs multiples of the app fare. The defenses are boring and complete: order in the app, or use the airport's official desk, or — if street-hailing is genuinely the only option — agree the fare aloud before the door closes. None of this reflects on Georgian drivers generally, who are overwhelmingly straight; it reflects on the incentives of unmetered curbs everywhere on earth.

Working the app like a local

Bolt is the operating system of getting around Tbilisi, and it has effectively retired the old street-taxi haggle. Set it up before you need it: install the app, add a card (or choose cash, which drivers accept), and you'll see an up-front fare rather than a meter to argue about. Rides across the center cost a couple of dollars, even the airport run stays cheap, and the app's record of driver and route is a real safety margin a flagged-down cab can't match. Yandex Go is the main alternative and works similarly; having both installed covers you when one is slow to match at peak hours or in the rain.

A few local frictions are worth pre-empting. Pin accuracy matters in the Old Town, where car-free lanes and stairs mean the app may set a pickup a short walk from where you're standing — move to a real street and watch the driver's dot rather than assuming they'll reach your door. Many drivers speak limited English, so the in-app destination and the map do the talking; a pinned drop-off beats a spoken address every time. Cars are often older and air-conditioning is not guaranteed in summer, which the fare reflects. Keep a little cash as backup for the rare card hiccup, and for the airport specifically, use the designated rideshare pickup and ignore the arrivals-hall touts entirely.

Frequently asked questions

How much do Bolt rides cost in Tbilisi?

Cheap enough that the question rarely matters: rides across the center cost pocket change by Western standards, and even airport runs stay modest. Surge pricing exists at rain-soaked rush hours and after big events — visible in the app before you commit, which is the entire point of the app.

Card or cash in the app?

Attach a card before you travel and the whole system goes frictionless — no change theater, receipts in-app, fares pre-agreed. Cash payment is supported if you prefer; drivers appreciate small notes.

Are street taxis ever the right call?

Rarely, for visitors. The unmetered street-taxi economy is exactly what Bolt displaced, and the remaining overcharge traps cluster where tourists do: the airport curb, the station, the bath district at night. If you must street-hail, agree the fare before the door closes — in lari, out loud.

Is Bolt safe at night / for solo travelers?

It’s the standard local answer to that exact question: tracked rides, named drivers, in-app sharing of your trip. Apply the universal checks — plate matches, name matches — and Tbilisi’s generally calm night streets do the rest. Our safety page covers the wider picture.

Any alternatives to Bolt?

Yandex Go operates too and occasionally undercuts; many locals run both apps and take the cheaper quote. The comparison takes ten seconds and the winner changes by hour.

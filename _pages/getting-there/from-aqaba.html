GETTING THERE

How to get to Petra from Aqaba

130 kilometers, two hours by road, and increasingly the better arrival point for Petra-focused trips. The JETT bus, shared minibus, and private taxi options compared, with current 2026 pricing.

Last reviewed April 28, 2026 · 7-minute read · See our editorial standards

Aqaba sits on the Red Sea at Jordan's southern tip, two hours by road from Petra and about an hour from the Wadi Rum entrance. King Hussein International Airport (AQJ) handles direct flights from London, Istanbul, and several European cities, which makes Aqaba an increasingly attractive arrival point for travelers focused on Petra and Wadi Rum without much interest in Amman. The trip from Aqaba to Petra is roughly half the distance of the Amman-Petra leg, and the road is essentially the same Desert Highway from a few hours further south.

Three transport options matter: the JETT bus (cheapest scheduled service), a shared minibus or "service" taxi from the local bus station (cheapest overall, but unreliable schedule), and a private taxi or hired driver (most flexible, particularly from the airport). Rental cars are also widely available — rates start around $30 USD per day from agencies at the airport.

The JETT bus

JETT runs scheduled bus service between Aqaba and Petra. The route is less heavily booked than the Amman-Petra run but follows similar logistics:

  • Aqaba → Petra: Typically 08:00 departure, arrives Wadi Musa around 10:30. A second daily departure runs in peak season.
  • Petra → Aqaba: Typically 17:00 departure, arrives Aqaba around 19:00.
  • Price: 22 JOD (~$30 USD) one-way. Children typically half-price.
  • Booking: jett.com.jo, or in person at the Aqaba JETT terminal in central Aqaba.

The JETT terminal in Aqaba is in the city center, not at the airport — if you're arriving by air, allow time for a taxi from the airport to the terminal (about 10–15 minutes, 10 JOD). Most Aqaba hotels can arrange airport transfers and onward connections; ask at check-in.

Shared minibus from the bus station

The local option. Shared minibuses leave Aqaba's main bus station for Wadi Musa when full — no fixed schedule, no advance booking, no English-language signage. Cost is around 7–10 JOD per person, payable in cash to the driver. The catch is the wait time: vans depart when 8–12 passengers have boarded, which can be 30 minutes in peak season or 2 hours in low season.

For travelers on a tight schedule or arriving in the late afternoon, this isn't the right choice — the last vans typically leave by 15:00 and you risk being stranded. For budget travelers with morning flexibility, it's the cheapest option available. The bus station is in the older part of Aqaba near the Royal Yacht Club; any taxi driver will know it.

Private taxi

From King Hussein International Airport (AQJ) to Petra: 70–100 JOD for the car (up to 4 passengers), 2 hours travel time. This is what most travelers arriving on European budget flights end up taking, particularly groups of 3+ for whom the per-person cost drops to ~25 JOD, comparable to the JETT bus.

From central Aqaba to Petra: 60–80 JOD for the car. Cheaper than from the airport because you've already absorbed the airport-to-city taxi cost.

Negotiate the fare before getting in the car. White taxis at the airport are metered in theory but the meter is often ignored for longer trips — better to agree on a flat rate. Most drivers speak some English and a few are fluent. Some drivers will offer waiting time at Petra (5–10 JOD/hour) so you can use the same car for the return trip, which can be cheaper than two separate one-way fares.

Rental car

Rental car prices in Aqaba start at $28–35 USD per day for economy cars from the major international agencies (Sixt, Avis, Hertz, Budget, Europcar all have airport counters). Aqaba is a special economic zone, which means some tax benefits — rates here are typically 10–15% cheaper than equivalent rentals in Amman.

The drive from Aqaba to Petra is straightforward: north on the Desert Highway for about 100 km, then northwest toward Wadi Musa for another 30 km. The route is well-signed, the road is in excellent condition, and parking at Petra is free. Most Wadi Musa hotels have free parking as well.

From the Wadi Araba border (if you're crossing from Eilat)

A meaningful share of Aqaba-region Petra visitors arrive via the Wadi Araba/Yitzhak Rabin border crossing from Eilat. After clearing the border, you have three onward options:

Direct shared taxi to Petra: 25–35 JOD per person, takes 4 travelers when full. The fastest option — the taxis pool travelers as they emerge from the border. Drivers wait at the Jordanian side of the crossing.

Direct private taxi to Petra: 80–100 JOD for the car. Better for groups of 3+.

Taxi to Aqaba, then bus or onward taxi: Around 25 JOD from the border to central Aqaba, then 22 JOD JETT bus or 60 JOD taxi onward to Petra. More expensive total cost and slower than the direct options, but useful if you wanted to stop in Aqaba anyway for a meal, the Red Sea, or to use Aqaba as a base.

See getting to Petra from Eilat for the full border-crossing logistics, fees, and timing.

Why flying into Aqaba is underrated

For travelers whose Jordan trip is essentially "Petra and Wadi Rum," flying into Aqaba instead of Amman saves three hours of driving on each end of the trip. King Hussein International handles direct flights from:

  • London (Wizz Air, Ryanair — seasonal, typically October through April)
  • Istanbul (Turkish Airlines, Pegasus — year-round)
  • Several European cities including Munich, Berlin, Vienna, Warsaw, Helsinki, Riga
  • Cairo (EgyptAir)

The trade-off is that Aqaba has limited direct flights from North American or East Asian origins — most travelers from those regions will still connect through European hubs or Amman. But for European travelers specifically, Aqaba is often the cheaper and faster option. Worth checking before booking flights through Amman by default.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take from Aqaba to Petra?
About 2 hours by road. The drive is 130 km north along the Desert Highway, mostly through open desert with occasional villages. Tours and private cars take roughly 2 hours; the JETT bus takes about 2.5 hours with stops. The same route in reverse (Petra to Aqaba) takes the same time.
How much does the JETT bus from Aqaba to Petra cost?
Around 22 JOD (~$30 USD) one-way. JETT runs the route twice daily in each direction in peak season; check jett.com.jo for the current schedule. The route is far less booked than the Amman-Petra run and rarely sells out, but booking 1–2 days ahead is still safer.
How do I get from Aqaba airport to Petra?
Two main options. A private taxi from King Hussein International Airport (AQJ) to Petra runs around 70–100 JOD for the car. The cheaper option is to taxi from the airport to the JETT bus terminal in central Aqaba (~10 JOD, 10–15 minutes) and take the JETT bus north — total cost around 32 JOD, total time about 3 hours. For groups of 3+, the direct taxi is usually the better choice.
Is the shared minibus from Aqaba worth using?
For budget-conscious independent travelers, yes. Shared minibuses (called "service" in Jordan) depart from Aqaba's main bus station when full — no fixed schedule. Cost is around 7–10 JOD per person. They leave most often in the mid-morning and can fill up slowly outside peak season. The trade-off is the uncertainty: you might wait 30 minutes or 2 hours for the van to fill. For travelers on tight schedules, this isn't the right choice.
Can I fly into Aqaba instead of Amman?
Yes, and it's underrated. King Hussein International Airport (AQJ) in Aqaba handles direct flights from London (Wizz Air, Ryanair seasonal), Istanbul (Turkish Airlines, Pegasus), and several European cities including Munich, Berlin, Vienna, and Warsaw. For travelers focused on Petra and Wadi Rum without Amman, flying into Aqaba shaves three hours off your drive on each end. Aqaba is also a duty-free zone, which sometimes means cheaper rental cars and slightly cheaper fuel than Amman.
What's the deal with shared taxis from the Aqaba border?
Distinct topic — relevant if you're crossing from Eilat. After clearing the Wadi Araba border crossing, you'll find taxis (white minibuses or sedans) waiting on the Jordanian side. A direct shared taxi from the border to Petra runs around 25–35 JOD per person and pools 3–4 travelers together. A direct private taxi from the border to Petra is 80–100 JOD for the car. Both options are dramatically more efficient than going into Aqaba first and then back out to the JETT bus.

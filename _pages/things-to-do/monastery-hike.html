WHAT TO DO

The Monastery hike

800 carved stone steps climbing to the largest carved facade at Petra. Moderate difficulty, 60-90 minute ascent, the single most important extension beyond the Main Trail. Here's the practical breakdown — when to climb, how to pace it, and what's actually at the top.

The Monastery hike is the most rewarding single extension beyond Petra's Main Trail. The 800-step climb from the lower city to the Monastery (Ad-Deir, the carved facade larger even than the Treasury) takes most travelers 60-90 minutes and gains about 220 meters of elevation. The climb is moderately challenging, the destination is genuinely spectacular, and the views from the top justify every step of the ascent. For travelers spending a full day at Petra, the Monastery climb belongs on the must-do list alongside the Siq and the Treasury.

This page covers the practical mechanics: when to climb, how to pace yourself, what to bring, what's at the top, and how to handle the descent. The framing throughout: a manageable hike with extraordinary payoff, but with planning that makes the experience much better.

The basic trail

The Monastery trail starts from the Basin Restaurant area at the western end of the Petra lower city. From the Basin, walk west past Qasr al-Bint and the Lion Tomb (a smaller carved facade marking the trail start), then begin the climb up the carved stone steps cut into the rock.

The numerical specs:

  • Number of steps: ~800 (the count varies slightly depending on what you count as a step — irregular natural stones aren't always counted)
  • Elevation gain: approximately 220 meters from base to top
  • Horizontal distance: approximately 1 km one-way
  • Ascent time: 60-90 minutes for most travelers
  • Descent time: 30-45 minutes
  • Time at the top: 30-45 minutes recommended
  • Total round trip: 2.5-3.5 hours from Basin Restaurant

The climb itself

The trail has several distinct sections:

The lower switchbacks (first 30 minutes). The initial portion climbs through a winding canyon with multiple Bedouin tea stalls along the way. The grade is moderate; the carved steps are reasonably even. Several spots offer good rest points with views back toward the lower city.

The middle ascent (next 20-30 minutes). The trail continues up through narrower sections with steeper grades. The carved steps become more variable — some sections are smooth, others involve uneven natural stone. Most travelers feel the climb most acutely during this section.

The plateau approach (final 10-20 minutes). The trail emerges from the canyon onto a more open plateau, with the Monastery becoming visible in the distance. The grade eases significantly; the final approach is more about distance than vertical climbing.

The Monastery plaza. The trail concludes at the wide plaza directly facing the Monastery facade. The vast carved facade dominates the view; the plaza has Bedouin tea stalls and informal seating areas.

The Bedouin tea stalls

Along the trail, multiple Bedouin tea stalls offer welcome rest stops. Most are operated by Bdoul community members (see the Bdoul page). Standard offerings:

  • Tea: Black tea with sugar (sometimes with mint or sage). 1-2 JOD per cup.
  • Coffee: Bedouin-style coffee with cardamom. 1-2 JOD.
  • Bottled water: 2-3 JOD per liter (higher than down in the lower city due to the carry-up effort).
  • Light snacks: Dates, dried fruit, occasionally pita and za'atar.

The tea stalls are part of the experience — slowing down for a 10-15 minute rest, conversation with the operator, and the small economic exchange supports the Bdoul community. Most travelers find a tea stop during the ascent meaningfully improves the hike.

When to climb

Best timing: mid-to-late afternoon (14:00-16:00)

Three converging reasons make afternoon the optimal window:

Light on the Monastery facade. The Monastery faces east-southeast. Direct sunlight illuminates the facade most strongly in late morning and afternoon. For photographers and visitors valuing the visual experience, the warm afternoon light produces the most rewarding views.

Crowd patterns. Tour groups typically reach the Monastery in late morning and early afternoon, then return to the lower city. By 14:00-15:00, the Monastery plaza is significantly less crowded than mid-day. The contemplative experience at the top is much better with fewer people.

Temperature. In all seasons, afternoon temperatures on the trail are more comfortable than mid-day. Summer climbs at noon are punishing; the same climb at 15:00 is meaningfully more bearable.

Avoid late afternoon (after 16:00)

Climbing late enough that you might be descending in low light is risky. The trail is uneven and the descent in dim conditions becomes a hazard. Plan to be back at the Basin Restaurant by 17:00 in winter and 18:00 in summer to allow for the return walk to the Visitor Centre before the gates close.

Morning climbs

Working but suboptimal. The morning light on the Monastery is less dramatic than the afternoon light. The crowds are heavier from late morning forward. Some photographers prefer morning for specific image compositions; most travelers should default to afternoon timing.

Pacing strategy

A reasonable approach for the climb:

  1. Lunch at the Basin Restaurant first. Not on a full stomach (heavy lunch + climb = uncomfortable), but properly fueled. A small lunch around 12:30-13:00 sets you up well for a 14:00 climb start.
  2. Start at a moderate pace. Don't sprint the first section; you'll exhaust yourself before the steeper middle portion.
  3. Plan a tea stop midway. A 10-15 minute rest at one of the Bedouin tea stalls around the 30-40 minute mark recovers significantly.
  4. Push through the final ascent. The last 10-15 minutes are the easiest; the plateau approach barely climbs at all.
  5. Allow time at the Monastery. 30-45 minutes minimum — viewing the facade from multiple angles, walking to the additional viewpoints, photographing, having a final tea at the Monastery plaza tea stalls.
  6. Don't rush the descent. The 800 steps are harder on the knees going down than up. Take it slow; the descent will still be substantially faster than the climb.

What to bring

  • Water: at least 1 liter for the climb, more in summer. Refills available at the tea stalls but more expensive than down in the lower city.
  • Sun protection: hat, sunglasses, sunscreen. Most of the climb is exposed.
  • Layered clothing: warm layers can be peeled off as you climb; the plateau at the top is sometimes windier than the lower city.
  • Sturdy shoes: proper hiking shoes or sneakers with good grip. Avoid sandals; the uneven steps are hard on feet in flimsy footwear.
  • Cash: 5-15 JOD for tea stops and snacks along the way. Tips (1-2 JOD) for Bedouin hospitality are appreciated.
  • Camera: the views deserve documentation. The Monastery facade and the surrounding mountain views are both photographically rewarding.

At the top

The Monastery facade

The structure itself is the destination. 48 meters wide, 47 meters tall, carved from a single rock face. Larger than the Treasury (which is 40 meters tall, 28 meters wide). The architecture is Hellenistic in vocabulary but Nabataean in proportion and detail. See the dedicated Monastery page for the architectural deep-dive.

The carved facade differs from the Treasury in several ways:

  • Larger overall scale
  • Simpler carved decoration (less sculptural detail than the Treasury)
  • More weathered surface (the higher elevation exposes the facade to more wind erosion)
  • Significantly less crowded plaza for viewing
  • Different lighting throughout the day due to the east-southeast orientation

The interior chamber

A small chapel-style chamber inside the Monastery is accessible via the lower entrance. The interior is plain (no carved decoration) but worth a brief visit for the spatial experience of being inside the structure.

The additional viewpoints

Walking around to the back of the Monastery and continuing along the plateau leads to several additional viewpoints with dramatic panoramic views:

  • "The best view in the world": A Bedouin-operated viewpoint (with that signage) on the cliff edge beyond the Monastery, with views down to Wadi Araba and the mountains beyond.
  • The Nabi Haroun (Aaron's tomb) viewpoint: Slightly further along, with views of Jebel Haroun in the distance.
  • Sunset viewpoint: A specific position used for sunset photography — though staying for sunset isn't typically feasible given the descent timing.

The additional viewpoints add 30-45 minutes to the total time at the top. Worth doing for the panoramic perspectives.

The descent

Going down is harder on the knees than going up. The 800 steps in reverse take 30-45 minutes for most travelers — significantly faster than the ascent but with different physical demands. Walking poles help (if you brought any); a careful steady pace prevents knee strain.

Donkey rides for the descent are available at the top — 10-15 JOD typically. Combining "walk up, donkey down" is a common strategy for visitors who want to do the climb but worry about the descent stress.

Skip the Monastery if:

  • You have only 4-5 hours total at Petra (day-trip visitors from Aqaba or Eilat) — the climb consumes too much of the limited time
  • You have significant cardiovascular limitations or knee problems
  • It's mid-summer (July-August) and you're climbing at midday — heat exhaustion risk is real
  • You're on a tight schedule for Petra by Night or evening departure

For most multi-day visitors and full-day visitors arriving at 06:00, the Monastery climb belongs on the schedule regardless of these factors.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the Monastery hike?
About 800 carved stone steps, climbing roughly 220 meters in elevation over a distance of approximately 1 km. Most travelers take 60-90 minutes to ascend, depending on fitness and stops. The descent takes 30-45 minutes. Including 30-45 minutes at the top, plan for 2.5-3.5 hours round trip from the Basin Restaurant in the lower city.
How difficult is the climb?
Moderate difficulty. The 800 steps are uneven, sometimes narrow, and steep in places. The total elevation gain (220 meters) is comparable to climbing a 60-70 story building. Most reasonably fit adults can manage the climb with stops; older travelers and those with limited cardiovascular fitness may find it challenging. The path is exposed to sun on most sections — summer climbs are particularly demanding due to heat. Pace yourself, take breaks at the Bedouin tea stalls along the way, drink plenty of water.
When should I do the Monastery hike?
Mid-to-late afternoon (14:00-16:00) is the best timing. Three reasons: (1) The Monastery faces east-southeast, so late afternoon produces the warmest light on the facade for photography. (2) Tour groups have mostly left by mid-afternoon, so the Monastery plaza is less crowded. (3) The cooler afternoon temperatures are more comfortable than midday. Morning climbs work but lose the afternoon light advantage. Avoid late-afternoon climbs that don't allow time to descend before sunset.
Can I take a donkey instead of walking?
Yes — donkey rides up the Monastery trail cost 15-25 JOD round trip, arranged with Bedouin operators at the base of the climb. Many travelers take a donkey up and walk down, splitting the difficulty. Animal welfare concerns about heavy tourist use of donkeys have been raised; ethical travelers should consider walking and politely declining where possible. The walk down is considerably easier than the walk up; doing both on foot is feasible for most reasonably fit visitors.
What's actually at the Monastery?
The largest carved facade at Petra — 48 meters wide and 47 meters tall, more than 30% larger than the Treasury. The structure dates to the mid-1st century CE (peak Nabataean period) and probably served as a religious site rather than a tomb. Beyond the facade itself, the small plaza in front offers Bedouin tea stalls, dramatic mountain views, and a small chapel-style interior chamber. A short walk further leads to additional viewpoints including 'the best view in the world' (a Bedouin-operated viewpoint with that signage).
